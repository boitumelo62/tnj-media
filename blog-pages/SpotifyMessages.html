Tech News Spotify Introduces In-App Messaging for Sharing Music, Podcasts and Audiobooks

August 27, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Spotify has rolled out a new feature called Messages, allowing users to send music, podcasts, and audiobooks directly to one another inside the app. The tool brings private, one-on-one messaging into Spotify for the first time in years, reviving a concept the company abandoned back in 2017.

How It Works

The new feature lets users start a conversation by tapping the share button while listening to content. Once a recipient accepts a message request, they can chat, react with emojis, and continue exchanging audio recommendations. Suggested contacts are generated based on prior activity, such as collaborative playlists, family plans, or previously shared sessions. Conversations can be accessed by tapping the profile icon in the app.

Availability

Messages is rolling out this week to both Free and Premium users who are 16 years or older, starting in selected markets. Spotify says the goal is to make sharing easier and keep recommendations within the platform, rather than forcing users to switch to other apps.

Privacy and Safety

The company has emphasized user control, allowing people to block senders, decline requests, or disable the feature entirely through settings. All messages are encrypted in transit and at rest, and Spotify has built moderation systems to detect harmful or illegal content.

A Strategic Push Into Social

With nearly 700 million monthly listeners worldwide, Spotify sees in-app messaging as a way to deepen engagement and make discovery more organic. By building a social layer directly into the listening experience, the company hopes to strengthen connections between users and give creators more ways to reach audiences.

Mixed Reaction

While some listeners welcome the convenience of sharing directly in the app, others argue that the feature is unnecessary and that Spotify should prioritize long-awaited improvements such as higher-quality audio streaming.
