Media Women in Advertising Deserve Power Not Just Presence

September 4, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Popular Blogs:

Sports

The conversation about women in advertising needs to be more than a once a year moment. It can't just surface during Women’s Month or awards season – it has to be ongoing, intentional, and backed by real change. That’s what the founders of Ko.Kreate, Koketso Masisi and Kgothatso Maditse, are pushing for.

We’ve all heard the phrase, “women need a seat at the table.” But in advertising, just like in many industries, that seat isn’t enough anymore. Being there doesn’t mean you’re being heard, or that your ideas are being taken seriously.

A seat without influence is just symbolic, and for many women, especially women of colour, that symbolism comes without any real power or recognition. They’re at the table, sure – but the opportunities, the rewards, and the leadership often remain out of reach. Advertising likes to think of itself as a progressive industry full of creativity, fresh thinking and cultural relevance. That’s part of what draws so many women to it. It promises space to express yourself, to build, to disrupt. But once you're inside, the promise doesn’t always hold up.

Behind the award-winning campaigns and flashy slogans, there’s a tougher reality. One of the biggest issues? Pay. The gender pay gap is very much alive in advertising. Women are often carrying heavy workloads – taking on more because of understaffed teams or vague job descriptions – and many are highly qualified, often overqualified, with multiple certifications and training programmes behind them. Still, they’re underpaid. And when fair pay doesn’t come, the options are either to leave the industry or to bounce from job to job hoping to find a better deal. It's a cycle that keeps women starting over again and again, which hurts their long-term growth and drains the industry of talent.

Then there’s motherhood – still treated like a professional liability instead of a normal part of life. Policies may look good on paper, but in practice, they’re often set up in ways that penalise women for having children. Some companies offer partial pay or no pay at all during maternity leave if certain conditions aren't met. Others tie women to the company for long periods after returning from leave, even when the environment is toxic. The message this sends is clear: having a child is seen as a career setback, instead of the valuable life experience it is. But parenting builds strength, patience, empathy, and problem-solving – all of which are huge assets in leadership and creative roles.

Leadership is another space where women are told they’re welcome but aren’t always given what they need to succeed. Yes, more women are getting leadership titles, but they’re often left to figure things out alone. There’s no roadmap, no mentoring, no support. Learning how to negotiate, how to lead confidently in male-dominated rooms, how to grow – that’s all left to trial and error. And for women of colour, the challenges run even deeper. Their leadership is too often questioned or reduced to a diversity box ticked, their ideas dismissed until someone else – often male or white – repeats them and suddenly gets applause.

Credit is another sore spot. Too many women can point to successful campaigns, increased revenue or major wins that they worked hard on – but never got their names on. Their ideas, energy and leadership made things happen, but when the spotlight came, someone else stepped into it. That kind of betrayal doesn’t just sting – it stays with you.

But in spite of all this, women bring something powerful to the table. Our lived experiences mean we understand what it means to juggle multiple responsibilities. We are the audience advertising is trying to reach – we know what resonates. That insight is an advantage, not an afterthought. And yet, it’s still not valued the way it should be.

Women's perspectives should be central, not supplementary, to how this industry evolves.

So what needs to change?

First, fair pay. That’s non-negotiable. Agencies need to back up their values with actual transparency and accountability.

Second, proper support for working mothers. It’s time to move beyond performative policy and actually design workplaces that support people through all stages of life, including parenthood.

And third, real mentorship and sponsorship. Women need more than a title – they need guidance, tools, and people who are actively invested in their growth and success.

This is the kind of future Ko.Kreate is working toward. Born out of frustration and firsthand experience, it’s more than just a platform – it’s a movement. It’s about creating space where women, especially women of colour, can thrive – not just survive. It’s about collaboration, creativity, and community. The official launch earlier this year wasn’t just symbolic – it was a statement: we are here, and we are setting the agenda. Because women deserve more than a seat. We deserve a voice, we deserve credit, and we deserve respect. Anything less isn’t inclusion. It’s just window dressing.
