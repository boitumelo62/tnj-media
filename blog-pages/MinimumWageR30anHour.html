Business Minimum Wage to Rise Above R30 an Hour From March

February 4, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s national minimum wage will increase to R30.23 an hour from 1 March, marking the first time the threshold has moved above R30. The adjustment follows a five percent increase linked to inflation.

The higher rate will apply to most workers covered by the minimum wage framework, including those in domestic work, agriculture, retail and hospitality. Government says the increase is aimed at easing pressure on workers facing rising food, transport and utility costs.

Introduced in 2019, the national minimum wage was designed to address income inequality and improve baseline earnings. While the latest increase has been welcomed by labour groups, unions argue that wages still lag behind the real cost of living.

Economists note that the increase offers limited relief but is unlikely to significantly change household financial conditions, especially for workers supporting extended families or servicing debt.

Business groups have expressed mixed reactions. Some employers warn that higher wage costs could strain small businesses, while others say predictable annual adjustments help with planning and stability.

Labour inspectors are expected to monitor compliance, as enforcement remains a challenge in some sectors. Trade unions have urged workers to report violations to ensure the new rate is properly implemented.
