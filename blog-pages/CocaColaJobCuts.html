Business Job Cuts Loom at Coca Cola SA as Company Seeks Realignment

September 18, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Coca Cola Beverages South Africa (CCBSA) is currently considering a major restructuring plan that could lead to the loss of up to 680 jobs and the closure of some of its plants in the country. While the company hasn’t made a final decision yet, it has confirmed that it's begun formal consultations with workers and unions as part of a possible retrenchment process. The Food and Allied Workers Union (FAWU) confirmed they’ve been notified of this plan. According to them, the retrenchments would affect mostly cleaning staff a group they argue plays a crucial role in the food and beverage production process. The 680 potential job cuts represent nearly 9% of CCBSA’s workforce in South Africa, which currently stands at around 7,700 people.

The company says it’s facing financial challenges, and that’s why it’s considering closing its Bloemfontein and East London plants. However, the union isn’t convinced. FAWU believes this isn’t about financial hardship, but rather a strategic decision by CCBSA to restructure and “realign” the business. They argue the company isn’t in crisis and point to a recent investment as proof. In July this year, CCBSA invested R365 million in a new bottling line at its Midrand plant. This high-tech line can produce 72,000 bottles an hour and was introduced to meet growing consumer demand. The investment was part of a broader push into African markets, with the company publicly stating that it has ambitious growth plans across the continent.

Given this, the union is questioning how a company that’s expanding can, in the same breath, claim it needs to cut jobs. They also take issue with how CCBSA is handling the process especially with the company allegedly offering separation packages to workers before fully consulting with the unions. FAWU says that’s not just premature but illegal, and they’ve vowed to challenge the retrenchments. This news doesn’t exist in isolation. Several other major international companies operating in South Africa are also downsizing. Ford South Africa plans to let go of nearly 500 workers, Goodyear is cutting 900 jobs, and mining company Glencore has sent out retrenchment notices that could affect more than 3,000 employees. These waves of job losses are putting increasing pressure on local communities, as every worker supports not just themselves, but their families meaning the impact spreads far beyond the factory floor. What complicates matters further is the broader history of Coca Cola Beverages Africa (CCBA), which CCBSA is a part of.

Formed in 2015 through a merger involving Coca Cola, SABMiller, and Gutsche Family Investments, CCBA has undergone various ownership changes since. Coca Cola currently owns the majority stake, and there have been plans to list the company publicly. FAWU argues that this trend of international buy ins has consistently been followed by layoffs once legal restrictions expire.

So, while CCBSA insists that no decisions have been finalised and that it’s committed to treating affected workers fairly, the uncertainty is already being felt. The union remains skeptical of the company’s motives and the broader consequences for workers, communities, and the economy at large.
