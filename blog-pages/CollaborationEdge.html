Business Collaboration is the New Competitive Edge

August 20, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Some of the most exciting breakthroughs happen when two different worlds come together. At the point where they meet let’s call it “X” new energy is created, fresh ideas emerge, and sometimes, entire industries are reshaped. This is the magic of the Power of Two: when distinct forces collide to create something more powerful than they could on their own. Finding your own version of X isn’t just smart it can be what sets you apart completely. One of the most common and effective intersections today is between brands and creators. This is the heart of influencer marketing. Creators bring their own audiences, storytelling skills, and trust factor things brands can’t simply buy or build overnight. A great example of this is the KFC and Uncle Waffles collaboration.

KFC saw that Uncle Waffles’ energy and appeal matched their target youth market, and it paid off with a 125% spike in sales and campaign sell outs. Brands are also getting more creative, moving beyond one-off influencer deals to collaborating with groups of creators. That collective power allows them to reach wider audiences through different voices and styles in one go. For instance, the OnePlus 12 launch brought together four photographers, each with their own approach, to capture California’s natural beauty using the phone’s Hasselblad camera. The result? An engaging, multi layered campaign that felt personal and authentic to each audience.

Then there’s the power of brands joining forces with their fans. When companies invite their audiences to co create, it naturally strengthens loyalty. Lego’s Ideas platform is a great example fans submit their own set designs, and the most popular ones can actually become real Lego products. This kind of collaboration makes fans feel valued, seen, and involved. Nike has done something similar with its “By You” sneaker customisation program, giving people a chance to design their own kicks, turning products into personal statements. Partnerships between brands can also create exciting opportunities. When two companies with overlapping values or audiences come together, they can amplify each other’s strengths. A clear example is Pick n Pay’s partnership with FNB’s eBucks program.

Shoppers benefit from rewards while both brands enjoy increased loyalty and foot traffic. In the early stages alone, the collaboration already showed a 20% uptick in activity on Pick n Pay’s clothing and delivery platforms. Purpose is another powerful connector. When brands team up with sustainability or socially driven organisations, the impact goes well beyond business. These collaborations work best when they’re real, long term, and measurable. Take the WWF Nedbank Green Trust it’s been running for 35 years and has funded over 150 conservation projects across South Africa. These efforts help brands walk the talk on values, building deeper trust with their communities.

Music is another key arena where brands can forge meaningful connections. It’s personal, emotional, and identity shaping. Research has shown that music is a stronger marker of identity for many people than even religion or age. Smart brands that align with music authentically especially live events can create moments that resonate. Castle Lite’s “Unlocks” campaign nailed this by creating South Africa’s biggest hip hop event, syncing perfectly with the vibe of its audience. And finally, there are those unique moments in time where stories and timing align so perfectly that they capture public attention instantly. Like when Jannik Sinner won Wimbledon and Nike immediately released the simple but powerful message: “Winning heals everything.” Or when Astronomer brought in Gwyneth Paltrow right after her viral kiss cam moment unexpected, on point, and brilliant.

So, what’s your X? Where does your brand intersect with something or someone that sparks new life, new energy, and fresh direction? As Jhene Nel from Nfinity Influencer puts it, “The Power of Two is about co creation at the interface where two things bump up against each other for the first time. That’s often where the spark of innovation lives.”
