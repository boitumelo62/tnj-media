Media Deployment of Over 2,000 Soldiers to Fight Illegal Mining Expected to Cost R823 Million

The South African government’s decision to deploy more than 2,000 soldiers to assist police in tackling illegal mining and gang related violence is expected to cost taxpayers over R823 million.

The deployment involves members of the South African National Defence Force working alongside the South African Police Service to confront criminal networks involved in illegal mining activities, commonly linked to organised gangs known as zama zamas. Illegal mining has become a growing challenge in several parts of South Africa, particularly in abandoned or closed mines where criminal groups extract minerals without permits.

Authorities say these operations are often associated with violence, extortion and dangerous working conditions. Government officials argue that the scale and organisation of illegal mining syndicates have made it necessary to involve the military in support of law enforcement operations.

The deployment is intended to strengthen security operations, disrupt criminal networks and restore order in affected areas. However, the cost of the operation has sparked debate among policymakers and analysts. Critics question whether such a large financial commitment will deliver long term results or whether deeper structural solutions are required to address the root causes of illegal mining.

Security experts note that illegal mining is often driven by economic hardship, unemployment and organised crime, making it a complex problem that cannot be solved through enforcement alone. Despite these concerns, authorities maintain that the deployment is necessary to curb escalating violence linked to illegal mining activities.

The presence of military personnel is expected to provide additional support to police operations and improve security in areas where criminal groups have operated with relative impunity. The operation is expected to run for several months as authorities attempt to dismantle illegal mining networks and restore stability in affected communities.
