Business Nielsen to Exit South Africa Within 12 Months After Business Review

September 3, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Nielsen, a well-known global company that specializes in audience measurement and data analytics, has announced that it will be leaving South Africa within the next 12 months. This decision comes after a business review, which the company says it regularly carries out in all its markets. Although it was a tough call, Nielsen confirmed it has informed its staff and key partners, including the Broadcast Research Council (BRC), about the closure of its South African operations.

For years, Nielsen has played a major role in providing audience data in South Africa, especially for the country’s Television Audience Measurement Survey (TAMS), which is managed by the BRC. It also offered other services like media planning, marketing analytics, and content insights from its offices in Rosebank, Johannesburg. The BRC, which handles audience measurement for both radio and TV, says it has already found a new company to take over these services from Nielsen. While the formal handover is still being finalized, the BRC is working closely with Nielsen to ensure there’s no disruption in data reporting during the transition.

The full changeover will take about 15 to 18 months, and interim plans are being put in place to keep the data flowing in the meantime. More details are expected within the next two weeks. Nielsen stressed that while it’s exiting South Africa, this doesn’t mean it’s pulling back from the broader Europe, Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) region. It plans to continue expanding and serving clients in other markets. In the meantime, it will continue to meet its obligations in South Africa during the transition period.

Nielsen’s exit adds to a growing list of international companies that have recently pulled out of South Africa. Consulting giant Bain & Company shut down its operations after losing trust in the local market. Historic players like Anglo American have also scaled back, moving away from certain sectors like platinum mining. Shell has downsized its operations too, selling off its local retail and fuel businesses. Several international banks including HSBC, BNP Paribas, Barclays, Standard Chartered, and Société Générale have also left, though most had relatively small footprints in South Africa. Still, these moves highlight a broader trend of global businesses rethinking their presence in the country.

That said, it’s not all one-way traffic. South Africa is still attracting investment, especially in travel, leisure, and mining. Club Med is opening its first resort here next year, and luxury hotel brand OKU is making its debut by revamping the old Ritz Hotel in Cape Town. Tata Motors is also making a comeback with new cars for the local market, and an Australian company, West Wits Mining, is set to launch the country’s first new underground gold mine in over a decade. While some big names are leaving, others are still finding opportunities in South Africa’s complex and shifting business landscape.
