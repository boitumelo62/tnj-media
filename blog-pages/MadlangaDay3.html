Media “The Gaps Are Starting to Show”: Madlanga Commission Wraps Day 3, Masemola to Testify

September 22, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Madlanga Judicial Commission of Inquiry Officially Begins

Day three of the Madlanga Commission delivered one of the most compelling sessions to date, with witnesses offering testimony that both strengthened and complicated the inquiry’s developing narrative. The commission, chaired by Justice Madlanga, is investigating allegations of misconduct, failures of oversight, and systemic weaknesses within several state institutions.

Yesterday’s proceedings featured a mix of corroborating evidence and contested claims. Several witnesses detailed lapses in procedure and raised questions about whether senior officials acted swiftly enough when concerns were first reported. Legal representatives pressed for clarity on timelines, with some exchanges becoming noticeably tense as the commission sought to separate fact from speculation.

Analysts say the testimony given on day three could be crucial in shaping the commission’s interim findings. “We are starting to see where the gaps were and who knew what, and when,” one legal observer commented, noting that the evidence presented appears to be building toward a clearer picture of institutional accountability.

Public interest has surged as the inquiry has progressed, with a growing number of citizens and media outlets closely following each development. The atmosphere in the hearing room reflected that heightened attention, with packed seating and intense scrutiny of every exchange.

Day four of the commission continues today, with several high-profile witnesses expected to appear, including Fannie Masemola, the National Commissioner of the South African Police Service (SAPS). His testimony is anticipated to address the role of law enforcement during the key incidents under investigation, as well as the measures taken to prevent similar failings in the future.

Observers expect Masemola’s appearance to be a critical moment in the commission’s work, potentially clarifying the extent of police involvement and decision-making at the highest level.
