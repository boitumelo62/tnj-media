Media Inside the Debate That’s Shaping the Future of African Marketing

September 29, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Marketing’s Mismatch: Consumer Attention vs. Media Investment

Last week in Johannesburg, something pretty powerful happened. Women in Marketing (WiM) Africa, in collaboration with Kantar, brought together more than 60 marketing leaders, brand experts, and Gen Z voices for the second round of their Real Talk Debate series. The big question on the table, Is the marketing industry truly ready for Africa’s Gen Z? It’s an important one to ask especially when you consider that over 60% of Africa’s population is under 25. This generation is not just another target audience; they’re reshaping the way brands think, act, and engage.

The event didn’t just bring in big names like Khensani Nobanda from Nedbank, Ammarah Issufo from Unilever, Farah Mehdi from TikTok, and others. It created a space where senior leaders and Gen Z themselves could have honest conversations real, raw, and future focused. There was even a fireside chat with Monalisa Zwambila of Riverbed Agency, led by Renissa Gounden from Kantar Consulting. What came out of the discussion was clear: traditional marketing just doesn’t cut it anymore. Gen Z wants brands that see them, listen to them, and create with them not just market to them.

This generation cares deeply about issues like social justice and identity. They’re not just scrolling they’re building businesses, protesting, live streaming activism, and challenging the status quo. They expect brands to be bold, authentic, and to back up their words with real action. According to live polls during the debate, 84% of the attendees said brands are suffering from “purpose fatigue” because they’re not following through on their promises. Co-creation came up as a major theme. Instead of just sending out briefs, brands should be working side by side with Gen Z creators.

Empathy, especially from women in leadership, was highlighted as one of the most underrated but powerful tools in the industry. Some stats from the room really hit home. 86% of attendees said the industry has made some strides in connecting with Gen Z but it’s still not enough. Almost half said that the biggest risk of missing the mark with this audience is losing long term loyalty. And more than a third pointed out that Gen Z’s real disruptive power comes from how they elevate each other through peers, not celebrities. Andrea Djan Krofa, who founded WiM Africa, put it simply, “Gen Z isn’t just changing the game they’ve already changed it.”

Looking ahead, all of the insights and perspectives from this debate will feed into a major white paper that WiM Africa is releasing in October 2025. And the conversation doesn’t stop here the Real Talk Debate series is heading to Nairobi and Lagos in early 2026. If anything’s clear, it’s this: Gen Z is not the future of marketing in Africa they’re the present. And if brands want to stay relevant, it’s time to really listen.
