Media Musk Accuses Apple of Unfair App Store Practices Amid Ongoing Feud with Altman

August 11, 2025 ● TNJ Staff

Elon Musk, owner of X and AI platform Grok, has threatened legal action against Apple, alleging the tech giant has made it “impossible” for apps to compete with OpenAI products in its App Store.

Musk also criticised OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman, calling him a “liar” in response to Altman’s claim that Musk uses his social media platform to advance personal and business interests. The remarks mark the latest escalation in a long-running public dispute between the two billionaires, who co-founded OpenAI before parting ways.

Apple partnered with OpenAI in June 2024, integrating ChatGPT into its services. However, there is no public evidence the company prioritises OpenAI over rivals, with AI tools such as DeepSeek and Perplexity regularly appearing at the top of App Store rankings.

In a follow-up post on X, Musk questioned why Apple’s “Must Have” section did not feature X or Grok, despite claiming X is the “number one news app” globally and Grok ranks fifth among all apps. According to UK download charts, ChatGPT holds the top spot for free apps, with Grok in third place, while X is outside the top 40.

Altman responded indirectly by sharing a report alleging that Musk’s posts on X were being given greater visibility in users’ feeds. The tension between Musk and Altman dates back nearly a decade, rooted in disagreements over OpenAI’s direction. Originally founded in 2015 as a non-profit dedicated to developing artificial general intelligence (AGI) for the benefit of humanity, OpenAI later created a for-profit arm in 2019, a move Musk has repeatedly criticised.

In March 2024, Musk sued OpenAI, claiming it prioritised profit-making, particularly for major investor Microsoft. Although he dropped the lawsuit later in the year, OpenAI filed a countersuit in April, accusing him of attempting to undermine its work. The rivalry has included legal battles, public accusations, and even Musk’s unsuccessful $100 billion bid to acquire the company earlier this year. Apple has not yet commented on Musk’s latest claims.
