Business Eskom announces major grid expansion

November 17, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Eskom has announced an ambitious plan to build fourteen thousand kilometres of new transmission lines in what is being described as one of the largest grid expansion projects in the country’s history. The initiative, projected to cost four hundred and forty billion rand, is intended to strengthen the backbone of South Africa’s electricity network and ease long standing supply pressures that have affected households, businesses, and municipalities.

Electricity Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa said the expansion forms a central part of government’s broader effort to stabilise the grid, following recent progress made after the extension of the Koeberg Nuclear Power Station’s operating licence. He said the country had reached a point where bold investment in new infrastructure was no longer optional but essential if South Africa hoped to support growing demand and accommodate new energy sources.

According to Eskom, the new lines will help integrate additional generation capacity from renewable projects which have been delayed in recent years due to grid congestion. The utility said the planned network will improve transmission reach in under served provinces, reduce bottlenecks, and make it easier to move power efficiently across regions.

Ramokgopa acknowledged the scale of the undertaking but insisted the benefits would be significant. He said a reinforced grid would allow Eskom to manage supply more predictably and reduce the risk of severe power interruptions as the country transitions toward a more diverse energy mix.

Industry analysts noted that the expansion could also support private energy investments which have accelerated since reforms opened the market for larger independent producers. However, they warned that delays in procurement, engineering capacity, and land acquisition could complicate timelines if not managed carefully.

Eskom said the project will be rolled out in phases over several years with priority given to areas experiencing the greatest strain. Communities located along the proposed routes will be consulted as part of the environmental and regulatory approval process.

The announcement has been welcomed by several business groups who said the investment sends a positive signal about the state’s commitment to resolving the electricity crisis. They added that a stronger grid, coupled with the progress reported by the minister, could help restore confidence in the country’s energy trajectory after years of uncertainty.

Work on the first sections of the transmission expansion is expected to begin once final permissions are secured.
