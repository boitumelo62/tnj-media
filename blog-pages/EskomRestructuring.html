Media Eskom Restructuring Aims to Open South Africa’s Energy Market

July 7, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa is advancing efforts to restructure its power utility, Eskom, with the goal of creating a more competitive and transparent electricity market. The changes are part of a broader national reform to improve energy reliability and encourage private sector participation.

The plan involves breaking Eskom into three separate entities: generation, transmission, and distribution. This separation is intended to ensure fair access to the national grid for all power producers, especially those involved in renewable energy projects.

One of the central pillars of the reform is the creation of an Independent Transmission Project Office. This body is responsible for unlocking private investment to help expand the country’s transmission network. The initial goal includes building over 1,000 kilometres of new power lines to allow for more renewable energy generation, particularly in provinces like the Northern Cape and Western Cape.

Operational improvements at Eskom have also provided some breathing room. The availability of power has improved slightly, with new units from Medupi and Koeberg power stations expected to boost capacity further. These upgrades support the gradual shift toward a more open and efficient energy system.

Despite progress, several key challenges remain. Energy regulators are still working to resolve disputes over grid access, enable virtual wheeling of power between producers and buyers, and issue renewable energy certificates. These delays could affect South Africa’s competitiveness in global trade, especially with new international carbon regulations on the horizon.

The government is encouraging collaboration between public entities and the private sector, with shared planning and decision-making being central to the reform strategy. This partnership is seen as essential for building a sustainable, investor-friendly power market that can meet growing demand and support economic growth.
