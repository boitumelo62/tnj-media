Media CPUT Students Warn of Crisis as Funding Delays Threaten Housing

March 5, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

A growing crisis is unfolding at Cape Peninsula University of Technology as students warn that delays in financial aid payments are leaving many at risk of eviction from their accommodation.

Student representatives say a number of learners who depend on funding from the National Student Financial Aid Scheme have not yet received their allowances. Without those funds, some students have struggled to pay rent or cover essential living costs, raising fears of homelessness and disruption to their studies.

One student leader described the situation as life threatening, arguing that the uncertainty around funding has placed enormous pressure on vulnerable students. Many rely entirely on financial aid to pay for accommodation, food and transport while pursuing their studies.

Reports from campus suggest that landlords and private accommodation providers are demanding payment, leaving students caught between outstanding allowances and the risk of being forced out of their rooms.

Student groups have called on both the university and NSFAS to urgently resolve the delays. They argue that prolonged uncertainty could severely affect academic performance and mental wellbeing, particularly for students who have relocated far from home to study.

Funding delays have periodically affected universities across South Africa, with administrative backlogs and verification processes often slowing down the release of payments. For students who rely on the support, even short delays can have serious consequences.
