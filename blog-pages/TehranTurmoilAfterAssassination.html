Media Tehran in Turmoil After Assassination of Iran’s Supreme Leader

March 2, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Iran has been plunged into political shock and regional tension following the killing of its Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, in a series of coordinated military strikes on Tehran.

Iranian state authorities confirmed that Khamenei, who had led the Islamic Republic since 1989, was killed after explosions struck a heavily secured compound in the capital late Saturday night. Several senior figures linked to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps are also reported to have died in the attack, marking one of the most significant blows to Iran’s leadership structure in decades.

The United States and Israel publicly acknowledged responsibility for the operation. US President Donald Trump described the strike as a decisive move against what he called a destabilising regime, while Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the mission was aimed at neutralising threats to regional security.

In response, Iran launched missile and drone attacks targeting Israeli territory and US military facilities in the Gulf. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian condemned the killing as an act of aggression and declared a period of national mourning, vowing that the country would respond firmly.

Scenes across Tehran showed thousands gathering in organised mourning ceremonies, many dressed in black and chanting slogans in support of the Islamic Republic. However, reports also indicate mixed reactions in some areas, reflecting divisions within Iranian society over Khamenei’s long and controversial rule.

International reaction has been swift. Russian President Vladimir Putin criticised the strike, calling it a dangerous escalation and urging restraint. Several global leaders have appealed for calm amid fears that the confrontation could spiral into a broader regional conflict.

With no clear successor immediately announced and tensions running high, analysts warn that the power vacuum in Tehran could trigger internal instability while further inflaming an already volatile Middle East.
