Media Mchunu defends task team decision

December 3, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Suspended Police Minister Senzo Mchunu has begun formally defending his decision to dissolve the Political Killings Task Team, arguing before the Madlanga Commission that the unit had become ineffective and needed structural overhaul rather than continued approval.

During a focused two-hour appearance, Mchunu said the task team was initially created to stabilise political violence, particularly in KwaZulu Natal, but evidence showed that the unit had drifted from its mandate. He described a pattern of “fragmented operations, low conviction rates and a lack of clear coordination” which he believed placed ongoing investigations at risk.

According to Mchunu, internal reviews conducted shortly before he suspended the unit revealed inconsistent investigative standards, duplicated case handling and frequent clashes with provincial structures. He argued that political murders required a more integrated model, with improved intelligence collection and a stronger central command, rather than an isolated team working in silos.

He told the commission that the disbandment was not a reckless move, but a step taken after long standing concerns were raised by senior police officials. Mchunu said replacing the task team with a restructured intelligence driven approach would strengthen case progression and reduce political interference.

Commissioners questioned whether dissolving the team created investigative gaps that allowed unresolved cases to pile up. Mchunu maintained that the intention was always to rebuild a more efficient unit and denied allegations that his decision was influenced by political pressure.

The commission will continue hearing evidence from senior officers, analysts and community representatives as it assesses whether the task team should have been reformed rather than shut down. The outcome may determine whether Mchunu’s decision was justified or whether it contributed to renewed instability in politically sensitive regions.
