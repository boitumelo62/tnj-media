Media NASA’s Hubble Captures Dramatic High Speed Interstellar Comet

August 13, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

NASA’s Hubble Space Telescope has delivered the most detailed imagery yet of an interstellar comet, designated 3I/ATLAS, traveling through our solar system at a remarkable speed of approximately 130,000 mph (209,000 km/h), making it the fastest comet ever recorded.

The comet was first observed on July 1, 2025 by the Asteroid Terrestrial Impact Last Alert System (ATLAS) network in Chile and confirmed as interstellar shortly thereafter. Hubble’s highly detailed images, taken on July 21 when the comet was about 277 million miles (446 million km) from Earth, showcase a pronounced teardrop-shaped dust plume and a faint dusty tail emitted from its icy nucleus.

Based on these observations, scientists have estimated the nucleus to measure between 1,000 feet (320 meters) and 3.5 miles (5.6 km) across, significantly narrowing earlier broader guesses. Astronomer David Jewitt likened the comet’s fleeting appearance to “glimpsing a rifle bullet for a thousandth of a second,” emphasizing the difficulty of tracing its origin path.

Though its speed and hyperbolic trajectory mark it as a true interstellar intruder, the comet poses no threat to Earth. Its closest approach to the Sun, known as perihelion, is expected around October 29 or 30, 2025, bringing it just inside the orbit of Mars at a distance of roughly 1.8 astronomical units (AU), safely away from our planet.

Astronomers are seizing this rare opportunity to observe 3I/ATLAS up close. Further investigations are underway using telescopes such as the James Webb Space Telescope, TESS, Swift Observatory, and ground-based facilities to analyze its composition and behavior as it continues its journey.

These interstellar visitors are exceptionally rare, with 3I/ATLAS being only the third confirmed interstellar object to enter our solar system after ‘Oumuamua in 2017 and Comet Borisov in 2019.
