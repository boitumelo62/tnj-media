Business Spur Defies Economic Pressures with Strong Sales and Bold Expansion Plans

August 21, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Despite the tough economic environment for consumers, Spur Corporation has managed to expand both locally in South Africa and internationally, and it has plans to keep growing. In its financial results for the year ending June 30, 2025, Spur highlighted how recent interest rate cuts by the South African Reserve Bank have helped boost consumer spending. But even with lower borrowing costs and slowing food inflation, people still aren’t seeing much of an increase in their disposable income, which means discretionary spending remains tight. Spur isn’t just competing with other restaurants anymore; it's now facing pressure from supermarket chains that are offering more ready-made meals and convenience food options. Despite these challenges, Spur says its franchisees and operational teams have stayed strong and focused.

The group saw solid trading performance, with franchised restaurant sales increasing by 8.3% compared to the previous year. Spur continues to focus on delivering high quality family dining experiences and is using AI-driven data analytics to better target its marketing, particularly to its customer loyalty clubs. Franchisees have responded well to the company’s updated branding, with 51 outlets now operating under the new look. The revamp at Panarottis is also gaining momentum, with more than half of its restaurants now featuring the refreshed design, thanks to good customer feedback.

By the end of June 2025, Spur operated 724 restaurants across 14 countries, up from 701 the previous year. In South Africa, the group opened 31 new restaurants but closed 15, while internationally it added 15 and closed eight. Every brand under Spur including Spur Steak Ranches, John Dory’s, Panarottis, RocoMamas, and Speciality Brands grew their number of outlets over the past year. Looking ahead, Spur plans to open 42 new restaurants in South Africa and 14 more overseas during the 2026 financial year. This growth is being supported by the group’s strategy of securing prime locations for new stores.

Financially, Spur had a strong year. Group revenue rose by 11.2% to just under R3.9 billion, helped by a 46.3% increase in retail store sales and a 10.1% jump in sales from its manufacturing and distribution division. Profits also grew solidly: profit before tax was up 17.5% to R401.7 million, and headline earnings rose by 16.5% to R275 million. As a result, the group increased its total dividend by a substantial 40% to 299 cents per share.

Despite a tough consumer climate and growing competition, Spur is expanding steadily and delivering strong financial results. With a portfolio of 10 restaurant brands and growing presence across regions and countries, the group is confident about its long-term future, even though it acknowledges that the economic challenges facing consumers are likely to continue in the near term.
