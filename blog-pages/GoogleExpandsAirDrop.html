Tech News Google Expands AirDrop Style Sharing Across Android Devices

Google is steadily expanding support for AirDrop style file sharing on Android, moving the feature beyond its early Pixel only availability and positioning it as a core part of the Android platform. What began as a limited experiment is now shaping up to be a broader push toward seamless cross platform sharing between Android and Apple devices.

Initially introduced through Quick Share in late 2025, the feature marked a notable shift in Android’s approach to interoperability. For the first time, Android users could send files directly to iPhones and Macs using AirDrop, without relying on cloud services or third-party apps. At launch, however, access was restricted to the Pixel 10 lineup, and transfers came with clear limitations tied to Apple’s visibility settings.

Despite those constraints, the move was widely seen as a breakthrough. Native operating system level compatibility between Android and AirDrop addressed a long-standing frustration for users who move between the two ecosystems.

That exclusivity is now starting to fade. Recent Android Canary builds show the feature appearing on additional devices, and Google has confirmed that Pixel 9 and Pixel 9 Pro models are next in line. More significantly, the company has stated that AirDrop compatible Quick Share will not remain limited to Pixel hardware.

Google has since clarified that the feature is being developed as a platform level capability built directly into Android. Rather than acting as a device specific workaround, the system is designed to function across manufacturers once enabled. Security has been a central focus, with Google relying on established protocols and external reviews to ensure the feature is suitable for wide deployment.

By embedding this functionality into Android itself, Google reduces the need for manufacturers to develop custom sharing solutions. This opens the door for brands beyond Pixel to adopt the feature through standard software updates.

While no detailed rollout timeline has been shared, Google has acknowledged plans to support non Pixel devices. Manufacturers such as Samsung and Nothing have already expressed interest, suggesting broader adoption is likely.

For users, the experience is straightforward. Supported Android devices can send photos, videos, and documents directly to Apple hardware over a local connection, without uploading files to the cloud. Apple’s existing AirDrop settings remain unchanged.

Although access is still limited, the expansion signals a clear shift. Cross platform file sharing is no longer being treated as a niche experiment, but as a standard feature of the Android experience.
