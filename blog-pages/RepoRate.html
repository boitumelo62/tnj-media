Business South Africa’s Repo Rate Cut to 7.00%: What It Means for You

August 5, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The South African Reserve Bank has lowered the repo rate from 7.25% to 7.00%, marking another step in its ongoing effort to stimulate the economy amid slow growth and stable inflation. The repo rate, short for repurchase rate, is the interest rate at which the central bank lends money to commercial banks. This rate directly influences the cost of borrowing across the country, from home loans and vehicle finance to credit cards and business loans.

When the repo rate decreases, as it has now, borrowing becomes slightly cheaper. This is because commercial banks typically pass the lower rate on to consumers by reducing their own prime lending rates. For households, this means lower monthly repayments on debt. For example, a person with a R1 million bond could see their monthly repayments drop by around R170 to R200, depending on the terms. On the flip side, savers may earn slightly less on interest-bearing accounts as deposit rates usually decline in line with the repo rate.

The Reserve Bank’s decision follows four consecutive rate cuts since late 2024, as inflation has remained comfortably within the bank’s target range of 3% to 6%. This latest 25 basis point reduction, announced on August 1st, was a unanimous decision by the Monetary Policy Committee. With inflation subdued and economic activity still lagging behind pre-pandemic levels, the central bank has room to loosen monetary policy to support growth without risking a spike in prices.

Economists believe this is a positive sign for consumers and businesses, particularly those looking to borrow, invest or expand. At the same time, the Reserve Bank has made it clear that it will remain cautious, making future decisions based on economic data rather than political or market pressure.

The move is expected to offer some relief to South Africans dealing with rising living costs and sluggish wage growth. It may also help stimulate sectors like property, small business, and retail, all of which rely heavily on access to affordable credit.

As it stands, the repo rate at 7.00% reflects a carefully calculated balance: supporting the economy without letting inflation expectations run wild. More cuts may follow if conditions remain favourable, but for now, South Africans can breathe a little easier knowing that the cost of borrowing has come down once again.
