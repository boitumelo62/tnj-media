Media New Qantas Flight Boosts Travel Between South Africa and Australia

December 11, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Qantas has introduced a new direct flight connecting Johannesburg and Perth, creating a much smoother travel experience between South Africa and Western Australia. The new service, operated as flights QF65 and QF66, also makes it easier for South Africans travelling onward to New Zealand thanks to the recently launched Perth Auckland route. Qantas says this addition strengthens its growing international hub in Western Australia, linking three continents and adding over 150,000 seats to its network.

The airline credits the new Perth Johannesburg connection partly to improved border services, as Perth Airport has invested in more staff, upgraded equipment, and expanded facilities to handle increased passenger flows. According to Qantas International CEO Cam Wallace, the customer response has been overwhelmingly positive since the route was announced in May. The first flight to Johannesburg is nearly full, and early bookings suggest strong interest in direct travel between Perth, South Africa, and Auckland. Wallace also highlighted that Qantas’ partnership with Airlink will give travellers easier access to other major South African destinations, including Cape Town.

South Africa’s Minister of Tourism, Patricia De Lille, welcomed the new route, saying it boosts the country’s connectivity with Oceania. She added that it will support the growth of inbound business travel, especially as South Africa prepares to host major events such as Meetings Africa 2026 and Africa’s Travel Indaba. Improved links with Australia also strengthen ties ahead of global sporting events like the ICC Men’s Cricket World Cup in 2027.

The Johannesburg Perth service will operate on Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Sundays with a flight time of about 9 hours and 55 minutes. Qantas will use its A330 aircraft, offering 27 business class seats in a 1-2-1 layout and 224 economy seats. The return trip from Perth to Johannesburg will take roughly 11 hours and 15 minutes. This new service puts Qantas in direct competition with South African Airways, which restarted its own Perth route last year. The demand is expected to be strong, as Australia remains a popular destination for South African emigrants, with more than 200,000 South African born residents currently living there.

South African travellers can also look forward to more international options soon. Air Europa has been approved to operate direct flights between South Africa and Spain, with plans for three flights a week once regulatory and operational steps are completed. Within Africa, Airlink is expanding with a new twice weekly Johannesburg Nacala service from February 2026, linking South Africa to an important port city in Mozambique. The airline is also preparing a new leisure route to Zanzibar, scheduled to begin in June 2026 and operated with its latest Embraer E195 E2 aircraft. Tickets for the Zanzibar flights are already on sale, giving travellers even more choice for the year ahead.
