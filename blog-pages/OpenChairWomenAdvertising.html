Why Real Mentorship Matters in Content Creation

During Loeries Creative Week 2025, something extraordinary happened at the V&A Waterfront in Cape Town. Open Chair’s “Friends in High Places” event took mentorship to new heights literally by transforming the iconic Cape Wheel into a moving boardroom. Sixty young women in advertising and marketing got the rare opportunity to meet and speak one on one with industry leaders, all while taking in sweeping views of the city from 40 metres above the ground.

Organised by Open Chair, a non profit focused on empowering the next generation of women leaders, the event broke the traditional mold of mentorship. Instead of sitting in a boardroom or a conference hall, mentees rotated through the Cape Wheel’s capsules each ride becoming a private, safe space for honest conversation. For 90 minutes, the sky wasn’t just a view, it became the setting for something far more powerful: career guidance, connection, and confidence building.

Inside those capsules, stories were shared openly. Topics like imposter syndrome, navigating career transitions, leading with purpose, and the challenges women face in the industry weren’t just discussed they were understood. It was personal. It was real. And for many of the young women involved, it was the first time they’d had such direct access to senior professionals who truly saw and supported them. The event wasn’t just about advice; it was about being heard, feeling empowered, and walking away with a renewed sense of possibility. That energy was echoed by the event’s key partner, KFC Africa, whose support went beyond just sponsorship.

Their team showed up fully, as mentors and role models. With women making up 60% of KFC’s workforce across 22 African countries, the company’s commitment to nurturing female talent is woven into its DNA. Leaders like Nolo Thobejane, Unathi Ncunyana, and Andra Nel shared personal journeys and practical insights that left a lasting impact. According to Suhana Gordhan, founder of Open Chair, the Cape Wheel became more than just a venue it became a metaphor. As the capsules ascended, so did the dreams and confidence of the young women inside them. That’s the heart of what Open Chair does: create spaces where women don’t just participate, but rise.

Board members Melina McDonald, Roanna Williams, and Fran Luckin all echoed the same sentiment mentorship matters, but it has to be real, and it has to be sustained. It’s not enough to open the door we have to keep it open long enough for women to step through, grow, and eventually lead. What happened on the Cape Wheel wasn’t just an event. It was a movement proof that when women are seen, supported, and given the chance to connect, the entire industry gets stronger. Because when we rise together, we lead tomorrow.
