Tech News Apple Pay Eyes India but Arrives with One Hand Tied Behind Its Back

Apple is preparing to bring Apple Pay to India later this year, setting the stage for a high profile entry into one of the world’s most competitive digital payments markets. However early reports suggest the service will launch with major limitations that could leave it struggling to match local rivals.

According to a new report, Apple is planning a late 2026 rollout of Apple Pay in India and has already begun talks with banks, card networks, financial institutions and government bodies to prepare for the launch. The service is expected to be available on iPhones and Apple Watches.

At launch, Apple Pay is likely to focus solely on tap to pay functionality. This would allow users to make contactless payments at physical stores using NFC technology, provided their debit or credit cards are added to their devices. While this enables basic in store payments, it represents only a small slice of how Indians typically transact.

Crucially, Apple Pay is not expected to support Unified Payments Interface integration. UPI dominates India’s digital payments ecosystem and is widely used for everyday purchases, bill payments and peer to peer transfers. Without UPI, Apple Pay would be unable to offer instant bank to bank payments that millions of users rely on daily.

This puts Apple Pay at a disadvantage when compared to Samsung Pay, which is already part of Samsung Wallet on Galaxy smartphones and smartwatches. Samsung Pay supports NFC and QR code payments and offers full UPI functionality, making it far more versatile for Indian consumers.

Samsung Wallet also goes well beyond payments. It allows users to store boarding passes, tickets, digital keys, identity cards, medical records, membership cards and passwords, turning the smartphone into a full digital wallet rather than just a payment tool.

Industry observers say Apple’s cautious approach may be driven by regulatory complexity and the dominance of UPI, but the limited feature set could slow adoption. In a market where convenience and all in one functionality are key, a tap only payment service may struggle to gain traction.

While Apple Pay’s arrival will still be significant given Apple’s growing presence in India, Samsung appears to have little immediate reason for concern. Unless Apple expands its offering to match local payment habits, Apple Pay may arrive as a premium brand name with surprisingly narrow appeal.
