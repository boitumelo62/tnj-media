Media Madlanga Commission to Hear Remote Testimony

October 15, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Madlanga Commission of Inquiry will hear testimony from its next witness remotely over the next two days to ensure their safety. Their identity will remain confidential, and the account will be delivered through an intermediary, a step the commission describes as extreme but necessary. Spokesperson Jeremy Michaels said the measure balances the need for transparency with protecting those involved in the inquiry.

The commission continues to expose serious allegations of corruption and criminality in South Africa’s justice system. On Monday, witnesses revealed that senior police officials facilitated illegal operations including drug trafficking and illicit mining, suggesting collusion with criminal syndicates that undermined the integrity of the police service. Tuesday’s hearings focused on political interference, with testimonies showing that some political figures pressured law enforcement agencies to influence investigations and outcomes.

The remote testimony included details of communications between Vusimuzi "Cat" Matlala, a known criminal figure, and Major General Lesetja Senona, head of the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation in KwaZulu-Natal, raising questions about corruption within the DPCI. Technical difficulties briefly halted the session, prompting the commission to read the witness’s full statement into the record to ensure the evidence was preserved while protecting their identity.

The commission is set to reconvene on Thursday, 16 October 2025, at 09:30, when it will also address legal disputes over whether future testimonies will be public or in-camera. The inquiry continues to shed light on deep-rooted issues in South Africa’s justice system, aiming to promote accountability and reform while keeping the safety of witnesses a priority.
