Business Momentum Group Announces R1 Billion Buyback

September 17, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Momentum Group has achieved record earnings in South Africa, with the company announcing plans to buy back another R1 billion in shares. For the financial year ending on June 30, 2025, the group reported normalized headline earnings of R6.26 billion, a 46% increase from the previous year, which had already been a record for the company. Normalized earnings exclude non recurring items like one off gains or losses, providing a clearer view of a company’s ongoing performance.

When looking at more conventional profit measures, the group’s basic earnings grew by 55% to R6 billion, while its basic headline earnings also grew by 48% to R6 billion. Operating profit surged by 52%, rising from R3.6 billion to R5.48 billion. The group attributes this strong earnings growth to its diversified portfolio and the effective execution of its impact strategy. Momentum Group’s various business units all contributed to the results, with key highlights being solid annuity profits from Momentum Investments, improved new business profitability at Metropolitan Life, higher earnings from Momentum Corporate’s group risk business, a much improved underwriting result in Momentum Insure, and strong underwriting performance in Guardrisk.

Additionally, positive changes in actuarial assumptions and favorable investment market returns further supported the group’s earnings. CEO Jeanette Marais expressed pride in the progress made toward the company’s strategic objectives, which include aggressively investing in advice to drive growth and leveraging the synergies between its businesses. She emphasized that Momentum is a clear market leader in independent financial adviser distribution and highlighted the innovative solutions created for clients, particularly in the areas of employee benefits and estate planning. The group also completed a significant systems migration involving Momentum Retail, Metropolitan, and Africa, which is expected to save over R100 million annually.

Given its strong financial position, the board has approved an additional R1 billion share buyback, building on the R2 billion in buybacks initiated in the previous year. The group also declared a final dividend of 90 cents per share, bringing the total dividend for the year to 175 cents per share. Despite the challenging economic environment in South Africa, Marais was optimistic about the group’s performance. She acknowledged that pressure on clients' disposable income could impact new business volumes and margins, but noted that the Impact strategy remains a solid foundation for disciplined execution and operational excellence.
