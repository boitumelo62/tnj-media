Netflix Deal Puts DStv’s Most Popular Content at Risk Despite the promising improvements coming to DStv in South Africa, there’s a real possibility that its content lineup could take a serious knock after Netflix’s move to buy Warner Bros. On the surface, things looked positive: subscribers were told to expect better value through free package upgrades, cheaper decoders and more content as Canal+ takes charge. But now the worry is that some of DStv’s most important international channels and shows could disappear.

About a week ago, MultiChoice warned customers that 12 major Warner Bros. Discovery channels might be removed before the end of the year. Negotiations to renew the rights to broadcast these channels after 31 December 2025 have stalled, reportedly because the two sides can’t agree on price. Channels like CNN, Cartoon Network, Discovery, Food Network, HGTV, TNT Africa and several others are all at risk.

The situation became even more complicated when Netflix announced its massive R1.4 trillion deal to buy Warner Bros. Although the deal still needs regulatory approval and is expected to be finalized only in late 2026, it has already caused uncertainty. Before the takeover happens, Warner Bros. Discovery plans to spin off its linear TV business into a separate company called Discovery Global. That might give MultiChoice a chance to keep some channels if Netflix chooses not to focus heavily on live TV and if Canal+ is willing to meet the new pricing demands.

The bigger danger lies in Netflix gaining control of Warner Bros. and HBO’s on demand catalog. This collection is a huge part of what makes both DStv and Showmax attractive. Netflix has already said it wants to add major titles everything from The Big Bang Theory and The Sopranos to Game of Thrones, The Wizard of Oz and the entire DC Universe. It also plans to pull in all HBO and HBO Max content, giving Netflix access to one of the strongest entertainment libraries in the world.

For Showmax, this is especially troubling. As of early December 2025, Showmax carried 191 HBO series alone. When you include Warner Bros.’ full movie and series lineup, hundreds of titles could vanish. Reports suggest that both the linear channels and the on-demand content were part of MultiChoice’s strained negotiations with Warner Bros. Discovery. Even if only the on demand titles fall away on 1 January 2026, it will significantly reduce the value of both DStv and Showmax subscriptions.

All of this is happening just as MultiChoice and Canal+ are trying to win back subscribers by offering more value without increasing prices. Decoder costs have been lowered, and DStv now allows more people to stream on the same account. These changes were meant to strengthen DStv’s position after Canal+ completed its takeover.

Canal+ now controls DStv, DStv Stream, GOtv and Showmax, giving the group access to over 14 million customers across Africa. The company has ambitious plans for the future. Canal+ CEO Maxime Saada previously spoke about creating a single “super app” that brings together local shows, African sport, international movies, global series and Canal+’s massive European catalog. David Mignot, who oversees Canal+ Africa and MultiChoice, highlighted that combining Canal+ and MultiChoice libraries would allow them to offer more variety than ever before. Canal+ brings a huge collection of European and American films, while MultiChoice remains unmatched in African entertainment and sports.

Still, even with these strengths, losing Warner Bros. and HBO content would be a major blow. Those titles are some of the biggest reasons South Africans subscribe and without them, DStv and Showmax could find themselves in a much tougher position.
