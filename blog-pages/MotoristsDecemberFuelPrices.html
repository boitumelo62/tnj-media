Media Motorists Warned of Higher December Fuel Prices as Under Recoveries Rise

November 19, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South African motorists have been advised to prepare for a sharp rise in fuel prices in December after new data from the Central Energy Fund revealed deepening under recoveries across all major fuel grades. The data shows that international oil prices have remained elevated while the rand continues to trade under pressure, creating a difficult cost environment for local fuel imports.

Officials say the under recoveries are widening daily and indicate that motorists could face increases across petrol, diesel, and paraffin when the next adjustment is announced. Analysts warn that this may be one of the most expensive festive seasons for travel in recent years if the current trends continue through the remainder of the month.

Industry experts point to three key drivers behind the expected increases. The first is global oil price volatility, which has remained stubborn despite efforts by major producers to stabilise supply. The second is the weakening performance of the rand, which raises the cost of importing petroleum products. The third is rising international shipping and refining costs that feed directly into the daily fuel price calculations.

Transport companies say they are bracing themselves for another round of price pressure that is likely to filter into food distribution and consumer goods. Long distance bus services and courier companies are also reviewing their December schedules and cost structures. Many expect to make adjustments once the official announcement is confirmed.

Consumer advocacy groups have urged the government to accelerate its review of the fuel price formula, arguing that households cannot absorb further increases without relief. They have also called for intervention to ease the impact on low income communities, especially those dependent on paraffin for cooking and heating.

For now, motorists are advised to plan for possible increases and monitor the daily indicators as the Central Energy Fund continues to track international market shifts ahead of the official price adjustment.
