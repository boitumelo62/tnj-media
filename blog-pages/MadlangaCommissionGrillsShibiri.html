Media Madlanga Commission Grills Shibiri Over ‘Cat’ Matlala Funds as Ramaphosa Defends Secrecy of Interim Report

March 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Madlanga Commission of Inquiry has intensified scrutiny of former officials and their financial dealings, with Richard Shibiri coming under pressure over claims that money from controversial businessman Vusimuzi “Cat” Matlala was used to cover expenses. The commission questioned Shibiri after financial records suggested that nearly R20,000 had been spent at luxury retail stores, raising doubts about whether the funds were truly used for legitimate purposes.

During testimony, Shibiri maintained that the money was intended to cover operational expenses.

However, commissioners challenged that explanation, noting that the spending pattern appeared inconsistent with basic work related costs. The purchases at high end stores triggered suspicion that the funds may have been used for personal benefit rather than the purposes initially claimed.

The inquiry is part of a broader investigation into alleged corruption, financial misconduct and the possible abuse of influence involving several individuals connected to Matlala. Evidence leaders have indicated that the commission aims to establish whether financial transactions were used to facilitate improper relationships between business figures and officials.

While testimony continues behind closed doors in some instances, the public has been waiting for the commission’s interim findings. However, President Cyril Ramaphosa has stated that the interim report cannot yet be released to the public. According to the presidency, making the document public at this stage could prejudice witnesses who have not yet appeared before the commission.

Ramaphosa’s comments have sparked debate about transparency and accountability. Critics argue that the public deserves insight into the commission’s progress, especially given the seriousness of the allegations being investigated. Supporters of the decision, however, say protecting the integrity of the process is essential to ensure that witnesses can testify freely without outside pressure or influence.

The presidency maintains that the commission must complete key phases of testimony before any preliminary findings are made public. Officials say the goal is to prevent the report from influencing or intimidating potential witnesses who may still come forward with crucial information.

As hearings continue, attention remains fixed on the testimony of figures like Shibiri, whose financial explanations may play a key role in determining whether deeper networks of influence and corruption existed within the structures under investigation.
