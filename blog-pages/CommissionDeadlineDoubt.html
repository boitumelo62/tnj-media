Media Commission Deadline in Doubt as Key Testimony Delayed

March 10, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s Madlanga Commission of Inquiry could miss its looming deadline after a crucial witness postponed his testimony, raising concerns about whether the investigation will complete its work on time.

The commission, which is probing allegations of criminality, corruption and political interference within South Africa’s criminal justice system, was expected to wrap up proceedings by 17 March. However, the testimony of suspended Ekurhuleni Metro Police Deputy Chief Julius Mkhwanazi has now been pushed back to 23 March, placing the commission outside its original timeframe. (EWN)

The delay came after Mkhwanazi requested a postponement due to medical reasons. His testimony is considered significant because he is expected to address serious allegations relating to misconduct within the Ekurhuleni Metropolitan Police Department.

The inquiry, chaired by retired Constitutional Court Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga, was established in 2025 following explosive claims about corruption and political interference within law enforcement structures. The commission has spent months hearing testimony from senior police officials, whistleblowers and government figures as it attempts to uncover the extent of alleged criminal networks operating inside state institutions

Several dramatic developments have already emerged during hearings, including testimony about alleged links between organised crime figures and senior police officials. The investigation has also drawn attention after the assassination of a whistleblower connected to the inquiry last year, which heightened concerns about the dangers faced by witnesses.

With Mkhwanazi’s appearance now scheduled after the commission’s deadline, legal experts say the government may need to formally extend the inquiry’s mandate for it to complete its work and compile a final report.

For now, the postponement means the country will have to wait longer for potential answers to some of the most serious allegations facing South Africa’s law enforcement agencies.
