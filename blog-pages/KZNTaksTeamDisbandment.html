Media “Disbanding of Killings Task Team Was Politically Influenced” – KZN Police Commissioner

September 18, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

KwaZulu-Natal police commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi has told the Madlanga Commission that the decision to disband the province’s political killings task team was “suspicious” and appeared to be politically motivated.

Mkhwanazi made the comments during testimony at the commission, which is probing political violence and the state’s response to it. He revealed that the unit, which had been set up to investigate a spate of high-profile assassinations, was shut down without clear justification, despite its ongoing investigations into sensitive cases involving politicians and local power struggles.

“The decision to disband the task team was unexpected and raised serious concerns,” Mkhwanazi said. “It came at a time when we were making significant progress in some of the province’s most complex murder cases.”

KwaZulu-Natal has long been a hotspot for politically linked killings, particularly around municipal elections and party leadership contests. The task team was originally created to coordinate efforts between provincial and national police, intelligence services, and prosecutors, in an attempt to break cycles of violence that have claimed dozens of lives.

Civil society groups and opposition parties have echoed Mkhwanazi’s concerns, calling for a full explanation from the government on why the task team was scrapped. They warn that the move risks undermining public confidence in the police and could embolden those behind political hits.

The Madlanga Commission is expected to make recommendations later this year on how to curb political killings, with many stakeholders pushing for the reinstatement of a dedicated investigative unit. The Commission continues today.
