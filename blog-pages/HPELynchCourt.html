Business London High Court Rules HPE Entitled to Over £700 Million ($943M) from Estate of Late Tech Entrepreneur Mike Lynch

A High Court judge in London has ruled that Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) is owed more than £700 million, roughly $943 million, in a civil fraud case involving the late British tech entrepreneur Mike Lynch and his former finance chief, Sushovan Hussain.

The dispute stems from HPE’s acquisition of Lynch’s software firm, Autonomy Corp., in 2011. HPE alleged that financial information from Autonomy was deliberately misrepresented, leading the company to overvalue the deal at approximately $11 billion.

Judge Robert Hildyard, citing HPE’s losses, awarded approximately £646 million, including £51.7 million for deceit claims and another £47.5 million for ancillary damages. This is significantly below HPE’s original £3.2 billion claim, a figure the judge termed “substantially exaggerated.”

The ruling lands amid renewed attention following Lynch’s tragic death in August 2024, when his superyacht sank off Sicily during celebrations of his earlier acquittal in U.S. criminal proceedings tied to the same deal. He and seven others, including his daughter, died in the accident.

HPE’s leadership, represented by CEO Antonio Neri, stated that the civil action was in shareholders’ interests. The company has already reached a confidential settlement with Hussain and awaits a further hearing in November to finalize interest payments and currency conversion rates.

In a posthumous statement, Lynch criticized HPE’s approach, accusing it of inflating its claims by up to 80 percent. He also took aim at the UK civil trial, lamenting the reliance on “hearsay evidence from the U.S. that we were never able to question or cross examine,” contrasting it with the more robust scrutiny he experienced in the U.S. criminal case.

Lynch’s estate, estimated to be worth around £500 million, now faces the threat of bankruptcy if the court’s ruling is upheld. His family has said it is considering an appeal, arguing that the damages were improperly assessed and the claims exaggerated.
