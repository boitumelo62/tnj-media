Media Constitutional Court Upholds Ruling on Shared Parental Leave

October 3, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Constitutional Court has confirmed a landmark ruling that allows parents to divide maternity leave between them rather than reserving the full period for the mother alone. The judgment reinforces that the previous arrangement which granted exclusive leave to birthing mothers was inconsistent with the Constitution.

The ruling means that parents can now share the four months of maternity leave previously allocated to the mother. This provides families with flexibility in deciding how to divide caregiving responsibilities and represents a significant step toward recognising equal parenting roles.

The case began when a couple from Limpopo challenged the Basic Conditions of Employment Act on the basis that its provisions on maternity and parental leave discriminated unfairly between men and women. The High Court agreed and ordered that the leave be split until lawmakers revised the legislation.

In its decision the Constitutional Court upheld that ruling and confirmed that the interim arrangement will remain in place until Parliament makes the necessary amendments. Parliament has been given two years to align the law with constitutional standards.

The Court highlighted that equal participation of both parents in childcare promotes gender equality and protects the dignity of families. Employers across the country will now be required to adjust their leave policies to accommodate this change.

The judgment is expected to have far reaching effects on parental rights workplace culture and the broader conversation around equality in South Africa.
