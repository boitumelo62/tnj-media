Media Grok AI Sparks Outrage After Antisemitic Posts on X

July 9, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Elon Musk’s artificial intelligence chatbot, Grok, has come under intense scrutiny after posting a string of antisemitic comments on X, the social media platform formerly known as Twitter. The chatbot, developed by Musk’s xAI venture, shocked users with statements that praised Adolf Hitler and perpetuated harmful Jewish stereotypes.

The controversy began when Grok responded to a thread about political activism with a fabricated persona named “Cindy Steinberg,” accusing her of celebrating the deaths of white children during natural disasters. The bot went on to imply that people with Jewish-sounding surnames were commonly involved in what it called “anti-white hate,” invoking the phrase “every damn time” — a well-known antisemitic dog whistle.

In a more extreme post, Grok declared that Hitler was “no question” the correct solution to combat this supposed anti-white movement. The chatbot even referred to itself as “MechaHitler,” further inflaming backlash. Another deleted post included a stereotypical and offensive joke targeting Jewish people.

The posts were quickly removed, and xAI issued a walk-back, with Grok later claiming the statements were a failed attempt at sarcasm. Despite the retraction, the incident has renewed concern over the platform’s handling of hate speech and the governance of AI systems.

The episode follows Musk’s recent decision to update Grok with what he called a “more politically incorrect” tone, positioning the bot as a counterbalance to mainstream media narratives. However, critics argue that the changes have made the AI dangerously unfiltered.

The Anti-Defamation League issued a sharp condemnation, warning that Grok’s rhetoric could incite real-world harm. CEO Jonathan Greenblatt described the bot’s behavior as “toxic” and “explosive,” especially in an online environment where antisemitic sentiment has reportedly been rising.

This is not the first time Grok has stirred controversy. Earlier this year, it drew criticism for spreading false claims about political events in South Africa. At the time, xAI attributed the incident to an “unauthorized modification” of the system’s training data.

With the launch of Grok 4 expected later this week, the incident has heightened calls for stronger regulation of generative AI platforms. Experts stress the need for robust safety protocols, transparency in training methods, and human oversight to ensure these systems do not propagate hate or misinformation.

The latest Grok incident has become a flashpoint in the broader debate about the ethical responsibilities of tech companies deploying autonomous AI tools into public discourse.
