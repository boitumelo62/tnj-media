Business Dawie Roodt Says More South African Banks Should Be Allowed to Fail

November 13, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Renowned economist Dawie Roodt believes that South Africa should allow more banks to go bankrupt because, in his view, this is a natural and necessary part of a free market. Speaking on The Main Stage Podcast with Aidan Brown, Roodt shared that South Africa’s banking system is one of the safest in the world, but perhaps too safe.

He explained that the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) has built a highly regulated environment with strict rules on how banks must operate. Through its Prudential Authority, the Reserve Bank makes sure all local banks are financially sound, requiring them to hold enough capital, maintain liquidity, and undergo regular stress tests to ensure they can survive tough economic times. If a bank ever faces a liquidity problem, the SARB can provide emergency support to keep it afloat.

Additionally, since April 2024, the Corporation for Deposit Insurance (CODI) has been protecting ordinary South Africans by guaranteeing deposits of up to R100,000 if a bank collapses. This system has helped make South Africa’s financial sector extremely stable and trusted.

However, Roodt argues that this very stability has a downside. As the chief economist at the Efficient Group and a former Reserve Bank employee of nine years, he praised the SARB’s competence but criticized it for being too controlling. He believes the strict oversight makes it nearly impossible for new banks to enter the market, resulting in an expensive, tightly held system dominated by just five major players.

Roodt said he would actually like to see more banks and even more businesses fail because that’s how a true free market functions. In such a system, he said, weak institutions naturally fall away, freeing up capital and opportunity for new, better-managed companies to emerge. According to him, preventing this natural cycle of “creation and destruction” makes the economy less dynamic.

He also believes that South Africans should take more personal responsibility in choosing which banks to trust, rather than relying on the Reserve Bank to guarantee safety. In his words, people should “shop around and select a bank which is well managed,” instead of assuming that all banks are equally secure.

For Roodt, allowing poorly run banks or “zombie” companies to fail is not a sign of weakness it’s a sign of a healthy, evolving economy. He believes South Africa should make it easier for new businesses and banks to emerge, even if that means accepting that some will inevitably go under along the way.
