Business South Africa’s Growth Outlook Improves, but Challenges Remain

January 15, 2026 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa’s economy is showing signs of gradual improvement, with the World Bank forecasting growth of 1.3 percent in 2025. While this projection points to a modest recovery, some analysts remain cautious, questioning whether economic expansion will even reach one percent.

The official growth rate for the year will only be confirmed once gross domestic product figures for the final quarter of 2025 are released in March. Even so, current estimates suggest a better outcome than in recent years. Economic growth remained well below one percent in both 2023 and 2024, making the latest projections an indication that momentum is slowly building.

According to the World Bank, several factors are supporting this upward trend. Improved electricity availability has eased pressure on businesses, while agriculture has delivered a strong performance. Rising business confidence has also played a role, alongside higher than anticipated prices for key commodities such as gold and platinum.

Despite these gains, South Africa continues to trail behind the global economy. The country’s growth rate has lagged international averages for more than a decade, a pattern that remains unchanged even with the current recovery. In comparison, the World Bank expects the global economy to expand by 2.6 percent this year, highlighting the gap South Africa still needs to close.

While the outlook is more positive than in recent years, economists caution that sustained reforms and investment will be needed to translate incremental growth into long term economic stability.
