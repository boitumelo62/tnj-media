Media Border officials seize counterfeit goods worth R2.8 million at Kosi Bay

September 25, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South African border authorities intercepted a large consignment of counterfeit goods at Kosi Bay on the border with Mozambique, valued at more than R2.8 million. The discovery marks one of the most significant seizures in the region this year and highlights the ongoing challenges facing border security.

According to officials, the haul included fake clothing, footwear, and electronic items, all intended for illegal distribution within South Africa’s retail markets. The goods were being smuggled across the border through informal routes often exploited by criminal syndicates.

Law enforcement agencies have noted a rise in counterfeit trade, which not only undermines legitimate businesses but also results in revenue losses for the government. Authorities say such operations are often linked to organized crime networks that profit from exploiting porous borders.

Customs and border police have vowed to intensify patrols and inspections at high-risk points, stressing that combating the counterfeit economy is crucial to protecting consumers and safeguarding jobs. The latest seizure at Kosi Bay is being treated as a warning that smuggling rings remain active and well-organized, requiring constant vigilance.

Officials confirmed that investigations are under way to track down those behind the consignment, with arrests expected as authorities close in on the syndicate responsible.
