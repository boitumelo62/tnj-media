Media Court Clarifies Limits on Strike-Related Compensation Claims

March 26, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s Constitutional Court has delivered a significant ruling that reshapes the legal landscape around protected strikes and employer claims. In a judgment involving retail giant Massmart, the court found that the Labour Court does not have the authority to award compensation for losses suffered due to conduct during protected strike action. This decision provides clarity on the boundaries of labour law and reinforces the legal protections afforded to workers engaging in lawful industrial action.

The case stemmed from disputes over damages incurred during a strike, with employers seeking financial recourse for operational losses. However, the Constitutional Court drew a firm line, emphasizing that protected strikes are a fundamental right under South African law. While misconduct during such strikes may still carry consequences, the court ruled that compensation for losses falls outside the Labour Court’s jurisdiction.

Legal analysts say the ruling strengthens the position of workers and trade unions, ensuring that the right to strike is not undermined by financial penalties. At the same time, it raises concerns among business leaders who argue that companies may now have limited avenues to recover damages caused during industrial action.

The judgment is expected to influence future labour disputes and could prompt calls for legislative review to address gaps in accountability. For now, it stands as a landmark affirmation of constitutional protections in the workplace, balancing the rights of employees with the operational realities faced by employers.
