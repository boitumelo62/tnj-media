Media Why the Wall Between Media and Creative Has to Fall

September 8, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

For a long time, people have talked about bringing creative and media teams together, but in reality, it rarely happened. That’s changing because it has to. Marketers no longer have the luxury of keeping these teams apart. Budgets are tighter, audiences are spread thin across platforms, and everyone expects results almost instantly. This shift isn’t just about new tools or technology. It’s about how people behave how they consume content and how they expect brands to show up.

Campaigns today run across TV, digital, social media, and outdoor all at once. And for that to work, every part of the campaign needs to be pulling in the same direction. If creative and media teams are working in separate silos, things fall apart. The way things used to work where creative and media operated on separate tracks just doesn’t cut it anymore. Even if the creative is brilliant and the media plan is solid, if they’re developed independently, the campaign won’t land the way it should. As Johanna McDowell, CEO of the Independent Agency Search and Selection Company, puts it: the old way of doing things is done.

Marketers now want partners who can think creatively about media and strategically about creative all in the same room, ideally at the same time. It’s not so much about who sits where in the org chart anymore. What matters is whether each campaign is hitting the right audience, with the right message, at the right moment and doing it across the right channels. Whether you’re working with internal teams, external agencies, or a bit of both, that alignment is what counts.

Marketing leaders today are under pressure from all sides. There’s a constant balancing act between being agile and scaling up. Smaller teams can move fast, but larger ones bring depth and infrastructure. The most effective setups manage to combine both.

And while some brands have moved parts of marketing in-house, that approach has its limits. Many are starting to realise that external partners bring something valuable: fresh thinking, a mix of skills, and a front row seat to what’s happening at the cutting edge. AI is only speeding things up. It can help generate and refine content in record time, but only if creative and media teams are sharing insights and learning from each other continuously. Otherwise, you end up with fast output that misses the mark.

What’s emerging now is a more integrated way of working. Briefs are created with both creative and media in mind from the start. Feedback is faster, so creative teams can make adjustments based on real performance data. Teams are hiring people with hybrid skills who understand both sides of the equation, breaking down the walls that used to separate them.

The most effective models share two key features: shared accountability where both creative and media teams are responsible for delivering business results and real time feedback, so everyone can adjust on the fly.

This doesn’t mean everything has to be perfect. It’s about creating systems that can respond quickly and work together. As McDowell says, no single structure will fix everything. What matters is that creative and media are in sync and moving together, no matter where they’re based.

At the end of the day, for CMOs, the message is clear: integration is no longer optional. In a world where every cent counts, the real question is no longer should you integrate but how fast you can get there.
