Media High Court Judge Ordered to Vacate State Residence in Cape Town

March 9, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Public Works Minister Dean Macpherson says a Western Cape High Court judge has been ordered to vacate a state owned residence in Cape Town after reportedly no longer qualifying for the government provided housing benefit.

The property forms part of a group of residences allocated to certain members of the judiciary while they are serving in positions that require them to live close to the courts where they work.

According to the Department of Public Works and Infrastructure, eligibility for these homes depends on several conditions, including the judge’s current role and location of assignment.

Macpherson confirmed that steps have been taken after it was determined that the judge no longer met the criteria for occupying the residence.

The department said the move forms part of a broader effort to ensure that state properties are used correctly and that government benefits are allocated fairly.

Officials have recently begun reviewing the use of several government owned properties to ensure compliance with regulations governing official housing.

Legal analysts say the situation highlights the importance of transparency in the allocation of government housing benefits and the responsible management of public assets.
