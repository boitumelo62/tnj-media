Media Matlala Accuses Former Minister Bheki Cele of Receiving R500,000 in Cash Payments

November 27, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Parliament’s Committee to Hear Testimony from Cat Matlala

Explosive allegations emerged at the corruption inquiry yesterday when Vusimuzi “Cat” Matlala testified that he personally delivered cash payments amounting to R500,000 to former Police Minister Bheki Cele. The testimony has intensified scrutiny around alleged private financial networks linked to political figures.

Matlala told the commission that the payments took place over multiple occasions during a period he described as “politically sensitive.” He alleged that he had been instructed by intermediaries operating within private security structures to collect envelopes containing cash and deliver them directly to Cele at predetermined private locations. According to Matlala, these exchanges formed part of a wider informal arrangement in which money was used to influence decisions, secure political protection, and maintain access to senior officials.

Detailing one of the alleged transactions, Matlala said he was contacted by a known business associate who arranged the handover of an envelope containing a substantial sum of cash. He claimed he then delivered it to Cele at a residence agreed upon in advance. He further stated that these handovers occurred discreetly and were coordinated verbally, leaving minimal traceable evidence.

Under questioning, Matlala conceded that he could not provide written messages, bank records, or electronic communications linking Cele to the payments. However, he insisted that there are individuals within the security sector who, if called to testify, can confirm the role they played in facilitating the payments. The commission is expected to subpoena some of these individuals to verify Matlala’s claims.

Cele has not responded publicly to the testimony, though sources close to him have described the allegations as unfounded and politically motivated. Legal analysts note that while the absence of physical evidence may complicate the investigation, corroborating witness testimony could be enough to widen the scope of the inquiry.

The allegations come at a time when public trust in policing remains under pressure, and the commission has signalled its intent to investigate any claims suggesting that senior officials may have engaged in off-the-record financial arrangements. Civil-society groups have called on investigators to ensure that the inquiry follows every lead and examines all witnesses implicated by Matlala.

Proceedings will continue later this week, with the commission expected to press Matlala for further detail while determining whether additional subpoenas are warranted. The allegations have already placed significant attention on the inquiry’s next steps, as investigators work to establish whether Matlala’s testimony reflects individual misconduct or a broader pattern of corruption.
