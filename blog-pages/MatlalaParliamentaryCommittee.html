Media Matlala set to face parliamentary committee at 11am today

November 26, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Vusimuzi Cat Matlala, a businessman widely referenced in political spaces and alleged to have ties to a drug ring, is scheduled to appear before the parliamentary ad hoc committee at 11am today. The appearance was confirmed by the committee chair Soviet Lekganyane on Tuesday evening after Matlala and his lawyers requested a later start to allow final preparation.

Lekganyane spoke to media from Kgosi Mampuru Prison where the committee has temporarily moved its hearings for the week specifically to receive evidence from Matlala. His legal team sent a formal request earlier in the day, noting that they were struggling to prepare due to the extensive documents and affidavits involved and the constraints of working with a client under detention

Matlala is currently held in Kgosi Mampuru Two Correctional Facility in Pretoria, following attempted murder charges in relation to his former girlfriend and actor Tebogo Thobejane. With the committee conducting proceedings inside the prison, concerns have been raised about consultation conditions between Matlala and his defence.

His lawyer, Matlhatsi Abram Madira, described the situation as extremely difficult, pointing to network failures inside C Max and an absence of proper facilities for lengthy consultations. He said that the building itself made preparation slow and unproductive and warned that meeting the original Wednesday deadline could undermine the quality of evidence.

Madira further stated that Matlala’s mental state had worsened during his incarceration. He reported signs of disorientation and confusion during recent consultations, saying the legal team believed the conditions in C Max were affecting their client’s capacity. These concerns had also been highlighted during bail proceedings.

The defence has asked for the hearing to be postponed to allow Matlala time to prepare properly, noting that he is willing to cooperate but currently lacks access to documents, laptops and consistent communication. They requested a response from parliament by Tuesday afternoon, warning that they would seek an urgent order if necessary.

ActionSA MP Dereleen James rejected the postponement request, calling it a strategy to delay the committee’s work. She insisted that the process would continue and that Matlala must appear and give evidence as planned.
