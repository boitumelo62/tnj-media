Media Why Agencies Still Matter and How They Must Evolve

November 12, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

To Stand Out, Stop Looking Sideways

So, what’s the real point of using an agency these days? It’s a fair question and one that both clients and agencies need to ask. The old model, where agencies worked separately, took briefs, and delivered neatly packaged outputs, is fading fast. The world has changed. Businesses today need more than just a supplier, they need partners who understand their goals, move quickly, and deliver real results not just a flashy campaign.

In the past, agencies were often go betweens. Clients talked to an account manager, who then passed instructions through a maze of internal teams. It was slow, rigid, and sometimes felt like working through a wall of fog. Today’s best agencies break down those barriers. They embed themselves into a client’s world, get to grips with the real challenges, and co-create solutions. It’s not about producing a TV ad or a press release anymore; it’s about helping businesses grow, influence audiences, and change behaviours in meaningful ways.

A few big forces are driving this shift. Artificial intelligence has transformed how agencies operate. Tasks that used to take weeks writing copy, editing videos, analysing data can now be done in days. But AI isn’t a magic fix. It can be biased, inaccurate, or lifeless if used blindly. The trick is using it wisely, combining speed with creativity and human insight. Think of AI as the moving walkway at an airport: it helps you move faster, but you still need to steer the direction.

Data is another major factor. With privacy laws like GDPR and South Africa’s POPIA, companies are becoming more careful about who handles their information. Many now prefer to manage their own data instead of outsourcing it. This makes trust more valuable than ever. Agencies that can help clients use their data ethically and intelligently without overstepping will build stronger, longer lasting relationships.

Economic pressures have also reshaped the landscape. Budgets are tighter, deadlines shorter, and expectations higher. Clients want to see real results increased sales, better engagement, improved sentiment not just “likes” or impressions. This has forced agencies to get smarter and leaner, proving that creativity often shines brightest under constraints. And in this global, digital world, borders matter less than ever. South African agencies, for instance, are proving their value on the global stage, offering high quality creativity and strategy at competitive rates. Flow Communications is a good example: international work now makes up almost half of its business, showing just how global the playing field has become.

So what does the agency of the future look like? It’s agile, integrated, and outcome-focused. It’s about blending strategy, design, PR, and digital thinking into one seamless approach. It’s purpose-driven, helping brands not just say what they believe, but live it. It’s powered by technology but always centred on people using AI to enhance, not replace, human creativity. And most importantly, it’s embedded in the client’s world, acting as a true partner rather than an outsider.

At the heart of it all, the reason for working with an agency hasn’t disappeared; it’s evolved. Brands today need consistency across every platform, from their website to their social feeds. They need stories that connect and strategies that deliver real impact. Agencies that can provide this balancing technology, creativity, and authenticity are the ones that will thrive. In the end, the agency of the future isn’t about producing outputs; it’s about driving outcomes. It’s not about selling services; it’s about shaping success. It’s less about campaigns and more about lasting growth.
