Business Durban Positioned as Alibaba’s Gateway to Africa’s E-Commerce Growth

November 10, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Alibaba, one of the world’s biggest e-commerce companies, is setting its sights on Africa, and Durban has been chosen as the main base for its operations on the continent. The eThekwini Municipality confirmed that Mayor Cyril Xaba recently met with Alibaba executives at their headquarters in Hangzhou, China, where plans were discussed to make Durban the company’s gateway into African digital trade.

Durban’s selection isn’t surprising. The city has one of Africa’s busiest ports, strong logistics infrastructure, growing warehousing facilities, and King Shaka International Airport all of which make it an ideal location for managing the flow of goods in and out of the continent. The municipality believes this partnership could open doors for local entrepreneurs and small businesses, giving them access to global markets through Alibaba’s online trading platforms.

Alibaba’s move into Africa is part of a wider plan to strengthen its presence in emerging markets. Its retail arm, AliExpress, has already been making strides in accommodating African consumers. In March 2025, the company expanded local currency payment options in several African countries, including South Africa, Algeria, Egypt, Ethiopia, Morocco, and Tanzania. This builds on earlier efforts like adding M-Pesa payments in Kenya and integrating Opay and Verve card systems in Nigeria important steps that have made it easier for people to shop online without international payment barriers.

As a result of these improvements, AliExpress can now deliver orders to countries such as South Africa, Kenya, Morocco, Nigeria, and Tanzania within just ten days, a major improvement compared to the long wait times that used to discourage online shoppers. The company has even begun experimenting with a physical presence in Africa, opening its first showroom in Ethiopia where wholesale buyers can view products and place bulk orders directly. Alibaba says its goal is to simplify logistics and payments for online shopping while tapping into Africa’s growing e-commerce market.

Alibaba’s expansion adds yet another strong competitor to a South African market already being transformed by Chinese e-commerce platforms like Shein and Temu. These two companies have completely reshaped the local online retail landscape in just a few years. According to a report by the Localisation Support Fund, Shein and Temu together generated about R7.3 billion in sales in 2024, which made up nearly 4% of South Africa’s total clothing market and an impressive 37% of online clothing sales. However, the same report warned that the surge in cheap imported clothing has cost the local economy about R960 million in manufacturing revenue and around 8,000 jobs since 2020.

Shein entered South Africa in 2020 and Temu followed in 2024, but their combined market share of 3.6% has already surpassed that of major global fashion brands like H&M, Zara, and Cotton On combined. Their strategy of connecting customers directly with factories allows them to sell products at extremely low prices, which strongly appeals to price sensitive shoppers. Surveys show that while many South Africans still avoid these platforms, those who do use them tend to become loyal, repeat customers.

The impact on online shopping has been enormous. Together, Shein and Temu made up more than 15% of South Africa’s online retail activity last year, second only to Takealot, which leads the market with 32%. Their user bases are now roughly on par with Checkers Sixty60. Temu’s share of South African consumers jumped from 10.7% to 16.6% between 2023 and 2025, while Shein’s rose from 12.3% to 15.1%. These figures make it clear that Chinese e- commerce platforms are no longer niche players they have become mainstream shopping destinations for millions of South Africans, setting the stage for even greater competition once Alibaba fully launches its African operations.
