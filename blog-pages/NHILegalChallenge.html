Media NHI Legal Challenge Heads for Court as Opponents Reject Settlement Talks

Organisations opposing South Africa’s National Health Insurance Act have dismissed calls to seek a settlement with government and say they are preparing for a full legal battle to challenge the law in court.

Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana recently urged litigants to consider negotiations, arguing that government and its critics share a common goal of universal access to healthcare. He suggested that dialogue could resolve disagreements without prolonged litigation.

Those challenging the Act have rejected this view, saying the process that led to the adoption of the NHI was deeply flawed. They argue that public participation was inadequate and that meaningful engagement with affected stakeholders did not take place during parliamentary processes.

The NHI Act aims to establish a single state controlled fund that would pay for healthcare services and reduce the role of medical schemes and provincial health departments. Government maintains that the policy is essential to address inequality in access to healthcare.

Opponents remain unconvinced. The Western Cape government is among those before the courts, with provincial officials arguing that the National Council of Provinces failed to properly consult provinces and healthcare stakeholders. They believe the law should be declared unconstitutional and set aside.

In total, eight parties have launched legal challenges against the Act. These include trade union Solidarity, organisations representing private hospitals, doctors, medical schemes, business groups, and private healthcare practitioners.

Some groups say they might not have gone to court had there been genuine engagement from government. Others claim that alternative proposals to expand healthcare access were ignored. While a few organisations have indicated a willingness to engage in dialogue, they insist that the Act itself would need to be withdrawn before any settlement could be considered.

With neither side backing down, the future of the NHI Act now rests with the courts as the legal battle moves forward.
