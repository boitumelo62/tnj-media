Media National Shutdown and G20 Lockdown Set to Converge on 21 November

November 21, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

On Friday, 21 November, South Africa is set to experience a national shutdown as thousands prepare to stop working to draw attention to the ongoing crisis of gender based violence. The action is being led by the NGO Women For Change, which has called on the country to pause all work for the day. The organisation hopes to use the global spotlight ahead of the G20 Leaders’ Summit, happening that weekend, to highlight the urgent need for stronger action against gender based violence and femicide.

Women For Change, which has accumulated over a million signatures on its petition, is demanding that gender based violence be declared a national disaster. In a strongly worded message to President Cyril Ramaphosa, the group said government leaders have failed to protect women, pointing to the thousands of women killed and countless survivors denied justice. They urged the country’s women and LGBTQI+ community to step away from all paid and unpaid work whether at workplaces, universities, or at home and to avoid spending any money for the entire day, showing the real economic weight of their presence.

Their message is firm South Africa cannot continue to operate while women are being harmed daily. They are asking everyone taking part to wear black as a symbol of mourning and resistance, to come to a complete standstill for 15 minutes at midday, and to contribute to a peaceful show of unity. The protest includes 15 national meet up points across the country, where participants will gather from 11:30 and take part in a silent “lie down” at noon. These gatherings range from Sea Point Promenade in Cape Town to Constitution Hill in Johannesburg, North and South Beach in Durban, campuses across multiple provinces, and locations in cities such as Gqeberha, Kimberley, Bloemfontein, and Nelspruit. The emphasis throughout is on peaceful participation, with organisers stressing that the demonstration is about unity, grief, and collective strength.

At the same time, Johannesburg will be under tight security because of the upcoming G20 summit. The National Joint Operational and Intelligence Structure has confirmed that from Friday through Monday there will be a hard lockdown around the FNB Stadium and Nasrec Expo Centre. Only people with official accreditation will be allowed near these areas, and all vehicles will be thoroughly inspected before entering. Traffic will be heavily affected, and major roads such as parts of the N1, N3, N12, R21, R24, and M1 are expected to face closures and delays. Road users and residents have been warned to expect disruptions as convoys move and security measures are enforced.
