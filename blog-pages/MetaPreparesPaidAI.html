Tech News Meta Prepares Paid AI Subscriptions Across Instagram Facebook and WhatsApp

Meta is planning to test premium subscription options for users of Instagram Facebook and WhatsApp as it continues to explore new revenue streams beyond advertising. The trials are expected to begin in the coming months and will focus on paid access to advanced features while keeping the core services free to use.

According to reports, the subscription tiers will unlock expanded artificial intelligence tools designed to enhance how users create and interact with content. Among the features under consideration are new AI powered creative tools and experimental apps that build on Meta’s growing AI ecosystem.

One of the products likely to be included is Vibes, a video generation app unveiled in September as part of the latest Meta AI release. Meta says the tool allows users to turn ideas into short videos using new AI driven visual creation features. Subscriptions could also extend to other AI capabilities currently being developed across the company’s platforms.

Meta is also expected to integrate technology from Manus, an AI company founded in China that it agreed to acquire in December in a deal reportedly valued at about $2bn. While Meta plans to continue offering standalone Manus subscriptions to businesses, the technology is also set to play a role in its consumer subscription strategy.

At the time of the acquisition, Meta said Manus would help strengthen its AI by introducing advanced agents capable of handling complex tasks with minimal user input. These agents are designed to plan and execute actions such as organising trips or preparing presentations, moving beyond the repeated prompting required by many existing chatbots.

Now based in Singapore after relocating from China, Manus has positioned itself as a developer of more autonomous AI systems. The acquisition has drawn regulatory attention, with Chinese authorities announcing in January that they would examine the deal for potential breaches of technology export rules or national security concerns.

Meta’s interest in subscriptions is not new. In recent years, the company has tested limits on certain features, including restricting how many links some Facebook users could share without paying. In 2023, it also launched a paid verification service that offers a blue tick on Facebook and Instagram for a monthly fee.

The latest trials suggest Meta is steadily expanding its paid offerings as it looks to monetise advanced tools and premium experiences across its social platforms.
