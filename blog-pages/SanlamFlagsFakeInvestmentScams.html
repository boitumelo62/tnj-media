Media Sanlam flags increase in fake investment and impersonation scams

December 17, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Sanlam, South Africa’s largest insurer, has warned consumers to be especially vigilant for scams during the festive season, saying fraud has become far more sophisticated and difficult to detect. The company says the international scamming ecosystem has evolved to industrial levels, exposing ordinary people to highly coordinated and convincing criminal operations.

According to Sanlam, there has been a sharp rise in impersonation and investment scams over the past year. Financial institutions, regulators and law enforcement agencies are seeing more cases involving fake investment opportunities, advance fee schemes and identity impersonation. These scams are no longer crude or obvious. They are increasingly polished, aggressive and persuasive, often using the names, images and reputations of well known brands and senior executives to lure victims into a false sense of trust.

Helen du Toit, Head of Forensics at Sanlam, said scammers deliberately take advantage of the festive “spirit of spending”. She warned that criminals are also acutely aware that January is financially stressful for many households, which makes people more vulnerable to offers of quick cash or seemingly guaranteed returns. Du Toit added that the global spread of highly organised scam operations should not be underestimated, particularly because they often target vulnerable groups such as the elderly.

One of the fastest growing threats involves fake investment groups on platforms like Telegram, WhatsApp and Facebook. These groups falsely claim to be run by credible institutions or well known business leaders. Scammers use professional looking profiles, fabricated testimonials and manipulated images of executives to appear legitimate. In some cases, they copy official looking emails so accurately that only a small spelling mistake or a slightly altered domain name reveals the fraud. The Financial Sector Conduct Authority has issued specific warnings about criminals impersonating authorised financial services providers, including individuals claiming to be linked to Sanlam.

Scams are not limited to social media. Sanlam has also noted an increase in call-spoofing or vishing scams, where criminals use technology to appear as though they are calling from SARS, a bank or another trusted institution in order to extract personal or financial information. Business email compromise remains another serious and growing threat. In these cases, criminals gain access to email accounts, often through weak or compromised passwords, and quietly monitor communications. They then intercept payment instructions and replace legitimate banking details with fraudulent ones. Hidden forwarding rules and deleted messages make these attacks especially difficult to spot.

Sanlam is urging South Africans to treat any unsolicited financial offer with caution and to slow down before acting. Legitimate financial institutions will not ask people to invest through messaging apps or social media, and no executive or public figure will personally offer investment deals or guaranteed returns. Consumers are advised to avoid clicking on links in unexpected messages, to carefully verify email addresses and social media accounts, and to never share personal information such as ID numbers, banking details or one time passwords over calls or chat apps. Warning signs include spelling mistakes, altered email domains, unusual sender names, unexpected attachments and mismatched links.

If there is any uncertainty, Sanlam advises consumers to check directly with the FSCA or consult an accredited financial adviser. The regulator has also cautioned the public to watch out for classic red flags such as unrealistic returns, pressure to act quickly, vague or unclear investment information, repeated requests for additional payments and the absence of an FSCA licence.
