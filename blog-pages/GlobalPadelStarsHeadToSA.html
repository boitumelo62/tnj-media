Lifestyle Global Padel Stars Head to SA for Biggest African Event Yet

August 3, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Padel fans have something special to look forward to this August. Some of the biggest names in international Padel are coming to Johannesburg, with the Wanderers Stadium set to host an exciting showcase of the fast growing sport. Known as a mix between tennis and squash, Padel has been gaining serious traction in South Africa, with new courts, clubs, and players popping up all over the country.

From 15 to 17 August, twelve top ranked players from Spain and Argentina will play on a specially built court at the famous cricket venue. Big names like Agustín Tapia, Arturo Coello, Juan Lebrón, Ale Galán, and Fernando Belasteguín will headline the event, called the South African Invitational. This isn’t just a series of matches it’s being presented as a full entertainment experience. Expect music, meet and greets, local talent, food stalls, and lots of fan focused fun. Organisers say it’s not just about the sport, but a celebration of what Padel has become and what it’s becoming in South Africa.

Mohammed Saloojee, part of the team behind the event, explained that bringing these global stars here wasn’t easy, but the timing was perfect. With international schedules packed and big tournaments everywhere, this might have been the only realistic window to make it happen. Saloojee said if they hadn’t seized the moment now, they might not have had the chance again anytime soon.

The matches will follow a team format, which aligns with how Padel leagues in South Africa already operate. While this style isn’t new for professional players, it’s expected to bring a fresh energy for local fans. Some of the pairings may never be seen again, which adds an unpredictable and exciting element to the tournament.

This Invitational is being hailed as the biggest professional Padel event ever held on the African continent. Until now, Egypt’s New Giza P2 was the only major international tournament in Africa, and even that hasn’t always drawn the top names. This event changes that, shining a spotlight on Africa’s growing presence in the global Padel scene.

To support local talent, each day will kick off with matches from the South African Championship, featuring 24 of the country’s top players. The final of this local tournament will be played on Sunday, just before the main event wraps up. It’s a big opportunity for South African players to share the stage and the locker room with the world’s best, and to gain valuable experience from them.

Organisers see this tournament as the beginning of something much bigger. Saloojee called it their “audition” to the world, hoping that strong support from fans can help lay the foundation for future events. The goal is not only to give spectators a great time but to show that South Africa and Africa as a whole is ready to be a serious player on the world Padel stage.
