Media Netcare Admits Role in Illegal Kidney Transplant Scheme Involving Children

January 28, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s largest private hospital group has admitted that it received millions of rand from an illegal organ trafficking operation that included kidney transplants involving children.

Netcare acknowledged that its KwaZulu-Natal subsidiary received R3.8 million from a criminal syndicate linked to unlawful kidney transplants carried out between 2001 and 2003. The scheme allegedly involved poor donors from Brazil and Romania who were paid for their kidneys, which were transplanted into wealthy Israeli patients.

Appearing in the Durban Regional Court, Netcare KwaZulu pleaded guilty to 102 charges related to the illegal activities and was fined just over R7.8 million. The offences included multiple counts of unlawfully acquiring and transplanting human kidneys, among them kidneys taken from five minors. While Netcare admitted that some donors were children, no further details were provided about their ages or nationalities.

The illegal transplants took place at Netcare St Augustine’s Hospital in Durban, one of the country’s leading private medical facilities. Court documents revealed that recipients were brought to South Africa for the procedures and that records were falsified to make donors appear to be family members of recipients, as required by law at the time.

According to the charge sheet, Israeli donors were initially used but were later replaced by Romanian and Brazilian donors because their kidneys could be obtained at a much lower cost. Donors from Eastern Europe and South America were reportedly paid about six thousand dollars, while Israeli donors received significantly more.

As part of a plea agreement, criminal charges against Netcare’s parent company and its chief executive at the time, Richard Friedland, were withdrawn. Netcare KwaZulu was fined separately for breaching the Human Tissues Act by allowing minors to donate organs, for participating in the illegal transplant scheme, and for the financial benefit it derived from the crimes. The R3.8 million received from the operation will be paid to the state’s asset forfeiture unit.

Several medical professionals and hospital staff were also summonsed to appear in court in connection with the matter.

Netcare, which had denied wrongdoing for years, issued a statement admitting that criminal conduct had taken place. The company said it became clear that certain employees were aware that donors were paid, were unrelated to recipients, and in some cases were minors. Netcare said those involved were no longer employed and stressed that the illegal operations ended in 2003.

The group added that it has since strengthened compliance with health laws and internal policies to prevent similar abuses in the future.
