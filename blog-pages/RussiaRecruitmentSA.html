Media South African Youth Offered Work in Russia But at What Cost?

August 25, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Russian companies are actively recruiting young African women to help address a growing labor shortage, but concerns are mounting that many of them are being misled into working in military related roles. One of the central players is Alabuga Special Economic Zone in Tatarstan, Russia, which is involved in manufacturing drones used in the war in Ukraine.

In South Africa, one of Russia’s BRICS allies, recruitment efforts have picked up significantly. The BRICS Women’s Business Alliance signed an agreement in May to send over 5,600 workers to Alabuga and another Russian company by next year. The BRICS Student Commission in South Africa also advertised jobs for young women aged 18 to 22 earlier this year. Some South African influencers have even promoted the program on social media. Lebogang Zulu, head of the alliance in South Africa, said the deal is meant to help solve unemployment, noting that Russia needs workers while South Africa has a large jobless youth population.

However, government officials in Pretoria are now investigating the recruitment campaigns and may summon Russian diplomats for answers. Although the government hasn't found direct proof of wrongdoing yet, it has taken note of reports linked to Alabuga. Alabuga has been accused by several watchdog organizations, including the Institute for Science and International Security, of misleading African women about the nature of the jobs. The reports suggest most end up working in the drone plant, despite no mention of it in official job descriptions.

These jobs reportedly expose workers to danger, especially since the plant has been targeted in Ukrainian attacks. Students at a school in Soweto said recruiters visited their school, handed out branded merchandise, and promised jobs, housing, and flights to Russia. They were asked to sign up via WhatsApp but not told they might work in a drone factory. On its website, Alabuga Start the recruitment arm makes no mention of drone production, but external reports claim otherwise. The BRICS Women’s Business Alliance and the Student Commission deny any involvement in placing workers in unsafe conditions.

A representative from the alliance said the group operates under South Africa’s Women’s Ministry, although the ministry later clarified it has no formal ties to the alliance or knowledge of the Russian program. Alabuga has previously recruited from other African nations like Burkina Faso and Ethiopia. It hired only 22 workers in 2023 but now aims for more than 8,000 by 2025. So far in 2024, only six recruits have come from South Africa.

Recruitment efforts have also extended to Botswana and Lesotho, with some women already departing for Russia in August. Analysts say many of these women come from vulnerable backgrounds with limited education and few job prospects. Once in Russia, they are often shocked by the actual work they’re assigned. While the opportunity may appear attractive due to promised salaries and stability, the reality may involve building weapons for a war they never signed up for.
