Media Usindiso Fire Trial Takes Shocking Turn as Accused Admits He Lied

September 11, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The trial of a man accused of starting the deadly Usindiso fire that killed 76 people in Johannesburg has taken an unexpected turn after he admitted in court that his earlier confession was a lie.

The accused had initially told investigators that he set the blaze intentionally, but during proceedings he revealed that he fabricated the story because he wanted food and shelter in prison. His admission has raised serious questions about the credibility of the original investigation and the circumstances under which the confession was obtained.

The fire, which broke out in August 2023 at the Usindiso building in the city centre, remains one of South Africa’s worst inner-city tragedies. The abandoned building had been occupied by hundreds of people, many of them vulnerable migrants and families living in extreme poverty. Survivors have long demanded justice and accountability for the conditions that led to the disaster.

Legal analysts say the man’s new statement complicates the case and may force prosecutors to reassess their approach. Families of the victims, meanwhile, continue to call for answers, stressing that responsibility should not only fall on individuals but also on the system that allowed unsafe buildings to exist in the first place.

The trial is ongoing, with the court expected to weigh the man’s retraction alongside forensic evidence and testimonies from survivors. For many, the focus remains on securing justice for the 76 lives lost and ensuring such a tragedy is never repeated.
