Media Fuel Industry Warns of End to Off Contract Fuel Purchases for Large Customers

March 16, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Fuels Industry Association of South Africa has warned that large scale customers will soon no longer be allowed to make off contract fuel purchases, a move that could significantly change how major fuel consumers source their supply.

According to the association, the shift is aimed at stabilising the fuel supply chain and ensuring that contractual agreements between suppliers and large buyers are respected. Off contract purchasing has historically allowed certain customers to buy fuel outside of established agreements, particularly when market conditions offered more favourable prices. Industry leaders say this practice has increasingly created instability in the fuel market.

Suppliers have argued that unexpected off contract purchases can disrupt supply planning and lead to logistical challenges, especially when demand spikes unexpectedly. Large consumers affected by the new restrictions include sectors such as mining, logistics and manufacturing. These industries typically rely on large volumes of fuel to sustain operations and have often used off contract purchases to manage fluctuating operational costs.

By enforcing stricter purchasing arrangements, the fuel industry hopes to create a more predictable market environment where supply and demand can be better managed. The association believes the change will strengthen contractual relationships between suppliers and customers while also protecting the broader stability of the fuel distribution system. However, some industry observers believe the change may increase operational costs for businesses that previously relied on the flexibility offered by off contract purchases.

Companies may now need to renegotiate longer term supply agreements or lock in pricing structures that could limit their ability to respond to market fluctuations. Energy analysts say the development reflects broader pressures within the fuel sector, including global supply volatility and rising transportation costs. Ensuring predictable supply chains has become a key priority for many industries as energy markets continue to face uncertainty.

The association says it will continue engaging with stakeholders to ensure a smooth transition as the new purchasing framework takes effect.
