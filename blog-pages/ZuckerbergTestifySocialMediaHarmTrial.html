Media Zuckerberg to Testify in High Stakes Social Media Harm Trial

Meta chief executive Mark Zuckerberg is set to take the witness stand in a landmark trial examining whether major social media platforms intentionally design their products to be addictive and harmful to children.

Zuckerberg is expected to face intense questioning from lawyers representing a 20-year-old woman, identified in court documents as KGM. She claims her early exposure to social media platforms led to addiction, deepened her depression, and contributed to suicidal thoughts.

Meta Platforms and Google’s YouTube are the remaining defendants in the case after TikTok and Snap reached settlements earlier in the proceedings. The trial is being closely watched as it has been selected as a bellwether case, meaning its outcome could shape how thousands of similar lawsuits against social media companies are handled in the future.

The courtroom is expected to again host bereaved parents who have lost children to suicide or self- harm and who argue that social media played a role in those deaths. Their presence has underscored the emotional weight of the proceedings and the broader public debate over youth safety online.

Meta has strongly rejected the allegations. A company spokesperson said Meta is confident the evidence will demonstrate its long-standing commitment to supporting young people and improving online safety tools. In opening statements, Meta attorney Paul Schmidt acknowledged KGM’s mental health struggles but argued that Instagram was not a substantial factor, pointing instead to a difficult home environment and suggesting she used social media as a coping mechanism. YouTube’s legal team has made similar arguments.

Zuckerberg’s testimony follows that of Instagram head Adam Mosseri, who told the court he does not believe people can be clinically addicted to social media. Mosseri said Instagram aims to protect young users and that it is not in the company’s long-term interest to prioritise profit over user wellbeing.

Plaintiff lawyers are expected to question Zuckerberg on Instagram’s algorithm, endless scrolling feeds, and cosmetic filters that alter users’ appearance. They argue these features are deliberately designed to keep users engaged for long periods and may worsen body image issues and mental health risks among young people.

Meta is also facing a separate trial in New Mexico, adding to mounting legal pressure on the company as regulators and courts worldwide scrutinise the impact of social media on children and teenagers.
