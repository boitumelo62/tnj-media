Lifestyle Lockdown Turned More Men into Home Cooks

September 21, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

One unexpected but positive outcome of the Covid-19 pandemic was how it helped challenge long held stereotypes about who belongs in the kitchen. With lockdowns forcing people to stay at home, many who had never cooked before including a lot of men found themselves having to step up and prepare meals. This shift didn’t go unnoticed by Chef Benny Masekwameng, popularly known as Chef Benny. He believes that everyone has a little cook inside them. Some people just needed the time and space to discover it, and lockdown offered that opportunity. It also helped that cooking shows became even more popular during this time, inspiring people to try new things in the kitchen.

Chef Benny shared his thoughts during the launch of the ninth season of Ultimate Braai Master, held at the e.tv studios in Hyde Park, Johannesburg. He’s one of the judges on the show, alongside Chef Pete Goffe Wood. This new season is a special one it’s an all-star edition featuring eight teams made up of past winners and wildcard entries. They take part in intense outdoor braai (barbecue) challenges all over South Africa, pushing their limits with fire, creativity, and skill. According to Chef Benny, the level of competition is higher than ever. The food quality has gone up too, and returning contestants were expected to bring something new and even more impressive than before.

He explained that this season was shot in a much shorter time frame than usual just 21 days, compared to the typical eight or nine weeks. This put a lot of pressure on both the production team and the contestants, but it added an extra layer of intensity to the show. Even with all their experience, some contestants still fell into old traps rushing, misinterpreting the brief, or not planning properly. It was surprising to see seasoned cooks make the same small mistakes they had made before. The new season of Ultimate Braai Master premieres on Sunday at 5 p.m. on e.tv.

Despite all the fame and years on TV, Chef Benny says his love for food remains at the center of everything he does. He’s been a professional chef for over two decades, working in hotels, restaurants, and private events. He joined Ultimate Braai Master in season four and was previously a judge on Master Chef South Africa for four seasons. That adds up to nine years of judging reality cooking competitions. Even though television has brought new opportunities, his passion hasn’t changed. At the heart of it all is still food cooking it, creating it, and sharing it. That, he says, is the thread that ties all of his work together.
