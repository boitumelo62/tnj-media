Media The Future of TV Advertising Is Connected

August 23, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

For a long time, TV was the go to option for advertisers looking to reach large audiences. If you wanted to be seen, you booked a spot during primetime, and that was that. But that world has changed. People don’t watch video content like they used to. The TV screen is still important, but now it’s connected to the internet, letting viewers stream content whenever and however they like. This shift is changing how advertising money gets spent.

At the center of this shift is connected TV or CTV. Viewers are no longer tied to a schedule or one device. They’re moving freely between apps like Showmax, YouTube, and Netflix, whether on a smart TV, a phone, or a tablet. That means the old promise of guaranteed reach through traditional broadcast TV isn’t as reliable anymore. Advertisers are starting to follow the audiences, who are clearly spending more time on these digital platforms. And because CTV behaves more like digital, advertisers can also measure their results in far more detail.

This trend isn’t just local. In the US, for example, CTV is expected to pull in $26.6 billion by 2025 and will make up a big chunk of overall video ad spend. The UK is seeing a similar pattern, with video on demand now accounting for a quarter of all TV ad revenue and that’s still growing. Even in markets like India, where linear TV has historically dominated, the growth of connected devices and better broadband is pushing ad budgets toward CTV. It already makes up over 4% of TV ad spend there and could hit 42% in just a couple of years. South Africa isn’t moving quite as fast, but the signs are clear. More people are buying smart TVs as prices come down, and streaming platforms are becoming part of everyday life.

Younger viewers have already made streaming their default, while older ones are slowly shifting their habits. All of this points to a broader move away from traditional TV viewing. The big game changer is going to be measurement. TV always offered big reach, but advertisers had to trust that their message landed. With CTV, they don’t need to guess. They get real-time data on who saw the ad, how often, and what the impact was.

This makes it easier to justify the spend and fine tune campaigns. As more agencies get comfortable with buying CTV programmatically, they’ll be more willing to shift budgets away from traditional TV. Then there’s the cost factor. CTV allows brands to be more precise targeting based on location, behaviour, or interests. Instead of paying to reach everyone watching a show, advertisers can focus only on the people they actually care about. That kind of efficiency matters, especially in a market where brands are under pressure to do more with less.

That said, South Africa won’t flip to a CTV first model overnight. Infrastructure challenges, audience habits, and cautious brand mindsets will slow things down. Historically, we’ve lagged global trends by a few years. But the direction is clear. The same shift that moved budgets from print and radio to social media a decade ago is now happening again with video. The real turning point will come when a few big advertisers move serious money into CTV and start seeing results. Once that happens, others will quickly follow. We’ve already seen this play out in India, where experimental spending has turned into full blown strategy. South Africa could follow suit sooner than expected.

So what can marketers do right now? First, take a hard look at where your audience is actually spending their time. The numbers show that streaming is rising across all age groups. CTV lets you tap into that audience more effectively. Second, start testing now. Build experience in planning and buying CTV campaigns, so when the market accelerates, you’re not scrambling to catch up. And finally, rethink your creative approach. CTV supports shorter, more dynamic formats that respond better to culture and context than a traditional 30 second ad ever could.

TV isn’t dying it’s evolving, It’s becoming smarter, more targeted, and more accountable. Brands that adapt with it will find new ways to connect and grow. Those who stay stuck in old habits may end up spending more just to be ignored. The shift is happening. The only question is: who’s going to take the lead, and who’ll be left behind?
