Business Building a Personal Brand in the Digital Age

October 14, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Building your personal brand isn’t about ego or self promotion it’s about staying relevant and visible in a world where people are constantly consuming content and discovering information from all directions. There was a time when being good at your craft and having a decent website or ad in the Yellow Pages was enough to grow a business. But that’s not how it works anymore.

Today, people are more skeptical of corporate messaging, and they crave realness. They want to know who’s behind the business what you believe in, how you lead, what you’ve been through. When trust in traditional institutions is low, people turn to people. So, if you’re leading a business, your personal story, your values, and your voice can be incredibly powerful tools. Being seen as a real, relatable person builds trust. It creates connection. And that connection can move your business forward in ways that polished branding alone just can’t.

In fact, leaders who show up online sharing their thoughts, values, and even behind the scenes moments are now more trusted than CEOs or government officials, according to studies like Edelman’s Trust Barometer. That’s especially true among younger generations. In Africa, for example, a huge majority of Millennials and Gen Z say they trust businesses more when leadership is visible on social media. This isn’t just theory it’s what the data says, and it’s what real people are responding to. So, if putting yourself out there online makes you nervous, think of it less like “becoming a social media influencer” and more like letting people see the real person behind the business.

You don’t have to be on every platform. In fact, you shouldn’t be. Pick one space that fits your message and your audience maybe it’s LinkedIn if you’re in B2B, or Instagram if you’re in lifestyle or creative work. Master that one platform, then, if it makes sense, repurpose content for others. The goal isn’t to be everywhere it’s to be intentional about where and how you show up. As for how often to post, it’s less about volume and more about consistency. If once a week is all you can manage right now, that’s fine. What matters is showing up regularly and engaging meaningfully. Posting, responding to comments, interacting with others in your industry these are all part of building a brand, not just what you publish on your own feed. Content wise, be real. Talk about your work, but also share personal insights and lessons. Let people in on your thought process. Give them a peek into what you stand for. A good mix of professional insights, personal experiences, and your point of view keeps things interesting and builds credibility.

And yes, track what’s working but don’t get caught up in vanity metrics like likes. Pay more attention to engagement, growth in your network, new business leads, and the overall tone ofthe responses you get. That’s where the real value is. If being front and center just isn’t your thing, you’ve got options. You can build a brand voice or persona that represents your business one that’s still authentic, but not tied to your face. You can highlight your team, your customers, your community. Or you can encourage your employees to share their own experiences working with your business this helps tell your story from multiple perspectives. And don’t just wing it. The best social media presence feels spontaneous but is actually backed by strategy.

Planning your content, capturing ideas when they come up, even working with someone to help shape your message these steps make a big difference. One good idea can become a week’s worth of thoughtful posts, if you take the time to plan it out. Ultimately, being visible online isn’t about becoming famous. It’s about being present. It’s about leading not just from behind a desk, but from the feed. So take the leap. Show up. Keep it real. Your audience and your business will be better for it.
