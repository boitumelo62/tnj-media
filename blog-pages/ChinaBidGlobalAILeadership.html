Tech News China Makes Bold Bid for Global AI Leadership at Shanghai Summit

July 31, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

At the recent World Artificial Intelligence Conference (WAIC) in Shanghai, China showcased its growing ambitions to lead the global AI race, positioning itself as a compelling alternative to U.S. dominance.

The spotlight fell on Chinese start-up DeepSeek, whose powerful and affordable chatbot rivaled top American models, signaling a shift in global AI competitiveness. According to nonprofit group Epoch AI, nearly 80 percent of Chinese AI models are now considered “state-of-the-art,” outpacing the U.S. share of 70 percent. China’s government aims to become the world’s top AI innovation hub by 2030.

Open-source models from firms like DeepSeek and Zhipu are key to this strategy, allowing other nations to adopt and adapt the technology affordably. Countries such as Mongolia and Pakistan are already exploring implementation. Former Google CEO Eric Schmidt noted that China’s open-source approach could appeal to developing nations, especially where affordability is key. Yet, challenges persist—including skepticism over data privacy, China’s closed internet, and concerns about government influence.

Still, Beijing used the WAIC stage to promote international cooperation. Premier Li Qiang pledged AI support to developing countries and announced plans for a China-led global AI alliance—though details remain unclear. Meanwhile, Washington is tightening chip export controls to slow China’s progress. But analysts suggest that the race is far from over. “The field is still maturing,” wrote analyst Grace Shao. “It’s too early to say who’s winning.”
