Business US Broadens Travel Restrictions

August 16, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The Trump administration has once again expanded its visa and travel bans, adding new layers to already strict immigration policies. This time, a total of 36 countries are under review, most of them in Africa. The move builds on earlier restrictions announced in June 2025, which already affected 19 nations. South Africa, however, is not currently on the list of targeted countries and its citizens can still travel to the US under existing rules.

A message from the US State Department was sent to embassies in the 36 countries now under review. It asked officials to evaluate whether those governments are doing enough to improve the quality of travel documentation and are cooperating when it comes to repatriating their nationals who overstay visas or live in the US without proper authorization. These countries were given 60 days to respond or risk being added to the growing list of banned nations, which now includes 12.

The Trump administration says these measures are aimed at national security and preventing visa misuse. President Trump has argued that many countries don't have reliable systems to properly vet travelers or don't cooperate when the US tries to send undocumented nationals back home. Zimbabwe is already feeling the consequences. The US embassy there has suspended most visa services, except for official and diplomatic categories. In a statement, the embassy said the goal is to protect Americans by enforcing strict security standards.

New policies have also been introduced for Malawi and Zambia. Citizens from these countries who want to visit the US under B1/B2 visitor visas now have to pay a security bond ranging from $5,000 to $15,000, even if they meet all other eligibility requirements. This new rule comes into effect on August 20, 2025. Travelers must also complete a Homeland Security bond agreement and make payments online through the US Treasury. So far, South Africa has avoided inclusion in the new restrictions. Professor Anthoni van Nieuwkerk from the University of South Africa believes that, for now, South Africa is not directly targeted. But he cautions that the political climate in the US could change.

There are members of Congress who see South Africa as increasingly unfriendly to US interests, especially due to its alignment with BRICS, its case against Israel at the International Court of Justice, and recent trade disagreements. Van Nieuwkerk warned that if South Africa’s trade talks with the US fall apart or its foreign policy decisions draw more attention, travel restrictions could eventually extend to South Africans as well.

Despite these concerns, the South African government is trying to keep the relationship with Washington on steady ground. Pretoria is particularly worried about the new US import tariffs, which have hurt local exporters. President Cyril Ramaphosa recently spoke directly with President Trump in a phone call where he raised these concerns.

Sources say Ramaphosa emphasized the impact on jobs and industries back home and tried to remind Trump of the strong historical ties between the two countries. Though the full details of their conversation haven't been released, officials close to the matter say it was a constructive discussion. At least for now, South Africa appears to be maintaining its diplomatic footing amid a tense and unpredictable global landscape.
