Tech News SpaceX Launches 28 Starlink Satellites After Global Service Disruption

July 28, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

SpaceX successfully launched 28 new Starlink satellites into orbit early Saturday morning, just days after a rare global outage temporarily disrupted the company's satellite internet service.

The Falcon 9 rocket lifted off at 5:01 a.m. Eastern Time from Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. Around eight minutes later, the rocket’s first-stage booster returned to Earth, landing smoothly on the autonomous droneship A Shortfall of Gravitas in the Atlantic Ocean. This marked the 119th successful landing for this booster and the 480th recovery in SpaceX’s history.

This mission was the company’s 91st Falcon 9 launch of 2025. The booster, known as B1078, had previously been used in the Crew-6 mission and 17 earlier Starlink launches.

Just two days earlier, on July 24, Starlink experienced a rare and widespread outage that lasted about four hours. The service disruption began in the late afternoon and affected users worldwide. SpaceX attributed the problem to an internal software error. Engineers were able to restore limited access within two and a half hours, and full service was resumed about 90 minutes later.

Starlink now has more than 8,000 satellites in low-Earth orbit and provides internet service to over six million customers globally, including approximately two million in the United States.

Later in the day, SpaceX was also scheduled to launch another 24 satellites from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California, continuing the company’s rapid expansion of its broadband satellite constellation.
