Tech News WhatsApp Blocks 6.8 Million Scam Accounts, Rolls Out New Safety Features

WhatsApp has stepped up its fight against online fraud with a suite of new safety features designed to protect users from scams spreading through the messaging platform. The move comes alongside the announcement that the platform blocked 6.8 million malicious accounts during the first half of 2025.

The new measures aim to limit the spread of financial and impersonation scams many of which have been linked to organized groups operating across Southeast Asia.

Major Account Takedown

According to parent company Meta, the nearly 7 million accounts were removed after being flagged for suspicious activity including mass messaging, phishing attempts, and participation in fraudulent investment groups. Many of these schemes used WhatsApp groups to lure victims with promises of easy money through tasks like liking videos or investing in fake cryptocurrencies.

In one high-profile operation, Meta traced a network back to Cambodia where scammers were using artificial intelligence tools including large language models to craft deceptive recruitment messages. Victims were led through WhatsApp and Telegram groups before being manipulated into transferring money into crypto wallets controlled by the scammers.

New "Safety Overview" for Group Chats

To reduce the risk of users unknowingly joining harmful groups, WhatsApp has introduced a "safety overview" screen. When users are invited to a group by someone outside their contacts, they will now see details like:

  • The inviter’s phone number
  • Group name and size
  • When the group was created
  • Security advice and the option to exit immediately

Notifications from these groups will remain muted unless the user chooses to participate further.

Context Warnings for Unknown Messages

In addition to group protections, WhatsApp is testing contextual warnings for one-on-one chats with unknown contacts. These alerts provide details such as whether the number is saved in the user’s phone and whether it has been reported by others encouraging users to pause and assess legitimacy before engaging.

A Safety Framework for Everyday Users

Alongside the feature rollout, Meta has partnered with cybersecurity advocate Rachel Tobac to promote a simple security strategy:

  • Pause before replying to unfamiliar messages
  • Question suspicious requests or offers that sound too good to be true
  • Verify the identity of the sender through another trusted platform or contact method

Combating Evolving Threats

WhatsApp’s updates come as scam operations grow more sophisticated often blending platforms like Telegram, TikTok, and AI-generated content to deceive victims. Meta says it’s investing in both proactive AI monitoring and human review systems to catch fraudulent accounts before they do harm.

“While technology can help us catch bad actors, users play a critical role in protecting themselves,” said a Meta spokesperson. “These tools are meant to give people more control and context so they can make informed choices.”

The changes are rolling out globally and will be expanded in the coming months based on user feedback.
