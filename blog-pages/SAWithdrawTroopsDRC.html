Media South Africa to withdraw troops from DRC mission

February 9, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa will withdraw its troops from a United Nations peacekeeping mission in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with the process expected to be completed before the end of the year.

The withdrawal marks a shift in South Africa’s peacekeeping commitments after years of involvement in stabilisation and security operations in the region. The Department of Defence has indicated that the decision forms part of a broader review of international deployments, operational costs, and national security priorities.

South African forces have been a key contributor to peacekeeping efforts in the DRC, particularly in protecting civilians and supporting regional stability initiatives. Analysts say the withdrawal could have implications for security dynamics in the conflict affected eastern regions of the country, where armed groups remain active.

The United Nations has not yet confirmed how the reduction of South African troops will impact the mission’s operational capacity. Defence experts say the move highlights the financial and logistical pressures facing peacekeeping contributors, as well as the need for regional bodies to strengthen their own security mechanisms.
