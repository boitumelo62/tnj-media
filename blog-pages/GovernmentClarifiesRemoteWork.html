Media Government Clarifies Remote Work Not an Official Policy Despite Fuel Concerns

March 27, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources has clarified that working from home is not an official government policy, despite growing public discussion around the idea as a response to rising fuel costs.

The statement comes as South Africans face increasing financial pressure linked to transport expenses and broader economic challenges. Speculation had emerged that remote work could be formally encouraged as a way to reduce commuting costs and ease congestion. However, the department has moved to dispel these assumptions, stating that no such directive has been adopted at a national level.

While some private sector companies have embraced flexible work arrangements, government policy remains focused on broader energy and economic strategies. Officials have indicated that addressing fuel price pressures requires structural solutions rather than temporary behavioural changes. The clarification may disappoint those who see remote work as a practical way to manage rising living costs.

However, it also reflects the complexities involved in implementing nationwide policy shifts, particularly in a country with diverse economic sectors and varying levels of digital access. The conversation around remote work is unlikely to fade, as both employers and employees continue to explore alternatives in response to economic pressures.

For now, the government’s position remains clear, with no formal move toward making work from home a standardized policy across the public sector.
