Business Apple Weathers Tariff Storm with Strategic Moves and Strong Q3 Growth

August 1, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Despite mounting trade tensions and rising tariffs, Apple has reported robust financial growth in its third fiscal quarter, revealing a 9.6 percent revenue increase compared to the same period last year. A surprising contributor to this growth was a wave of early consumer spending, as buyers rushed to purchase Apple hardware ahead of expected price hikes due to new tariffs.

CEO Tim Cook noted that around $825 million in revenue came from this early buying spree, which occurred mostly in April, the first month of the quarter. Chief Financial Officer Kevan Parekh confirmed the figure during the company's quarterly earnings call, attributing the bulk of the purchases to US-based customers. Although Apple faced $800 million in tariff-related costs during the quarter, the early surge in sales helped balance the financial impact. Looking ahead, Parekh warned that tariff costs are expected to rise to $1.1 billion in the next quarter.

Cook clarified that most of Apple’s tariff exposure stems from products affected by the Section 232 Investigation, particularly items imported from China. However, Apple’s supply chain has diversified in recent years. The majority of iPhones sold in the US now originate from India, while laptops and other hardware are predominantly manufactured in Vietnam. Yet, the company may soon feel the pressure from a new 25 percent US tariff on goods from India, a measure scheduled to take effect Friday under President Trump’s evolving trade agenda.

Despite this challenge, India has quickly emerged as the top exporter of mobile phones to the United States, thanks to its growing electronics manufacturing sector.

Apple is also reinforcing its domestic efforts. The company plans to open a new Manufacturing Academy in Detroit next month, developed in partnership with Michigan State University. The facility will serve as a training ground for businesses looking to modernize their manufacturing capabilities and adopt AI-driven processes. Virtual courses in programming and project management will also be offered.

As part of its ongoing pledge to invest $500 billion in the US over the next four years, Apple recently entered a $500 million partnership with MP Materials, the operator of America’s only rare earth mine. Under the agreement, Apple will build a recycling center for the firm in exchange for access to critical raw materials for its devices.

In terms of financial performance, Apple posted quarterly revenue of $94 billion, a 10 percent year-on-year increase, while profits rose nine percent to $23.4 billion. The company saw growth across all major regions and product lines, with the exception of the iPad. Apple’s ability to absorb tariff-related shocks and adapt its supply chain underscores its evolving global strategy, even amid escalating geopolitical and trade uncertainties.
