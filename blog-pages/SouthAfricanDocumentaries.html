Media South African Stories Deserve More Than Highlights

October 6, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Popular Blogs:

South Africans know what it means to have too much of something. Everyone’s got a braai, though not every fire’s worth lighting. Everyone’s got an opinion on rugby, though not every one is worth your time. And these days, it feels like every week brings a new documentary, all claiming to be “must watch.” It’s natural to roll your eyes and mutter, “Eish, another one.” But then along comes Chasing the Sun 2 on SuperSport, a follow up to the Springboks' World Cup journey. It could’ve been just another highlight reel big tackles, big tries, big egos. Instead, it goes deeper.

It lingers in moments most sports docs speed past the crack in a coach’s voice, the emotional weight stitched into a jersey. It reminds you that rugby in South Africa is never just a game. It’s about who we are, where we’ve been, and what we’re still trying to become. It holds space for both pride and pain.

What makes this kind of storytelling powerful isn’t just access to behind the scenes footage or big name interviews. It’s the way it earns emotional truth. That kind of connection can’t be faked in the editing room. It comes from staying with the mess, the contradictions, the uncomfortable silences.

"I’ve learned that firsthand. While making Young Gifted and Queer, I found that truth didn’t sit neatly in the scripted interviews or sweeping social commentary. It lived in the quiet moments a hand squeeze before coming out to a parent, a laugh that somehow made the heaviness bearable. Those small, unscripted details said more than any statistic ever could. They didn’t just show what happened they let you feel it." – Renaldo Schwarp

That’s where South African storytellers stand apart. We’re shaped by layered identities, by unresolved histories, by the weight of what’s never quite been healed. Our best documentaries don’t try to impress international audiences. They start from the inside raw, honest, unvarnished. They don’t tidy things up. They go straight for the nerve.

So no, there aren’t too many documentaries. There are just too many that play it safe. The ones that matter are the ones with teeth.
