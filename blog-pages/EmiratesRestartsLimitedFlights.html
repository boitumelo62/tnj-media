Media Emirates Restarts Limited Flights as Thousands of South Africans Remain Stranded

March 6, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Emirates has resumed limited flights between the United Arab Emirates and South Africa as authorities attempt to assist thousands of South Africans who remain stranded in the Middle East amid escalating regional tensions.

More than 6 400 South Africans have reportedly been unable to travel home after flights were disrupted due to the growing conflict in the region. The disruptions have affected several major travel routes linking the Gulf and southern Africa, leaving many travellers stuck in transit hubs or awaiting confirmation of new travel arrangements.

The airline confirmed that a restricted number of flights have been reinstated while aviation authorities continue to assess safety conditions in affected airspaces. Emirates is currently operating a reduced schedule, prioritising passengers who were already booked on earlier flights that were cancelled.

South African travellers have expressed concern about extended waiting periods and uncertainty around when normal flight schedules will resume. Some have been forced to extend hotel stays while others have sought assistance from the South African government and diplomatic missions in the region.

Officials have urged travellers to remain in contact with airlines and embassies for updated travel information. Government representatives have also been monitoring the situation closely as diplomatic and humanitarian efforts continue to address the challenges faced by citizens abroad.

Air travel in the Middle East has faced significant disruptions in recent days as airlines adjust routes and suspend operations in certain areas due to security concerns. These developments have created ripple effects across international flight networks, affecting passengers travelling to and from Africa, Europe and Asia.

While the resumption of limited Emirates flights offers some relief to stranded travellers, aviation analysts warn that disruptions could continue if tensions in the region intensify.

Travellers have been advised to check flight schedules regularly and remain prepared for further delays while authorities and airlines work to stabilise operations and safely transport passengers back to their destinations.
