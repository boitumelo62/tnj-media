Media Zakes Bantwini raises concern over Samas decline

December 16, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Zakes Bantwini has offered a candid assessment of the current state of the South African Music Awards following the conclusion of the 31st edition held on Sunday, questioning how a once unifying cultural moment has lost its national significance.

The Grammy award winning DJ and producer shared his reflections in a strongly worded opinion piece, tracing the journey of the Samas from a celebrated national event to what he believes has become a largely overlooked ceremony. He recalled a time when the awards commanded the attention of the entire country, drawing in not only musicians but South Africans from all walks of life.

According to Bantwini, the Samas once carried the same cultural weight locally as the Grammys do in the United States. He described how families, professionals and public figures would all gather around their television screens, making the ceremony more than an industry affair but a shared national experience.

He contrasted that era with the present reality, saying this year’s ceremony passed with little public interest. Bantwini noted the lack of social media engagement, poor artist attendance and minimal fan excitement. He said the buzz that once surrounded the red carpet, interviews and post event conversations has all but disappeared, allowing the awards to fade from public consciousness.

Bantwini argued that local recognition has increasingly been eclipsed by international acclaim, with South African audiences often waiting for global platforms to validate their own artists. While acknowledging the importance of international success, he warned that prioritising overseas approval risks undermining local institutions and sends a damaging message to emerging talent.

He further criticised the judging process, questioning the relevance of some award recipients and accusing the Recording Industry of South Africa of failing to lead meaningful reform. Bantwini called for greater transparency, accountability and diversity within judging panels, including younger voices and genre specialists who are closely connected to current trends.

Emphasising his personal investment in the industry, Bantwini said his criticism comes from a place of gratitude and responsibility. He maintained that rebuilding trust in the Samas is possible, citing international examples where music awards have successfully reformed. However, he stressed that real change will require bold leadership and a genuine commitment to recognising excellence across the full spectrum of South African music.
