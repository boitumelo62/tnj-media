Media Ramaphosa Receives Interim Madlanga Commission Report as Focus Turns to Police Accountability

December 19, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

President Cyril Ramaphosa has received the interim report of the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry, marking a significant milestone in a process aimed at examining serious allegations within the KwaZulu Natal police leadership. The report was handed to the President three months after KwaZulu Natal Police Commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi became the first witness to testify before the commission.

The Madlanga Commission was established to probe claims of misconduct corruption and political interference within the provincial police service. Its work has attracted national attention due to the gravity of the allegations and the broader implications for public trust in law enforcement. The interim report is expected to outline preliminary findings emerging from testimony and evidence presented so far while also highlighting areas requiring further investigation.

Although the contents of the interim report have not yet been made public the Presidency has confirmed that it will be studied carefully. Ramaphosa is expected to consider its recommendations once they are formally communicated. In previous commissions of inquiry interim reports have often served as an early warning system pointing to systemic weaknesses governance failures or urgent corrective steps that cannot wait until the final report.

Commissioner Mkhwanazi’s testimony set the tone for the inquiry with claims that shook the policing establishment. His appearance was followed by evidence from other senior officials and experts who painted a picture of deep rooted challenges within the provincial police command. Issues such as alleged political pressure breakdowns in command structures and failures in discipline have featured prominently during hearings.

Civil society organisations have welcomed the submission of the interim report saying it reflects progress in a process many South Africans view as critical to restoring integrity within the police. At the same time they have urged the President to act decisively once the findings are considered especially if the report identifies immediate risks to effective policing.

The commission continues its work and is expected to call more witnesses in the coming months. Its final report will likely include detailed recommendations on reforms disciplinary action and possible criminal referrals. For many communities in KwaZulu Natal and beyond the outcome of the inquiry is seen as a test of the state’s commitment to accountability transparency and the rule of law.

As the President reviews the interim findings the spotlight remains firmly on how government responds and whether the commission’s work will translate into tangible change within the police service.
