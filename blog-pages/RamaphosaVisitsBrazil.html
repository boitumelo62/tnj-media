Media Ramaphosa Visits Brazil to Strengthen Strategic Ties

March 10, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

President Cyril Ramaphosa has begun a two-day state visit to Brazil as South Africa seeks to deepen diplomatic and economic relations with one of its key international partners.

The visit includes high level talks with Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and is expected to focus on expanding cooperation between the two nations across several sectors, including trade, energy, agriculture and technology.

South Africa and Brazil share strong historical ties and are both members of the BRICS bloc, which also includes Russia, India and China. The grouping represents some of the world’s largest emerging economies and has increasingly positioned itself as an alternative platform for global economic cooperation.

Officials say the visit is aimed at strengthening bilateral relations while also exploring opportunities for new investment and trade partnerships. Brazil is one of South Africa’s largest trading partners in Latin America, with economic exchanges covering agricultural products, machinery and mineral resources.

Ramaphosa’s trip also comes at a time when South Africa is seeking to expand its international partnerships amid shifting global economic dynamics. Strengthening relations with fellow developing nations has become a key component of the country’s foreign policy strategy.

During the visit, delegations from both countries are expected to discuss ways to boost collaboration in renewable energy, infrastructure development and food security. Brazil’s expertise in biofuels and agricultural production is of particular interest to South Africa as it works to improve energy resilience and agricultural output.

Political analysts say the meeting between Ramaphosa and Lula could also reinforce cooperation within BRICS as the group seeks greater influence in global financial institutions and international diplomacy.

The visit is expected to conclude with a joint statement outlining areas of cooperation and potential agreements between the two countries.
