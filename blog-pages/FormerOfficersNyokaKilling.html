Media Two former officers guilty in Nyoka killing

December 3, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Two former apartheid era police officers have been convicted for their role in the 1987 killing of student activist Caiphus Nyoka, closing one of the many unresolved cases linked to security branch operations during apartheid.

The pair, whose identities were long shielded by years of silence and delayed investigations, were found guilty after the court accepted evidence showing they were directly involved in the operation that resulted in Nyoka’s death. The ruling follows a renewed push to revisit stalled apartheid era crimes after the Truth and Reconciliation Commission left hundreds of cases incomplete.

The court heard how Nyoka, a prominent organiser in his community, had been repeatedly harassed, monitored and targeted by state operatives as part of a broader crackdown on political resistance. Testimony from surviving witnesses revealed that Nyoka was detained, beaten and ultimately killed under circumstances that were deliberately concealed at the time.

Investigators presented declassified documents, forensic reconstructions and sworn statements that placed the two officers at the scene and confirmed their participation. Attempts by the defence to argue that the officers were following orders did little to sway the court, which ruled that the killing was intentional and unlawful.

Nyoka’s family, who have waited nearly four decades for justice, expressed deep emotion after the verdict. Relatives said the ruling brings long needed closure and restores dignity to Nyoka’s memory. Human rights groups also welcomed the conviction, calling it an essential step in addressing historical abuses that were never prosecuted.

The court will now move into sentencing, where arguments are expected to focus on the passage of time, the gravity of the offence and the families’ call for meaningful accountability. More apartheid era cases are expected to move forward in the coming months as authorities continue reviewing unresolved files.
