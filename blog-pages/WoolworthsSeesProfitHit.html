Business Woolworths Sees Profit Hit Despite Strong South African Sales

July 31, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Woolworths has issued a warning that its earnings are expected to take a significant hit in the 2025 financial year, mostly due to issues with its Australia-based Country Road Group (CRG). While the group did see an increase in turnover and concession sales up 6.1% and 6.8% respectively when adjusted for currency this wasn't enough to counter broader challenges. Both South Africa and Australia are facing tough economic conditions, and global trade uncertainties have added further pressure.

Despite the growth in sales, Woolworths expects its basic earnings per share to fall by about 5%, landing between 263.4 and 277.3 cents per share. Even more concerning, its headline earnings per share are expected to decline between 22% and 27%, ranging from 257.2 to 274.8 cents.

In Australia, Woolworths did manage to sell a major property that houses David Jones for A$223.5 million, making a profit of R792 million. But this positive outcome was overshadowed by a major blow an impairment of R917 million after reassessing the value of some of CRG’s brand assets. The weaker-than-expected performance from CRG, especially in the second half of the year, has added to the problem. Sluggish sales and shrinking profit margins made the group’s operational challenges even worse.

Back home in South Africa, the picture is somewhat better, though not without its own difficulties. Even with interest rates easing and inflation slowing, consumers are still cautious about spending. Despite that, Woolworths South Africa showed strong performance. Turnover and concession sales rose by 9.4%, with the food division doing particularly well. Food sales went up by 9.2%, not counting the newly acquired Absolute Pets. Online sales in food also grew significantly, with Woolies Dash up by 41.6% and total online food sales up 32.9%, now making up 6.6% of food revenue.

Fashion, Beauty and Home (FBH) also saw modest improvement. Sales in this category increased by 4.7%, and even more so (5.1%) in comparable stores. Online sales for FBH grew by 22.8%, now making up 6.6% of total sales in that segment. Beauty stood out in particular, with strong growth of 14.7%, helping to solidify Woolworths' position as a top beauty retailer in South Africa. The company has also been streamlining operations reducing physical retail space by 2.3% to focus more on efficiency.

Woolworths Financial Services saw a small drop in its book value by 2.7% compared to last year, though it would’ve been up slightly if not for the sale of a R1.6 billion portion of its legal book. On a positive note, the impairment rate improved to 6.1% from 7.0% the previous year. Overall, while Woolworths is seeing decent results in South Africa, especially in food and beauty, its performance in Australia is dragging down the group’s overall financial health going into 2025.
