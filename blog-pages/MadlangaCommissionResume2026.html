January 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Madlanga Commission of Inquiry is set to resume public hearings on 26 January, marking a renewed phase in its investigation into matters of public concern. The commission had previously paused proceedings to allow for administrative preparations and the review of submissions already received.

The inquiry is tasked with examining allegations related to governance failures and institutional conduct, with the aim of establishing accountability and recommending corrective measures. Its work has drawn significant public interest, given the broader national focus on transparency and ethical leadership.

Commission officials confirmed that upcoming hearings will include testimony from a range of witnesses, including senior officials and expert analysts. These sessions are expected to shed further light on systemic weaknesses and decision making processes within affected institutions.

Legal observers note that the resumption of hearings signals a critical moment for the commission, as evidence presented could influence future policy reforms and potential legal action. The commission has reiterated its commitment to fairness, due process and public accessibility.

Members of civil society have welcomed the announcement, urging the commission to maintain momentum and ensure that its findings lead to tangible outcomes. The final report is anticipated to play an important role in strengthening oversight mechanisms and restoring public confidence.
