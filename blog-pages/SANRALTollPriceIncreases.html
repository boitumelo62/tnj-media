Media SANRAL Confirms Toll Price Increases from March, Motorists Brace for Higher Costs

February 20, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The South African National Roads Agency has announced that toll tariffs across major national routes will increase from 1 March, adding to the financial pressure on motorists and transport operators.

SANRAL confirmed that toll fees will rise by 3.12 percent in line with inflation and the Consumer Price Index. The adjustment was published in the Government Gazette and is part of the agency’s annual tariff review process. Officials say the revenue is needed to maintain, operate and improve toll roads and to service debt linked to major road infrastructure projects.

According to SANRAL, motorists driving light vehicles between Johannesburg and Durban currently pay around R341.50 in toll fees. This will increase to about R352.15 after the new tariffs take effect. Fees on other major routes such as the N1, N2, N3 and N4 will also rise, although the exact amount will vary depending on distance travelled and specific toll plazas.

Light motor vehicles, including cars, minibuses and motorcycles, will see modest increases per toll gate, while medium and heavy vehicles such as buses and trucks will face higher increases due to their impact on road infrastructure. Industry groups warn that these increases could translate into higher transport and logistics costs, which may ultimately be passed on to consumers through increased prices for goods and services.

SANRAL says it is aware of the economic strain on households and businesses but insists the adjustments are necessary to ensure safe and reliable national roads. The agency argues that well maintained roads are critical for economic activity, trade and daily commuting.

Motorists and transport operators have expressed frustration, citing rising fuel prices, vehicle maintenance costs and other living expenses. Some road users have called for greater transparency on how toll revenue is spent and whether alternative funding models could reduce the burden on drivers.

Despite criticism, the tariff increases are set to proceed as scheduled, and motorists are advised to budget for higher toll costs from March.
