Business Shoprite Leaves More African Markets as South African Sales Surge

October 6, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

hoprite, South Africa’s biggest grocery retailer is closing down its operations in Ghana and Malawi. This move is part of a larger shift in strategy where the company is pulling back from several other African countries to concentrate more on its South African market. At one point Shoprite had big plans to lead food retail across Africa. It managed to expand into about 15 countries and outpaced competitors like Pick n Pay and even Walmart owned Massmart. But that ambitious expansion didn’t go as expected. Running stores outside of South Africa proved to be tough. Many of these markets had serious economic issues things like weak currencies, high inflation, costly import duties, and landlords demanding rent in US dollars all of which made it hard for Shoprite to stay profitable.

In Malawi, Shoprite is wrapping up after 25 years. It already signed a deal in June to sell its five stores and in September, Malawi's Competition and Fair Trading Commission gave the green light but with conditions. The new owner Karson has to keep any workers who want to stay give proper benefits to those who leave and report back every few months for two years to prove they’re following the rules. Karson will rebrand the stores as "Shopwise Trading Limited" and continue selling groceries. Still there’s some concern about whether they’ll manage to keep the business running smoothly or protect jobs. Malawi’s retail sector in general has been struggling.

Even though it’s the second biggest part of the country’s economy it’s been shrinking. The reliance on imports and constant lack of foreign currency are making things very difficult for businesses like Shoprite. In Ghana Shoprite is also selling off its stores seven outlets and a warehouse. The sale hasn’t been finalized yet but the company says it’s “highly probable,” so it’s almost certain they’ll exit there too. These steps follow earlier exits from Nigeria Kenya, Uganda, Madagascar and the DRC. Along with leaving these markets Shoprite has also stopped pouring money into operations outside of South Africa. Instead it’s investing heavily at home and the results are showing. In South Africa the story is completely different. While it’s shutting down in other parts of Africa Shoprite is growing fast in its home market.

For the year ending in June 2025, the company brought in over R250 billion in sales, which is R20.6 billion more than the previous year. Its main supermarket brands Shoprite and Usave added R6.5 billion in turnover, growing by 5.9%. Checkers their more premium but still affordable brand did even better. It grew by 13.8%, adding R11.6 billion. Checkers has been key to the company’s recent success opening 68 new outlets over the year including 36 LiquorShop stores. Now Checkers runs 350 supermarkets with 40 of those being large format Hypers. CEO Pieter Engelbrecht credits a lot of the growth to Checkers’ “FreshX” format, which focuses on offering fresher, higher quality food.

He said converting more stores to this model, while also expanding into areas where Shoprite has less presence, remains a top goal. In total Shoprite added 225 new stores in South Africa this past year bringing its total to 2,577 local supermarkets. Even with the exit from Ghana and Malawi and the sale of its furniture division Shoprite still finished the year with more stores overall growing its total network to 3,908 outlets after adding a net 269. So while Shoprite’s dream of being Africa’s retail giant has taken a step back its focus on South Africa seems to be paying off.
