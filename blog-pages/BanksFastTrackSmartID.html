Media Banks Set to Fast Track Smart ID Applications Through New Digital Partnership

March 12, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africans may soon find it far easier to apply for a Smart ID thanks to a new partnership between the Department of Home Affairs and major banking institutions. Seventeen bank branches across the country are expected to begin offering a streamlined digital service that allows qualifying clients to apply for Smart ID cards within minutes.

The initiative forms part of government efforts to modernise identity services and reduce the long queues that have become a common feature at Home Affairs offices. Through the system, clients visiting participating bank branches will be able to submit their applications digitally, complete biometric verification and arrange collection through the same channel.

Home Affairs has partnered with Capitec and Standard Bank to roll out the service in selected branches as part of an initial expansion phase. Officials say the programme builds on earlier collaborations with banks that allowed South Africans to collect Smart IDs and passports through certain financial institutions.

The department believes the new process will significantly reduce pressure on physical Home Affairs offices while improving convenience for citizens. Clients will still be required to meet identification and verification requirements, but the digital process is expected to shorten the time needed to complete an application.

Government has also indicated that more bank branches may be added in future if the pilot phase proves successful. The broader goal is to create a national digital ecosystem where identity related services can be accessed quickly and securely through trusted institutions.

Industry observers say the move reflects a growing trend in which banks play a role in delivering certain public services. With millions of South Africans already visiting banks regularly, officials believe the model can help bring services closer to communities.

If the rollout proceeds smoothly, the partnership could mark an important step toward a more efficient identity management system in South Africa.
