Media Australia Shuts Down Millions of Underage Social Media Accounts

January 16, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Social media companies have removed or restricted about 4.7 million accounts linked to children in Australia since a nationwide ban on under 16s using major platforms came into effect, officials have said.

Communications Minister Anika Wells said the figures showed the government had succeeded in enforcing the law despite opposition from major technology firms. She said parents could now be more confident that children were being protected from harmful online environments.

The data was reported by 10 social media platforms and provides the first indication of the scale of the ban introduced in December. The law was passed amid concerns about child safety mental health and online harm.

Platforms including Facebook Instagram TikTok X YouTube and Snapchat face fines of up to 49.5 million Australian dollars if they fail to remove underage accounts. Messaging services such as WhatsApp and Facebook Messenger are exempt.

eSafety Commissioner Julie Inman Grant said the figures were encouraging and showed progress in limiting social media access for children. She added that companies are now expected to focus on preventing new underage accounts from being created.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said the policy was already influencing other countries to consider similar measures.
