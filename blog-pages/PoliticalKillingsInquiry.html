Media Suspended Police Minister Defends Disbanding of Political Killings Task Team

October 17, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Suspended Police Minister Senzo Mchunu appeared before the Ad Hoc Commission of Inquiry into Political Killings on Thursday, where he strongly defended his decision to disband the Political Killings Task Team. Mchunu explained that his choice was guided by long-standing departmental policy, stating that such task teams are “never intended to exist indefinitely” and have limited operational lifespans regardless of their funding or success.

The former minister’s appearance before the commission forms part of the inquiry into allegations that his decision may have contributed to a rise in politically motivated violence, particularly in KwaZulu-Natal. Mchunu rejected this claim, insisting that the task team’s role had been fulfilled and that its continuation risked overlapping with existing structures within the South African Police Service (SAPS).

He told the commission that disbanding the team was a “management decision rooted in resource optimization”, not a political move. Mchunu further noted that maintaining short-term task teams beyond their intended period creates inefficiency and confusion within permanent law enforcement units.

The commission is expected to continue hearings with other former senior officials to determine whether Mchunu’s decision had any bearing on unresolved political killings in the province.
