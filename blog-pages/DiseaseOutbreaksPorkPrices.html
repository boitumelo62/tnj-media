Media Disease outbreaks set to push pork prices higher as supply tightens

February 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South African consumers and retailers are likely to face further increases in pork prices in the coming months as disease outbreaks disrupt supply in the formal pork market. African swine fever and foot and mouth disease have affected commercial piggeries in parts of the Free State, North West, KwaZulu Natal and areas northeast of Pretoria, tightening the number of animals available for slaughter.

Industry stakeholders warn that the pork market is particularly sensitive to supply shocks, even when the overall reduction appears modest. According to industry estimates, South Africa slaughters roughly 72,000 pigs a week across both formal and informal markets. Losses linked to recent outbreaks are estimated at around 7,000 pigs in the formal sector, a shortfall that could have an outsized impact on pricing.

Market analysts say pork demand tends to be relatively inelastic, meaning even small disruptions can lead to sharp price movements. Wholesale pork prices have already climbed in recent weeks, rising from about R32 per kilogram to around R40 per kilogram, with further increases expected as the reduced supply moves through the value chain from producers to retailers.

Producers that have avoided direct losses from the outbreaks are not insulated from broader market pressures. Large industry players have indicated that while strict biosecurity measures have protected their farms so far, prices are determined by overall market supply and demand rather than individual producer performance. The depletion of surplus stock that previously helped stabilise prices has further exposed the sector to volatility.

Industry bodies have moved to reassure consumers that pork remains safe to eat. African swine fever and foot and mouth disease affect animals and do not pose a risk to human health, and pork entering the formal market continues to be subject to veterinary inspection and regulatory oversight under the Meat Safety Act.

The South African Pork Producers Organisation says it is working with farmers, abattoirs and state veterinary services to contain the outbreaks and maintain confidence in the supply chain. However, with recovery in pig populations likely to take time, consumers may need to brace for sustained price pressure in the months ahead.
