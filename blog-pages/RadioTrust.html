Media Why Radio Still Holds Our Trust in a Noisy World

November 25, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The New Era of Radio Marketing

In a world where information moves fast and attention is divided, radio still holds on to something rare: trust. It isn’t earned through clever algorithms or automated feeds, but through tone, consistency, and genuine care. It’s built by real people who show up every day with intention, and by listeners who return because they believe in the voices they hear. Radio might not always be the loudest part of the media landscape, but it remains steady. It listens, it reflects, it responds. And when things go wrong, it’s still the place people turn to first.

Back in 2016, when KwaZulu Natal faced a punishing drought, East Coast Radio made a simple request. The station asked listeners to drop off sealed bottles of water for hard hit communities. We expected a small response. Within hours, the parking lot was overflowing. People drove from far away. Children spent their pocket money. Schools and companies arrived with truckloads. Traffic piled up outside the station. A marquee had to be set up to shield the growing mountain of water from the sun. In just a few days, over a million litres were collected, later distributed safely by Gift of the Givers. What made it possible was trust listeners believed the call was genuine and urgent, so they acted immediately.

At Jacaranda FM, the trust built with listeners has grown steadily through many community focused efforts. One of the most recognisable is Good Morning Angels, which turns twenty in 2025. Since 2005, it has grown from a simple act of kindness into one of South Africa’s most respected upliftment initiatives. More than R120 million has been distributed to individuals and organisations over the years. In 2024 alone, listeners helped raise over R14.8 million, assisting dozens of organisations, families, and thousands of children. Contributions flowed in because listeners knew the station would use their support responsibly. Every request is carefully vetted, openly discussed, and handled with integrity. That kind of trust takes a long time to build, and it is reinforced every time listeners see their help genuinely changing lives.

One story in particular captures what this kind of connection can do. A young student who collected recycling to pay for his studies was on the edge of losing his chance to graduate because of unpaid fees. He reached out to Good Morning Angels, and listeners stepped in. They paid off his fees, covered the following year’s tuition, and helped with essentials so he could continue his studies. Today, he is a centre manager in his hometown. His journey is a reminder that when credibility and compassion meet, lives change.

Trust isn’t built in one moment. It grows through repeated acts of honesty, fairness, and humanity. Each time a station follows through, keeps a promise, or responds with empathy, that bond becomes stronger. And each time listeners give whether it’s money, water, or simply encouragement they’re drawing from that well of trust built over years.

The success of these efforts has nothing to do with how much airtime they get. It comes from the authenticity of the relationship between the station and the people who listen. Audiences give because they know their contributions matter and will be handled with care. Because trust grows slowly, stations must protect it with the same care and consistency that earned it in the first place.Even as platforms change and digital storytelling expands, radio’s greatest strength remains the same: it is live, local, human, and connected. It builds relationships that feel personal and real.

At Kagiso Media Radio, home to East Coast Radio and Jacaranda FM, trust is not a side effect of the work it’s the purpose behind it. The stations serve different audiences and operate independently, but both share a common commitment to honesty, accountability, and community connection. Whenever people respond to a call whether it’s donating R50, dropping off water, or simply sending a message it reveals something important. Radio is not just background noise. It’s a companion and a connector.
