Media Zuma ordered to repay R29 million in legal fees

October 23, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Former President Jacob Zuma has been ordered by the courts to repay R29 million in legal fees that were unlawfully covered by the state during his corruption trials. The ruling, delivered this week, brings to a close a lengthy legal battle over who should bear the costs of Zuma’s private defence.

The case stems from legal bills accumulated during Zuma’s years-long fight against corruption and fraud charges linked to the 1999 arms deal. Between 2006 and 2018, the state covered millions in legal expenses on Zuma’s behalf, a decision that has since been deemed unconstitutional.

The court found that the former president had no lawful entitlement to have the taxpayer fund his personal defence and that the payments amounted to an abuse of public resources. Zuma’s legal team is expected to challenge the ruling, arguing that the decision to cover the costs was approved by government officials at the time.

This is not the first time Zuma’s legal funding has come under scrutiny. In 2018, the North Gauteng High Court ordered him to repay similar costs, and the issue has resurfaced several times amid his ongoing corruption trial in the Pietermaritzburg High Court.

The latest order marks another legal setback for the former president, who has consistently maintained that he is the victim of political persecution. Government lawyers have confirmed that steps will now be taken to recover the R29 million from Zuma personally.
