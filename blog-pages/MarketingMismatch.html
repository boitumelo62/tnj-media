Media Marketing’s Mismatch: Consumer Attention vs. Media Investment

September 25, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Popular Blogs:

Media

Media

From Media Player to Global Pioneer

According to new research from Kantar, three of the top five advertising platforms that consumers actually prefer belong to Amazon: Amazon itself takes the top spot, while Twitch and Prime Video come in fourth and fifth. This comes from Kantar’s Media Reactions 2025 report, which is based on feedback from over 21,000 consumers and nearly 1,000 senior marketers around the world. Interestingly, there’s no overlap between the top five ad platforms consumers prefer and the ones marketers favor.

Consumers find Snapchat ads fun and less intrusive compared to last year, while TikTok ads are seen as especially attention grabbing and entertaining. Marketers, on the other hand, continue to stick with more traditional digital platforms YouTube, Instagram, Google, Netflix, and Spotify mirroring the exact ranking from 2024. Kantar’s media expert Gonca Bubani says there’s often a disconnect between where consumers like to see ads and where marketers choose to advertise. She points out that Amazon’s ad ecosystem stands out because it offers a mix of experiences.

Twitch, for example, is especially trusted by users, though many marketers mistakenly see its gaming audience as too niche. One platform that’s notably absent from both the consumer and marketer rankings is X (formerly Twitter). A year after Elon Musk sued brands for pulling their ad spend, things haven’t improved. In fact, almost 30% of marketers say they plan to cut their spending on X in the next year, with about one in eight planning to leave the platform altogether. X has now ranked dead last for trust among global brands for three consecutive years, largely due to ongoing issues with content moderation.

Looking ahead to 2026, many marketers plan to boost their budgets for influencer marketing and social commerce. Around 61% expect to increase spending on creators, and 53% plan to invest more in social shopping. Streaming TV is also getting a bump 54% of marketers will invest more in it, while traditional (linear) TV continues to decline, with over a quarter planning to scale back their spend there. That said, Bubani warns that working with influencers means giving up some control. Creators aren’t actors following a script they bring their own voice, and that’s part of the value. Brands that succeed with influencers are the ones that allow for creative freedom, within clear guidelines.

On the TV front, while many love to declare the "death of linear TV," it’s not quite that simple. Consumers still trust traditional broadcast ads, and those ads still have a strong impact. However, because TV ads are expensive to produce, marketers often spend more than they should. Bubani argues it’s smart to shift some of that budget to other platforms but stresses that creative should be tailored to each channel from the start not just repurposed from TV. There’s some good news for advertisers consumer receptiveness to ads is at an all time high. This year, 57% of people say they’re generally open to advertising, up from 47% last year.

According to Kantar, ads are seven times more effective among these receptive audiences. Still, trust remains a big issue. Concerns about AI generated ads are rising, with 44% of people saying they’re bothered by them up slightly from last year and 57% worried about fake AI created content. But on the flip side, attitudes toward AI are becoming more positive overall. Most marketers are already using AI to work more efficiently, though fewer are tapping into it as a tool for creative innovation. Bubani believes that AI’s true potential lies in treating it not just as a timesaver but as a creative partner.

When used with curiosity and openness, it can help marketers test ideas faster, better understand audiences, and make smarter decisions. Kantar’s findings are based on a robust study of over 21,000 consumers across 30 markets and nearly 1,000 senior marketers worldwide. The takeaway? Consumers are more open to advertising than ever but to truly connect, marketers need to rethink where and how they’re showing up.
