Business Governance Failures Force SARB Takeover of Ditsobotla Bank

August 6, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The South African Reserve Bank (SARB) has stepped in and taken control of Ditsobotla Primary Savings and Credit Co-operative Bank (DCB) because the bank was struggling with serious governance and financial management issues. DCB is based in Lichtenburg, located in the Ditsobotla Local Municipality in North West province. The bank is open to anyone in the Ngaka Modiri Molema District who wants to become a member.

DCB was formed by combining three savings and credit co-operatives Aganang, Ikageng, and Itireleng. These groups were originally made up of workers from Alpha Cement, the Lichtenburg Municipality, and Lafarge Cement. It officially became a registered bank with the SARB on March 1, 2014. By May 2025, it had 1,452 members and claimed to have assets worth R8.3 million, although this has not been audited yet.

The bank offered standard services like accepting deposits and giving out loans to its members. But things started going wrong. On August 1, 2025, Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana, based on SARB’s advice, placed DCB into “resolution.” This basically means the SARB has taken over running the bank in order to protect the interests of depositors and creditors.

Before June 2023, troubled banks used to be placed under curatorship where a curator would be appointed to manage things. But a law passed in 2021 changed this, creating a new resolution process that includes a deposit insurance system. SARB is now the authority responsible for managing banks under resolution.

DCB landed in this situation because it repeatedly failed to meet important regulatory requirements. It was losing money heavily, even though it technically could still pay its debts. The problem was that it didn’t meet the legal requirements for managing its liquidity (how easily it could access cash) and overall capacity.

On top of that, SARB found the bank's governance and internal systems to be weak and risky for depositors. Despite repeated warnings and oversight from the Prudential Authority (PA), DCB didn’t make the necessary improvements. It didn’t diversify its membership base enough to reduce the risk of relying too heavily on just one municipality, and its management and staff didn’t get the training needed to properly handle risk.

Because of the resolution process, a moratorium has been placed on DCB. That means all legal actions against the bank are paused, and the bank’s obligations are on hold for now. As a result, DCB members cannot access their funds at this time.

However, there is some protection for depositors. If you have money in DCB, you may be covered by the Corporation for Deposit Insurance (CODI), which will reimburse up to R100,000 per depositor. The person in charge of managing the resolution will first verify the depositors so that these payouts can happen smoothly. Once that’s done, members can also choose to close their accounts and move their money to another bank.

If you have a loan with DCB, you still need to keep up with your payments. Your legal obligation to repay hasn’t changed, and skipping payments could lead to penalties, increased loan balances, and a hit to your credit record.
