Media Pretoria Urges Calm as Middle East Conflict Deepens

March 2, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The South African government has called for maximum restraint as tensions escalate in the Middle East following military strikes and retaliatory attacks involving the United States, Israel and Iran.

In a statement issued on Sunday, the Department of International Relations and Cooperation expressed grave concern over the rapidly deteriorating security situation, warning that continued aggression could destabilise the broader region and disrupt global markets. Officials stressed that diplomacy and dialogue remain the only sustainable path to resolving disputes.

The latest flare up follows direct military action between the United States and Israel on one side and Iran on the other, raising fears of a prolonged confrontation. The conflict has already resulted in casualties and damage to infrastructure, with regional allies closely monitoring developments.

Pretoria reaffirmed its long-standing position that disputes must be settled through peaceful means in accordance with international law and the principles of the United Nations Charter. Government spokespersons cautioned that further escalation could have severe humanitarian consequences, particularly for civilians caught in densely populated urban areas.

South Africa also voiced concern about the potential economic fallout. The Middle East remains central to global energy supply chains, and instability could affect fuel prices and trade flows. Analysts warn that prolonged conflict may trigger volatility in oil markets, placing additional pressure on developing economies.

Political parties across the spectrum have echoed calls for de-escalation, though opinions differ on the role of global powers in the crisis. Some have urged a stronger stance against military intervention, while others emphasise the need for balanced diplomacy.

As tensions continue to mount, Pretoria says it is engaging with international partners and monitoring the situation closely. The government has appealed to all parties involved to prioritise negotiations over further military action in order to prevent a wider regional war.
