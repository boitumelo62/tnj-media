The use of buy now pay later services in South Africa has surged dramatically, doubling between 2024 and 2025 as more consumers turn to short term digital credit to manage rising living costs.

A new report by digital payments provider Payfast by Network International shows that South Africans are increasingly relying on deferred payment options to make everyday purchases, highlighting both a shift in consumer behaviour and growing financial pressure on households.

Buy now pay later products allow shoppers to split payments into smaller instalments, often without interest, making them attractive in a tough economic climate marked by high food prices, fuel costs and elevated interest rates. The report indicates that these products are no longer used mainly for luxury or discretionary spending, but are now being applied to essential goods and services.

Industry analysts say the rapid growth reflects how cash strapped consumers are seeking flexibility as disposable income continues to shrink. For many, buy now pay later has become an alternative to credit cards, particularly among younger consumers and first time credit users who may not qualify for traditional lending products.

However, the sharp rise in usage has raised concerns about over indebtedness. Financial experts warn that while these services appear convenient and affordable, they can encourage overspending and mask the true cost of purchases if consumers juggle multiple repayment plans at the same time.

Regulators are also watching the sector closely. Unlike traditional credit products, some buy now pay later services fall into regulatory grey areas, raising questions about consumer protection, affordability checks and the long term impact on credit health.

Payfast notes that merchants are increasingly adopting these payment options to boost sales and reduce cart abandonment, particularly in online retail. The company says the trend is likely to continue as more retailers integrate flexible payment solutions to meet consumer demand.

As buy now pay later becomes a mainstream feature of South Africa’s payments landscape, experts caution that financial education and clear regulation will be crucial to prevent short term convenience from turning into long term financial strain.
