Media ANC Admits Concern Over Losing Support from Black Middle Class

September 10, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The African National Congress (ANC) has acknowledged growing concern about waning support from South Africa’s black middle class, a group that has historically formed a significant part of its voting base. Party leaders say the trend poses a serious challenge ahead of upcoming elections, warning that the ANC must adopt more creative and responsive strategies to regain trust.

According to insiders, the party’s internal assessments show that middle-class black voters feel increasingly alienated due to issues such as poor service delivery, corruption scandals, and ongoing economic stagnation. Rising living costs, power cuts, and high unemployment among younger professionals have further deepened frustrations.

ANC spokespersons admitted that if the party fails to reconnect with this demographic, opposition parties could exploit the gap, especially in urban centres such as Johannesburg, Tshwane, and Cape Town. “We cannot take this bloc for granted,” a senior ANC official was quoted as saying. “They are educated, politically aware, and expect accountability. If we do not respond, we risk losing ground.”

Political analysts note that the black middle class is uniquely positioned in South African politics. While they have benefited from post-apartheid opportunities in education and employment, many feel that the government has failed to address inequality and provide reliable services. Some have shifted support towards newer opposition movements that promise reform, while others have withdrawn from voting altogether.

Experts believe the ANC will need to offer clear policy solutions, particularly around job creation, energy stability, and corruption, if it hopes to reverse the trend. “This is not just about slogans,” political analyst Professor Susan Booysen explained. “It’s about whether the ANC can demonstrate it is capable of delivering meaningful change.”

With local and national elections looming, the ANC faces mounting pressure to repair its relationship with this key constituency or risk significant electoral setbacks.
