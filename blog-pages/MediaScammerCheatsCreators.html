Media The Media Scammer Who Cheats Creators and Clients Alike

October 23, 2025 ● TNJ Media

In the fast moving world of media, where creativity and opportunity meet, there are unfortunately people who see trust as something to exploit rather than respect. One such man has made a name for himself not because of his talent, but because of how he scams others out of their money. He pretends to be a media professional, offering services like video production, event coverage, and brand promotion. He promises top quality work, quick delivery, and impressive results but behind the scenes, his only goal is to take people’s money and avoid any accountability.

When a potential client reaches out, he presents himself as confident and experienced, talking about his supposed connections in the industry. He often insists on getting paid upfront, claiming he needs the money to book equipment or secure a venue. To make everything seem legitimate, he sends fake invoices, contracts, and receipts. But once the payment is made, his true nature shows. He either disappears altogether or keeps delaying the project with one excuse after another. Time passes, and clients end up with nothing no videos, no event coverage, and no refund. He hides behind multiple fake business names and social media accounts that make him look professional at first glance.

But in reality, there’s nothing real behind those polished profiles. The damage he causes isn’t just about the money. His victims lose valuable time, miss important opportunities, and sometimes even face setbacks to their own reputations for trusting someone so unreliable. Scammers like this hurt more than just the people they cheat they harm the entire creative community. The media industry depends on trust, word of mouth, and collaboration. When someone takes advantage of that trust, it makes others more hesitant to work together, and that slows down creativity and progress for everyone. This situation should serve as a reminder that doing background checks is essential.

Before hiring anyone, take the time to verify their past work, talk to previous clients, and confirm whether they actually own the content they show. Look for reviews or complaints online, and if they claim to run a business, make sure it’s legally registered. Most importantly, never hand over full payment upfront unless there’s a clear, written contract outlining exactly what you’ll receive and when. Anyone who’s been scammed by this man should speak up. Sharing your experience can help others avoid falling for the same trap and bring attention to a growing issue that needs to be addressed. The media industry thrives on creativity, collaboration, and honesty values that scammers like this threaten to destroy.

By staying alert, supporting one another, and calling out unethical behavior, we can protect the integrity of the industry and make sure it remains a safe, fair space for everyone who works hard to create something meaningful.
