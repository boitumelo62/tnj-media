Media Prosecutors Confident in Senzo Meyiwa Murder Trial Case

March 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The National Prosecuting Authority says prosecutors handling the Senzo Meyiwa murder trial have presented enough evidence to suggest the case could succeed in court. National Director of Public Prosecutions Andy Mothibi expressed confidence in the progress made by the prosecution team as the high profile trial continues.

Meyiwa, the former national football team captain, was shot and killed during a robbery at a house in Vosloorus in 2014. His death shocked the nation and led to years of investigation before suspects were eventually arrested and charged.

Since the trial began, prosecutors have presented witness testimony, phone records and other evidence that they believe connects the accused to the crime. The case has attracted widespread public interest, with many South Africans closely following developments in court.

Mothibi said the prosecutors involved have worked carefully to build a detailed case. According to him, the evidence presented so far suggests that the prosecution has established a strong narrative around what happened on the night of the murder.

The defence has challenged several aspects of the evidence, including the credibility of certain witnesses and the interpretation of some forensic details. Lawyers representing the accused have argued that inconsistencies remain in the state’s case.

Despite those challenges, the prosecution believes the overall body of evidence supports the charges brought against the accused. Mothibi stressed that the trial process will ultimately determine whether the evidence meets the standard required for conviction.

Legal experts say the case remains complex because of the long period between the murder and the arrests. Over the years, the investigation faced criticism and numerous delays, which made gathering reliable evidence more difficult.

For many South Africans, the trial represents a chance for long awaited answers about the circumstances surrounding Meyiwa’s death. As proceedings continue, attention remains focused on the court’s evaluation of the evidence and whether it will finally bring closure to one of the country’s most prominent unresolved murder cases.
