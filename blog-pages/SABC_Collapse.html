Media SABC on the Brink of Collapse, Warns Parliamentary Committee

September 9, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The South African Broadcasting Corporation (SABC) is facing a deepening financial and structural crisis that could threaten its survival, according to warnings issued in Parliament this week.

Communications and Digital Technologies Committee chairperson Khusela Sangoni-Diko told MPs that the public broadcaster is struggling to operate due to outdated infrastructure and chronic underfunding. She cautioned that unless urgent interventions are made, the SABC could collapse altogether.

The broadcaster, once regarded as a cornerstone of South African media, has battled financial instability for years. Revenue shortfalls, declining advertising income, and widespread non-payment of TV licences have left it in a precarious position. At the same time, years of underinvestment in technology mean the SABC is falling behind competitors in the rapidly evolving digital space.

Sangoni-Diko emphasised that the crisis is not only about business viability but also about public access to information. Millions of South Africans rely on the SABC for news, educational programming, and entertainment. Its decline could leave large segments of the population without affordable access to local content.

Unions and media watchdogs have also raised concerns that the collapse of the SABC would undermine democracy by weakening one of the few platforms able to provide independent news to a national audience.

This is not the first time the public broadcaster has been in financial distress. In 2019, the government approved a R3.2 billion bailout to stabilise operations, following years of poor management and governance scandals. Despite this, the SABC continued to struggle with debt, falling advertising revenues, and outdated broadcasting systems. Earlier bailouts had similarly provided temporary relief without solving the corporation’s structural challenges.

Critics argue that stop-gap measures are no longer enough, and that only a comprehensive turnaround strategy will ensure the SABC’s survival. Such a plan would need to include financial restructuring, digital transformation, and a sustainable funding model that reduces dependence on government bailouts.

With its future hanging in the balance, the SABC has become a test case for the state’s ability to rescue struggling public institutions while safeguarding essential services for citizens.
