Business Economic Relief for Provinces as South Africa Posts 0.8% GDP Growth in Q2 2025

November 4, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Four provinces in South Africa the North West, Mpumalanga, Limpopo, and Gauteng have managed to avoid slipping into a technical recession after facing contractions in the first quarter of 2025. In the second quarter, all four provinces bounced back, each recording growth of at least 0.8%. Nationally, South Africa’s economy grew by 0.8% in the second quarter, a welcome improvement from the sluggish 0.1% growth in the first quarter.

A technical recession happens when an economy shrinks for two quarters in a row. It’s a quick way of gauging short-term economic health, while a full recession is much broader and looks at long term trends like employment, productivity, and income. Although South Africa’s short-term numbers show small signs of recovery, the bigger picture remains concerning. Over the past decade, average annual growth has stayed below 0.7%. Since the population continues to grow by about 1.2% to 1.5% each year, the economy isn’t expanding fast enough to keep up, meaning that the average South African is effectively becoming poorer despite the recent growth.

Still, escaping a technical recession is a positive sign. It gives struggling provinces a bit of breathing space after a tough few years. Every province in the country recorded some growth in the second quarter of 2025. The Northern Cape and Free State led with strong performances, growing by 1.0% and 0.9% respectively. The Western Cape, which had the highest growth in the first quarter, saw slower growth of 0.7% in the second quarter.

On a year to year basis, national GDP rose from 0.3% in mid 2024 to 0.9% by mid-2025. The biggest gains were seen in North West, Mpumalanga, and Limpopo, while Gauteng and the Western Cape experienced more modest improvements. Limpopo continued to stand out, building on its solid 0.9% growth from 2024 with another 1.0% in the second quarter of 2025. Its economy has been driven mainly by finance, real estate, and business services, along with steady performance in sectors like government, manufacturing, and trade.

Gauteng followed closely, supported by its strong financial sector. The North West province’s rebound was especially encouraging, as it went from a small contraction in 2024 to strong growth in the latest data. Both the Eastern Cape and Northern Cape also turned their fortunes around after declines last year. Despite these improvements, South Africa’s overall economic picture remains fragile. With GDP expected to grow by only about 1.0% for 2025, and population growth outpacing that at roughly 1.5%, people are still feeling poorer in real terms. In simple terms, the economy might be growing, but not fast enough to improve the standard of living the pie is slightly bigger, but there are more people sharing it.
