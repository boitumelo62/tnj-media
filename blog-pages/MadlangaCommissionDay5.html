“The Gaps Are Starting to Show”: Madlanga Commission Wraps Day 3, Masemola to Testify

Day 5 of the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry delivered more dramatic exchanges as National Police Commissioner General Fannie Masemola took the stand to answer to serious allegations raised earlier by KwaZulu Natal Police Commissioner Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi.

Testimony and points of conflict

General Masemola’s appearance was one of the most anticipated moments of the inquiry. He was questioned about claims that political interference had undermined the independence of the South African Police Service. Much of the focus was on the controversial decision to disband the Political Killings Task Team, which Mkhwanazi had previously argued was done under political pressure.

Masemola defended his actions but admitted that ministerial orders had placed him in a difficult position. At one point he said, “Mchunu’s order encroached on my mandate,” acknowledging that political instructions had interfered with his authority as police chief.

Commission chair Justice Madlanga pressed Masemola on why he had followed directives that may have undermined policing independence. Masemola denied that he had acted in bad faith, insisting that his decisions were guided by procedure and the responsibilities of his office.

New discoveries

While no single revelation overturned the narrative built in earlier sessions, Day 5 sharpened the conflict between two versions of events. Mkhwanazi has consistently argued that political leaders drove the dismantling of vital policing structures. Masemola, however, attempted to frame the decision as an operational matter rather than political interference.

The exchanges left the public with more questions than answers. Did Masemola submit to political pressure or did he act within the scope of his duties as he claims? And can the commission uncover the full truth behind the decisions that affected policing in KwaZulu Natal?

Quotes that mattered

  • “Mchunu’s order encroached on my mandate.” — General Fannie Masemola
  • “To me, it was a wrong conclusion that the minister has reached, perhaps someone influenced the minister to do this.” — Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi
  • “I want to demonstrate that the criminal justice system has been subject to a continuous threat. This commission will help us prevent total collapse of the justice system.” — Mkhwanazi on his first day

Looking ahead

Day 5 has highlighted the deep rift between the country’s top police officials and their conflicting versions of how politics may have shaped policing decisions. The inquiry remains focused on whether the independence of the police was compromised and whether accountability can be restored.

Day Six of the Madlanga Commission will continue on September 25 as today South Africans are celebrating Heritage Day. Wishing all South African readers a Happy Heritage Day.
