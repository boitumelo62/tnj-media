Business Lower Interest Rates on the Horizon for South Africa in 2026

January 12, 2026 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africans are looking increasingly likely to benefit from lower interest rates in 2026, as developments in the United States create room for further cuts by the South African Reserve Bank. Over the past 15 months, the repo rate has already come down to 6.75%, after peaking at 8.25% in September 2024. Decisions by the US Federal Reserve play an important role in the SARB’s thinking, as they influence the relative strength of the rand and the dollar, which in turn affects inflation and capital flows.

According to Investec Chief Economist Annabel Bishop, the renewed pace of US interest rate cuts after a largely stagnant 2025 has weakened the dollar. This has helped the rand strengthen by more than 10% against the dollar in the early days of 2026 compared to a year ago. Much of this improvement is linked to global investors moving away from traditional safe haven assets like US government bonds and looking instead for higher returns in riskier markets, including emerging economies such as South Africa.

A stronger rand is good news for local inflation, as it lowers the cost of imports and helps keep fuel and food prices in check. With the US having cut interest rates by a total of 175 basis points in the current cycle, compared to South Africa’s 150 basis points, the interest rate gap now favours South Africa. This supports the rand and improves investor sentiment. While financial markets are currently pricing in only one additional 25 basis point cut, Investec expects two cuts of 25 basis points each in 2026, likely in March and September, which would bring the repo rate down to 6.25%. A further cut is also expected in March 2027.

That said, the SARB has made it clear that interest rates will still need to remain somewhat restrictive for some time, until inflation expectations ease closer to its 3% target. Bishop cautioned that while the current environment supports further cuts, the outlook will still depend on how global conditions evolve, particularly around US policy decisions such as the possible removal of universal tariffs, which could change expectations around US rate cuts.

There is also some optimism that relief could come sooner than expected. Aluma Capital’s Frederick Mitchell believes the SARB may even cut rates at its upcoming January meeting, pointing to the rand’s strength, subdued inflation and rising gold prices, which support South Africa’s foreign reserves. He argues that these factors create favourable conditions for easing policy without reigniting inflation, and that a rate cut could help stimulate spending and investment at a time of ongoing global uncertainty. The SARB’s next Monetary Policy Committee meeting is scheduled for 29 January, and markets will be watching closely for any signs of an earlier than expected move.
