Media Amnesty International Slams Government Over Informal Settlement Crisis

November 5, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Amnesty International South Africa has accused the government of failing millions of citizens living in informal settlements as ongoing floods continue to destroy homes and displace families across multiple provinces.

In a strongly worded statement, the human rights organisation said authorities have shown “a pattern of neglect and poor disaster preparedness,” leaving vulnerable communities exposed to repeated climate-related crises.

Heavy rains over the past month have caused widespread flooding in KwaZulu-Natal, Eastern Cape, and Gauteng, washing away homes made from tin and wood and contaminating water supplies. Emergency services have struggled to reach affected areas due to poor road conditions and limited resources.

Amnesty researchers visiting flood-hit settlements reported dire conditions, including children sleeping in makeshift tents and families sharing scarce food and clean water. The organisation said that despite years of government pledges to upgrade informal settlements, progress remains minimal.

South Africa has an estimated 5 million people living in informal housing, according to census data. Many of these communities lack drainage systems, stormwater management, and reliable access to electricity.

Amnesty’s executive director, Shenilla Mohamed, said the state’s continued failure to act violates residents’ rights to adequate housing, health, and dignity. “The government’s response has been reactive instead of proactive. People cannot rebuild their lives every rainy season while officials make empty promises,” she said.

Humanitarian groups have urged the Department of Human Settlements to accelerate its relocation and upgrading programmes before the summer rainfall peaks.

The Department has acknowledged the challenges but said it is working with municipalities to prioritise high-risk areas. However, for thousands of families still wading through mud and debris, the promises offer little comfort.
