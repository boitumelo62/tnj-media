Lifestyle The Brand Bringing SA Sports Nostalgia Back to Life

November 22, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Sports in South Africa has felt alive again, with Springbok triumphs, Bafana Bafana’s resurgence, and the Proteas’ Test cricket form pulling fans back into stadiums and making the country’s sporting culture feel vibrant and connected. This renewed energy hasn’t been limited to the field fashion has become another way for fans to show pride, and the young brand Old School has become one of the favourites.

Old School was started in 2019 by brothers Daneel and Stephan Steinmann while they were still students in a Stellenbosch dorm room. They were hunting for the perfect vintage South African rugby jersey, and after posting their find on social media, thousands of people showed interest. That one moment kicked off what would become the Old School brand. What began with Bok jerseys has since grown into collaborations with Orlando Pirates, Kaizer Chiefs, and soon even English Premier League kits.

The brand recently opened its 15th store at MultiChoice City and announced a partnership with SuperSport. Daneel Steinmann says the broadcaster has been the constant force behind almost every major South African team for decades, and the collaboration felt natural. As part of the partnership, some SuperSport presenters will wear Old School apparel, and the new store even includes a recording space for SuperSport’s podcast productions. Steinmann says they saw an opportunity to help SuperSport tell stories on platforms like Instagram and TikTok, places where fans spend their time and where culture is shaped.

Old School brings freshness and creativity, while SuperSport brings legacy and trust something the brothers acknowledge they’re still building. Steinmann explains that the heart of the brand is not simply selling merchandise but telling stories that link fans to their teams. People buy Old School items because they feel part of something cultural, something meaningful not just because it’s clothing on a shelf. Fans have made that clear. Even with the higher price tag, Old School’s popularity is undeniable. When they set up a pop up store in Durban during the Nedbank Cup final, their Kaizer Chiefs and Orlando Pirates stock sold out so quickly that they had to fly in more from Johannesburg.

For Steinmann, this proves how much these teams mean to supporters and how much fans value quality pieces they can wear proudly long after the season ends. Since Old School’s rise, the brand has often been compared to Kasi Flavour, founded by Kabelo Kungwane, son of the late football legend Shakes Kungwane. Steinmann has nothing but praise for them, saying that in fashion, people often draw inspiration from the same moments and eras. Both brands tap into the nostalgia of 1990s Bafana Bafana, but that doesn’t mean one is copying the other. He believes there’s space for both, and the variety only benefits fans who get to choose what fits them best.

Steinmann also highlights that supporting Old School isn’t just about buying clothing it’s also supporting the teams themselves. Their agreements with clubs like Pirates and Chiefs ensure that a portion of every sale goes directly back to the teams, strengthening the connection between the brand, the fans, and the sport they all love.
