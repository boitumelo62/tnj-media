Media Out of Home Advertising High Impact, Low Unity

August 25, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Out of home (OOH) advertising is steadily gaining ground in the media landscape, standing out as one of the few traditional advertising formats that’s still growing. With more people living in cities and constantly on the move, OOH offers brands high visibility where people are already paying attention on streets, in transit, and across busy public spaces.

It’s hard to ignore a well placed billboard or digital screen, and with the rise of digital out of home (DOOH), the channel is becoming smarter, more targeted, and increasingly measurable, offering advertisers a way to reach broad audiences without being skipped or blocked. But despite this growth, the industry faces some real hurdles. One of the biggest issues is the lack of consistency across the sector. Different media owners use different ad sizes, measurement systems, and research methods. This makes it tough for advertisers to plan campaigns effectively and even harder to trust the numbers when measuring results.

Compared to digital platforms that offer detailed and standardised metrics, OOH still feels like it’s playing catch up in some areas. For media planners, this inconsistency creates unnecessary complications. When there’s no clear way to compare results across different providers, it becomes difficult to prove value, and that puts more pressure on individual media owners and agencies to do the legwork themselves.

If the industry came together to agree on shared standards like uniform ad formats, measurement tools, and trading systems it would make the entire planning and buying process smoother. This kind of alignment would also help OOH integrate more easily with broader campaigns that include digital and other above the line media. Another major concern is the growing disregard for municipal bylaws. Some media owners and advertisers continue to use illegal or non compliant ad sites, often without consequences. Unfortunately, many clients overlook this, focusing only on cost and visibility instead of whether the site is legal or ethical.

This kind of behaviour damages the credibility of the industry and puts responsible players at a disadvantage. Worse, it risks drawing the attention of regulators, which could lead to stricter enforcement or limitations on OOH advertising in general. To keep growing and earn long term trust, the OOH industry needs more cohesion, more accountability, and a stronger commitment to doing things right. That means not only embracing data and technology but also aligning on standards and upholding ethical practices before the cracks start to show.
