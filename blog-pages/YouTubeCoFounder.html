Media YouTube Co-Founder Warns of Short-Form Video Impact on Children's Attention

July 28, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Steve Chen, co-founder of YouTube, has voiced serious concerns about the increasing consumption of short-form video content by children, warning that the trend could hinder their ability to focus and process longer, more meaningful material.

Speaking during a recent appearance at Stanford Business School, Chen noted that while short videos on platforms like TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts offer fast entertainment, they may also contribute to addictive viewing habits and reduce children's capacity for sustained attention.

As a parent of two, Chen emphasized the importance of introducing children to longer-form content and creating digital boundaries. He suggested that tech companies should consider age-based usage limits, acknowledging the dilemma between user engagement and long-term well-being.

“There’s value in helping children slow down and engage with more substantial content,” Chen stated. He expressed concern that fast-paced visuals and continuous scrolling are rewiring young minds to expect immediate stimulation.

Chen’s perspective aligns with similar warnings from other tech leaders. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has previously commented on the cognitive risks associated with short-form media, pointing out the potential long-term effects on brain development. Psychologist and author Jonathan Haidt has also criticized the influence of social media and smartphones on youth, arguing that the erosion of attention spans may pose a greater threat than rising mental health issues among young people.

As debates around technology’s role in child development intensify, Chen’s remarks highlight the growing call for more mindful digital consumption, especially among younger audiences navigating an era of nonstop media exposure.
