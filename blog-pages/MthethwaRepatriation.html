Media Mthethwa’s Remains to Be Repatriated After Formal Processes Complete, Family Says

South African Ambassador Nathi Mthethwa Found Dead in Paris Hotel

The family of South Africa’s ambassador to France, Nathi Mthethwa, has confirmed that his remains will be brought back to South Africa once all official procedures are concluded.

Mthethwa was found dead this week outside a Paris hotel after he had checked into a room on the 22nd floor. French authorities are investigating the circumstances of his death, while also maintaining transparency and cooperation with the family, according to the family statement. (Source: EWN)

In the statement, the Mthethwa family said they had taken comfort from the compassion and unity shown by South Africans during this difficult time. They also expressed dissatisfaction with how they were informed of his passing. The family is awaiting clarity from ongoing investigations before making further public remarks.

Parliament’s international relations committee chairperson, Supra Mahumapelo, has called for a full and transparent investigation, stressing that speculation must be avoided. He said the government owes the Mthethwa family and the nation a clear account of what happened.

Senior provinces of the ruling party are expected to visit the Mthethwa family in KwaZulu-Natal this week to offer support and condolences. The late ambassador was a significant figure in government, having held roles including minister of police and minister of arts and culture.

Meanwhile, the Department of International Relations and Cooperation (Dirco) is working closely with the family and relevant authorities to coordinate the repatriation. But the minister has confirmed that South Africa will await the outcome of the French investigation before proceeding with formal repatriation arrangements. (Source: SABC)

French investigators have revealed that Mthethwa’s room window was found tampered with, and a note or message to his wife has been reported in which he expressed regret and intention to end his life. Authorities say no signs of a struggle or presence of drugs or toxins have been identified thus far. (Sources: AllAfrica, SABC, IOL)

A timeline compiled by local media shows that Mthethwa’s final known phone activity was around 3 p.m. on the day before his death. Later that evening, his wife received a distressing message. The next morning his body was discovered in the hotel’s courtyard at about 11 a.m. (Source: The Citizen)

At the time of his death, Mthethwa did not have a protection detail, a Dirco spokesperson confirmed. Ambassadors are not ordinarily provided with security. (Source: Citizen)

Mthethwa’s death has sparked calls from experts for parallel investigations in South Africa, including making use of international cooperation tools to obtain evidence, witness statements, autopsy reports, and forensic materials. Some have urged that a domestic inquiry run concurrently with the French probe. (Source: IOL)

As the country mourns, attention now turns to the investigation’s outcome, the repatriation of his remains, and the accountability process ahead.
