Media Foot and Mouth Disease Spreads to All Nine Provinces

March 4, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s foot and mouth disease outbreak has now spread to all nine provinces, with 935 cases formally reported nationwide.

The highly contagious viral disease affects cloven hoofed animals such as cattle, sheep, and goats. While it poses no direct threat to human health, it has serious economic implications for the agricultural sector, particularly livestock farmers and meat exporters.

Agricultural authorities say containment measures are being intensified as surveillance and vaccination efforts continue. Movement restrictions on livestock have been implemented in affected areas to prevent further spread of the disease.

The nationwide spread is a significant setback for farmers who were already grappling with rising input costs and challenging market conditions. Industry bodies have warned that prolonged outbreaks could impact food security, trade agreements, and rural livelihoods.

Export markets are especially sensitive to outbreaks of foot and mouth disease, with some countries imposing immediate bans or restrictions on animal products from affected regions.

Officials are urging farmers to comply strictly with biosecurity measures, report suspected cases promptly, and avoid moving animals without the necessary permits.

Government has indicated that additional resources will be allocated to manage the outbreak, while stakeholders across the agricultural value chain work to contain what has become one of the most widespread animal health crises in recent years.
