November 4, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The leaders of South Africa’s Government of National Unity (GNU) have declared the coalition united and strong, sixteen months after its formation. The announcement came after a high-level meeting in Pretoria, where coalition partners assessed progress and reaffirmed their commitment to shared governance.

President Cyril Ramaphosa chaired the session alongside representatives from major coalition partners, including the Democratic Alliance, Inkatha Freedom Party, and smaller regional parties. In a joint statement, the leaders expressed satisfaction with the coalition’s stability and its ability to manage challenges faced since its inception.

The GNU, formed in mid-2024 following an inconclusive national election, initially faced skepticism over whether rival parties could cooperate effectively. However, officials now claim that the coalition has demonstrated maturity, compromise, and shared focus on economic growth, energy reform, and institutional rebuilding.

Key achievements highlighted include improvements in electricity supply stability, anti-corruption measures within state institutions, and the beginning of fiscal reforms aimed at restoring investor confidence. Despite political tensions at times, the coalition has managed to maintain a working relationship that observers say has stabilized South Africa’s political landscape.

Analysts note that while ideological differences remain, the GNU’s endurance signals a growing acceptance of power-sharing as a viable model for governance. Still, challenges persist, including public frustration with unemployment and service delivery failures.

Coalition leaders acknowledged these concerns and pledged to intensify their efforts to meet citizens’ expectations. President Ramaphosa emphasized that unity within the GNU was not about uniformity but about commitment to national renewal. The meeting concluded with an agreement to hold a public accountability forum early next year to evaluate progress on major policy goals.
