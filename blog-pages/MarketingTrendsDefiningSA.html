As fractional CMOs working across South Africa and beyond, we’ve had the privilege of helping organisations build, repair, and scale their marketing and communications functions. Looking back at the work we’ve done over the past year, one thing is very clear even though marketing tools and technologies are advancing at breakneck speed, the fundamentals strategy, leadership, and the patience needed to build a world class marketing engine remain unchanged.

We’ve seen a handful of trends shaping the marketing landscape, and they’ve shown up consistently across different industries and company sizes. A recurring tension we encounter is the struggle between in-house marketing and outsourced support. Leaders often swing from wanting a fully in house team to leaning heavily on agencies and freelancers. Having everything in house gives you control, cultural alignment, and responsiveness, but it comes at a high cost and usually limits access to the wide set of specialist skills needed today.

A fully outsourced model can be efficient, but without someone owning the strategy and leading the work, things quickly become fragmented. One of our clients had a single marketing manager overseeing an entirely outsourced ecosystem. The output was exceptional, but she was stretched so thin that she had no space for strategic thinking. She was managing execution non stop, with no capacity to guide suppliers or shape the direction of the work.

This mix creates both consistency and agility and it’s where we’re seeing the strongest results. Another pattern we’ve repeatedly seen is the lack of strategic marketing thinking, especially in start ups and scale ups. Many rely on agencies to act as a bridge between business needs and marketing execution. Agencies are brilliant at delivering on a clear brief, but they are not typically positioned to lead commercial decisions, particularly in B2B environments. That gap results in scattered, isolated campaigns that look good on their own but don’t contribute meaningfully to growth.

This is exactly where fractional CMOs add tremendous value giving organisations strategic depth without the cost of a full time executive. Some of the most regretful feedback we’ve ever received is: “I wish I had brought you in a year ago.” AI and automation have reshaped marketing in profound ways. Things happen faster, data is cleaner, and creative tools are more accessible. But even with all this acceleration, quality still takes time. AI can streamline processes, but it cannot replace human judgment or creativity. We recently tested a supplier who claimed they could build a website in 48 hours.

AI can help us move more efficiently, but it doesn’t eliminate the time and care required to build something truly world class. On the measurement front, there is still a noticeable gap. Many organisations are swimming in dashboards but focusing on the wrong metrics. Vanity numbers impressions, likes, clicks dominate conversations, while the metrics that actually drive decisions often sit untouched. Real progress happens when marketing metrics integrate with sales and business intelligence, giving leaders visibility into acquisition cost, pipeline quality, retention, and customer lifetime value.

The marketing landscape is evolving fast, but not always in the ways we expect. Speed and automation are important, but without strategy, clarity, and patience, they mean very little. The organisations that thrived this year were the ones that blended both worlds. They embraced new technologies but stayed grounded in the fundamentals. They took the time to think before acting, measured the things that matter, and invested in people who could connect the dots not just complete tasks.
