Media Podcast Sparks Outrage Over Racist Remarks About Coloured Community – PA and DA Take Action

August 7, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

A storm of backlash has erupted after a recent episode of the Open Chats Podcast featured racially offensive remarks about the Coloured community, prompting swift action from major political parties.

During the episode, host Mthokozisi Methula made the inflammatory statement, “they chow each other,” insinuating that incest is common among Coloured people. His co-host, Sinothando Kama, added that Coloured people are “crazy,” further reinforcing harmful and racist stereotypes.

The comments sparked national outrage, with widespread condemnation from the public and civil society. The Patriotic Alliance (PA) responded immediately, filing criminal charges against both hosts at Brooklyn Police Station in Pretoria. PA spokesperson Liam Jacobs called the remarks “a vile and deliberate insult to the Coloured community” and announced plans to pursue the matter in the Equality Court and the South African Human Rights Commission (SAHRC). The party also demanded that YouTube remove the episode and that the SABC reconsider its platforming of the podcast.

The Democratic Alliance (DA) also took action, with Federal Chairperson Dr Ivan Meyer confirming that the party has reported the incident to the SAHRC. “These comments are not just ignorant they are an assault on human dignity and equality,” he said.

The podcast team later issued an apology on social media and removed the episode, stating they failed to handle the conversation with sensitivity. However, many, including advocacy group Cape Forum, rejected the apology as hollow and self-serving.

Prominent voices, including comedian Marc Lottering, responded with defiance and pride, sparking a wave of online solidarity. “Proud to be Coloured” trended on social media as communities rallied against the racism.

Legal consequences now loom for the podcast team, with growing calls for accountability and media reform in how racial discourse is handled in South Africa.
