Tech News How AI Overviews Are Reshaping Search and What Marketers Must Do Next

December 10, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Google has been moving toward a world where people get the information they need without ever leaving the search results page. Features like instant answers, knowledge panels and now AI Overviews mean users often find what they want instantly, without clicking on any website at all. While this feels smooth and convenient for the user, it creates real challenges for advertisers who rely on clicks, visits and measurable interactions to understand performance.

Because more answers now appear directly on the page, traditional metrics such as clickthrough rate don’t tell the whole story anymore. It’s harder to understand how users discover a brand, especially when their first interaction might come from an AI-generated summary rather than a website visit. This shift forces marketers to rethink what visibility means and how success is measured.

A practical first step is to optimise toward conversions, because they still show genuine intent even when clicks drop. Keyword strategies need to adapt as well. With fewer opportunities to earn a click, brands must focus more on high intent, transactional keywords the types of searches where users are close to buying and where AI Overviews are less likely to answer everything on their own.

It’s also important to find ways to stay visible on the page without getting dragged into costly bidding wars. Using SERP features such as sitelinks, callouts and structured snippets helps a brand stand out and claim more space without overspending. At the same time, relying solely on paid search becomes risky in this new environment. Strong brand campaigns across social, programmatic and other channels can boost awareness and help maintain visibility even when search behaviour becomes more unpredictable.

Google’s new AI Max for Search adds another layer of opportunity. It expands reach by using AI to match ads with a wider range of queries, including those advertisers didn’t explicitly target. That means when AI Overviews answer broad informational questions, advertisers still have a chance to appear when the user shows signs of being ready to act. AI Max also helps by generating and refining ad creative headlines, descriptions and other elements so messages stay relevant and competitive. Better controls and deeper reporting let marketers refine their ads as user behaviour continues to shift.

Still, AI Overviews don’t eliminate the challenges of zero click searches. Even with AI Max, brands need to rethink measurement and strategy. If a campaign is built mainly around broad, top of funnel keywords, it may lose both visibility and efficiency. Targeting more conversion driven, transactional terms remains essential. To use AI Max effectively in this changing landscape, start by enabling it and keeping a close eye on the new queries it brings in. Shift more of your keyword strategy toward high intent terms while letting AI Max explore less obvious opportunities.

Make sure your first party and CRM data are in order so you can feed accurate signals back into the system. And keep testing new creative, landing pages, bidding strategies and attribution models all matter more than ever. AI Overviews mark one of the biggest changes we’ve seen in search in many years, and it’s still unclear how things will look in the future. What is clear is that old measurement habits won’t be enough. As search evolves, AI Max offers a bridge between traditional keyword targeting and a more intent focused world. Brands that begin adjusting now will be in a much stronger position to capture high intent users as search continues to shift.
