Media Phala Phala Farm General Manager Claims No Knowledge of Hidden Cash

October 3, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

In proceedings before the Modimolle Regional Court in Limpopo, the general manager of President Cyril Ramaphosa’s Phala Phala estate testified that he had no knowledge of money being concealed on the property.

The case stems from a 2020 incident in which $580,000 in cash was reportedly stolen from the farm. The accused are Imanuwela David and siblings Floriana and Ndilinasho Joseph, who face charges related to theft and burglary of foreign currency.

During his testimony, the general manager was questioned about earlier evidence indicating that foreign currency had been placed in one of the rooms at the main residence. The prosecutor asked whether the lodge manager had ever informed him that cash was hidden in furniture or a couch. He responded that he knew nothing about any such stash.

He was also asked whether after the break-in he was made aware of the foreign currency. He replied again that he had not been informed of any hidden cash.

Earlier testimony in the trial had come from a manager responsible for the lodge on the farm who claimed responsibility for hiding the money inside a sofa. He also testified that he had told President Ramaphosa about the hiding place.

The general manager’s statements contribute to a broader contest of facts over who knew what and when in the run up to and following the theft. The trial continues as the court hears from additional witnesses and examines evidence regarding responsibility, access, and disclosure of the funds.
