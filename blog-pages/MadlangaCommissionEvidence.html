Media Madlanga Commission hears fresh ballistics evidence

October 29, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Madlanga Commission of Inquiry resumed hearings this week with new revelations pointing to possible criminal infiltration within the police and interference in high profile investigations. Testimony from ballistics experts and senior officials has drawn attention to irregularities at the national Forensic Science Laboratory and how key murder cases are connected.

Police forensics analyst Solomon Modisane testified that he was the first to conduct a comparative examination of firearms seized from suspects linked to the April 2024 murder of Vereeniging engineer Armand Swart. Modisane told the commission that microscopic analysis showed the same 7.62 by 39mm Kalashnikov AK47 used in the Bramley arrests was also the weapon that killed DJ Sumbody, DJ Vintos and Don Tindleni in Orlando, Soweto.

“I found that these cartridge cases were fired from the same firearm. They share a common origin,” Modisane said. “The firearm in question is a Russian made AK47, which was recovered from suspects arrested in the Bramley case.” He explained that the process of ballistic examination involves comparing class and individual characteristics on cartridge cases and bullets using a microscope. These tiny marks, he said, can link multiple crime scenes to a single firearm.

Modisane also revealed that he discovered duplicate entries of the same case in the national Forensic Science Laboratory database. In December 2024, he found that the Bramley case had been listed twice, once in Pretoria and again in KwaZulu Natal. Investigators had requested that the firearms be re-examined at the Amanzimtoti FSL Ballistics section following alleged irregularities in the original Pretoria analysis. The Pretoria laboratory management later approved the second examination, raising questions about the chain of custody and why multiple tests were conducted.

The commission is also examining broader allegations of corruption and political interference in police investigations. Testimony from former KwaZulu Natal police officials has suggested that the disbanding of the Political Killings Task Team may have been intended to shield certain individuals from prosecution. These claims led to the suspension of Police Minister Senzo Mchunu earlier this year and to the establishment of the Madlanga Commission to investigate the extent of interference in policing structures.

The latest evidence presented by Modisane has added weight to claims that several killings across provinces are linked by a small number of firearms, suggesting a deeper web of criminal coordination and possible manipulation of forensic results. Legal experts say that if these findings are confirmed, they could have far-reaching consequences for both criminal prosecutions and the integrity of police investigations.

The commission will continue hearing evidence from additional forensic analysts and police witnesses in the coming days. It is expected to focus on the handling of evidence at the Forensic Science Laboratory and the roles of senior officers involved in approving duplicate examinations. Public interest in the inquiry remains high as South Africans await clarity on how political influence may have affected justice in several major murder cases.
