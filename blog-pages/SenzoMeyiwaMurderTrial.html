Media Senzo Meyiwa murder trial resumes with cross examination set to continue

February 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The long running Senzo Meyiwa murder trial is set to resume today in the Gauteng High Court in Pretoria, with proceedings expected to pick up from ongoing cross examination of state witnesses and further legal arguments around the credibility of testimony presented so far.

The trial, which relates to the 2014 killing of the Bafana Bafana captain at the home of singer Kelly Khumalo in Vosloorus, has been marked by delays, witness disputes, and procedural challenges. Five accused stand trial on charges including murder, attempted murder, armed robbery, and possession of firearms and ammunition.

In recent sittings, the court heard testimony from forensic and ballistic experts as well as police witnesses who detailed the crime scene, evidence collection, and the investigation that followed Meyiwa’s fatal shooting. Defence counsel has questioned the integrity of the investigation, raising concerns about alleged contamination of the crime scene, missing evidence, and inconsistencies in witness statements.

One of the key developments before the adjournment was the continuation of cross examination of investigating officers, with defence teams probing how suspects were identified and whether alternative leads were adequately pursued. The defence has also indicated that it intends to challenge aspects of the state’s ballistic evidence and the chain of custody of critical exhibits.

The trial is expected to resume with further cross examination, followed by possible additional state witnesses as the prosecution seeks to reinforce its case. The court may also hear interlocutory applications as defence teams continue to test the admissibility and reliability of evidence presented.

Meyiwa’s killing shocked the nation and has remained one of South Africa’s most high-profile unsolved cases, drawing intense public and media scrutiny. His family has repeatedly called for justice, while the drawn-out legal process has highlighted broader concerns about policing, forensic capacity, and the pace of high-profile criminal prosecutions in the country.

As proceedings continue, the court will determine whether the state has presented a coherent and credible case against the accused, with further developments expected as the trial moves deeper into the evidence phase.
