Media Two more suspects arrested in Pretoria e hailing driver murder

February 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Police have arrested two more suspects in connection with the murder of an e hailing driver in Pretoria West, bringing the total number of people in custody to three.

Gauteng SAPS spokesperson Colonel Mavela Masondo said the two men, aged 30 and 26, were arrested over the weekend and will join a 25-year-old woman who was arrested on Friday. The trio is expected to appear in the Pretoria Magistrate’s Court on Monday on charges including murder and car hijacking.

Masondo said police are still searching for a fourth suspect believed to be linked to the crime. Preliminary investigations indicate that the victim, Isaac Satlat, was murdered by a man and a woman who had requested an e hailing service in Pretoria West. The hijacked vehicle and Satlat’s body were later found in Atteridgeville on the same day.

Dashcam footage of the attack circulated widely on social media and assisted investigators in identifying the suspects.

Satlat had borrowed the vehicle to earn extra income while completing an automotive mechanical engineering internship in Centurion. He was expected to finish the programme in early March and would have celebrated his birthday on 12 March.

In other news, KwaZulu Natal Transport and Human Settlements MEC Siboniso Duma has warned that no area in the province should be considered a no go zone for e hailing drivers, following the alleged assault and extortion of a Bolt driver in Phoenix.

Duma said the driver was allegedly attacked after dropping off a passenger and was later held at ransom at the offices of a local taxi association, where R5,000 was allegedly demanded for the return of his confiscated vehicle.

He condemned the incident and said officials have been assigned to work with police and stakeholders to stabilise relations between e hailing operators and the taxi industry. Plans include stronger law enforcement, engagement with industry role players, and an upcoming e hailing summit to promote coexistence in the public transport sector.
