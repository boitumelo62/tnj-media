Media Higher Education Department Moves to Deregister Three Private Colleges

December 15, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Higher Education Minister Buti Manamela has moved to cancel the registration of three private colleges citing ongoing non-compliance with regulatory requirements. The affected institutions are Damelin City Varsity and ICESA City Campus a decision that has sent shockwaves through the private education sector and among enrolled students.

The department says the move follows repeated interventions and engagements with the institutions over an extended period. Despite being given opportunities to address compliance concerns the colleges allegedly failed to meet the standards required under the Higher Education Act and related quality assurance frameworks.

According to the department the issues range from governance failures and administrative shortcomings to concerns about academic quality and student support systems. Officials stressed that deregistration is not taken lightly and is usually a last resort when institutions consistently fail to protect students’ interests.

The announcement has raised anxiety among thousands of students currently registered at the affected campuses. The department has assured learners that contingency plans are being put in place to minimise disruption. These include possible placement at alternative registered institutions and guidance on credit transfers where applicable.

Student organisations have called for clear communication urging the department to prioritise students who may be left in limbo particularly those nearing completion of their qualifications. They argue that learners should not be punished for failures by institutional management.

The private higher education sector has also reacted with concern warning that while regulation is necessary there must be transparency and consistency in enforcement. Industry bodies say they support efforts to uphold standards but want assurance that decisions are fair and evidence based.

Minister Manamela has defended the move saying the department has a constitutional obligation to safeguard the integrity of the higher education system. He added that institutions operating outside the law undermine public trust and compromise the future of students.

The department is expected to release further details outlining the compliance failures and the support measures available to affected students in the coming days.
