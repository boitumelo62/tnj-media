Media Police informant linked to Madlanga Commission dies

February 9, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Claims of Police Influence Surface at Madlanga Commission

Police informant Wiandre Pretorius has died following an apparent suicide in Brakpan, just days after surviving an attempted assassination. Pretorius had been linked to the Madlanga Commission, which is investigating allegations of corruption, criminal syndicates, and misconduct within the South African Police Service.

Pretorius was believed to be a key source of information for investigators, providing insights into networks allegedly involving senior officers, organised crime figures, and illicit activities within law enforcement structures. His role as an informant placed him at significant personal risk, particularly given the sensitive nature of the commission’s work and the potential legal consequences for those implicated.

The Madlanga Commission, chaired by Constitutional Court Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga, was established to probe deep seated corruption and failures within policing structures, including allegations of collusion with criminal syndicates. Informants such as Pretorius are considered crucial in uncovering internal wrongdoing, often operating under extreme pressure and threats to their safety.

Authorities have launched an investigation into Pretorius’ death to determine whether there was any foul play, especially given the recent attempt on his life. His death has sparked renewed debate about the protection of whistleblowers and informants in South Africa, with civil society organisations calling for stronger witness protection mechanisms and mental health support for individuals involved in high-risk investigations.

Legal experts warn that the loss of key informants could undermine accountability efforts and weaken public trust in law enforcement reform initiatives.
