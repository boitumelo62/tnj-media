Media Western Cape Maintains Easter Roadblocks to Contain Livestock Disease

March 24, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Western Cape authorities have confirmed that roadblocks will remain in place throughout the Easter period as part of ongoing efforts to contain the spread of foot and mouth disease.

The decision underscores the seriousness of the outbreak and the potential risks it poses to the agricultural sector. The disease is highly contagious and primarily affects livestock such as cattle, sheep, and pigs.

If not effectively controlled, it can result in severe economic consequences, including restrictions on exports and disruptions to supply chains. For a province where agriculture plays a key role in the economy, the stakes are particularly high.

Officials have implemented strict measures to limit the movement of animals between regions. Roadblocks have been established at key points, where vehicles are inspected to ensure that no infected livestock or contaminated materials are being transported.

Farmers have also been urged to strengthen biosecurity practices on their properties. While the extended roadblocks may inconvenience travellers during the busy holiday period, authorities maintain that these measures are necessary to prevent further spread. Public cooperation has been identified as a critical factor in managing the situation effectively.

Government representatives have emphasised that prevention remains the most effective strategy. By maintaining strict controls and encouraging compliance, they aim to protect both livelihoods and the broader economy from the potentially devastating impact of the disease.
