Business Universal Basic Income policy delays highlight challenges in social support reform

February 12, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Plans to finalise South Africa’s Universal Basic Income Support Policy have been delayed again, with the revised proposal now expected to reach Cabinet in March 2027. The delay underscores the complexity of designing a sustainable social support system in a country grappling with high unemployment, poverty, and fiscal constraints.

Universal basic income support has been widely debated as a potential tool to reduce poverty and provide a safety net for vulnerable households. Proponents argue that a guaranteed income could stimulate local economies, improve household stability, and reduce reliance on fragmented social grants. Critics, however, warn about the cost implications and potential strain on public finances, especially in an environment of slow economic growth and competing budget priorities.

Government officials have indicated that further consultations and technical assessments are needed before the policy can be finalised. Key issues include funding mechanisms, eligibility criteria, integration with existing social grants, and administrative capacity. Economists have also called for detailed modelling to assess long term fiscal sustainability and potential economic impacts.

Civil society organisations have expressed frustration with the repeated delays, arguing that millions of South Africans continue to face economic hardship and food insecurity. They have urged government to prioritise decisive action and transparent timelines for implementation.

As the policy process continues, the debate around universal basic income is likely to intensify, with stakeholders weighing social justice goals against economic realities. The eventual Cabinet submission will be a critical step in determining whether universal basic income becomes a central pillar of South Africa’s social protection framework.
