Business Debt and Deficits Force South Africa to Consider Raising VAT

November 5, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa is likely heading toward a value added tax (VAT) increase because the government has run out of other options to raise money. Economist Dawie Roodt from Efficient Wealth believes it’s only a matter of time before VAT goes up again. He explained that the government can’t increase income or company taxes any further without damaging the economy, and cutting spending would be political suicide for the ANC.

Roodt reminded people that VAT was first introduced in 1978 at just 4%. At the time, Finance Minister Owen Horwood promised it would never go higher but history has proven otherwise. VAT has gradually increased over the years, and Roodt said that once governments introduce a tax, it rarely goes away.

Right now, South Africa’s finances are in bad shape. Roodt said company tax is already too high at around 27%, and personal income tax can’t go up because only a small number of people pay most of it. Raising those taxes further would likely backfire, as it would push people to work less, avoid taxes, or even leave the country. This idea comes from the Laffer Curve, which shows that beyond a certain point, higher tax rates actually bring in less money.

That leaves VAT as the only real option. VAT brings in about 30% of South Africa’s total tax revenue, second only to personal income tax. It’s relatively easy for the government to collect, but it’s considered unfair because poorer people end up spending a bigger share of their income on it.

Earlier this year, the government came close to increasing VAT from the current 15%. Plans were floated for a rate as high as 17%, then adjusted to 16% and 15.5% before being scrapped. Roodt said that many political parties quietly supported the idea but later claimed credit for stopping it, including the ANC itself. This, he said, shows the political hypocrisy around taxes politicians promise more spending but never admit they’ll need to raise taxes to pay for it.

South Africa’s growing debt is another major problem. The country is spending more and more on interest payments, and because the economy isn’t growing fast enough, that debt keeps rising as a share of GDP. Roodt warned that this could lead to a financial crisis if nothing changes. The ideal solution would be to cut government spending, but he said that’s politically impossible, so a VAT increase is the only realistic path left.

He also noted that the South African Revenue Service (SARS) has become more aggressive in collecting taxes because of the government’s financial pressures. Although SARS is doing its job well, Roodt pointed out that every rand taken from the private sector is usually spent less efficiently by the state.

If spending keeps rising faster than revenue, Roodt warned that the government might eventually rely on inflation as a hidden tax. Inflation reduces the value of money and helps shrink the real value of debt, which is why politicians often tolerate it but it hurts everyone else.

In the end, Roodt said that unless the economy starts growing faster, South Africa will have no choice but to raise VAT. The country can’t keep borrowing forever, and since politicians won’t cut spending, there’s only one option left. A VAT increase may not happen this year, but it’s inevitable. It’s not a question of if – it’s a question of when.
