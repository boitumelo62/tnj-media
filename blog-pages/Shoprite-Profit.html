Business Shoprite Delivers Strong Profit Growth

September 26, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Shoprite, as South Africa’s largest food retailer, continues to show strong results. For the year ending June 2025, they brought in R252.7 billion in sales an 8.9% increase and R256.7 billion in total revenue. These gains were driven mainly by the core Supermarkets RSA segment, which includes familiar brands like Shoprite, Checkers, Usave, and LiquorShop. This core division grew its sales by 9.5%, contributing over R213 billion alone. Even the Supermarkets outside South Africa (Non RSA) added to the momentum, growing by 6.4% to over R20 billion.

One standout success was Sixty60, Shoprite’s on demand delivery platform, which saw sales grow nearly 48% to R18.9 billion. The service also maintained high performance standards with 94% on-time delivery and almost 97% order fulfilment both impressive for a logistics heavy operation. The business didn’t just grow in revenue. Trading profit jumped by 16.6% to R14.97 billion, giving it a healthy 5.9% trading margin. EBITDA rose nearly 19% to R23.8 billion, and earnings per share grew by almost 18%. The company also opened 363 new stores this year, expanding its footprint to 3,478 locations.

The non restated revenue tells a slightly different story. Shoprite had to adjust its 2024 revenue figures, dropping them from R246 billion to R236 billion. This was mainly because of discontinued operations furniture businesses in Angola and Mozambique are being shut down, and most of the group’s furniture operations are being sold off to Pepkor. The group is also exiting several African markets, including Malawi, Ghana, Uganda, Nigeria, and Madagascar. Altogether, these discontinued operations removed R9.6 billion from Shoprite’s books.

It is important to understand why these restatements happen. Under international accounting rules (specifically IFRS 5), companies are required to restate past results to exclude operations that are no longer ongoing. It’s done to give a clearer picture of how the current core business is performing, without being skewed by businesses that are no longer relevant. Shoprite says that if they didn’t do this, year on year comparisons would include revenue from operations they’ve already exited, which would distort the growth trend.

And there’s a silver lining here by shedding underperforming or unprofitable segments, Shoprite actually improved its bottom line. Its net profit margin rose from 2.5% in 2024 to 3% in 2025 a meaningful improvement in profitability, even if overall revenue growth slowed. It shows the group is focusing on quality over quantity, streamlining its operations, and doubling down on what works. While the raw revenue numbers may seem less flashy than previous years, the overall health of the business looks better than ever.
