Media X Limits Grok AI Image Features After UK Investigation

January 15, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Social media platform X has announced new restrictions on its AI chatbot Grok, blocking users from editing images of real people in revealing clothing in countries where such content is illegal.

The decision follows growing concern from the UK government and media regulator Ofcom after reports that Grok was being used to generate sexual images of women and children. Ofcom confirmed earlier this week that it had launched an investigation into the platform.

In a statement on Wednesday, X said it had restricted the editing of images of real people in clothing such as bikinis, adding that the move would help prevent abuse and ensure compliance with the law. The company said it has now geo-blocked the generation of images showing real people in bikinis, underwear or similar attire in affected jurisdictions.

The restrictions apply to all users including paid subscribers, though X said image creation and editing tools will now be limited to premium accounts.

Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer welcomed the action but said the government would not back down, confirming that Ofcom’s independent investigation would continue. The government has also announced new legislation to criminalise the creation of non-consensual intimate images.

Elon Musk said Grok is designed to refuse illegal content and claimed any misuse was the result of attempts to exploit the system. He added that content rules would vary by country in line with local laws.

Technology Secretary Liz Kendall said Ofcom would have full government backing to act against X if further changes were not made, including potential severe measures, as the UK tightens laws around AI generated sexual content.
