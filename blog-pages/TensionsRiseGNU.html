Media Tensions Rise in GNU as Ramaphosa Defends ANC Policies

December 12, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Cyril Ramaphosa used his closing speech at the ANC’s National General Council to make it clear that while the party remains committed to the Government of National Unity, it expects this unity to support its long standing transformation agenda. He stressed that joining the GNU was never the ANC’s first choice but rather a temporary and strategic move, and that the party has no intention of watering down its policies because of it.

Ramaphosa repeated that broad based black economic empowerment and the National Health Insurance scheme remain central to the ANC’s vision, even though some partners in the GNU strongly oppose them. He said the ANC would not back away from BEE and that the GNU should actively help strengthen black owned and previously excluded businesses, especially in areas like manufacturing, steel and local content development.

On the NHI, he said universal healthcare is a goal rooted in the Freedom Charter, and the NGC agreed that implementation must go ahead. He argued that the health system must be strengthened so that every South African can access affordable, quality care.

Ramaphosa also hinted at growing tension inside the GNU, saying some parties were using the unity government to undermine the ANC’s transformation agenda. Although he didn’t mention names, his comments pointed directly at the DA and other smaller parties that have openly challenged the ANC in court on issues like the NHI, employment equity and even the recent VAT hike. These challenges have shown that the ANC no longer holds the political dominance it once did, and its policies can be contested and even overturned.

Still, despite the friction and deep policy differences, the GNU has not collapsed. The NGC has reaffirmed its support for the broad coalition, suggesting that at least for now the arrangement will hold. As the country heads into a politically charged period leading up to the 2026 municipal elections, this ongoing cooperation may help maintain a degree of stability, even if the debates within the GNU continue to intensify.
