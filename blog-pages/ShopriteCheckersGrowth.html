Business Checkers Drives Growth as Shoprite Adds 225 New Stores

September 1, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Shoprite has continued to grow strongly, both in terms of financial performance and physical store expansion. Over the financial year ending 30 June 2025, the group exceeded R250 billion in sales for the first time, with a notable R20.6 billion increase from the previous year. Much of this came from its core Supermarkets RSA segment, where Shoprite and Usave saw a 5.9% increase in turnover, which translates to R6.5 billion in additional sales.

Checkers stood out with particularly strong momentum, growing sales by 13.8%, which added R11.6 billion. This reflects the success of its premium but value driven positioning, which continues to gain ground in the South African market. The retailer opened 68 new Checkers stores, including 36 LiquorShop outlets, pushing the total number of Checkers supermarkets to 350. CEO Pieter Engelbrecht credits the success to Checkers’ approach of making premium food more accessible and says they plan to keep expanding, especially by upgrading stores to the FreshX format and entering areas where they’re still underrepresented.

In total, the group added 225 new stores under its Supermarkets RSA division, increasing the count to 2,577. Even when factoring in the sale of its furniture division and exit from Ghana and Malawi—businesses that included 430 stores—the overall store count still grew by 269, reaching 3,908. Looking ahead, 223 more stores are planned to open in the next financial year. Sixty60, Shoprite’s on demand grocery delivery platform, also had a standout year, with sales jumping nearly 48% to R18.9 billion. Improvements in customer experience were a key part of that, with a high rate of on-time deliveries and accurate order fulfilment.

Shoprite also launched a new Checkers website that offers its full product range for delivery within an hour, and re platformed Sixty60 to expand delivery options to include general merchandise from Checkers Hypers. Financially, Shoprite remains in excellent shape. Earnings per share rose by 22% to 1,401.2 cents, and total profit climbed nearly 22% to R7.6 billion. Revenue was up 8.6%, reaching R257 billion, while the total dividend for the year increased by 7.9% to 781 cents. Although the Competition Commission approved the sale, a competitor was later granted intervention rights, slowing down the process.

Engelbrecht expressed concern that such delays are unproductive and create uncertainty for both the business and its employees. Despite being profitable, the furniture segment remains classified as a discontinued operation and doesn’t contribute to the group’s ongoing financial metrics until the deal is finalized. Shoprite appears to be firing on all cylinders, with strong earnings, aggressive expansion, and a growing presence in digital and delivery channels, even as it navigates regulatory hurdles in its divestment plans.
