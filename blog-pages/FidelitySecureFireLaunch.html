Business Fidelity Expands Emergency Services with Launch of SecureFire

October 9, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Former Springboks John Smit and Wahl Bartmann recently joined forces to showcase an exciting new addition to South Africa’s private emergency services Fidelity SecureFire, a firefighting unit developed by the Fidelity Services Group. Bartmann, now the CEO of Fidelity, has been leading the country’s largest security company, while Smit, best known for captaining the Springboks to their 2007 Rugby World Cup victory, serves as CEO of SSG Holdings. Fidelity announced in September that it had acquired SSG, which will continue operating under its own name and leadership, now as a subsidiary of Fidelity. SSG, founded just over a decade ago, provides services like security, hygiene, cleaning, and facilities management across several sectors, including mining, retail, and government.

With nearly 9,000 staff across 14 locations, the acquisition significantly expanded Fidelity’s reach, pushing its workforce to around 70,000 and its operational footprint to over 255 sites nationwide. Bartmann described the merger as more than a business move it's about preparing the country for emergencies in a more efficient and unified way. The combination of Fidelity’s and SSG’s resources, he said, creates South Africa’s most advanced private protection capability. The two rugby legends recently took to the skies literally to spotlight SecureFire’s capabilities. They were lifted 32 meters above the N1 highway on a hydraulic platform ladder attached to one of SecureFire’s specialized fire trucks.

This particular ladder can reach the equivalent of a 10 story building and features a rescue basket that can carry up to 400 kilograms, boosting SecureFire’s ability to conduct rescues in tall buildings and challenging urban environments. This isn’t just about one ladder, though. SecureFire has built a serious operation since launching in 2023. Its national fleet includes 32 fire engines and water tankers, 34 Rapid Intervention Units, and around 600 trained first responders spread across 19 regional offices. They’re active in Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal, and the Western Cape, and all deployments are coordinated through a National Command Centre that provides real time data and oversight. The service doesn’t just respond to fires it also handles fire prevention, compliance checks, maintenance, installations, and digital reporting.

The high altitude stunt was meant to honour the bravery of emergency responders who face danger every day, especially those working at extreme heights. Speaking from the platform, Smit reflected on how his rugby career shaped his perspective. “There are heroes who climb to these heights every day to save lives,” he said. “We’re up here to show our absolute commitment to giving them the best equipment and support possible. As a Springbok, I learned what it means to stand tall under pressure. That’s exactly the mindset needed in emergency services.” The message was clear: whether on the field or in an emergency, preparation, courage, and teamwork save lives.
