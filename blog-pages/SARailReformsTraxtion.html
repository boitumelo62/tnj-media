Business SA Rail Reforms Spur Traxtion’s Massive R3.4bn Investment

December 8, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Traxtion is making a huge move in South Africa’s rail sector, committing R3.4 billion to new rolling stock an investment that stands as the largest ever by a private rail company in the country. The plan includes buying 46 diesel electric locomotives from KiwiRail in New Zealand, which is currently replacing its own fleet.

According to CEO James Holley, this investment shows the company’s confidence in the rail reforms underway in South Africa. He believes these changes have been overdue and that opening the network to private operators is creating real opportunities to improve the country’s freight system. Traxtion has been actively involved in the public consultations leading up to the reforms, and Holley feels the new policies and legislation give the company the certainty it needs to invest.

The locomotives coming from KiwiRail offered a rare window to secure a large, top quality fleet that matches southern Africa’s narrow gauge requirements. Holley said the timing aligned perfectly with Traxtion’s growing confidence in the direction South Africa is taking. The locomotives are known for their high reliability and capacity, and Traxtion will be working with Wabtec the global leader in diesel electric locomotive manufacturing to upgrade them even further. The goal is to put powerful, dependable locomotives into service quickly and at a competitive price.

Holley stressed that this investment marks the beginning of true private rail freight services in the country. Traxtion already operates similar locomotives elsewhere in Africa, and the technology closely matches what Transnet currently uses, which gives the company confidence that the rollout will go smoothly.The project will also deliver major local economic benefits. Almost 80% of the contract value will flow to South African companies, and about 60% of that will count as direct local content. Holley pointed out that Transnet moves around 160 to 165 million tons of freight annually, far below the national demand of 250 million tons.

By adding more capacity, Traxtion hopes to help relieve one of South Africa’s biggest economic bottlenecks.Job creation will be another major outcome. The project will directly create 662 jobs, and with broader economic ripple effects, the total impact is expected to be several times larger. Holley also highlighted that regional rail reforms now make it possible for private trains to operate across borders, which opens new long term opportunities for trade within southern Africa.

For now, the 46 locomotives and 920 wagons are intended mainly for South African customers, but cross border expansion is definitely on the horizon. The rollout is expected to happen quickly by industry standards, with the first locomotives entering service within the next 12 months.
