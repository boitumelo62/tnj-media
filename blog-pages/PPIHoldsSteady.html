Business Producer Price Inflation Holds Steady as Cost Pressures Remain Contained

December 22, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s producer price inflation remained unchanged at 2.9 percent in November, according to the latest figures released by Statistics South Africa. The steady reading suggests that price pressures at the factory and production level are currently under control, offering some relief to businesses and consumers heading into the end of the year.

Producer price inflation, often seen as an early indicator of consumer inflation, measures the average change in prices received by local producers for their output. Economists closely monitor this data as it can signal future movements in retail prices. The unchanged figure follows several months of gradual moderation, reflecting easing input costs in certain sectors such as fuel and some manufactured goods.

Stats SA data shows that intermediate manufactured goods continued to experience subdued price increases, while energy related costs remained relatively stable compared to the volatility seen earlier in the year. Agricultural input prices also showed mixed trends, with some categories rising modestly while others declined due to improved supply conditions.

Economists say the stable producer inflation figure supports the view that inflationary pressures are not accelerating at present. This may provide the South African Reserve Bank with additional room to maintain its current monetary policy stance, depending on developments in consumer inflation, global oil prices and exchange rate movements.

However, analysts caution that risks remain. Global geopolitical tensions, potential supply chain disruptions and currency fluctuations could still place upward pressure on production costs in the months ahead. Local challenges such as electricity supply constraints and logistics inefficiencies also continue to affect operating costs for producers.

For consumers, the stable producer price reading is seen as a positive signal, as it reduces the likelihood of sharp price increases being passed on at retail level in the short term. As the festive season continues, households and businesses alike will be watching closely to see whether this stability can be sustained into the new year.
