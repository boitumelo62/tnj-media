Media Bolt Denies Compensation to Family of Murdered Driver Over Policy Breach

February 19, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Ride-hailing company Bolt has confirmed that the family of 22-year-old driver Isaac Satlat will not receive compensation following his murder in Pretoria West, citing a violation of company policy related to profile sharing.

Satlat was killed last week during an e-hailing trip after being attacked by passengers. Dashcam footage from the vehicle circulated widely on social media and helped police identify suspects. Six individuals have since been arrested and are facing charges including premeditated murder and robbery with aggravating circumstances.

In a statement, Bolt said its protections and benefits apply only to drivers operating under their own verified accounts. The company stated that Satlat was using another driver’s profile at the time of the incident, which placed him outside the scope of its compensation policies.

“Where impersonation or profile sharing occurs, the individual is operating unlawfully and outside the scope of the platform’s protections. In such cases, compensation policies do not apply,” Bolt said.

The company confirmed that the account belonged to registered driver Wiseman Makobe and that Satlat operated under that profile after the required verification process had been completed. Bolt said the profile has since been permanently blocked and banned from the platform.

Bolt stressed that profile sharing undermines passenger trust, compromises safety and violates agreements with drivers. The company said it uses identity verification, in-app selfie checks, device monitoring and data-led risk detection systems to prevent such practices, with accounts suspended or banned where violations are found.

The case has also drawn attention to ongoing efforts to regulate the e-hailing sector in South Africa. The National Land Transport Amendment Act requires platforms to register with authorities and mandates measures such as proper vehicle branding, panic buttons and licensing for drivers and operators.

Non-compliance can result in fines of up to R100,000, imprisonment of up to two years and vehicle impoundment. Bolt said it supports formal regulation and is committed to aligning with the new framework to strengthen safety and accountability across the industry.

The company added that it is cooperating with police and remains focused on improving platform integrity while working with government and industry stakeholders to support drivers and protect livelihoods.
