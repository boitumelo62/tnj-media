Business Boxer Faces Earnings Pressure from IPO Share Increase

September 29, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Boxer, one of the standout performers in the Pick n Pay Group, is preparing investors for a sharp drop in earnings per share not because business is slowing down, but because of the way it was listed on the JSE last year.

While Pick n Pay itself has been going through a rough patch, racking up major losses from its main grocery operations, Boxer has continued to shine. It’s been growing fast, opening more stores, and delivering strong sales and profit numbers. That momentum helped fuel interest when Boxer was spun off and listed separately in 2024, a move aimed at helping Pick n Pay raise R8 billion to steady its finances.

Despite the listing, Pick n Pay still holds about 65% of Boxer. Now, however, the company is feeling the effects of that IPO. In a recent update, Boxer warned that for the six months ending 31 August 2025, both its earnings per share (EPS) and headline earnings per share (HEPS) will drop by more than 20% compared to the same time last year. This isn’t because the business is underperforming in fact, operations remain strong but rather due to the increased number of shares in circulation following the listing.

Back in November 2024, when Boxer went public, the number of shares increased significantly by over 51%. As a result, even if profits stay the same or go up, those earnings are now being spread across more shares, which brings the per-share numbers down. In its trading statement, Boxer said it expects EPS to fall between 30% and 36%, landing somewhere between 105.21 and 115.18 cents. HEPS is expected to be down by 28% to 34%, coming in at between 108.57 and 118.85 cents per share.

Still, the overall picture remains positive. Despite the drop in per-share earnings, headline earnings for the group as a whole are expected to rise as much as 9%, thanks to strong trading performance that helped balance out the added costs of being a listed company. Sales numbers also back up the growth story. Turnover jumped 13.9%, and like-for-like sales which strip out the impact of new stores grew by 5.3%. More details are expected when Boxer releases its interim results on Monday, 13 October 2025.
