Asia’s tech, science, and political landscapes are in the spotlight this week following a groundbreaking space discovery, a high-profile cybersecurity breach, and rapid advances in AI services.

Astronomers in Japan have discovered a distant object beyond Pluto, named 2023 KQ₁₄, that could challenge existing theories about the solar system. Detected through the Subaru Telescope in Hawaii and nicknamed Ammonite, the object has an unusual orbit that does not match the alignment of other known sednoids. Researchers, led by Dr. Yukun Huang, believe this finding weakens the case for a hidden “Planet 9,” instead suggesting that ancient events, such as the ejection of a planet from the solar system, may better explain these orbital anomalies. Currently, 2023 KQ₁₄ is near its closest approach to the Sun at around 71 astronomical units.

In Australia, Clive Palmer’s political movement, recently renamed from the United Australia Party to “Trumpet of Patriots,” has confirmed a ransomware attack that exposed sensitive personal information. The breach reportedly includes phone numbers, email addresses, identification documents, and financial data. The party stated that it is “impracticable” to notify all individuals affected by the attack, urging its supporters to remain vigilant. With over 900,000 votes in its last election, this breach is being regarded as one of the most significant political cybersecurity incidents in the country this year.

Meanwhile, Bharti Airtel in India has announced a partnership with Perplexity AI, offering subscribers access to its AI-powered Pro plan as part of Airtel’s premium monthly package. The plan includes 500 deep-research queries per day, marking a significant step in expanding AI services to mainstream telecom customers.

Japan’s NEC is also making waves with the announcement of a new submarine internet cable, “AUG East,” designed to connect Japan and Singapore while branching out to Malaysia, the Philippines, Brunei, South Korea, Indonesia, and Taiwan. The high-capacity cable will support massive data flows, including millions of ultra-HD video streams, with tech giants like AWS and Microsoft among the first customers.

Other developments across the region include an investigation into Google Indonesia over a potential $600 million Chromebook contract scandal, Atlassian’s migration of over 4 million PostgreSQL databases to reduce AWS hosting costs, Canada’s order for Hikvision to leave its market, and reports of a Chinese AI-powered infomercial generating around $7.5 million in sales.
