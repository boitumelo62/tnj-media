Media Remembering the Voice of Tshidi Madia

August 28, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Some people really are just born with it the instinct, the drive, the mind for it. They don’t have to force it, it’s in them. The way they ask questions, the way they listen, the way they turn complicated things into something we all can understand. They’re natural communicators, and more than that, they’re natural broadcasters. Radio isn’t just a job to them it’s something they live and breathe.

That’s exactly who Matshidiso Madia Tshidi was. Listening to her on 702 made it undeniably clear, Tshidi was the voice many South Africans leaned on. A calm, intelligent, clear voice in a time where there’s often too much noise. Her grasp of South African politics wasn’t just surface level, it was deep, insightful, and sharp. She never tried to sway anyone just laid things out, asked the right questions, and let the truth sit there, unshaken. In a world where misinformation spreads faster than facts, Tshidi was someone people trusted. Not because she shouted the loudest, but because she showed up the same every time clear, honest, no spin.

That kind of consistency is rare, and people noticed. People felt it. She had this way of making you feel like she was speaking directly to you even if you’d never met her. I hadn’t. We weren’t friends in the usual sense. No messages, no meetings, not even a passing hello. But somehow, she was my friend. My friend from the radio. Driving home on Wednesday, inching down the M1 in traffic, the radio played tribute after tribute. Colleagues, listeners, friends all calling in, all remembering. Some thanked her for what she taught them. Others remembered how proudly South African she was.

Some were just sad, heartbroken that her voice is now part of memory. The grief was real, raw, and so deeply felt. And there were the stories her journey from junior traffic reporter to Power FM, and back to Primedia, eventually rising to become the associate editor for politics at Eyewitness News. She was serious, but she could laugh. She was sharp, but always human. Even the title of her podcast, Politricking, captured that perfect balance playful, but pointed. It’s a word we now use in my house when we talk about the political circus. Tshidi understood that circus better than most but she never lost her grounding. She brought both wisdom and humour, truth and tact. She wasn’t just a politics expert. She could slide into a hosting slot and make it her own, something only the most gifted can do.

I often found her better than the regulars. She had a way of drawing you in, of making you stay. That’s the sign of real talent. Tshidi Madia was born with it. Her passing is more than a loss to radio it’s a loss to everyone who had the privilege of being her listener, her colleague, her friend. The kind of friend who never knew your name, but somehow still made your day better, just by being on the air. Rest in power, Tshidi. Thank you for your voice, your mind, and your heart. We were listening.
