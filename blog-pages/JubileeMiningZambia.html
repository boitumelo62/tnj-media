Business Jubilee Sells SA Assets to Focus on Copper Growth in Zambia

October 7, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Jubilee, a mining company based in London, is moving ahead with selling off its South African operations as part of a broader shift seen among international companies reducing their footprint in the country. Back in August, the company announced it had accepted a firm offer from One Chrome a private mining and metals trading business to buy its chrome and platinum group metals (PGM) operations in South Africa.

The deal is worth $90 million (about R1.5 billion), and the company says these operations had matured to a point where further growth would require heavy capital investment something it wasn’t keen to pursue. By offloading these assets, Jubilee plans to focus more on its copper business in Zambia, which it sees as a key growth area. The company believes there’s strong potential to scale up its existing copper sites in Zambia, especially given the global push toward electrification, which continues to drive demand for copper.

Copper also offers higher profit margins than chrome, which strengthens Jubilee’s shift in focus. Even though Jubilee is pulling out of some operations in South Africa, it’s keeping its rights to the Tjate Platinum mining project. That means it still holds a stake in South Africa’s platinum sector and could benefit if that market picks up again in the future. Shareholders approved the sale in late August, and Jubilee has already received the first $15 million (R255 million) as part of the transaction. The company is working through final steps, including regulatory requirements with the Competition Commission and audit related conditions, which are progressing well.

ubilee is one of several big international names either leaving or scaling back in South Africa. Others include Anglo American, HSBC, BNP Paribas, IG Group, Nielsen, Bain & Company, and Shell. In the mining sector specifically, Anglo American exited its platinum business during a global shake up to fend off a takeover bid from BHP. That move saw the creation of Valterra Platinum, which is now listed in London and led to a major capital outflow from South Africa about R73.5 billion in the second quarter of 2025, according to the South African Reserve Bank. On the upside, Anglo American decided to hold onto its Kumba Iron Ore business, which BHP had wanted to drop, and the company is set to merge with Canada’s Teck Resources.

Despite the broader trend of exits, South Africa is still attracting new investment in areas like tourism and travel. Club Med is opening a resort in KwaZulu-Natal next year, luxury brand OKU Hotels recently bought the Ritz Hotel in Cape Town, and Tata Motors is returning to the market with new passenger vehicles. In the mining sector, West Wits Mining is launching the country’s first new underground gold mine in 15 years, signaling there’s still interest and opportunity in South Africa’s economy especially in specific sectors.
