Media Mchunu defends move to disband killings task team

December 5, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Suspended Police Minister Senzo Mchunu used his latest public appearance to push back against criticism of his decision to dissolve the Political Killings Task Team. He urged South Africans to look past emotion and focus on the facts surrounding the decision, saying the unit had become a point of political tension rather than an effective crime fighting structure.

Mchunu argued that the task team had strayed from its original purpose and was no longer delivering results in the way the public believed. He said its existence had begun to interfere with broader policing priorities and that the time had come for a reset. According to him, the country needed a more coordinated, long term approach to political violence, one that places stability and proper investigative capacity at the centre.

He told the public that disbanding the unit does not amount to abandoning the fight against political killings. Instead, he described the move as the first step in building a stronger, more streamlined strategy. Mchunu also criticised what he called emotional narratives pushed by his opponents, insisting that leadership sometimes requires difficult decisions that will not please everyone.

The minister’s comments come as political tensions continue to simmer in provinces where intra party violence remains a major concern. His stance has drawn mixed reactions from analysts and community leaders, some calling the move necessary while others warn it may leave dangerous gaps. For now, Mchunu maintains that the restructuring is essential for restoring credibility to the policing response.
