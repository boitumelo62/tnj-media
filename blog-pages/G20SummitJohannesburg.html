Media G20 Summit closes in Johannesburg with strong South African imprint

November 24, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The G20 Leaders’ Summit came to a close in Johannesburg after three days of high-level discussions on global stability, economic cooperation and the shifting balance of international power. President Cyril Ramaphosa described the gathering as a meaningful victory for multilateralism and said South Africa had fulfilled its responsibility as host by steering talks toward practical solutions rather than political posturing.

The joint statement issued at the end of the summit carried a noticeable South African influence. It included several initiatives proposed by Pretoria, from climate transition support for developing economies to expanded investment channels for African industrialisation. The final text also delivered a soft yet unmistakable rebuke of China, signalling concern about rising global tensions while emphasising the need for responsible engagement among major powers.

A significant portion of the statement focused on Africa. South Africa pushed for commitments that would strengthen continental infrastructure, improve access to global markets and protect vulnerable economies from disruptive geopolitical competition. Leaders who attended the summit noted that Johannesburg offered a tone of balance and maturity, aiming to steady relations at a time of deepening fractures in world politics.

For Pretoria, the successful conclusion of the summit serves as a reaffirmation of its growing diplomatic weight and its ambition to position Africa as an indispensable voice in global affairs.
