Media SARS Outlines 14-Year Tax Noncompliance Case Against Former PRASA CEO Lucky Montana

October 13, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The South African Revenue Service has presented a 14-year timeline outlining what it describes as a pattern of persistent tax noncompliance by former Passenger Rail Agency of South Africa Chief Executive Officer Lucky Montana.

According to the report, the timeline tracks alleged irregularities stretching back to Montana’s tenure at the helm of PRASA, where he oversaw several large-scale infrastructure and locomotive procurement projects. SARS claims the findings reveal discrepancies and undeclared income that amount to millions in unpaid taxes.

Officials within the tax authority said the extensive investigation was part of ongoing efforts to clamp down on long-standing tax avoidance among senior executives in public institutions. The 14-year scope, they explained, was meant to provide a full picture of Montana’s compliance record and to strengthen the state’s case should formal legal proceedings follow.

Lucky Montana has previously denied any wrongdoing in matters involving his personal finances or his time at PRASA, maintaining that all his income declarations were transparent and in line with the law.

The matter has reignited public discussion around accountability and financial transparency in state-owned enterprises, with analysts saying it underscores the need for stronger internal auditing systems within government entities.

SARS is expected to make a formal submission on the case before the end of October, after which potential recovery actions could be initiated.
