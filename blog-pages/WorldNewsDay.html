Media World News Day Unites Media in Defence of the Public’s Right to Know

October 16, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

At a time when journalism is under attack in so many parts of the world from the spread of authoritarian governments to constant threats against reporters there was a powerful moment of unity. For one week, ending on Sunday, September 28, newsrooms and media advocates around the globe came together to show their commitment to protecting truth, trust, and the public’s right to know.

The threats facing journalism today are real and growing. Even in the United States, long seen as a stronghold for democracy and press freedom, we’ve seen alarming signs of how fast those freedoms can erode. And this isn't just a U.S. issue. Around the world, disinformation, censorship, and political pressure are rising right when people need accurate, fact-based reporting the most. That’s where the World News Day campaign stepped in. It became a global platform for news organizations to highlight why journalism matters. Throughout the week, 11 powerful opinion pieces were published by veteran reporters, respected commentators, and members of a high level panel of economists focused on media in the public interest.

These pieces weren’t just commentary they were calls to action, offering real solutions and confronting the deep challenges the media faces today. This year’s campaign also aligned itself with the International Day for Universal Access to Information, which happens to fall on the same day an intentional move to reinforce the connection between press freedom and the public’s right to know. World News Day made its mark in nearly every corner of the globe. Messages in support of journalism reached people from Tonga to Afghanistan, Canada to Venezuela, Korea to Estonia.

These weren’t just symbolic gestures they showed up across newspapers, websites, videos, podcasts, and more. In South Africa, editors came together to urge President Cyril Ramaphosa who’s often avoided media engagement to finally speak to the press. The country’s media was especially active in supporting the campaign, from small community outlets to national publications. They used everything from editorials and press releases to videos and podcasts to drive home the importance of trustworthy journalism.

Meanwhile, in the Philippines, a group of media organizations teamed up to do something different. They created a satirical AI-generated video that imagined a world without real journalism a chilling warning wrapped in humor, and a powerful reminder of what’s at stake. In a time of growing fear and confusion, the message was clear journalism still matters. And the world isn’t ready to give it up.
