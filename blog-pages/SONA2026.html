Business SONA 2026 sets ambitious reform agenda amid economic and social pressures

February 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

President Cyril Ramaphosa’s 2026 State of the Nation Address outlined an ambitious reform agenda aimed at stabilising the economy, accelerating infrastructure investment, and addressing persistent social and governance challenges. The address framed 2026 as a pivotal year for transformation, drawing historical parallels with the 1956 Women’s March, the 1976 Soweto uprising, and the 1996 Constitution, positioning the year as a turning point in South Africa’s democratic journey.

At the core of the address was a reaffirmation of constitutional values, including commitments to dignity, equality, non-racism, and non-sexism, alongside protections for persons with disabilities and LGBTQI+ communities. The president also acknowledged rising geopolitical tensions and shifting global trade dynamics, noting that South Africa must navigate an increasingly volatile international environment.

Ramaphosa pointed to signs of economic recovery, citing four consecutive quarters of GDP growth, improved credit ratings, lower inflation and interest rates, and a stronger rand. Structural reforms under Operation Vulindlela were highlighted as key drivers of improved competitiveness, particularly in energy, logistics, and network industries.

Energy reform featured prominently, with government declaring that load shedding has ended and committing to a competitive electricity market with more than 40 percent renewable energy supply by 2030. Infrastructure investment was also prioritised, with more than R1 trillion committed over three years, supported by public private partnerships and strong demand for an infrastructure bond. Modernisation of ports and rail was emphasised, including private rail access and major partnerships in port terminals such as Durban Pier 2, alongside plans to prepare proposals for long-distance high- speed rail corridors linking major cities.

The address outlined an industrial and trade strategy focused on supporting vulnerable sectors such as steel and automotive manufacturing while expanding exports and strengthening trade diplomacy. Agricultural expansion plans include increased exports, blended finance for black producers, and the deployment of 10,000 new extension officers. Government also outlined its response to foot and mouth disease, including mass vaccination, disaster classification, and a dedicated task team.

Tourism growth was highlighted, with 10.5 million international arrivals recorded and plans to extend electronic travel authorisation to enable 24-hour digital visa processing. A green industrial strategy was announced, featuring a 150 percent tax deduction for investment in new energy vehicles, support for battery manufacturing, and green product exports.

Mining and critical minerals were identified as strategic priorities, with increased geological exploration funding and new rare earths project funding. Government also set a target of raising R2 trillion in new investment over five years, with the sixth Investment Conference scheduled for March 2026.

Support for small businesses includes more than R2.5 billion in funding for SMEs, guarantees, refinement of the Business Licensing Bill, and amendments to the National Credit Act to improve access to affordable credit. Job creation programmes will be expanded, with reforms to youth employment services and targeted support for youth and women employment pathways.

On crime, the president announced intelligence led crackdowns on organised syndicates, military support for police in hotspot provinces, tighter firearm laws, AI driven disruption of the illicit economy, and the recruitment of 5,500 additional police officers. Water security was also elevated to a national priority, with the establishment of a National Water Crisis Committee, R156 billion allocated over three years, and potential constitutional interventions and criminal accountability for failing municipal managers.

While the address set out a broad reform blueprint, analysts say the success of the agenda will depend on implementation capacity, political stability, and sustained investment. With multiple commitments spanning infrastructure, energy, industry, and social development, SONA 2026 positions the coming years as a decisive test of South Africa’s reform trajectory.
