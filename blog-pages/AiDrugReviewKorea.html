Tech News South Korea to Introduce AI in Drug Review Process by 2026

9 July 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Korea’s Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) has announced plans to implement artificial intelligence (AI) tools in its pharmaceutical evaluation process beginning in 2026. The initiative is part of a broader national push to modernize healthcare regulation through digital transformation.

According to the MFDS, AI will assist in analyzing vast amounts of clinical, chemical, and pharmaceutical data submitted during drug approval applications. Officials say this will improve review accuracy, reduce evaluation times, and ensure greater consistency across assessments.

The regulatory agency began laying the groundwork for AI integration in recent years. In 2023, it issued guidance for AI-based medical devices, becoming one of the first regulatory bodies in Asia to formally support the use of AI in healthcare product evaluation.

The shift toward AI in drug review mirrors a global trend. Several countries are exploring ways to accelerate drug development and approval through advanced digital technologies. Within South Korea, private companies have already begun adopting AI platforms to identify new drug candidates, signaling strong industry support for the government’s direction.

Expected benefits of the transition include faster detection of safety risks, reduced manual workload for reviewers, and enhanced transparency in regulatory decisions. However, MFDS emphasized that human oversight will remain essential. The agency noted that ensuring the reliability, ethical use, and data privacy of AI tools will be critical to maintaining public trust.

Pilot reviews using AI systems are expected to begin in early 2026, with full-scale implementation planned for later in the year. The move is part of South Korea’s larger strategy to foster innovation in pharmaceutical research and establish a leadership position in global drug regulation.
