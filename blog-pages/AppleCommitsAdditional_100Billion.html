Business Apple Commits Additional $100 Billion to U.S. Manufacturing Amid Pressure from Trump

Apple is injecting another $100 billion into its U.S. operations, expanding on its previously announced $500 billion domestic investment plan set for the next four years. The decision comes as the company faces mounting political pressure from former President Donald Trump to ramp up its American manufacturing footprint.

The new commitment includes a major American Manufacturing Program, which aims to shift more of Apple’s supply chain and advanced manufacturing efforts to the United States.

As part of this expansion, Apple has deepened its partnership with Corning to produce all iPhone and Apple Watch cover glass at Corning's facility in Kentucky. Additionally, Apple will collaborate with Samsung at its Austin, Texas chip plant to develop a cutting-edge chip-making technology that has yet to be used anywhere else in the world.

Apple’s Houston-based server plant, announced earlier this year, is expected to begin full-scale production in 2026. The company is also enlarging its data center operations in Maiden, North Carolina. Meanwhile, a new manufacturing academy in Michigan will help train workers and businesses in advanced manufacturing practices.

Despite Apple’s efforts, it remains unclear if this expanded initiative will satisfy Trump, who has repeatedly urged the tech giant to bring iPhone production back to American soil. Apple has recently diversified its global supply chain by moving production to India and Vietnam in response to tariffs and disruptions in China.

During Trump’s first term, Apple CEO Tim Cook took several steps to align with the administration’s policies, including lobbying for tariff relief and announcing that Apple’s 2019 Mac Pro would be assembled in Texas. Cook reaffirmed Apple’s long-term strategy in a recent briefing, stating the company is working toward a complete U.S.-based silicon supply chain, from design through fabrication and packaging.

Apple recently reported that tariffs could cost the company an additional $1.1 billion in the upcoming quarter, on top of the $800 million already spent. Trump has also introduced new import taxes affecting countries like Vietnam and India, where a significant portion of Apple’s products are now made.
