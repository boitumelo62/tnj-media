Media Mbalula Rejects Claims That Ramaphosa Was Asked to Step Down

November 20, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

ANC Secretary General Fikile Mbalula has dismissed as false the reports suggesting that President Cyril Ramaphosa has been asked to step down in the aftermath of the G20 Summit. The speculation gained momentum overnight after claims circulated on social platforms and in political commentary circles that senior party figures had allegedly held discussions about leadership changes.

Speaking at an ANC briefing this morning, Mbalula labelled the reports as unfounded and misleading. He said no structure of the ANC has tabled or entertained such a discussion and that President Ramaphosa remains firmly in office, supported by the party’s highest decision making bodies. Mbalula urged South Africans not to be swayed by what he described as deliberate attempts to generate political instability ahead of the summit.

The rumours appear to have been fuelled by broader internal tensions within the governing party. Recent corruption cases, economic pressures, and criticism over the government’s handling of public service failures have intensified scrutiny on senior leadership. Analysts say that such moments often create an environment where misinformation spreads rapidly, even when there is no formal process underway.

Mbalula also used the briefing to criticise those spreading the allegations, saying that such narratives risk weakening public confidence in democratic institutions. He argued that the ANC is focused on governance stability, especially during a week in which South Africa is hosting world leaders for the G20 Summit.

While the Secretary General firmly rejected the claims, he did acknowledge that the ANC is dealing with ongoing internal debates about the direction of the party and its leadership. He insisted however that these conversations occur within official structures and are not reflective of the speculation currently circulating in the public domain.

Political analysts note that this incident highlights the fragility of political messaging at a time when South Africa is under international scrutiny. They say that the leadership rumours, even if untrue, point to deeper concerns about factional battles within the party and the perceived vulnerability of key leaders.

For now, the ANC maintains that President Ramaphosa has not been asked to step down and is continuing with his duties both as head of state and as host of the G20 Summit.
