Media State Flags Inconsistencies in Testimony of Self-Confessed Killer in Child Murder Case

February 20, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The State has raised concerns about multiple inconsistencies in the testimony of Amber Lee Hughes, the self-confessed killer accused in the rape and murder of four-year-old Nada Jane Chalita.

During court proceedings, prosecutors highlighted contradictions in Hughes’ account of events, arguing that her statements contain gaps and discrepancies that undermine the reliability of her confession. The case has shocked the nation and drawn widespread attention due to the age of the victim and the brutal nature of the alleged crime.

State prosecutors told the court that while Hughes admitted to the crime, her version of events has changed over time, with conflicting details about the timeline, the location of key incidents, and her actions before and after the child’s death. These inconsistencies, the State argues, are critical in determining intent, culpability, and whether additional individuals may have been involved.

Legal experts say confessions are not always straightforward and can sometimes include inaccuracies due to psychological pressure, mental health issues, or attempts to mitigate responsibility. However, the State maintains that it must present a coherent and factual narrative supported by forensic evidence and witness testimony.

The defence is expected to challenge the State’s interpretation of Hughes’ statements, arguing that any inconsistencies do not negate the core admission of guilt. The court is also expected to hear further expert testimony, including forensic specialists and psychologists, to assess the credibility and mental state of the accused.

The case continues to evoke strong public reaction, with child protection groups renewing calls for stronger safeguards and harsher penalties for crimes against children. Proceedings are ongoing, and a verdict is expected later in the trial process.
