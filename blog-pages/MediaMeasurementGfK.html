Media South Africa Prepares for a New Era in Media Measurement

October 3, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Popular Blogs:

Media

Designing Africa’s Media Future

Media

Why Real Mentorship Matters in Content Creation

South Africa is about to experience one of the most significant changes in media measurement in decades. In September, the Broadcast Research Council of South Africa (BRC) announced a major shift: it has chosen GfK, a global analytics company, to create and roll out a new system called Total Video Measurement. This will replace the existing Total Audience Measurement Survey (TAMS), as Nielsen the long-standing measurement provider exits the South African market. While this change is overdue, there’s no denying that Nielsen played an important role in shaping the local TV advertising landscape.

For years, it provided a consistent, if imperfect, benchmark for television viewership. Without that, advertisers would have been flying blind. Just look at what’s happened with radio the industry has been struggling ever since the Radio Audience Measurement Survey was paused in 2023, leading to less data, slower campaign tweaks, and lower trust. Now, with Total Video Measurement on the horizon, things are set to improve dramatically. This isn’t just a technological upgrade it’s a fundamental rethinking of how audiences are measured in a world where TV isn’t just something you watch on a big screen in your living room.

Today, people consume video across phones, tablets, laptops and TVs and the new system aims to reflect that reality. In many ways, South Africa now has the opportunity to leap ahead, blending the wide reach of traditional TV with the detailed, person-level insights of digital platforms. That’s a big deal, especially considering how quickly streaming is growing here. Right now, 15.6 million South Africans stream content, compared to 10.8 million who subscribe to pay TV. One in four people in the country is streaming, and heavy users watch about five hours a day.

Richer measurement means more clarity. Brands won’t just know how many people saw their ads they’ll understand who those people are, how they reacted, and what made them take action. I often tell clients to imagine if broadcasters had access to the kind of data Netflix has. Planning and measuring ad campaigns would shift from generic numbers to sharp, audience-specific insights. Every rand would stretch further. That’s what’s coming. And that’s what marketers need to prepare for. The future of media measurement isn’t just about counting views it’s about truly understanding the people behind the screens.

And let’s drop the debate about what counts as “TV.” Is it YouTube Shorts? TikTok? A Netflix drama? The average viewer doesn’t care. What matters is the viewing experience, and today, that experience flows across multiple screens and platforms. If we can measure that journey accurately, we won’t need to argue about definitions. For brands, this means content is still the vehicle that carries your message. But data is the compass that guides where and how you deliver it.

Whether someone is bingeing a long-form series or scrolling through short clips on their phone, you need to be able to track their behaviour across platforms and plan accordingly. So what should you actually focus on as this new era begins? First, know your audience. Don’t waste money on broad reach when you can be precise about who matters most to your brand. Second, pay attention to engagement. Impressions alone don’t cut it anymore. You need to understand how content is being shared, discussed, and remembered.

Third, stay flexible. Your campaigns need to work across screens, formats, and schedules. People move fast your brand needs to keep up. Finally, be authentic. Your messaging should fit naturally into the content people love, not feel like an interruption. Given South Africa’s unique mix of urban and rural audiences, along with inconsistent access to technology and the internet, these principles are even more critical. The real challenge isn’t the tech itself it’s finding a way to unify the one-to-one world of digital with the one-to-many nature of broadcast.
