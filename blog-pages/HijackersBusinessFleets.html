Media Hijackers Shift Focus to Business Fleets, With Mpumalanga Crime Surge

November 24, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Tracker South Africa’s latest data shows a clear and troubling shift in hijacking trends, business owned vehicles are now far more likely to be targeted than private cars, and Mpumalanga has suddenly emerged as one of the country’s new hotspots. Based on information gathered from more than 1.1 million Tracker equipped cars between January and June 2025, business vehicles are now 48% more likely to be hijacked than privately owned ones.

Criminals seem to be favouring hijackings over traditional theft, adapting their methods as opportunities change. Even when taking into account how many business vehicles are on Tracker’s network, the number of incidents involving them is still 32% higher than expected. Gauteng continues to record the most vehicle crime, making up 57% of all cases nationally. But what stands out in the latest data is how the landscape of hijackings is shifting.

Mpumalanga, a province that previously didn’t feature as a major hotspot, now shows levels similar to those in the Western and Eastern Cape. Incidents are also happening more often during the week rather than on weekends, which aligns with business traffic such as deliveries and fleet movements. The split between hijackings and theft paints an even clearer picture. Across the country, business vehicles are hijacked nearly twice as often as they are stolen. In the Western Cape, they are five times more likely to be hijacked, and in the Eastern Cape the ratio is four to one. Mpumalanga records hijackings at triple the rate of theft.

For private cars, hijacking and theft occur at roughly equal levels, though some provinces like the Eastern Cape, Mpumalanga, and Limpopo still show strong hijacking dominance. SAPS figures offer some additional insight. The police recorded 4,533 hijackings in the most recent three month reporting period down 15.1% from the year before but it still means about 50 vehicles are violently taken every day. When unreported cases from the Victims of Crime survey are considered, the situation appears even more severe. The survey shows hijackings dropped from 114,000 to 81,000 year on year, but that still works out to an average of about 222 incidents a day.

Tracker COO Duma Ngcobo says these shifting trends highlight just how important situational awareness has become. Criminals respond quickly to changes in demand and opportunity, especially when certain vehicles or parts are valuable. He encourages businesses to strengthen their fleet security with technology such as AI enabled dashcams, 360 degree monitoring, safe zone management systems, cargo sensors, and emergency alert tools for drivers. Having rapid armed response support can also reduce the risk of harm during an incident.

Security experts add that awareness remains one of the most powerful defences. Many victims are targeted when distracted or caught off guard. Drivers are urged to stay alert at driveways, intersections, and traffic slowdowns, and those operating high demand or business vehicles should consider a layered security approach combining vigilance with the right technology.
