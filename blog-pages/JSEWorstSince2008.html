Media Markets in Turmoil as JSE Faces Worst Month Since 2008

March 24, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Johannesburg Stock Exchange is on course to record its worst monthly performance since the global financial crisis of 2008, reflecting deepening anxiety among investors as geopolitical and economic pressures intensify.

The downturn has been driven largely by escalating tensions in the Middle East, which have rattled global markets and triggered widespread uncertainty. Over recent weeks, the JSE has experienced persistent declines across key sectors including mining, banking, and retail.

Investors have been steadily shifting capital into safer assets such as gold and government bonds, leading to a noticeable drop in equity prices. This flight to safety has been accompanied by rising oil prices, which are feeding into global inflation concerns and placing further strain on economies already grappling with high living costs.

Analysts warn that South Africa’s already fragile economic recovery could face additional setbacks if the trend continues. Higher inflation expectations may prompt the central bank to maintain a cautious stance on interest rates, limiting prospects for economic growth.

Businesses are beginning to respond by revising earnings forecasts and delaying expansion plans, while consumers face increased financial pressure. Global sentiment remains highly sensitive to developments abroad, with traders reacting quickly to any signs of escalation or de escalation.

While some market watchers believe conditions could stabilise if tensions ease, others caution that prolonged uncertainty could result in deeper and more sustained losses.

As March draws to a close, policymakers and investors alike are watching closely. The coming weeks will be critical in determining whether the market can recover or if further declines are on the horizon.
