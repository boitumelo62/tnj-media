Media Iran Achieves Successful Suborbital Qased Rocket Test

July 22, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Iran has announced the successful completion of a suborbital test of its Qased satellite carrier rocket, marking another step forward in its space technology program. Conducted under the supervision of the Iran Space Agency (ISA), the test was designed to evaluate new systems and improve the efficiency of Iranian satellites, launch vehicles, and related infrastructure.

The Qased, which translates to "Messenger," is a three-stage orbital launch vehicle that first gained international attention in 2020 when it successfully placed the Noor satellite into low-Earth orbit. Since then, it has become a symbol of Iran’s growing ambitions in space exploration and its drive toward technological self-sufficiency.

Iranian officials highlighted that the suborbital test reflects the country’s efforts to boost its space capabilities, particularly in satellite deployment and data-gathering technologies. This achievement comes at a time when Iran continues to face mounting Western and US-led pressure, including sanctions aimed at limiting its technological advancements. Despite these challenges, Iran has steadily expanded its domestic expertise in aerospace engineering.

According to the ISA, the suborbital trial provided critical data on flight performance and stage separation, helping engineers prepare for future orbital missions. The successful test is expected to pave the way for the development of more advanced launch systems and the expansion of Iran’s satellite network, which could have applications in telecommunications, Earth observation, and scientific research.

Iran has been investing in its space sector for more than a decade, launching several satellites into orbit using domestically developed rockets. The recent Qased test underscores the nation’s determination to advance independently and maintain a presence in the rapidly growing global space industry.
