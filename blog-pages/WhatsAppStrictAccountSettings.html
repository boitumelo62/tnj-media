Tech News WhatsApp Introduces Strict Account Settings for High-Risk Users

Meta has announced a new security feature for WhatsApp aimed at users who face a higher risk of targeted cyber-attacks. Known as Strict Account Settings, the option is now rolling out more widely after a period of testing with beta users.

While WhatsApp has long promoted its built-in end to end encryption, Meta says some users require an extra layer of protection. The new setting is designed for individuals such as journalists, activists and public facing figures who may be more vulnerable to sophisticated digital threats.

Strict Account Settings is not enabled by default. Meta explains that this is because the feature significantly changes how WhatsApp functions and may limit convenience for everyday users. Once activated, the setting automatically applies the most restrictive privacy and security options across the app.

Under the new mode, two step verification is switched on and locked, and security notifications are enabled. Users who back up their chats are encouraged to use end to end encrypted backups. Messaging behaviour is also altered, with link previews disabled and high volumes of messages from unknown accounts automatically blocked.

Profile visibility becomes more limited. Information such as last seen status, online presence, profile photo, about details and profile links are restricted to contacts only or to a smaller pre-selected group. Group controls are tightened so that only known contacts or approved users can add someone to a group.

Some features are deliberately restricted to reduce risk. Attachments and media from people who are not in a user’s contacts may be blocked, and calls from unknown numbers can be silenced. Meta says these measures are intended to reduce the chances of spyware, phishing attempts and other advanced attacks.

In announcing the feature, WhatsApp said privacy should feel as natural online as it does in person. While default encryption remains the foundation of the platform, Meta acknowledged that a small number of users need more extreme safeguards against rare but serious threats.

Strict Account Settings can be enabled by navigating to Settings, then Privacy, and then Advanced within the WhatsApp app. The rollout is expected to continue gradually over the coming weeks.

Meta also noted that this update forms part of a broader security push. Behind the scenes, WhatsApp has adopted the Rust programming language to strengthen protection against exploits and spyware, helping to keep messages, photos and videos secure.

The company says the new feature gives vulnerable users greater peace of mind while reinforcing WhatsApp’s long-standing commitment to privacy and security.
