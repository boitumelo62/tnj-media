Tech News The Hidden Trade Off Behind AI Agent Convenience

December 17, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

If you’re wondering how AI agent companies are likely to make money, you probably don’t need to look any further than how web and app services have made money for the past decade or two through you.

Services like Facebook, Google Search, and Instagram are free because you are the product. They design platforms that capture your attention and collect your data liking posts, searching for answers, wishing a friend happy birthday and then they sell that attention and data to advertisers, ad tech firms, research organizations, and often governments. In that sense, the model is fundamentally parasitic. You get convenience and connection, and they get a business built on monetizing your behavior.

Fast forward to 2025, and AI agents are everywhere. You’re constantly being offered digital helpers that promise to work for you, solve problems, manage tasks, and improve your personal life and your business. It sounds almost too good to refuse. After all, what’s the downside?

The most obvious concern people talk about is security. IT teams warn employees not to feed confidential or proprietary information into open models, and those warnings are valid. But beyond data leaks, there’s a deeper issue that’s easier to ignore. Why should we assume AI agent companies will behave any differently from the consumer web and app companies that came before them?

In business settings especially, the risks go beyond simple privacy concerns. Many AI agent providers will prioritize their own incentives first. Even while claiming to work on your behalf, their systems may be extracting value from you at least as aggressively as they are helping you. Your workflows, decisions, preferences, and internal processes can all become valuable assets to the company running the agent.

For a lot of people and a lot of use cases, this may be an acceptable trade off. Hundreds of millions of users continue to use Facebook fully aware of how it operates. The important thing isn’t moral outrage it’s clarity. What matters is understanding what’s really happening under the hood.

So when you think about how “your” AI agents are likely to make money, remember this: there’s a very real chance that you are still the product. Selling access to you, your behavior, and your data is what keeps many of these systems running. The help may be real, but it’s rarely free.
