Business Minister Defends R5.8bn Diesel Spend to Prevent Load Shedding

August 13, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Electricity Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa has tried to reassure South Africans not to be alarmed by Eskom spending billions on diesel to keep the lights on. According to him, this is all part of how the system is meant to work.

He explained that while Eskom did face serious power supply issues earlier in the financial year, especially around April, the situation has been steadily improving. At times, the system is even generating more electricity than needed. But during peak demand periods when everyone’s using electricity at the same time Eskom turns to its diesel generators, often called “peakers,” to help carry the load.

Ramokgopa said that burning diesel in these moments is intentional, not a sign of failure. It’s exactly what the peaker plants were built for to act like a backup when the main power stations are struggling or when demand spikes. So far, Eskom has used about R5.8 billion worth of diesel out of its R12 billion annual budget. While that seems like a lot already, especially just halfway through the year, Ramokgopa said it was necessary to get through the tough winter months.

He compared it to using a safety net and pointed out that this winter, Eskom used much less diesel than last year, when it spent over R14 billion. He also shared some good news: the overall health of Eskom’s power fleet is improving. The energy availability factor the percentage of time that Eskom’s plants are actually working is now averaging about 66% and even hitting 70% on some days. That’s a significant improvement. In fact, there are now moments when Eskom is producing more power than needed, and they have to switch off some units and keep them in "cold reserve" to protect the grid.

Still, Ramokgopa made it clear that 66% availability isn’t the final goal they’re aiming for 75% and beyond. He also acknowledged that while things are looking up, it’s still too early to say that load shedding is over for good. However, he believes we’re very close to that point and promised that once they can confidently declare the end of load shedding, the public will be the first to know.
