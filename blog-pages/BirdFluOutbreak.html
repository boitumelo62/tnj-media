Media Bird flu outbreak confirmed near Paarl, authorities insist poultry remains safe to eat

August 8, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Western Cape Agriculture Department has officially confirmed an outbreak of avian influenza (commonly referred to as bird flu) near Paarl, raising concerns across the province’s poultry sector. The department moved quickly to contain the virus, assuring consumers that poultry products currently in stores remain safe for consumption.

According to a spokesperson from the department, the outbreak was detected during routine surveillance at a commercial poultry farm in the vicinity of Paarl. All affected birds have since been culled in line with biosecurity protocols, and the farm has been placed under quarantine.

“We are working closely with industry partners and veterinary services to monitor the situation. At this stage, there is no cause for alarm regarding the safety of poultry products sold in retail outlets,” the spokesperson stated.

Experts from the South African Poultry Association (SAPA) echoed the department’s position, noting that the virus poses no threat to humans through properly cooked poultry and eggs. However, the association warned that a prolonged outbreak could disrupt supply chains and lead to price increases if not contained.

The last major bird flu scare in South Africa occurred in 2023, leading to significant losses for the poultry industry. This time, authorities say early detection and improved outbreak management strategies are helping mitigate the damage.

Consumers are encouraged to continue practicing standard food safety measures, including thorough cooking and proper handling of raw poultry.

An investigation is currently underway to determine the source of the infection and assess the risk of further spread. The department is expected to provide a full update within the coming days.

For now, poultry farmers and retailers in the region remain on high alert as containment efforts continue.
