Media Court Battle Begins Over Expropriation Act

March 3, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The legal challenge against South Africa’s Expropriation Act officially got under way in the Western Cape High Court on Tuesday, marking the start of what could become a landmark constitutional case.

Civil rights organisation AfriForum is seeking to have key provisions of the Act declared unconstitutional, arguing that they infringe on property rights protected under Section 25 of the Constitution. The organisation maintains that the law opens the door for expropriation without compensation in certain circumstances, which it believes creates uncertainty and threatens investor confidence.

Government, however, has defended the legislation, stating that it is necessary to accelerate land reform while remaining within constitutional bounds. Officials argue that the Act provides clear procedures and safeguards, including requirements for public interest considerations and fair processes before any property is expropriated.

At the centre of Tuesday’s proceedings were arguments around how various interested parties may participate in the case. Several organisations have applied to join as friends of the court, reflecting the wide public interest and political sensitivity surrounding the matter.

Legal analysts say the case could ultimately reach the Constitutional Court, depending on the outcome in the High Court. The judgment is expected to clarify how the law should be interpreted and applied, particularly regarding the contentious issue of nil compensation.

The Expropriation Act has been one of the most debated pieces of legislation in recent years, with supporters describing it as a tool for justice and transformation, and critics warning of economic fallout.

Proceedings are expected to continue throughout the week, with a ruling likely to be reserved.
