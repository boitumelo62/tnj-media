Business BP South Africa Is Upgrading Its Petrol Stations

July 7, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

BP South Africa is rolling out a major upgrade to its petrol stations across the country. The company officially launched the project in May and plans to build 40 brand-new service stations while upgrading over 500 existing ones.

The new and improved sites will offer more than just fuel. BP says they’ll include electric vehicle (EV) charging stations and even low carbon battery rental services. These changes are part of the company’s push to innovate while also tackling energy challenges and reducing carbon emissions.

As part of its wider energy efforts, BP is also upgrading its delivery network by partnering with two logistics companies. The goal is to make fuel deliveries more efficient across South Africa.

Supporting Small Businesses

BP isn’t just focusing on infrastructure — it’s also investing in people. The company has partnered with the Small Enterprise Finance Agency (SEFA), putting R58 million towards helping black-owned small businesses. This funding will support entrepreneurs with both money and technical training to run BP service stations.

Where BP Stands in the Market

BP currently operates around 500 service stations in South Africa, making it the fifth-largest fuel retailer in the country. It sits just below TotalEnergies and ahead of Sasol. Even with the 40 new stations, BP will likely remain just behind TotalEnergies in station count but almost on par.

Interestingly, BP is already the third most popular fuel brand among heavy road users in Gauteng, according to research from analytics firm Lightstone. Gauteng is a key area, home to over 3.3 million of South Africa’s 8 million registered passenger vehicles.

Petrol Stations Are Changing

The way South Africans use petrol stations is evolving. While many still rely on them for late-night convenience shopping, more people are looking for stations that offer a wider range of services.

With the rise of electric vehicles, service stations are shifting from being quick pit stops to more lifestyle-focused spaces. Think less “fill up and go,” and more “grab a coffee, check your email, maybe get some groceries.”

BP’s upgrades fit into this shift. Drivers can expect to see more coffee shops, fast Wi-Fi, clean restrooms, and family-friendly rest areas at service stations in the near future. Some fuel brands are even experimenting with gym pods and co-working spaces to make longer charging stops more productive.

Retail Is the New Fuel

Petrol stations are becoming more like retail hubs, with popular brands like Woolworths Food and Pick n Pay Express teaming up with fuel companies. Location is still key — stations in busy areas will have an edge — but what’s offered at those stations is becoming just as important.

In urban areas, we may see compact EV charging stations. In rural areas, fuel stops might include mechanic services, shops, or even tourism friendly features. Whatever the location, the focus will be on offering smarter, more localised services to stay competitive.
