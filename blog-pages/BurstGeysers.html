A burst geyser can cause serious damage to your home in just a few minutes. The good news is that quick, calm action can reduce the impact and help keep repair costs under control. Below are simple steps homeowners can follow in an emergency plus tips to prevent it from happening again.

Why geysers burst

Geysers operate under pressure, which means several things can go wrong over time. The steel tank naturally weakens as it heats, cools and corrodes. If the inner protective coating wears away or the sacrificial anode isn’t replaced, the tank becomes more vulnerable and may eventually burst.

High water pressure is another major cause. Faulty or blocked pressure control valves can push the tank beyond its limits, putting strain on both the geyser and the plumbing connected to it.

Poor installation also increases the risk of failure. Incorrect fittings, missing valves or non-compliant installations can shorten the geyser’s lifespan. And when small problems like minor drips or strange noises are ignored, they often develop into bigger, more expensive issues.

What to do when your geyser bursts

1. Switch off the power

Go to your electrical distribution board and turn off the circuit labelled “geyser.” If nothing is labelled, switch off the main power supply until an electrician can safely identify the correct circuit. This helps prevent electrical shock.

2. Record the damage

Take photos and short videos of the burst geyser, any leaks, and all affected areas. This evidence is important for insurance claims and for contractors who will assess the damage.

3. Call your insurer and a licensed plumber

Once things are under control, contact your insurance provider and a qualified plumber. A registered professional will inspect the installation, determine what caused the failure and make sure the replacement meets national safety standards.

How to prevent future failures

Routine care goes a long way in avoiding another burst geyser. Make sure to:

  • Service the unit every two to three years.
  • Test the pressure control valve regularly.
  • Replace the sacrificial anode when it becomes worn.
  • Keep the drip tray and overflow pipes clean and clear.
