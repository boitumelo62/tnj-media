Lifestyle Gautrain Brings Art to the People with Market Theatre Collaboration

October 5, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

A Tapestry of Cultures and Traditions

There’s a common belief that the Gautrain is only for the elite sleek, stylish, and far removed from the old yellow and grey trains many South Africans are used to. The older trains were filled with everyday life, including music, dancing, and spontaneous performances. That culture is missing in the quiet, polished carriages of the Gautrain or at least, it used to be. To shift that perception and open the Gautrain up to a broader public, the Gautrain Management Agency has partnered with the Market Theatre for a project called Art in Transit.

This initiative was launched in Sandton during Transport Month, and it's not just about putting art on display it's about changing how people view both transport and art. As Tshepo Kgobe, CEO of the Gautrain Management Agency, puts it: people’s perceptions are shaped by what they know and see. If they believe the Gautrain isn’t for them, then that becomes their reality. But by integrating projects like Art in Transit ,the Gautrain is actively challenging that mindset. The goal is to reimagine spaces like platforms, trains, and even buses as more than just places to pass through they’re becoming living, breathing parts of South African culture.

With curated art and performances, the project aims to reflect the country's rich diversity and creativity. It’s about creating a space where everyone feels represented and welcomed. When Kgobe stepped into his role in early 2024, he made it clear that he saw transport as a lifestyle not just a way to get from A to B. It’s about how people connect with the city, with culture, and with each other. Late night trains for events like the Scorpion Kings concert are part of that shift making the train system more accessible, flexible, and relevant to people’s real lives. Art has often been seen as something reserved for the few, especially in theatre, which some see as exclusive or intimidating.

That’s something Tshiamo Mokgadi, CEO of the Market Theatre Foundation, is also trying to change. He believes this partnership is about meeting people where they are, instead of waiting for them to come to traditional spaces like theatres. He points out how the old township trains were filled with performance and culture, and this is a way of bringing that energy back but in a new setting. Both the theatre and the train move people one physically, the other emotionally and mentally. That’s what makes this collaboration feel natural, even necessary. Kgobe says the Market Theatre’s deep connection to Johannesburg’s cultural heritage made them a natural partner. The theatre has helped shape generations of South African artists, and its story is deeply tied to the story of the country itself.

This isn’t just a short term campaign. Art in Transit will continue to bring different forms of art to the Gautrain network over the coming months and into 2026, when the Market Theatre celebrates its 50th anniversary. Greg Homann, the theatre’s artistic director, says plans for that milestone are already underway, with events lined up to reflect on its powerful legacy and its role in the country’s history especially considering it opened during the same week as the historic June 16 student uprising in 1976. So while the Gautrain may still look sleek and polished, don’t be surprised if your next trip includes a little bit of storytelling, music, or visual art. It’s not about making art fancy it’s about making it part of daily life, just like catching a train.
