Media Equality Court Rules Julius Malema Guilty of Hate Speech

August 29, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Equality Court has found Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF) leader Julius Malema guilty of hate speech over remarks he made at a Cape Town rally nearly three years ago.

The ruling follows a case brought by civil society organisations and political opponents, who argued that Malema’s words incited harm and violence. The court agreed, finding that his comments went beyond protected political speech.

Judge Mpho Dlamini, delivering the judgment, said: “While freedom of expression is a cornerstone of our democracy, it does not extend to speech that promotes hatred or incites violence. Leaders must be held accountable for the power of their words.”

Malema has previously defended his rhetoric as part of robust political debate. The EFF has not yet issued an official response to the ruling, but in earlier submissions to the court, the party argued the case was an attempt to silence dissent.

Analysts say the outcome could influence how future cases of political speech are handled. Political analyst Thandi Maseko noted: “The ruling signals that courts are prepared to step in when rhetoric poses a threat to social cohesion. It’s likely to shape the way politicians address crowds going forward.”

The Equality Court is expected to decide on appropriate sanctions in the coming weeks, which could range from fines to further restrictions on Malema’s public statements.
