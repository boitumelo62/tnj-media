Business Big Business Invited to Buy Direct from Eskom’s Solar Projects

Eskom has just launched something pretty significant a new programme aimed at boosting solar energy use in South Africa. For the first time ever, the power utility is opening the door for big energy users to buy renewable power directly from Eskom owned solar sites. This is all part of its shift away from coal and toward a cleaner energy mix.

Here’s how it works: Eskom is asking for proposals from large commercial and industrial users who want to lock in long-term deals anywhere from 5 to 25 year to buy solar energy. In total, they’re offering 291 megawatts of capacity, and the power will come online in phases from various Eskom projects, with the first one expected to be ready by the end of 2027.

The goal is to give businesses a way to meet their sustainability goals, cut carbon emissions, and green their supply chains all while helping the country move toward a cleaner energy future. Eskom says this is part of its bigger plan to shift its energy mix to include more renewables, along with gas, nuclear, energy storage, and some remaining coal. By 2040, they’re aiming to have up to 32 gigawatts of renewable energy, including projects involving green hydrogen.

This new approach also involves setting up a dedicated renewable energy business within Eskom that will fast track projects targeting 2 gigawatts by 2026. However, there’s a bit of a contradiction here. While Eskom is pushing to modernize and open up to private collaboration, it’s also been taking legal action against energy regulator Nersa. The reason? Nersa issued licences to other companies to trade electricity, which Eskom claims goes against its own licensing rights.

Critics, including energy experts and even Electricity Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa, argue that Eskom is just trying to block competition and hold onto its monopoly something that could actually work against South Africa’s broader energy reform goals. Despite this tension, Eskom insists that the new programme shows its commitment to moving forward.

The company says this initiative gives customers a real chance to switch to cleaner operations without sacrificing reliability or competitiveness. According to Eskom, working with the private sector in this way is a major step toward building a more sustainable energy future for the country.
