Business Prosus Acquires 90% of Just Eat Takeaway, Plans Delisting

October 2, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Naspers, South Africa’s most valuable company, has successfully completed its offer to buy Just Eat Takeaway (JET), a major food delivery company based in the Netherlands. The deal, worth €4.1 billion (around R83 billion), was made through MIH Bidco Holdings, a subsidiary of Prosus. Prosus, which is based in the Netherlands, has a close ownership relationship with Naspers. Naspers has a market value just under R1 trillion, and a significant part of its wealth comes from its large stake in the Chinese tech company Tencent.

Now, with this acquisition, it's strengthening its presence in the global tech and food delivery space. Just Eat Takeaway is an on-demand food delivery company, operating in 17 countries, and is known for its visibility during major football tournaments like the UEFA competitions. It's comparable to services like Uber Eats and Mr D in South Africa. Prosus recently announced that its offer to buy JET is now unconditional. More than 180 million JET shares were tendered during the offer period, which accounts for about 90% of the company's total share capital. This means all the conditions for the deal have been met, and the acquisition is officially complete.

Prosus CEO Fabricio Bloisi expressed his excitement about the deal, saying he's eager to bring JET into the Prosus family and help transform it into a leading European tech company, focusing on product innovation and customer experience. Bloisi, who previously led Latin America’s largest food delivery service, iFood, stepped into the CEO role at Prosus earlier this year. JET CEO Jitse Groen also welcomed the acquisition and said he’s looking forward to working with the new owners to grow the business and shape its future. For JET shareholders who didn’t tender their shares during the initial offer period, there’s still an opportunity to do so in the post-closing period, under the same terms.

Prosus and JET also plan to delist JET shares from the Euronext Amsterdam stock exchange soon. Once that happens, the liquidity and market value of any remaining untendered shares might be negatively affected. While the company’s management and supervisory boards will stay the same for now, changes will be made once the delisting is complete. Five current board members will step down, and three new members Bloisi, Roberto Gandolfo, and Fahd Beg will join from Prosus.
