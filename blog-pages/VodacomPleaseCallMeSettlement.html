Business Vodacom Confirms Settlement in Long Running ‘Please Call Me’ Case

November 6, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The long running “Please Call Me” case between Kenneth Makate and Vodacom has finally come to an end, with Makate set to earn as much as R750 million. Vodacom announced late on Tuesday night, 5 November, that the two parties had reached an out of court settlement, bringing the 20 year legal battle to a close. In its statement, the company said both sides were pleased that the matter had finally been resolved.

As part of the settlement, Vodacom withdrew its appeal from the Supreme Court of Appeal and abandoned the High Court judgment handed down on 8 February 2022. This officially ends one of South Africa’s most talked about corporate legal disputes.

The case dates back to 2001 when Makate, then a Vodacom employee, claimed he came up with the idea for a “Please Call Me” service—a free text that allowed users without airtime to request a call back. He said he shared the idea with his superior, who promised him compensation if the idea succeeded. After not receiving any payment, Makate took legal action, arguing that Vodacom had unfairly benefited from his concept, which became one of the company’s most successful services.

The matter eventually reached the Constitutional Court, which ruled that Vodacom must pay Makate a fair amount for his idea. Vodacom initially offered him R10 million, later increasing it to R47 million, but Makate rejected both offers, saying they were far below what he deserved. The case bounced between the High Court, Supreme Court of Appeal, and Constitutional Court several times before finally being settled this week.

While the exact settlement figure hasn’t been officially disclosed, Vodacom’s latest financial updates provide strong clues. The company said the settlement would result in a “one off cost” affecting its earnings for the first half of its 2026 financial year. Based on changes in Vodacom’s trading statements, analysts estimate that the settlement could range between R353 million and R748 million—putting Makate’s payout likely closer to the upper end of that range, around R750 million.

Vodacom will include the final settlement details in its interim financial results, which are expected to be released next Tuesday, 10 November 2025. This marks the end of a landmark case that has shaped debates about innovation, corporate responsibility, and fairness in South Africa’s business world.
