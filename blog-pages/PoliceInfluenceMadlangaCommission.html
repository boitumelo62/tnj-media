Media Claims of Police Influence Surface at Madlanga Commission

February 4, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Evidence presented before the Madlanga Commission has suggested that tender businessman Vusimuzi “Cat” Matlala attempted to use his relationship with a Gauteng police officer to access information linked to a murder investigation.

Testimony heard by the commission indicated that Matlala allegedly sought inside details on the progress of the case through informal channels rather than official legal processes. The evidence forms part of a broader inquiry into alleged corruption, interference and compromised conduct within law enforcement structures.

The commission was told that communication records point to efforts by Matlala to obtain sensitive information while the investigation was still active. Such actions, if proven, could represent a serious breach of police protocol and undermine the integrity of criminal investigations.

Matlala has long been a controversial figure due to his links to government tenders and recurring allegations of improper influence. He has consistently denied wrongdoing and has not been convicted in relation to the claims currently before the commission.

The Madlanga Commission is tasked with examining whether senior police officials have abused their positions or allowed private individuals to interfere in policing processes. Legal analysts say the allegations highlight ongoing concerns about the vulnerability of the criminal justice system to external pressure.

Commission leaders emphasised that the hearings are aimed at uncovering the truth and making recommendations rather than determining criminal guilt. Further testimony is expected as the inquiry continues.

The case has renewed public debate around accountability in law enforcement, with civil society organisations calling for stronger safeguards to protect investigations from political or personal interference.
