Why Good Design is a Game-Changer for Your Brand

In today’s world, where people often search your name on Google before shaking your hand, reputation can move faster than any resume ever will. What you post, say, or share online can either build you up or tear everything down in seconds. That’s why understanding public relations (PR) isn’t just for companies and celebrities — it’s a vital life skill, especially for young professionals. PR isn't only about damage control after a scandal, it’s about how you show up in the world every day.

It’s about being intentional with your words, your actions, and how you present yourself both online and in real life. People don’t always wait to meet you to form an opinion. Often, they've already made up their minds from your digital presence. Think about it: your social media, how you dress, how you speak to others all of this forms your personal brand. And that brand matters when you're applying for a job, seeking new opportunities, or just trying to build trust. In the same way education opens doors, so does your reputation. In fact, in many cases, your image gets you in the room before your CV even has a chance. This is where three core PR skills come into play skills that every young professional should learn and practice.

First, there's crisis communication. Mistakes happen. We’re human. But how you respond when things go wrong can either save or sink your reputation. The key isn’t to be perfect it’s to be accountable. Owning up to your actions shows emotional maturity. Being defensive, on the other hand, can come off as cold or arrogant. Look at the United Airlines case from 2017. Even though they followed policy, the lack of empathy and poor communication cost the company over a billion dollars in market value in just days. That’s how powerful perception is.

Next is personal branding. This is about shaping how people see you, and consistency is everything. What you post on TikTok, Instagram, or X (formerly Twitter) creates a digital footprint that employers and the public can and will trace. A well-managed online presence can make you look professional, credible, and confident. But controversial content or outdated posts can cost you everything. Just ask former DA Member of Parliament Renaldo Gouws. After being sworn in, an old video of his resurfaced and led to his removal from parliament. It’s a tough lesson: the internet never forgets. But personal branding isn't just digital.

Your appearance and the way you carry yourself matter too. Studies have shown that many interviewers form their first impression within the first five minutes, heavily influenced by how you look and act. So, while we like to say "don’t judge a book by its cover," first impressions still hold power. Your brand is more than a logo or a bio it’s everything people see, hear, and feel about you.

Lastly, there's reputation recovery. We all mess up it’s unavoidable. But when it happens, taking responsibility and making a genuine apology goes a long way. Recovery isn't about saying sorry once and moving on. It’s about showing growth, maintaining consistency, and proving over time that you’ve learned from your mistakes. The internet might not forget, but people can forgive if you give them a reason to.

In South Africa, where our society is as diverse as it is complex, these PR skills are even more important. We interact with people from many different backgrounds, cultures, and perspectives. That means we need to be especially mindful of how we communicate, how we represent ourselves, and how we build trust. Digital missteps, cultural insensitivity, or a lack of professionalism can burn bridges quickly in such a diverse environment.

At the end of the day, being trusted matters more than being liked. Trust opens doors, builds relationships, and creates leaders. And good leadership starts with accountability, empathy, and a strong personal brand. Public relations isn’t just about telling a good story it’s about becoming someone worth listening to. In a digital world where every action is public and every word can be shared, PR is no longer just a corporate function. It’s a personal responsibility. One that every young professional should take seriously.
