Media Madlanga Commission Regulations Gazetted, Clearing Way for Inquiry into Police Corruption

August 22, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

President Cyril Ramaphosa has formally signed off on the regulations for the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry, paving the way for the body to begin investigating police corruption and political interference in law enforcement. The regulations were published in the Government Gazette this week, giving them legal effect.

The gazetting process is a constitutional requirement that ensures transparency and public accountability. By appearing in the Government Gazette, the rules governing the commission gain official status, allowing it to operate with full legal authority. This step also signals the government’s commitment to enabling the inquiry to proceed without procedural delays.

The commission, chaired by Constitutional Court Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga, has been tasked with probing long-standing allegations of corruption within the South African Police Service (SAPS) and claims of political meddling in operational matters. Civil society organisations and opposition parties have for years voiced concerns about compromised policing, arguing that political influence has undermined accountability and effective crime fighting.

Justice Madlanga will have the power to summon witnesses, compel the submission of documents, and cross-examine individuals implicated in wrongdoing. The inquiry is expected to cover high-profile cases where allegations of corruption and interference have eroded public confidence in the police.

Government spokespersons described the gazetting of the regulations as a critical milestone, noting that the commission can now begin its work with full independence and authority. While the scope and timeline of the inquiry remain to be clarified, analysts say its findings could have far-reaching implications for law enforcement reform and political accountability in South Africa.

The establishment of the Madlanga Commission reflects growing pressure on the state to restore trust in the criminal justice system amid rising crime and persistent allegations of police misconduct.
