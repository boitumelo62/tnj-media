Business Government Bets Big on Black Business with R100bn Fund

November 7, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The government has reaffirmed its commitment to establishing a R100 billion Transformation Fund aimed at boosting black owned businesses over the next five years. The Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (DTIC), under Minister Parks Tau, said the proposal for the fund has been updated to strengthen accountability and ensure effective use of resources. Companies contributing to the fund will also be able to earn Broad Based Black Economic Empowerment (B-BBEE) scorecard points for enterprise and supplier development (ESD).

On 19 March 2025, Minister Tau released the Draft Transformation Fund concept document for public comment. The plan allocates around R20 billion annually to black owned enterprises equivalent to roughly R55 million per day intended to help them expand and participate more fully in strategic sectors of the economy. The initiative will merge existing ESD contributions that businesses are already required to make, meaning it will not create additional financial burdens. Currently, companies must allocate about 3% of their net profit after tax to support black suppliers, black industrialists, and small and medium enterprises.

The DTIC says the fund will make these efforts more coordinated and impactful by providing both financial and non financial assistance, such as mentorship, market access, and capacity development. Its primary goals include improving access to finance for black owned companies, increasing their presence in key industries, and pooling public and private resources to advance inclusive growth. According to the department, the fund addresses structural barriers that have long limited the growth of black entrepreneurs beyond early stages of development.

However, the proposal has attracted criticism. Some argue that it ignores deeper challenges such as policy uncertainty, low investor confidence, and South Africa’s unreliable electricity supply. Critics like Toby Chance of the Democratic Alliance and economist Stuart Theobald have questioned whether the government can effectively deploy R20 billion a year, noting that no previous development initiative has operated at such a scale. Theobald also warned that the emphasis seems to be on spending large sums rather than achieving measurable results.

In response, the DTIC’s revised plan introduces stronger governance structures. The fund will be overseen by an independent board comprising representatives from both the public and private sectors, with private stakeholders appointing their own directors. Independent investment committees will manage funding decisions, management teams will be rewarded based on performance, and all results will be publicly reported. A broader oversight council representing government, business, labour, and civil society will further ensure transparency and accountability.

A key improvement is that companies contributing to the fund will immediately receive their BEE scorecard points for ESD in the same financial year, reducing administrative burdens. Research by the BEE Commission shows that only about 61% of firms currently meet their ESD targets, partly due to challenges in tracking real outcomes. The government believes that pooling contributions through a central mechanism will improve both efficiency and impact.
