Media Matjeng Denies Cash Allegation as Scrutiny Intensifies Over Scrapped Police Contract

February 6, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

A senior South African Police Service official has firmly denied allegations that she received a cash payment linked to a controversial police tender, as questions continue to mount over governance and procurement within the service.

SAPS Brigadier Rachel Matjeng has rejected claims that she accepted R300,000 from alleged tender tycoon Vusimuzi “Cat” Matlala in connection with a cancelled R360 million police contract. The allegation surfaced amid ongoing scrutiny of the procurement process that led to the awarding, and subsequent termination, of the deal.

Matjeng has dismissed the claim as false and without basis, maintaining that she did not receive any payment from Matlala or any individual associated with the tender. Her denial comes as law enforcement agencies and oversight bodies face renewed pressure to demonstrate accountability and transparency, particularly in cases involving large public contracts.

The cancelled contract has already drawn public attention due to its value and the circumstances surrounding its approval. While details of why the deal was terminated have not been fully disclosed, the matter has added to broader concerns about tender processes within state institutions and the influence of politically connected business figures.

Matlala, who has previously been linked to high value government tenders, has not publicly responded to the latest allegations. The absence of formal charges at this stage means the claims remain untested, though the issue continues to fuel debate about corruption and ethical conduct within the police service.

Analysts say the case underscores the importance of clear procurement safeguards and swift investigations when allegations arise. Public trust in the SAPS, already under strain, depends heavily on how decisively such matters are handled.

As the situation develops, attention will likely turn to whether any formal investigation is launched and what steps are taken to clarify the circumstances surrounding the cancelled contract. For now, Matjeng’s denial stands, with authorities yet to indicate their next move.
