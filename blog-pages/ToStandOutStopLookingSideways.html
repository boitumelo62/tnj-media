Business To Stand Out, Stop Looking Sideways

October 21, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

In a business world where standing out is everything, it’s strange how many companies end up looking, sounding, and behaving exactly the same. They focus so much on what their competitors are doing that they forget who they’re actually supposed to be serving: the customer. When you're stuck thinking only within the rules of your category whether that’s banking, insurance, beer, or streaming you're just trying to outdo someone else in your lane, instead of asking if the lane itself still makes sense. It becomes a race to be slightly better, not radically different.

This kind of thinking leads to tiny, incremental changes that feel like progress but rarely move the needle. Think of telecom companies offering a few extra minutes or data, banks adjusting fees by a few cents, or consumer brands launching endless variations of the same product. It all feels like busywork changing without truly improving anything meaningful for customers. The result? Everyone ends up offering some version of the same thing, just wrapped in a slightly different package. A big part of the problem is that most innovation isn’t led by consumer insight it’s driven by competitor behavior. One player tries something new, the rest follow, and before long it becomes the industry standard.

That’s how once fresh ideas like vertical bank cards or cash back incentives become just another expected feature. It’s what academics call “path dependence” where something that started as a good idea becomes a habit, then a rule, and finally a locked in way of doing things. It becomes the default, even when it no longer serves the customer well. And that’s when you hear lines like “That’s just how we do it.” But it doesn’t have to be that way. Real innovation the kind that breaks industries open often comes from looking outside your category altogether. This is the thinking behind the Outlooking Safaris approach a way to shake companies out of their routine by exposing teams to entirely different worlds.

It’s about seeing your category through fresh eyes by immersing yourself in spaces that have nothing to do with your product or service. If you work for a bank, why not spend time at a music concert or a luxury fashion boutique? If you’re in telecom, why not explore a food market or an art exhibit? These experiences aren't random they’re structured, guided, and purposeful. Teams are given a brief, sent out to observe, and then regroup to share what they’ve seen. It’s not about copying others, but about “stealing with pride” borrowing ideas, experiences, or structures from other industries and imagining how they could work in your own. It’s about sparking fresh ideas by breaking out of the usual frame.

This idea comes from a concept known as cross domain analogical reasoning essentially, finding useful patterns or ideas in one area and applying them to another. Nature has always been a master of this birds inspired the design of bullet trains, burrs led to the invention of Velcro. Human industries can do the same. Think of Formula 1 pit stops inspiring more efficient hospital procedures. Or gamification from the video game world now helping people learn languages and track their health.

It’s not just about technology or process, either. Entire cultures offer ways of thinking that businesses can learn from. The African philosophy of Ubuntu “I am because we are” has helped global leaders build more human cantered organizations.

Japanese Omotenashi the art of deep, thoughtful hospitality has shaped the way Lexus treats its customers. These aren’t just soft ideas they are powerful ways to bring emotion, purpose, and care into industries that may otherwise feel cold or transactional. When you keep looking sideways watching what your competitors are doing you end up making decisions out of habit or fear. You create features and products not because customers asked for them, but because everyone else is doing it. It becomes a treadmill of sameness. But when you give yourself permission to explore beyond your category, you open up entirely new ways to solve problems and connect with people.
