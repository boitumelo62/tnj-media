Business Shell Faces Final Legal Battle Over Wild Coast Offshore Survey

September 16, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s highest court is preparing to hear arguments in a long running legal dispute between Shell and environmental campaigners over a seismic survey proposal off the Wild Coast. The case, several years in the making, could have major implications for offshore oil exploration in South Africa.

The conflict began in 2021, when Shell and a local partner planned to carry out a seismic survey in the Indian Ocean off a rugged coastline known for its rich marine life and frequent whale sightings. Critics raised concerns about risks to wildlife and coastal communities, along with claims that affected residents had not been properly consulted. A court order halted the survey at the time.

Now the matter has reached the country’s top court, which will decide whether the conditions placed on Shell’s project and the legal arguments from activists are strong enough to block or permit the work. The case focuses on environmental law, community consent and the requirements for exploration licenses.

Shell has argued that the project would bring economic and energy benefits to South Africa. Activists counter that the survey poses a serious threat to marine ecosystems and the livelihoods of communities along the coast. The ruling is expected to establish precedent for how future exploration applications are reviewed, especially in ecologically sensitive areas.

Observers believe the case highlights a broader struggle in South Africa between the drive for energy development and the need to protect the environment. The court’s decision will likely influence how regulators and communities approach similar disputes in the years ahead.
