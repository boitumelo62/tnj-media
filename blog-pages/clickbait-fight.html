Media Why Communicators Must Lead the Fight Against Clickbait

September 12, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Client Relationships Are the Heart of Public Relations

Clickbait should be keeping communication and reputation professionals wide awake and more importantly, pushing us to take immediate action. In recent months, concern over the growing presence of clickbait in mainstream media has become a common theme among clients. What used to be a fringe annoyance is now a global problem, fuelled by economic pressures and changing platform dynamics.

Misleading headlines, incorrect reporting of financial results, AI generated content, and manipulative rage baiting tactics are no longer rare they’re becoming the norm. And with that, media credibility is eroding fast. The numbers tell a worrying story. Around 30% of global mainstream headlines can be considered clickbait, with higher rates on social media driven platforms. In sub Saharan Africa, it’s reportedly above 40%. Trust in media is falling 58% of people worldwide view misinformation as a major concern, rising to 73% in Africa and the US.

Alarmingly, 1 in 3 people believe journalists themselves are part of the problem. In South Africa, news avoidance sits at 41%, among the highest in the world. Globally, this trend has risen from 29% in 2017 to 40% in 2025, driven by media fatigue and growing anxiety around distinguishing real from fake news. The root causes lie in how news is consumed today, via social media and video platforms that favour engagement over accuracy. Algorithms push sensationalist content, users live in personalised echo chambers, and content creators from podcasters to influencers are incentivised to prioritise clicks over truth.

Video first formats thrive on emotional manipulation, and platforms profit from outrage. The situation escalates further when clickbait morphs into rage bait content deliberately designed to provoke hate and division. In South Africa, political influencers have adopted transnational rage bait tactics, especially during the 2024 elections. Yet no current laws address this specific threat, even though the social harm is clear and growing. Professional communicators can no longer sit on the sidelines. This is a direct threat to organisational trust and reputation. We must lead with urgency.

Second, we must strengthen owned media platforms from corporate newsrooms to direct to audience channels to control messaging and reduce dependence on external media. Third, we must invest in real time monitoring tools to track and respond to misleading narratives. Training young journalists and supporting quality journalism are essential long term moves. Ultimately, tackling clickbait is not just about fixing media relations.
