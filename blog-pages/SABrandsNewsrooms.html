Media Why South African Brands Need to Think Like Newsrooms in 2026

December 11, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

One of the biggest advantages of working in a company led by a former financial journalist is that it completely changes the way you see content. You start thinking like a newsroom. You ask what is timely, what has public value, what needs explaining and where the true story sits. That mindset shapes how we approach B2B communications and why we believe South African brands need a more news driven, insight led approach in 2026.

Right now, South African corporates are sitting on huge reserves of knowledge and insight, but very few treat their content the way a newsroom would. Every year, marketing budgets come under pressure. PR teams must prove their value. Brand and corporate affairs leaders struggle for attention in a very crowded digital space. Yet the solution is often already inside the business: the ability to produce timely, credible, relevant content that meets audiences where they are.

In 2026, communication is becoming sharper, faster and more dependent on data. Research shows that professional audiences increasingly prefer news style content over traditional branded material. The content that performs best is the kind that explains, clarifies and guides decisions, not the kind that simply promotes. For B2B brands to stay visible, they need to let go of occasional campaigns and build internal newsroom models that run on beats, quick response cycles and steady insight from subject matter experts.

The challenge is that the news cycle has sped up and many brands haven’t kept pace. South African journalists are under pressure, working in smaller teams with tighter deadlines. According to the Ornico PR Measurement Report, journalists now expect context, relevance and data immediately often in the first line of an email because they don’t have capacity for lengthy back and forth communication. In this environment, slow, corporate style content simply doesn’t stand a chance.

Many B2B companies still rely on quarterly campaigns or the odd press release. Those approaches may have worked ten years ago, but they can’t keep up with real time changes in energy markets, infrastructure policy, cybersecurity, ESG regulations, funding cycles or labour issues. A newsroom model gives brands the structure to respond to these developments within hours instead of weeks, which is essential if they want meaningful media coverage.
