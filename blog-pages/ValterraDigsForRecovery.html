Business Valterra Digs for Recovery

July 28, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Valterra Platinum, the company that recently broke away from Anglo American and became independent, has had a tough start. In the first half of 2025, its earnings dropped sharply by 91%, falling from R6.3 billion to just R600 million. The company also cut its interim dividend significantly, from R9.75 per share to R2.00.

Valterra used to be Anglo American Platinum until it was spun off earlier this year. This move was part of Anglo American’s broader strategy to restructure and fend off a takeover attempt by rival mining giant BHP. As part of the plan, Anglo American started shedding some of its key assets, including the platinum business, to focus its efforts and resources elsewhere.

Platinum itself faces a cloudy future. While it’s a crucial material used in catalytic converters to reduce vehicle emissions, the global shift towards electric vehicles is weakening demand. EVs don’t need catalytic converters, so the long-term role of platinum is uncertain — especially since South Africa, where Valterra operates, is the world’s biggest producer.

On top of that, Valterra had to deal with operational setbacks. Flooding at its Amandelbult mine led to a 12% drop in output, and there were additional costs tied to the company’s recent spin-off. Anglo American still holds nearly 20% of Valterra but plans to sell off that stake over time. Despite all these challenges, Valterra’s leadership is still hopeful about the rest of the year.

CEO Craig Miller says the company expects a better second half, thanks to improvements at key mines and the completion of scheduled maintenance work that should boost production efficiency. He mentioned that production should still fall within the company’s original guidance range, although likely at the lower end due to the earlier disruption.

The company has also trimmed its capital spending plans by about R1 billion, now expecting to spend between R17.0 billion and R17.5 billion in 2025. But with production expected to pick up and platinum prices having risen 55% this year due to strong industrial demand, Valterra hopes to benefit from better market conditions going forward. Miller emphasized the company’s focus on maintaining strong operations, keeping costs in check, and making smart investments as it moves through the rest of the year.
