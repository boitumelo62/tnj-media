Media Musicians Intensify Calls for Transparency at SAMRO

November 14, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Artists from across the country marched to the Southern African Music Rights Organisation yesterday in a strongly coordinated demonstration demanding improved governance and financial accountability. Musicians who participated in the march said years of delayed royalty payments, vague reporting, and limited access to financial information have damaged trust between creators and the organisation tasked with protecting their rights.

Representatives of the artist collective delivered a detailed memorandum calling for clear accounting practices, independent oversight, and a transparent royalty distribution system. Participants stated that their intention was not only to correct past concerns but also to pave the way for a new era in the creative sector where financial fairness is non-negotiable. SAMRO officials received the document and signalled that an internal review may soon follow.

Industry experts note that calls for reform at SAMRO are not new. For more than a decade, artists have raised concerns about the opacity of its systems and the unpredictability of payments. The latest march marks one of the most unified displays of frustration to date and reflects a broader demand for accountability across South African institutions.
