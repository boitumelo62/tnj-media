Media Sibiya grilled over political killings unit and alleged police interference at Madlanga Commission

February 23, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Suspended Deputy National Police Commissioner Shadrack Sibiya has continued his testimony before the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry, where he is facing intense questioning over his role in the disbandment of the Political Killings Task Team and allegations of interference in sensitive police investigations.

Sibiya returned to the witness stand as part of the ongoing public inquiry examining claims of corruption, political interference and criminal infiltration within South Africa’s criminal justice system. The commission, chaired by retired Constitutional Court Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga, was established to probe allegations that senior police officials, politicians and organised crime figures colluded to undermine law enforcement efforts.

During his testimony, Sibiya claimed that senior figures within the South African Police Service played a key role in decisions that led to the disbandment of the Political Killings Task Team, which had been investigating politically motivated murders, particularly in KwaZulu Natal. He told the commission that draft meeting records and internal discussions pointed to high level involvement in the decision-making process.

He has also denied giving instructions for critical case dockets to be removed from KwaZulu Natal and transferred to Gauteng, arguing that he never authorised such a move and that the relocation of the files was carried out by other officials. However, documentary evidence presented at the commission suggests he issued a directive regarding the handling of the dockets, raising questions about his version of events.

The Madlanga Commission forms part of broader efforts to address allegations that organised crime networks infiltrated law enforcement and political structures. Evidence presented at the inquiry has linked senior police officials and political figures to alleged criminal syndicates and attempts to obstruct investigations.

Sibiya, a senior career police officer who previously headed the crime detection division, was suspended in 2025 amid mounting allegations of misconduct and interference in high profile investigations. His testimony is expected to influence possible disciplinary proceedings and potential criminal action, depending on the commission’s findings.

The inquiry continues to hear evidence from key witnesses, with its final report expected to shape reforms within the police service and the broader justice system, amid public calls for accountability and stronger oversight of senior law enforcement officials.
