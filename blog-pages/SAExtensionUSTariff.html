Business South Africa Seeks Extension on U.S. Tariff Deadline

July 7, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa is actively requesting a postponement of the upcoming 90-day deadline imposed by the United States for its tariff measures. This deadline, set for July 9, 2025, marks the expiry of a pause on potential U.S. tariff hikes.

The Department of Trade, Industry and Competition has already submitted a proposed framework agreement to Washington. This proposal focuses on improving bilateral trade by addressing non-tariff barriers, reducing trade imbalances, enhancing commercial ties through reciprocal procurement strategies, and boosting investment flows. The goal is to resolve long-standing market access issues in a way that benefits both nations.

Minister Parks Tau emphasized that, in light of discussions during President Cyril Ramaphosa’s visit to the U.S. and talks held alongside the US Africa Business Summit, South Africa and other African countries are pushing for an extension. This would give them enough time to align their proposals with the new U.S. template for such agreements.

Tau encouraged South African businesses to adopt a strategy of “strategic patience,” highlighting the importance of maintaining open communication with U.S. authorities to protect the country’s interests in American markets.

In parallel, South Africa is pursuing expansion of its trade partnerships. It is working on a Clean Trade and Investment Partnership with the European Union, backed by an initial investment of R90 billion, which would offer duty-free access for key sectors such as dairy. Additionally, negotiations with Saudi Arabia aim to finalize a Joint Economic Commission to boost agricultural investment and tap into Gulf opportunities.

Citrus Industry Concerned Over Uncertainty

Dr. Boitshoko Ntshabele, CEO of the Citrus Growers’ Association, warned that the looming U.S. tariff decision is increasing anxiety among producers, hampering their ability to plan for the full season. He highlighted the need to preserve favourable access to both the U.S. and other markets, like China and India, to create jobs and capitalize on rising citrus volumes.

“Securing favourable access to the US market should be a priority... Without improved access in all markets and opening new markets, the increase in citrus production cannot be channelled into new jobs.”

Dr. Ntshabele expressed concern that, without clarity and stable access across multiple markets, the sector’s growth and its employment benefits remain at risk.
