Media Solidarity Warns of Massive Job Losses After Talks with Government Collapse

November 25, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Trade union Solidarity and the government are now officially in dispute after a mediation process at NEDLAC failed to resolve concerns about the growing wave of retrenchments across the country. NEDLAC, created in 1994, is meant to be a place where government, business, labour, and community groups work together to find solutions to socio economic challenges. However, Solidarity says this latest round of talks didn’t lead to any progress, and the forum is expected to formally confirm the failed mediation within two weeks.

According to Solidarity, the country could see hundreds of thousands of job losses before Christmas, and the union plans to release more details soon. It also intends to organise protests to draw attention to the seriousness of the situation. The second round of mediation included major employers such as Columbus Steel, Ferroglobe, Trans Alloys, Samancor, MMC Nelspruit, and Glencore, with the Minerals Council and Business Unity South Africa also represented. Solidarity claims that a presentation by the Ferro Alloy Producers Association contained alarming retrenchment figures, although the union cannot share details due to mediation confidentiality rules.

Earlier this year, Solidarity declared a dispute with the government under section 77 of the Labour Relations Act. CEO Dirk Hermann said the union had hoped to make progress through dialogue but warned that, without action, the country could face another “work bloodbath” before the festive season. One of the union’s main proposals was that government create a multi departmental team to tackle the various issues driving the crisis, but Hermann says there was little urgency from government and no willingness to continue discussions.

Solidarity argues that many recent retrenchment notices point directly to government failures. These include problems with rail, road, and port infrastructure, high electricity prices, policy uncertainty, excessive regulation, the cost of empowerment requirements, and even the impact of US tariffs. The union says major companies referenced in its submissions would be invited to mediation should it continue.

The warning of mass job losses comes at a time when South Africa is already struggling with extremely high unemployment. Official unemployment stands at 32.6%, while the expanded rate is more than 41%. The situation is especially bleak for young people aged 16 to 24, who face an unemployment rate of over 62%. Most unemployed South Africans have been out of work for at least a year, and the country has the lowest employment to population ratio in the G20.

Economists from Coronation note that this crisis stems from years of weak economic growth, falling investment, and a growing mismatch between the skills people have and the skills employers need. They warn that if these issues aren’t addressed, South Africa’s long term growth will remain stagnant and the financial situation will worsen. They also point out that unemployment is one of the biggest drivers of inequality, and that only faster growth and more job opportunities can turn things around. South Africa’s unemployment rate remains among the highest in the world and far worse than that of countries with similar income levels.
