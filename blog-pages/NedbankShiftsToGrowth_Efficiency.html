Business Restructure Done, Nedbank Shifts Focus to Growth and Efficiency

August 5, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Nedbank has completed its organisational restructuring as it works to boost profitability in the face of a difficult economic climate. CEO Jason Quinn confirmed that the changes to its Retail and Business Banking and Nedbank Wealth divisions were finalised on schedule.

The restructuring focused on streamlining the bank’s operations by bringing together its personal and business banking units into more focused clusters. Since July 1, 2025, the newly formed Personal and Private Banking division has been offering services to a wide range of clients—from young individuals to high net worth customers. Meanwhile, the Business and Commercial Banking division now handles services for small to medium enterprises (SMEs), commercial clients, and mid-sized corporates.

According to Quinn, the response to these changes has been positive across the board—from employees to clients and investors. With leadership positions now filled, the bank’s focus is shifting toward execution. This includes unlocking new growth opportunities and improving both efficiency and productivity. He also took the time to thank all Nedbank staff for their dedication and resilience throughout the restructuring process.

This update was shared alongside Nedbank’s financial results for the first half of 2025, which reflected the pressure of a tough operating environment. Global uncertainty, largely due to US tariffs and ongoing geopolitical tensions, has led to market instability and reduced business confidence. In South Africa, the slow pace of economic recovery due to issues like poor municipal services, water shortages, and ongoing problems with crime and corruption has made matters worse.

Despite this, the bank managed to grow its corporate lending by 8.1% as of June. However, household credit growth remained sluggish at just 3.1%, even though interest rates have been lower since September 2024. Still, Nedbank’s headline earnings rose by 6% to R8.4 billion, and return on equity (ROE) edged up to 15.2%. This growth was largely thanks to higher non-interest income, improved management of credit impairments, and tight control over costs.

With its balance sheet in good shape, Nedbank announced a 6% increase in its interim dividend, bringing it to 1,028 cents per share.

Looking ahead, the global economic outlook remains uncertain. US tariffs are expected to continue weighing on business confidence, investment, and global trade. Locally, South Africa might see some economic improvement thanks to higher real incomes, reduced inflation, and lower interest rates. However, challenges like the steep 30% tariffs on exports to the US, weak global growth, and soft commodity prices could hold back business confidence and investment.

Nedbank expects South Africa’s GDP to grow by just 1% in 2025 and 1.5% in 2026, though it acknowledges there are risks to even these modest forecasts. Following a 25-basis-point rate cut in July 2025, the bank expects interest rates to remain steady for now.

Given the tough economic environment, Nedbank has adjusted its outlook for 2025. It now expects diluted headline earnings per share to grow in the low single digits and anticipates ROE will end the year at around 15%. In the longer term, the bank aims to improve ROE to 17% in the medium term and above 18% further down the line.
