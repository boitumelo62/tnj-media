Media G20 Attendance Confirmed Despite Withdrawals and Fears of Disruptions

November 19, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Forty two nations have now confirmed their attendance at the G20 Summit set to take place in South Africa this weekend. The announcement was intended to project confidence ahead of the major global gathering, but it comes at a time when the summit has been overshadowed by the controversy of countries pulling out.

The absence of the United States remains the most prominent, but diplomatic officials say at least three other nations quietly withdrew from participation over the past month. Some of these withdrawals stem from concerns about South Africa’s direction on global issues, while others relate to shifting alliances and pressure linked to disputes in international trade and security. Government representatives have attempted to minimise the impact of these withdrawals, insisting that the summit still captures a broad global consensus.

Analysts say however that the absence of key states has created questions regarding South Africa’s standing on the world stage. Critics argue that the withdrawals point to deeper unease about South Africa’s political stability and its diplomatic balance between global powers.

The summit is also expected to face pressure from local developments. Civil society organisations have announced a large women’s march for the same day as the G20 opening. Organisers say the march is meant to highlight ongoing failures to address gender based violence and the lack of investment in safer public spaces. They insist the demonstration will be peaceful but warn that they intend to be visible to both the government and international observers.

Security agencies are now preparing for two parallel events that will require heavy coordination. The presence of heads of state demands strict protection measures, while the planned march will bring thousands of participants through inner city routes. Officials say contingency plans are being drawn up to avoid clashes or disruptions, but final details will only be released closer to the weekend.

Residents are bracing for traffic restrictions, road closures, and delayed access to key routes. Some hospitality businesses have expressed concern that uncertainty around demonstrations and security could affect bookings and access for guests.

Despite the tensions, government leaders remain confident that the summit will proceed smoothly. They say they welcome peaceful protest and believe the city can accommodate both international diplomacy and national activism. Observers expect however that the interaction between global politics, local frustration, and tightened security will shape the tone and public perception of the event.
