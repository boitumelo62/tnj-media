Media Government Moves Closer to Naming New SARS Commissioner

February 6, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa is nearing the appointment of a new commissioner for the South African Revenue Service, with Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana indicating that the process could be concluded before the end of February.

Godongwana said the selection process is at an advanced stage, following interviews conducted by an independent panel tasked with identifying suitable candidates for the role. The panel is chaired by former finance minister Nhlanhla Nene and has already assessed a wide pool of applicants.

While the panel identified a strong candidate during the initial round of interviews, the process was expanded to include additional headhunted candidates. According to Godongwana, this was done to ensure a broader and more competitive shortlist before a final recommendation is made.

The panel’s recommendation will be submitted to President Cyril Ramaphosa, who is responsible for making the final appointment. The position has drawn significant interest, given the central role SARS plays in revenue collection and the broader fiscal health of the country.

The appointment will mark a leadership transition following the departure of current commissioner Edward Kieswetter, who has led the institution since 2019. Kieswetter’s tenure focused on stabilising SARS and rebuilding public confidence after years of institutional decline. His contract was previously extended to allow sufficient time for a smooth succession process.

Speaking during a visit to SARS headquarters, both Godongwana and Ramaphosa highlighted the importance of continuity at the tax authority, particularly as South Africa navigates ongoing economic pressures. SARS remains a critical engine for funding public services and supporting government priorities.

Among those believed to be under consideration is a senior SARS executive with extensive experience in tax administration and organisational leadership. However, officials have stressed that no final decision has yet been made.

As the end of February approaches, attention will remain firmly on the outcome of the process, with business, labour, and government stakeholders keen to see who will steer the revenue service into its next phase.
