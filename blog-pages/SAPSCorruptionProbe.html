Media Police Corruption Probe Faces Another Setback

October 14, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Parliament’s ad hoc committee investigating corruption within the South African Police Service has encountered another obstacle, as several Members of Parliament were asked to recuse themselves due to potential conflicts of interest.

Committee Chairperson Nkhensani Maseko confirmed that the recusal requests followed revelations from yesterday’s session, where evidence suggested that some MPs had past professional or financial links to individuals under investigation.

Yesterday’s hearings exposed further irregularities in procurement processes within the police’s Crime Intelligence Division, including contracts awarded without proper oversight and payments made to inactive suppliers. The committee also heard testimony alleging that senior officers accepted “facilitation fees” to fast-track tenders for security equipment.

These discoveries have renewed calls for an independent inquiry, with civil society groups warning that parliamentary oversight alone may be insufficient to address entrenched corruption within the service.

Maseko expressed concern over the repeated disruptions but maintained that the committee would continue its work. “We cannot allow vested interests to derail accountability. The public deserves answers,” she said.

The committee is expected to resume its hearings later this week after the Parliamentary Ethics Committee finalises its review of the members in question.
