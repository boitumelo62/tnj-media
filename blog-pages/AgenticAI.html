Tech News Agentic AI’s Race Against Time and It’s Losing

August 4, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

AI is moving so quickly right now that it’s getting harder and harder for ambitious projects especially those involving agentic AI to keep up. By the time a team plans, builds, and tests something, there’s a good chance the tech has already leapt ahead or the market has shifted in a different direction. It’s like building a cutting-edge airplane just to discover mid-way that the world now travels by teleportation.

This idea that many agentic AI projects will be obsolete before they mature is powerful and, frankly, makes more sense the more you think about it. One of the core reasons is what you might call a “failure of corporate imagination.” Big companies often want safe, predictable results. But building advanced AI systems especially agentic ones that can take initiative, make decisions, and adapt on their own requires vision, experimentation, and a high tolerance for risk. Most corporations just aren’t wired that way.

Another big issue is that these projects are trying to apply AI to incredibly complex business cases too early. Companies are throwing every tangled process and data stream at these models, hoping they’ll untangle everything and spit out magic. But AI isn’t quite there yet. You might spend months tailoring a model to your company’s needs, only to find that your data’s a mess, your market has changed, and the model is already outdated.

Add to that the usual culprits bad data quality, ballooning costs, weak oversight, and a lack of measurable value. Gartner pointed out that these are the same reasons other overhyped technologies like early CRM systems or big data platforms flopped in the past. It’s history repeating itself, just with more GPU cycles and smarter software.

Even though I’m genuinely excited about where AI is going and how it will reshape industries, I’m also realistic. There will be lots of mistakes, half-baked initiatives, and expensive failures. That’s why conversations with seasoned founders and executive coaches are so valuable. They’ve seen these cycles before. They know the difference between hype and substance.

So, will “obsolescence before maturity” be the legacy of many current agentic AI efforts? It’s starting to feel that way. Many teams are running as fast as they can just to stay in place on a treadmill powered by hype, with unclear goals and fragile plans. The solution probably lies in more imaginative, adaptive project design but unfortunately, most companies don’t reward that kind of thinking. Too often, these projects aren’t really about solving meaningful business problems.

They’re more about signaling innovation. “Look, we’re doing AI too!” It’s more buzz than business. And without a solid foundation, even the flashiest AI initiatives risk collapsing under their own weight. Where do you stand on all this? Do you think there’s a way to escape the cycle, or are we just watching the latest version of tech chasing its own tail?
