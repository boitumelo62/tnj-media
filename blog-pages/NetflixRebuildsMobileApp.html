Media Netflix Rebuilds Its Mobile App Around Short Vertical Video

January 26, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Netflix is planning a major redesign of its mobile app, with short-form vertical video set to play a central role when the update launches in late 2026. The move was revealed during the company’s fourth quarter 2025 investor call and reflects a broader push toward mobile-first viewing.

Chief financial officer Spencer Neumann said Netflix has been testing vertical video features for around six months. The current mobile experience already includes a vertical feed filled with clips from Netflix series and films, and this is expected to expand. Future additions could include clips tied to new formats such as video podcasts, as the company experiments with how audiences discover content on their phones.

According to Neumann, the changes are part of a wider overhaul of Netflix’s mobile user interface. The goal is to build an app that can support the company’s growth and evolving business over the next decade, following a similar refresh of its TV interface. The redesigned mobile experience is scheduled to roll out later in 2026.

The shift comes as competition for attention intensifies. Short, vertical video popularised by social media platforms has changed viewing habits, forcing traditional broadcasters and streamers to rethink how they keep users engaged on mobile devices.

Netflix is not the only streaming service embracing short-form content. Earlier this month, Hong Kong-based Viu introduced a vertical video section in its mobile app, offering one- to three-minute micro-dramas across markets including Africa, Southeast Asia and the Middle East. Viu South Africa country manager Elouise Kelly said the move was driven by clear changes in consumer behaviour influenced by platforms like TikTok and the growing popularity of professionally produced short-form shows.

Short clips are only one part of Netflix’s wider strategy to increase engagement. Gaming continues to grow on the platform, with plans for cloud gaming and live gaming events. Live sports are also expanding, following additions in 2025 such as WWE Monday Night Raw, NFL games on Christmas Day and major boxing events. Planned 2026 highlights include Major League Baseball and World Cup related analysis.

Despite these additions, Netflix says its core focus on series and films remains unchanged. Co-CEO Ted Sarandos said the company’s bid for Warner Bros Discovery is aimed at strengthening its film and television business, with newer formats designed to complement, not replace, traditional content.
