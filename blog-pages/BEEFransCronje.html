Business Public Opinion Turning Against BEE, Says Frans Cronje

November 19, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Dr. Frans Cronje argues that South Africa needs to rethink some of its long standing economic policies, especially black economic empowerment and the Expropriation Act. Speaking on Mike Sham’s State of the Nation podcast, he said the country actually has the potential to grow far faster, but its own policies are slowing it down. According to him, the economy is stuck at about 1% growth when it should realistically be closer to 5%, and government rules are a big part of the problem.

Cronje believes BEE has become a kind of upfront tax on anyone wanting to invest in South Africa, because companies are expected to hand over part of their ownership before they can operate. He says this makes many projects financially unworkable, so investors choose other markets instead. He also pointed to the new Expropriation Act, saying that it allows the state to take property without paying full market value, and that this creates uncertainty and room for political abuse rather than genuinely helping land reform.

He noted comments from ANC spokesperson Mahlengi Bhengu Motsiri, who said businesses that dislike the country’s policies are free to invest elsewhere. For Cronje, this attitude reinforces why foreign and local investment has been declining.

Cronje also said the national mood around BEE has shifted significantly. He mentioned President Ramaphosa’s recent remarks that any changes to BEE should go through Parliament, which Cronje found surprising given how central empowerment has always been to the ANC. Today, he said, even people close to the ANC admit that BEE is holding back economic growth. A poll he referenced showed that 80% of South Africans feel that companies that already create jobs and pay tax should not be burdened with extra empowerment requirements.

Because of this shift in public opinion, Cronje believes the ANC risks falling out of step with its own supporters if it refuses to acknowledge the problem. He described investor confidence as having halved over the last twenty years, which he sees as evidence of how damaging these policies have become.

His view is that South Africa will only unlock strong economic growth if it removes barriers to investment, particularly BEE requirements and the Expropriation Act. Doing so, he said, would encourage local businesses to expand and make the country far more attractive to international investors as well.
