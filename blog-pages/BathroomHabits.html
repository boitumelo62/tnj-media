Lifestyle The Bathroom Habits That Could Be Harming Your Health

September 13, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Most people don’t give much thought to how they use the toilet, but everyday bathroom habits may be doing more harm than we realise. According to Dr. Jonathan Redelinghuys, many of us are unintentionally placing strain on our bodies, especially our bladder and pelvic floor, simply by the way we pee or sit. One of the biggest mistakes is pushing or straining to urinate in an effort to speed things up. This tightens the pelvic muscles and can prevent the bladder from emptying fully, which raises the risk of infections and even pelvic organ prolapse over time.

Women often make another common error hovering over public toilets instead of sitting down. Though it seems hygienic, it prevents pelvic muscles from relaxing properly, which again stops the bladder from fully emptying. The best posture is sitting fully with feet flat on the ground. Another habit to avoid is going to the toilet “just in case.” It may feel like a smart move before a long trip or movie, but doing it too often can actually confuse the bladder, making it send false urgency signals even when it’s not full. Rushing through a pee is also risky. The bladder has a muscle called the detrusor that needs time to fully contract and empty out the urine.

Hurrying this process can leave behind urine, increasing the risk of infection. Posture matters here too sitting upright or slightly forward, breathing normally, and relaxing the arms can help. After peeing, waiting a few seconds and trying again a method called "double voiding" is especially helpful, particularly for older adults. Simple practices like wiping front to back also help prevent infection, especially in women. One unexpected danger zone? Your phone. Many people take their phones into the bathroom, turning quick trips into long scrolling sessions.

Studies show that people who use their phones on the toilet are significantly more likely to develop haemorrhoids. Sitting too long on a hard seat puts pressure on the rectal veins, weakening tissue and causing swelling. The posture of scrolling leaning forward with your head down adds strain on your spine and neck, and weakens pelvic muscles over time. This increases the chance of issues like urinary leaks, constipation, and pelvic discomfort.

Plus, when your brain is distracted, your body doesn’t eliminate waste as efficiently, leading people to strain unnecessarily further harming the pelvic floor. The takeaway is simple, sit properly, relax, don’t rush, go only when needed, and keep the phone out of the bathroom. Small adjustments now can prevent years of bladder and pelvic health problems later.
