Media Madlanga Judicial Commission of Inquiry Officially Begins

September 17, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Madlanga Judicial Commission of Inquiry officially commenced today, marking a major step toward addressing corruption allegations within South Africa’s criminal justice system.

The inquiry is chaired by retired Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga, a respected former Constitutional Court judge known for his rulings on constitutional rights and accountability. The commission’s mandate is to investigate claims of systemic corruption affecting key institutions, including the police, prosecuting authority, and correctional services, sectors that have faced criticism for years over state capture-era scandals and failures to prosecute high-profile cases.

Justice Madlanga emphasised the importance of transparency, stating that the commission’s work aims to restore public trust in a justice system that many believe has been compromised. Public hearings are expected to run for several months, with evidence from whistle-blowers, law enforcement officials, and civil society organisations forming a crucial part of the process.

This commission follows a series of similar inquiries in recent years, including the Zondo Commission into state capture, but is more narrowly focused on the functioning and integrity of the criminal justice system itself. Government officials have urged citizens to follow proceedings closely, describing the inquiry as a “critical moment” for rebuilding faith in South Africa’s legal institutions.
