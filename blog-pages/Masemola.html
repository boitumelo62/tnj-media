“The Gaps Are Starting to Show”: Day 3 Wrap

The Madlanga Commission of Inquiry entered its fourth day yesterday in Pretoria, with National Police Commissioner General Fannie Masemola under the spotlight. His testimony centred on the controversial disbandment of the KwaZulu-Natal Political Killings Task Team, which had been set up to investigate a spate of politically motivated murders in the province.

Masemola told the commission that the order from Police Minister Senzo Mchunu to dissolve the unit was, in his view, unlawful and an “encroachment” on the powers of the national commissioner. He insisted that the task team had been delivering results, pointing to more than 600 dockets it had handled, with nearly half of them finalised.

Despite describing the directive as unlawful, Masemola admitted that he ultimately complied with it. He explained that political pressure left him with little room to resist, even though he initially tried to negotiate a phased approach rather than an abrupt shutdown. Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga pressed him on the apparent contradiction. “You say the instruction was unlawful. So why obey it if you’ve told us you would never follow such an order?” the judge asked.

Masemola responded that government officials often faced impossible choices. “When a minister is insistent, you are forced into a corner. I said, ‘If we do it your way, I will not accept, so let’s rather do it my way.’ But to flatly refuse would have had consequences. No commissioner would survive that.” At another point he stressed that, in principle, he would never obey an unlawful order. “If it is very clear that it is not within the minister’s powers, then I will not do it,” he said.

The commissioner also revealed that he raised his concerns directly with President Cyril Ramaphosa earlier this year, warning that the decision to scrap the unit had “serious implications” for ongoing prosecutions and investigations.

The day’s testimony highlighted the growing tension between political authority and the operational independence of the police. It raised difficult questions about whether senior officials can realistically defy ministers when instructions cross legal or constitutional boundaries.

Day 5 of the hearings is already underway today, with the commission expected to push Masemola further on the fallout from the disbandment and the impact it has had on criminal cases in KwaZulu-Natal. Allegations of senior police figures having links to organised crime are also likely to come under the microscope as proceedings continue.
