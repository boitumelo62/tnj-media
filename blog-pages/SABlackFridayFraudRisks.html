Business South Africans Urged to Shop Safely as Black Friday Fraud Risks Rise

November 28, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Standard Bank and Nedbank are urging South Africans to stay alert this Black Friday as scams typically spike during this busy shopping period. Black Friday, which falls on 28 November 2025, is one of the biggest retail days of the year, and with many people still feeling financial pressure, the promise of big discounts on essentials and luxury items is especially appealing. It’s also a chance for early Christmas shoppers to get their gifts at lower prices.

But with the rush of online and in store activity, cybercriminals take advantage of distracted shoppers. Dr Belinda Rathogwa from Standard Bank says this time of year always sees an increase in scams, and she encourages shoppers to stay cautious. She reminds people to keep their PINs and passwords private, stick to trusted websites, avoid using public Wi-Fi for payments, and watch out for fake links or websites. She also suggests using a virtual card for online shopping and never approving a payment you didn’t start yourself.

Nedbank shares the same concerns and advises its clients to be extra careful. They stress the importance of never sharing banking details over the phone or through links sent in messages or emails. They encourage clients to read banking notifications closely, use virtual cards for once off payments, and immediately report any lost devices or suspicious activity. They also recommend researching unfamiliar retailers, checking reviews before buying, and being wary of deals that seem too good to be true.

Meanwhile, early data shows that this year’s Black Friday is shaping up to be strong. Discovery Bank says its clients’ spending for November 2025 is already ahead of both 2023 and 2024. The average number of transactions per client is up 5% from last year and 13% from 2023, while average spending per client has risen by 3%.

In store shopping patterns have stayed fairly consistent, with the same merchants remaining at the top. Online, however, there are signs of change as more South Africans are turning to international platforms, hinting at a more global mix of purchases.

Real time spending data from Discovery Bank shows that by 5:00 am on Black Friday, clients had already spent an average of R872. Johannesburg shoppers were spending the most, averaging R965, followed by Cape Town at R906 and Durban at R880. One Discovery Bank client even made a single purchase of just under R100,000 in the early hours of the morning.
