Media Parliament’s Ad Hoc Committee Resumes Police Corruption Hearings

October 8, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Parliamentary Ad Hoc Committee on Police Corruption has officially resumed its hearings following delays caused by concerns over the testimony of KwaZulu-Natal Police Commissioner, Lieutenant General Nhlahla Mkhwanazi.

The committee, established earlier this year, was formed to probe long-standing allegations of corruption, political interference, and organized criminal activity within the national police structure. Its mandate, sanctioned by Parliament, includes examining networks of influence between senior police officials, politicians, and private entities that may have enabled systemic corruption within law enforcement.

According to sources close to the inquiry, the committee’s current focus is on KwaZulu-Natal, a province where several high-profile cases of police corruption and alleged protection rackets have drawn national attention. The committee has been investigating reports of senior officers being implicated in corruption-linked operations, particularly surrounding procurement contracts, policing budgets, and internal accountability procedures.

The recent delay in proceedings stemmed from questions surrounding Mkhwanzi’s incomplete statement. The commissioner’s testimony is expected to shed light on the chain of command and the mechanisms by which information and decisions were allegedly manipulated within the provincial and national policing structures.

Members of the committee, chaired by the Honorable Minister from the Democratic Alliance, stated that Mkhwanzi’s insights are pivotal to connecting several allegations raised in previous hearings. Despite the temporary suspension, the committee now intends to continue its work without further delay, as Parliament pushes to restore integrity and transparency within the police service.

As the hearings resume, the public and media are closely watching for any evidence linking the ongoing corruption scandal to broader political networks—an issue that has been under scrutiny since the committee was first established in response to pressure from civil society and opposition parties.
