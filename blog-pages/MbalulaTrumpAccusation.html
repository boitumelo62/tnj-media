Media Mbalula Hits Back After Trump Accuses South Africa Of Persecution

November 10, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

ANC secretary general Fikile Mbalula has launched a firm response to United States President Donald Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio after they accused South Africa of persecuting white Afrikaners and announced a planned United States boycott of the upcoming G20 Summit in Johannesburg.

The comments from Washington sparked outrage across South Africa’s political space. Trump and Rubio claimed that the country had engaged in targeted discrimination and had failed to protect the interests of white farmers and business owners. Their statements were released shortly after a closed door briefing in Washington, where they labelled South Africa a hostile environment for their diplomatic mission.

Mbalula dismissed the accusations as reckless, saying they were based on misinformation and political grandstanding. He said South Africa remains committed to the protection of all its citizens and operates under a constitution that does not allow discrimination under any circumstances. He argued that the United States leadership is trying to create international tension by spreading a distorted picture of local affairs.

Government officials have echoed this view, noting that the United States has failed to provide evidence to support its claims. They argue that the move to boycott the G20 Summit undermines diplomatic relations and ignores the strong trade and cultural ties between the two nations. Analysts believe the statement from Washington is also tied to internal United States political battles ahead of the next election cycle.

South Africa has insisted that preparations for the G20 Summit will continue as planned and that the absence of the United States delegation will not affect the country’s readiness. Mbalula said South Africa will not be intimidated by pressure from foreign governments and will continue engaging with global partners who approach international cooperation in good faith.

The dispute has now grown into one of the most direct confrontations between the two countries in recent years, raising concerns about whether the United States will escalate its stance or eventually soften it before the summit begins.
