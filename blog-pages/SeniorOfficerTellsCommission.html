Media Senior Officer Tells Commission of Alleged Political Pressure on KZN Police

January 27, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

A senior KwaZulu-Natal police official has told the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry that Police Minister on special leave Senzo Mchunu allegedly interfered in policing matters during an ANC by-election campaign.

Acting Deputy Police Commissioner in KZN Anthony Gopaul testified that the incident took place in March last year while Mchunu was campaigning in KwaDukuza for an ANC by-election. The vote followed the resignation of Vishnu Govender as an ANC ward councillor after he defected to the uMkhonto weSizwe Party. According to Gopaul, Mchunu visited the local police station in Ward 28 while wearing ANC branded clothing and accompanied by individuals dressed in the party’s colours. During the visit, Mchunu allegedly told the station commander that residents had complained police officers were supporting Govender.

At the time, Govender was contesting the by-election as an MK Party candidate. Gopaul said he informed the minister that there were no active criminal cases against Govender and no factual basis for claims that police were acting improperly. Gopaul told the commission that the allegations did not align with the political reality on the ground. He said Govender enjoyed strong community support, which made claims of widespread anger against him difficult to reconcile. Govender went on to win the by-election with 62 percent of the vote.

The testimony has raised concerns about possible political influence over police operations, particularly during an active election campaign. The commission continues to hear evidence relating to alleged misconduct and interference within law enforcement structures.

In related proceedings, KwaZulu-Natal Hawks head Major-General Lesetja Senona is expected to testify before the Madlanga Commission in Pretoria. Senona and his son Thato have been linked to businessman Vusimuzi Cat Matlala, who is facing criminal charges.

The commission has previously heard that Thato Senona had business dealings with Matlala and that the two were involved in discussions around a potential property transaction. Senona is expected to respond to allegations suggesting an inappropriate relationship with Matlala. Evidence presented last year by Crime Intelligence head Dumisani Khumalo included WhatsApp messages between Matlala and Senona. In the exchanges, Matlala allegedly asked Senona to provide FICA documents for his son. In one message, Senona referred to Matlala as brother, prompting questions about a possible corrupt relationship with the businessman accused of exerting influence over the Ekurhuleni Metropolitan Police Department.

Commission spokesperson Jeremy Michaels confirmed that Senona had previously appeared briefly before the inquiry in November and was ordered by Justice Madlanga to return and be ready to testify on Tuesday 27 January.
