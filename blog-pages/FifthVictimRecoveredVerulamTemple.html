Media Fifth Victim Recovered as Verulam Temple Collapse Investigation Deepens

December 15, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Rescue teams have recovered a fifth body from the rubble of the collapsed four storey temple in Verulam north of Durban as authorities continue search and recovery operations at the site. The latest recovery was confirmed several days after the structure gave way trapping worshippers and workers beneath tons of concrete and steel.

Emergency services have been working around the clock since the collapse using heavy machinery and specialised rescue equipment to clear debris while ensuring the safety of personnel on the ground. Officials say the recovery of the fifth victim marks another grim milestone in an incident that has shaken the local community and raised serious questions about building safety and oversight.

KwaZulu Natal disaster management teams confirmed that all recovered bodies have been handed over to forensic services for formal identification and postmortem examinations. Families of the missing have been receiving psychosocial support as they wait for confirmation about their loved ones with counselling services deployed near the scene.

Authorities have not yet confirmed whether there are still people unaccounted for but say efforts will continue until it is deemed safe to conclude operations. Engineers have also been brought in to assess the stability of the remaining structure amid concerns that further collapses could occur.

Preliminary information suggests that the building was undergoing construction or renovation at the time of the incident. This has prompted investigations into whether safety regulations were followed and whether the structure complied with municipal approval and engineering standards. The Department of Labour and local municipality are expected to conduct parallel probes into possible negligence.

Community leaders in Verulam have called for accountability stressing that places of worship should be safe spaces for congregants. They have also urged authorities to speed up investigations and ensure that those responsible are held to account.

As the death toll rises the tragedy has renewed national conversations about construction safety enforcement and the consequences of non-compliance. Officials have promised that a full report will be made public once investigations are concluded.
