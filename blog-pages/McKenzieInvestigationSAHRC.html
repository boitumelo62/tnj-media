Media SAHRC Investigates Allegations Against Minister McKenzie

August 19, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The South African Human Rights Commission (SAHRC) has launched an investigation into allegations of racism against Sport, Arts and Culture Minister Gayton McKenzie. The inquiry relates to derogatory remarks he is alleged to have made more than two decades ago, which have resurfaced in recent weeks.

According to the SAHRC, the decision to proceed was taken after receiving multiple public complaints. The commission said the investigation will be conducted in line with its constitutional mandate to promote and protect human rights. It will focus on establishing whether the alleged statements fall within the definition of hate speech or discrimination as set out in South African law.

The process will include reviewing the historical remarks attributed to McKenzie, considering their social and legal context, and inviting submissions from affected parties. McKenzie will also be given the opportunity to respond formally to the allegations as part of the inquiry.

The Department of Sport, Arts and Culture has confirmed that it is aware of the investigation. While no official statement has yet been released by the minister, sources within the department indicated that McKenzie intends to cooperate with the commission.

The SAHRC has not placed a timeline on the process but confirmed that a report will be issued once all evidence has been reviewed and findings have been finalised. The commission emphasised that its role is not to determine political outcomes but to assess whether the remarks, if proven, constitute a violation of constitutional protections.

This development has drawn widespread public attention, given McKenzie's high profile as a cabinet minister and long history in South African politics. The commission urged the public to allow the investigation to proceed without interference or speculation until its work is complete.
