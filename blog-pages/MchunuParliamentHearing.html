Media Mchunu to Face MPs Again as Ad Hoc Committee Probes Police Interference Allegations

October 21, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Embattled Police Minister Senzo Mchunu will return to Parliament on Tuesday to conclude his testimony before the ad hoc committee investigating allegations of police corruption and interference.

Mchunu is expected to face further questions from Members of Parliament as he continues to defend his decision to disband the political killings task team. The minister has been under scrutiny over whether the directive was influenced by individuals allegedly implicated in criminal activity.

Currently on a leave of absence, Mchunu has firmly denied any involvement in a criminal conspiracy or attempts to divert attention from organised crime through his actions. Appearing before the committee last week, he insisted that his decision to dissolve the task team was lawful and made within his constitutional powers.

Mchunu also rejected claims made by KwaZulu-Natal Police Commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi, who previously told the committee that the minister’s actions undermined efforts to combat politically motivated killings in the province.

“I know that, at the same time, statements have been made in this committee, and I’m no criminal. General Mkhwanazi knows in his conscience that I’m not a criminal and I have nothing to do with criminal syndicates,” Mchunu said.

On Tuesday, the committee is expected to continue examining recorded audio exchanges between Mchunu’s chief of staff, Cedric Nkabinde, and Mkhwanazi, in which the KZN police commissioner allegedly made threats against Nkabinde. The recordings are seen as central to determining whether there was interference in the work of the police and its task teams.
