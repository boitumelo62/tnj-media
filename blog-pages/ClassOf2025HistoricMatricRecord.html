Media Class of 2025 Sets Historic Matric Record for South Africa

January 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa has recorded its highest matric pass rate since the introduction of the current national examinations, with the Class of 2025 achieving a national pass rate of just over 88 percent. Education officials have described the outcome as a landmark moment for the schooling system, pointing to sustained improvements in teaching support, curriculum management and learner resilience.

KwaZulu Natal emerged as the top performing province, recording a pass rate of 90.6 percent. The province showed notable gains in mathematics, physical sciences and technical subjects, reflecting targeted interventions aimed at improving subject mastery and learner confidence. Other provinces also reported steady improvements, narrowing historical performance gaps.

The Department of Basic Education acknowledged that the results were achieved despite ongoing challenges such as infrastructure backlogs, overcrowded classrooms and socioeconomic pressures faced by many learners. Officials credited the commitment of teachers, school management teams and parents, particularly in under resourced communities, for maintaining academic focus throughout the year.

Education analysts say the results strengthen confidence in the public schooling system and could have long term benefits for higher education and skills development. Universities and colleges are expected to see increased enrolment numbers, while employers may benefit from a larger pool of matriculants meeting minimum entry requirements.

Learners across the country have celebrated the achievement, with many describing it as validation of years of hard work under demanding conditions. Government has indicated that the focus will now shift toward improving quality outcomes, ensuring that higher pass rates translate into stronger literacy, numeracy and critical thinking skills.
