Business BHP Backs Off Fresh Takeover Bid for Anglo American

November 24, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

BHP Group has decided to step back from a new takeover attempt of Anglo American, bringing an abrupt end to what was shaping up to be a brief and unexpected effort to disrupt Anglo’s planned merger with Canada’s Teck Resources. The company confirmed on Monday that it had held early talks with Anglo but was no longer considering a deal and would instead focus on the assets it already owns. This followed a Bloomberg report that BHP had quietly approached Anglo again, after failing with a similar attempt last year.

People familiar with the matter say Anglo reviewed the latest proposal but ultimately rejected it, concluding it didn’t offer a better outcome than the company’s ongoing plan to merge with Teck. Although Anglo is now headquartered in London, its roots go back to 1917 in South Africa, where it started as a gold miner before expanding into diamonds, coal, and other minerals. Today, its only South African operations are through Kumba Iron Ore, after spinning off Anglo American Platinum now Valterra Platinum earlier this year.

BHP’s renewed interest highlights the pressure big miners are facing as they look for growth, especially in copper. Supply of the metal has been tightening while demand is expected to rise sharply as global electrification accelerates. The timing is significant: shareholders of both Anglo and Teck are preparing to vote in just a few weeks on their own plan to merge into a company valued at more than $60 billion.

News of the abandoned approach initially lifted BHP shares in Sydney, though most of the gains faded by the close. Some investors were taken aback, partly because CEO Mike Henry has repeatedly said the company was focused on strengthening its existing portfolio after last year’s failed bid. Analysts say the move raises questions about BHP’s broader strategy and its ability to secure high quality copper assets, which are scarce and increasingly expensive.

Observers note that the renewed bid shows how challenging it has become to pursue major copper deals. BHP’s latest proposal was said to be simpler than last year’s offer, which would have required Anglo to break itself up. Anglo’s recent exit from its South African platinum business may also have made it more attractive as a target. Still, since BHP’s last attempt, its own share price has slipped while Anglo’s has climbed, and the Teck deal has gained solid backing from Anglo shareholders.

Some analysts believe BHP saw a narrow opening to try again but ultimately found the environment too difficult. They say the company will now need to concentrate on progressing its major copper projects, including the huge Escondida mine in Chile, developments in Argentina, and operations in South Australia. In its statement, BHP maintained that a merger with Anglo would have created significant value but said it remains confident in the potential of its own long term growth plans.

Anglo declined to comment. Shareholders of Anglo and Teck will vote on 9 December, and the deal still needs approval from regulators in several countries, including China, the United States and Canada.
