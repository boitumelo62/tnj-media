Media Family Brings Body to Bank in Funeral Claim Dispute

February 19, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Capitec Bank has responded to a disturbing incident in which a grieving family allegedly carried the body of a deceased relative into a bank branch in Stanger, KwaDukuza, KwaZulu-Natal, in an attempt to validate a funeral insurance claim.

The incident reportedly occurred on Wednesday morning and led to the temporary closure of the branch as shocked staff and customers struggled to process what they had witnessed. According to reports, the family believed they had exhausted all other options after struggling to have their claim processed and decided to physically present the body as proof of death.

The deceased was reportedly wrapped in blue plastic, and images and videos of the scene quickly spread across social media platforms. The footage sparked widespread outrage and debate about the challenges families face when navigating funeral insurance claims during periods of grief.

In a statement, Capitec Bank expressed regret and offered condolences to the family. The bank confirmed it is working with authorities to handle the matter with sensitivity and respect.

“We are deeply saddened by the incident that occurred at our Stanger branch. We extend our sincere condolences to the family during this difficult time and apologise to those affected. We are engaging with the authorities to ensure all processes are followed with care, dignity and respect,” the bank said.

Capitec also reassured customers that funeral policies remain valid and secure. The bank explained that payouts are dependent on the submission of required documentation, including official death certificates issued by the Department of Home Affairs.

“Once we receive all the correct documents, we process payouts as swiftly as possible. Our team works to ensure families get the support they need without delays,” the statement added.

The incident has renewed scrutiny of funeral insurance practices in South Africa, where such policies are often critical for low- and middle-income households. Funeral cover is widely used to ensure families can afford burial costs, which can be significant and often must be paid within days.

This is not the first time such a desperate act has been reported. In 2019, a similar case in KwaZulu- Natal involved a family taking a body to an Old Mutual branch after an alleged dispute over a funeral claim. In that case, the insurer later stated that the claim had been paid.

The KwaDukuza incident has highlighted gaps in communication and understanding around claim procedures, as well as the emotional and financial pressures families face when trying to bury loved ones with dignity.
