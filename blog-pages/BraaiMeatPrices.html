Business Braai Still Burns the Pocket as Meat Prices Stay High

August 31, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Meat prices in South Africa are still high, and although economists are hopeful that inflation will cool down over the next few months, it’s unlikely that prices will actually drop anytime soon. Hosting a braai is still more expensive than it was a year ago but there is a bit of relief when you compare prices to last month. This slight improvement is mostly thanks to a drop in vegetable prices between July and August 2025.

According to the latest Braai Index, the cost of putting together a braai fell by about 3% from the previous month. That means some good news for anyone stocking up on sides things like potatoes, spinach, tomatoes, onions, and carrots all saw their prices fall for the second month in a row. So, if you're more into the salads than the steaks, you’re in luck. The Braai Index, which was originally put together by Bloomberg and is now updated monthly using data from the Pietermaritzburg Equity Justice and Dignity group, tracks the cost of common braai ingredients.

It includes meats like beef, chicken, and wors, as well as a variety of vegetables and pantry items like samp, maize, curry powder, and salt. It's a way to measure inflation more specifically through the lens of a typical South African braai. But while vegetables have gotten cheaper, meat is still the biggest pain point. Prices for beef and chicken continue to inch up, although wors has come down a bit, which helps. On the whole, the basket of braai items is still 6.7% more expensive than it was a year ago. That’s better than the 9% jump seen in July, but it’s still high.

Some ingredients are bucking the trend, though. Salt, potatoes, green peppers, and onions are all cheaper than they were this time last year. But others like samp, spinach, carrots, and especially beef have seen steep year on year increases. Beef in particular has been under pressure, largely due to a foot and mouth disease outbreak at one of the country’s biggest feedlots earlier in the year. Stats SA’s own data lines up with this trend. In July, food and non-alcoholic beverages rose by 5.2%, with meat alone jumping 10.5%. Over the last year, beef prices have soared stewing beef has gone from around R94.80 per kilo in July 2024 to R123.87 in July 2025. Mince also shot up from R102.95 to R126.79 in the same period.

These price hikes aren’t expected to reverse in the short term. Even though inflation should ease a bit by the end of the year, there’s no sign that meat prices will actually come down. Recent producer price index data also suggests that prices for live animals are still rising up 18.4% year on year, and potentially going over 20% due to last year’s low base. While grain prices have remained more stable thanks to good harvests and oversupply meat still carries more weight in consumer inflation. And as long as meat stays expensive, both the CPI and the Braai Index will likely remain elevated.
