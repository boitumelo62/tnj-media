Business Coca-Cola HBC Moves to Take Control of African Bottling Giant

October 21, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Coca-Cola HBC AG is set to buy a majority stake in Coca-Cola Beverages Africa (CCBA) for $2.6 billion, which is about R45 billion. This move will make Coca-Cola HBC the second largest bottling partner for Coca-Cola by volume worldwide. Coca-Cola HBC, which started in Greece and is now based in Switzerland, already operates in 29 countries across three continents including Nigeria and Egypt in Africa.

By acquiring 75% of Johannesburg based CCBA, it’s adding 14 more African countries to its reach. With this deal, Coca-Cola HBC will now handle about two thirds of the Coca-Cola system’s total volume across the continent and cover more than half of Africa’s population. They’re buying this majority stake from Coca-Cola Co. in the U.S. and Gutsche Family Investments (GFI). There’s also an option for Coca-Cola HBC to buy the remaining stake within the next six years. This transaction is part of a broader move by Coca-Cola Co. to step away from the bottling side of the business.

Earlier this year, they also sold a stake in their Indian bottling operations. The current deal is expected to be completed by the end of next year. To give some context, CCBA was formed in 2015 after a merger of several Coca-Cola bottling operations in Southern and Eastern Africa those of Coca-Cola itself, SABMiller, and GFI’s Coca-Cola Fortune. The merger was approved in late 2015, and CCBA officially started operating in July 2016. A year later, after SABMiller merged with Anheuser Busch InBev (AB InBev), Coca-Cola Co. acquired AB InBev’s stake in CCBA.

Currently, Coca-Cola owns 66.5% of CCBA, and GFI holds the rest. Back in 2021, Coca-Cola and CCBA said they were planning to take the company public through a stock market listing, but now it looks like the sale to Coca-Cola HBC is the route they’ve chosen instead. As part of this new agreement, Coca-Cola HBC also plans to have a secondary listing on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange. This move is meant to signal its long term commitment to South Africa and the wider African region.

However, the news of this large deal comes at a time when Coca-Cola Beverages South Africa (CCBSA) a subsidiary of CCBA is facing serious internal challenges. Reports surfaced recently that the company might cut up to 680 jobs and shut down some of its plants in South Africa. Although a final decision hasn't been made, the company has confirmed that it’s consulting on possible job cuts due to financial pressures. According to the Food and Allied Workers Union (FAWU), the proposed job losses would mostly affect cleaning staff who make up a crucial part of the food and beverage production process.

The planned closures would impact facilities in places like Bloemfontein and East London. FAWU says it was informed through a formal retrenchment notice and noted that this move could affect about 9% of the company’s 7,700 employees in South Africa. While Coca-Cola Beverages South Africa has mentioned offering separation packages as an alternative to forced layoffs, the overall message is that the company is preparing to make tough restructuring decisions to adapt to changing industry conditions.
