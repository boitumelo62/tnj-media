Business SARS Targets Social Media Influencers in Tax Crackdown

September 9, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The South African Revenue Service has confirmed that social media influencers are now included in its focus areas for tax compliance. Influencers join other groups such as gig economy workers and government entities under a broader strategy to close gaps in the system.

SARS has stressed that all forms of income must be declared, whether they are cash payments, sponsored products, free services, or travel perks. The agency warned that even non-cash rewards are considered taxable and should be recorded in annual returns.

To help improve compliance, SARS is rolling out educational campaigns aimed specifically at digital creators. These include videos, webinars, and seminars designed to explain tax obligations and reporting requirements. Commissioner Edward Kieswetter has encouraged influencers to cooperate, noting that the agency is prepared to assist taxpayers who engage openly and honestly.

Tax experts say many influencers fall short not because of dishonesty but because of a lack of understanding of the rules. For example, a free meal offered in exchange for an online post counts as income. However, unsolicited gifts such as free products or tickets with no agreement attached may not need to be declared.

The move shows SARS’s intention to strengthen oversight of the digital economy. Accountants recommend that influencers keep detailed records of all earnings and benefits, and seek professional advice where necessary to avoid penalties.
