Business South Africa Earns Rare Credit Rating Boost as Rand Eases After Strong Rally

November 17, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The rand slipped slightly after hitting its strongest level in more than two years, as traders took some profits and waited cautiously for S&P Global’s review of South Africa’s credit rating. By Friday, the currency was trading at 17.17 to the dollar, about 0.7% weaker than the previous day.

That same day, South Africa received some encouraging news, S&P Global delivered the country’s first credit rating upgrade in almost twenty years. This marked a shift in sentiment after all three major agencies had pushed South Africa into “junk” status back in 2020. The upgrade reflects growing confidence in the country’s efforts to improve its economy and fix long standing structural problems.Attention now turns to the week ahead, which brings important economic updates. On Wednesday, October’s consumer inflation numbers and September’s retail sales figures are expected, followed by the Reserve Bank’s interest rate decision on Thursday.

Economists at Nedbank believe the environment is favourable enough for the Monetary Policy Committee to cut interest rates by 25 basis points, which they say would bring the real policy rate closer to neutral. By Monday, 17 November, the rand was trading at R17.11 to the dollar, R22.50 to the pound and R19.85 to the euro. Oil prices were slightly lower at $63.80 per barrel.South Africa has also seen a surge in foreign investment.

The FTSE/JSE Top 40 has climbed 55% in dollar terms, strongly outperforming both emerging market and developed market indices. This performance shows renewed optimism toward South African assets.New rules are also coming for motorists. Anyone who loses their driving licence under the country’s new demerit system will have to undergo driving simulator tests and psychological evaluations as part of a rehabilitation programme. This forms part of the Aarto Act, which targets frequent offenders who exceed the points limit.

Meanwhile, Gauteng endured a Level 9 storm over the weekend that uprooted trees, flooded roads and left many areas without power. The severe weather comes shortly after Amnesty International warned about rising flood risks in the country. Political tensions are also simmering, with some voices inside the ANC calling for President Cyril Ramaphosa to be removed, the NEC dissolved and a temporary leadership structure formed under former president Thabo Mbeki.

Despite the stormy weather and political noise, the country ended the week with a major win. South Africa’s first credit rating upgrade in almost two decades. S&P Global lifted the foreign currency long term rating from “BB” to “BB”, highlighting stronger growth prospects and a healthier fiscal outlook, supported in part by better performance at Eskom.
