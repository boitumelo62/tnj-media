Media Woolworths Trials Self-Service Checkout as Retail Automation Expands in South Africa

February 18, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Woolworths has begun testing its first self-service checkout till at a Cape Town store, marking a significant step in the company’s push toward retail automation and digital transformation. The pilot is part of a broader trend among retailers seeking to streamline operations and enhance the customer shopping experience.

Self-service checkout systems allow shoppers to scan and pay for items without the assistance of a cashier, reducing waiting times and improving store efficiency. For retailers, these systems can help lower operational costs and reallocate staff to other customer-facing roles such as floor assistance and product support.

Woolworths has not yet indicated whether the technology will be rolled out nationwide, stating that the trial phase will be used to assess customer uptake, operational performance, and potential challenges such as shrinkage and system reliability. Retail analysts say the move reflects growing pressure on retailers to modernise in response to changing consumer expectations and rising labour and operating costs.

South African shoppers have increasingly embraced digital retail tools, including mobile apps, contactless payments, and online shopping platforms. Self-service checkout technology aligns with this shift, offering convenience for tech savvy customers who prefer a faster, more independent shopping experience.

However, the introduction of automation in retail has also raised concerns about job security. Labour groups have previously warned that widespread adoption of self-service technologies could reduce the demand for entry level retail workers. Retailers counter that automation can create new roles in technology support, data analytics, and customer service.

Industry experts say Woolworths’ pilot could influence other major retailers to accelerate their own automation strategies. If successful, self-service checkout could become a more common feature in South African supermarkets and department stores, reshaping how consumers interact with physical retail spaces in the years ahead.
