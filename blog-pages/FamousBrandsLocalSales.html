Business Local Sales Lift Famous Brands Amid Global Challenges

October 22, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Famous Brands has made impressive progress over the past six months, opening 70 new restaurants despite a tough retail environment. In the six months leading up to 31 August 2025, the group reported solid growth across its network, completing not only the new store openings but also revamping 103 existing outlets. The group also continued expanding its drive thru offering, adding five more locations and bringing the total to 77. In South Africa, the company’s well known brands like Wimpy, Steers, Debonairs, and Mugg & Bean led the charge with 53 new stores opened during this period.

While 103 stores were revamped and one was converted to a different brand, the group also closed 10 stores. Still, its core brands saw solid sales growth system wide sales were up 6%, and like for like sales rose 2.6%. The return of local tourism and more people heading back to offices helped boost restaurant traffic. The group said that it focused on expanding smaller formats and drive thru models to better meet the growing demand for convenience. However, not all areas of the business performed equally well.

The Signature Brands division which includes more premium restaurants like Turn n Tender, Vovo Telo, and Mythos saw softer results. Six new stores were opened in this category, but 10 were closed. Sales here were slightly down, with a 0.6% drop in like for like sales and a 0.4% dip in total system wide sales. The operating loss margin also widened slightly, from 6.7% to 7.0%, reflecting the ongoing pressure in the premium dining segment. Internationally, performance was mixed. In the SADC region, revenue grew 2.7% to R224 million, but operating profit fell by nearly 12%, and five stores closed while eight opened.

In the Africa and Middle East region, challenges were more pronounced 12 restaurants closed and only one opened. Revenue in this area dropped by 5.4% to R33 million, largely due to tough trading conditions and high inflation. The group is also in the middle of a legal dispute with a franchise partner in the UAE, where six stores have been marked as closed. In Côte d’Ivoire, the company opened a second Debonairs Pizza in Abidjan, taking a more cautious approach to expansion in the region. In the UK, revenue fell 5.7% to R65 million.

Two restaurants were closed and two opened, reflecting an industry still struggling with rising costs, cautious consumer spending, and economic uncertainty. Despite the challenges, Famous Brands has continued to grow its footprint, especially in South Africa, and is adjusting its strategy in more volatile regions. The focus on convenience driven formats like drive thrus and smaller outlets appears to be paying off in the current climate.
