Tech News Inside the Fastest Tech Evolution of Our Lifetime

December 5, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

It’s hard to believe it’s only been three years since ChatGPT first arrived, because the world around it has changed so dramatically. In late 2022, headlines were dominated by the death of Queen Elizabeth, the war in Ukraine and the Depp Heard trial. Generative AI, something many of us now use daily without a second thought, hadn’t even officially launched yet. The idea that a tool on your laptop could draft documents, generate images, write code and answer complex questions in seconds would have sounded impossible.

Yet here we are. The speed of progress has been astonishing. The AI models of 2025 can do things that felt like pure science fiction just a few years ago. A good way to see this evolution is through AI benchmarks those standardised tests that measure reasoning, language, coding and more. Early ChatGPT models struggled with basic maths; today’s ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok 4 are solving graduate level problems and generating impressive visual code like vector illustrations. Even on extremely difficult tests, such as “Humanity’s Last Exam,” which consists of 2 500 of the toughest questions in the world, performance has jumped from about 5% accuracy to around 25% in top models like Grok 4. It’s still not a pass, but it’s a huge leap in such a short time.

Working with AI today means using a whole collection of tools, each with its own strengths. At Flow Communications, we combine models depending on what we need. ChatGPT and Gemini are our everyday generalists, ideal for quick answers and creative brainstorming. Claude is outstanding for writing and reviewing code. 11 Labs produces some of the most natural voice overs we’ve heard. Gemini leads in generating high quality images and video, while DeepSeek is an impressive open source competitor closing the gap with the major proprietary models.

A few big trends are shaping the field. First, modern AI is getting much better at reasoning, not just relying on massive amounts of data but actually breaking down problems step by step. Second, today’s models can use built-in tools, meaning they don’t just give answers, they can perform actions like browsing or writing code. Third, multimodal AI is now the norm we expect a model to understand and generate text, images, audio and video interchangeably. Fourth, efficiency has skyrocketed. What once needed a giant data centre can now run on a laptop, which means AI will soon be everywhere in our phones, appliances and cars. And finally, open source AI is catching up fast. What used to be controlled by a handful of big tech companies is now open for global collaboration, pushing innovation even further.

Looking toward 2026, it’s likely we’ll see the rise of truly agentic AI systems that can take actions on your behalf. These tools might help you shop, manage your admin, handle your banking or run small tasks online without you lifting a finger. Building software will become something anyone can do with simple language prompts. And because the technology keeps getting lighter and more efficient, AI will weave itself into every connected device around us.

In a world where every tool is getting smarter, the most important skill is learning how and when to use them. That’s what we look for when hiring, and it’s what we encourage our clients to focus on as well.
