Media Lifetime Licence Bans Considered for Drunk Driving Offenders

January 22, 2026 ● Tebogo Zongwana

Motorists convicted of drunk driving in South Africa could face lifetime bans from holding a driving licence under proposed new measures aimed at reducing road deaths. The proposals are currently being considered by Parliament’s Transport Portfolio Committee as part of a wider review of road safety legislation.

South Africa continues to record one of the highest rates of road fatalities globally, with alcohol use remaining a major contributing factor. Despite existing fines, licence suspensions and prison sentences, drunk driving remains widespread, particularly during weekends and holiday periods. Lawmakers argue that current penalties have not gone far enough to deter repeat offenders or change public behaviour. Under the proposed measures, courts would be granted greater discretion to impose permanent licence bans, especially in cases involving multiple convictions or serious injury and loss of life. Supporters of the proposal believe treating drunk driving as a long-term public safety threat rather than a once off offence is necessary to protect road users.

Road safety advocates have welcomed the discussions, saying stronger consequences could save lives and reduce the emotional and financial burden placed on families and the healthcare system. They argue that driving is a privilege that should be permanently removed from those who repeatedly show disregard for the safety of others.

However, some experts have cautioned that enforcement and rehabilitation must also be strengthened to ensure the effectiveness of any new law. The proposals are still under consideration, with further debate and public input expected before any legislative changes are finalised.
