Business Job Cuts Among High Earners Pull Down National Pay Average

August 20, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Fewer South Africans are earning more than R40,000 a month, according to new salary data from BankservAfrica, which suggests higher income jobs are being lost. Overall, average take home pay has also slipped dropping slightly in July 2025 compared to the previous month, and nearly 7% lower since the beginning of the year.

BankservAfrica tracks the salary payments of around 3.8 million South Africans through its Take home Pay Index. In July, the average salary paid was R17,144, down from R17,339 in June. This dip reflects the pressure many workers are facing, caught between a tougher job market and rising interest rates and taxes, says BankservAfrica’s Shergeran Naidoo. Independent economist Elize Kruger added that this isn't just about a drop in salary amounts it's also about the types of jobs being lost. The data shows fewer salary payments in the R40,000 to R100,000 range, indicating that many high income earners are losing their jobs. At the same time, there’s been a rise in lower-paying jobs, especially those under R10,000 and between R20,000 to R30,000 a month.

This shift toward lower paying positions is pulling down the average salary figure. Stats SA's latest unemployment figures back this up, with job losses increasing and the unemployment rate now sitting at 33.2%. Despite this, average take home pay for the full year is still expected to be higher than it was in 2024, which means 2025 could still be considered a better year for salaries overall at least on paper. When adjusted for inflation, real take home pay in July was R14,660 slightly down from June, but still better than a year ago. Inflation for 2025 is forecast to average 3.5%, lower than 2024’s 4.4%, and industry data shows salary increases averaging over 5%.

This means most workers will see a real increase in earnings their money will go further than it did last year. It’s the second year in a row where that’s the case. Kruger sees this as good news for consumers and the economy, but she also warns that the longer term picture remains difficult. While salaries have recovered somewhat from the weak period between 2021 and 2023, the gains haven't fully made up for those losses. Many people are still struggling with high living costs. One of the biggest pressures in July came from the rise in municipal service tariffs, which go up every year at this time.

These increases often exceed the national inflation rate, largely due to inefficiencies, mismanagement, and corruption in local governments. Johannesburg, for example, saw municipal service costs jump significantly in July 2025 compared to last year, with similar patterns in other major cities. Because these kinds of costs aren’t influenced by competition or efficiency, they make it harder to keep inflation in check and they continue to erode the buying power of many South Africans.
