Media United States Issues Formal Warning Over Joint G20 Statement

November 20, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The United States has formally cautioned South Africa against supporting or pushing for a joint statement at this weekend’s G20 Summit in Johannesburg. The warning comes at a sensitive moment as the Trump administration has already confirmed that it will boycott the summit, creating a diplomatic gap that has drawn global attention.

According to senior diplomatic sources, the US expressed concern that South Africa may use its position as host to promote a statement that either criticises Western aligned policies or leans toward positions supported by emerging blocs. The US caution reportedly emphasised that Washington does not want its allies or strategic partners endorsing any statement that could be interpreted as misaligned with its foreign policy direction.

This is the second indication in recent weeks that tension between Pretoria and Washington is deepening. While South African officials have not disclosed the full content of the communication, insiders say the message was firm and framed as a direct instruction rather than a negotiation point.

The US boycott has already placed pressure on the summit, which was meant to be a showpiece of South Africa’s global diplomatic stature. The absence of a major economy has forced organisers to recalibrate expectations and adjust certain program elements to avoid empty chair optics during key sessions.

South African officials, however, have insisted that the summit will continue as planned and that all invited nations are free to express their positions independently. They argue that the role of the host is to facilitate dialogue, not to take instructions from any single country. Government representatives have also stressed that the principle of multilateral decision making remains central to the G20 and that the grouping should not be swayed by unilateral diplomatic pressure.

International relations analysts say the US warning is symbolically significant. It reflects rising competition between major powers and the pressure placed on middle powers such as South Africa to align themselves in broader global tensions. The coming days will show whether South Africa proceeds with a joint statement, whether such a statement gains broad support, or whether the summit concludes without one due to divisions among participants.

The warning marks another layer of complexity surrounding an already politically charged G20 gathering, where global economic instability, shifting alliances, and domestic political pressures in several countries continue to shape the agenda.
