Business Market Shocked by TFG’s Results, but Shapiro Sees Strong Recovery Ahead

November 18, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The Foschini Group’s share price has taken a hard knock in recent weeks, but veteran investor David Shapiro from Sasfin Securities still sees a strong opportunity in the stock. Back in August, during its Capital Markets Day, TFG’s management painted an upbeat picture of the company’s performance and South Africa’s outlook. They highlighted market leading positions, steady gains in market share and growth driven by new stores, acquisitions and diversification.

But things changed when the company released a trading statement in October 2025. Once the recently bought White Stuff business was excluded, TFG’s sales grew by only 3.5% for the six months ending 30 September. This underwhelming update immediately sent the share price tumbling. According to Shane Watkins from All Weather Capital, the problem wasn’t that the results were terrible it was that the results were far worse than what management had led investors to expect during the Capital Markets Day. Investors felt blindsided by the disconnect between the earlier optimistic messaging and the reality of the tougher trading conditions in South Africa, the UK and Australia.

These challenges were confirmed in the interim results released on 7 November 2025. Basic earnings per share dropped by 21%, headline earnings per share fell by 21.3%, and the interim dividend was cut by nearly 19%. It was a clear sign that the operating environment had been far weaker than initially communicated.

Despite all this, Shapiro remains upbeat. Speaking on Business Day TV, he said he was taken aback by how sharply the share price had fallen around 50% in just a few weeks, taking it back to levels last seen five years ago. He acknowledged that TFG is dealing with some balance sheet strain, particularly being overgeared, but he believes the underlying business remains solid. In his view, TFG’s strong brands, experienced management, wide store footprint and diversified operations make the current share price extremely attractive.

Shapiro is positive about the broader market too, expecting a strong year ahead, and he has named TFG as his “punt of the year.” While he admitted the share could slip further if stores were forced to close, he doesn’t think that scenario is likely.

The numbers also support the idea that the stock is undervalued. TFG is trading at a price to earnings ratio of around 10 and offers a dividend yield of more than 4%. The board seems to agree that the market is undervaluing the business, as the company has started a large share buyback programme. It plans to repurchase R1 billion worth of shares and had already bought back R377 million by the end of September 2025.
