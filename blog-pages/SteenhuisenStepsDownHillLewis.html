Media Steenhuisen Steps Down: Hill-Lewis Prepared to Lead DA into 2026

February 5, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

In a move that has sent shockwaves through South Africa’s political landscape, Democratic Alliance (DA) Federal Leader John Steenhuisen announced on Wednesday that he will not seek a third term at the party’s elective congress in April.

Speaking from Durban, Steenhuisen, who also serves as the Minister of Agriculture—cited the need to focus entirely on his ministerial duties, particularly the ongoing national foot-and-mouth disease crisis. "It would not be fair to South Africa’s farmers for me to split my time between a leadership campaign and the urgent work of this ministry," he stated.

However, insiders suggest the decision follows mounting internal pressure and a desire for "fresh blood" ahead of the 2026 local government elections. Steenhuisen’s withdrawal has cleared a definitive path for Cape Town Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis to emerge as the frontrunner for the leadership.

Hill-Lewis, often described as a "rising star" with a pragmatic style, confirmed he would "very seriously" consider the role. His potential elevation is seen as a strategic pivot for the DA; as the party prepares for the 2026 polls, leadership is looking for a figure who can bridge the gap between the DA’s traditional base and the broader electorate within the Government of National Unity (GNU).

With nominations set to open on February 24, the party now looks toward a transition that will define its strategy for the pivotal municipal elections next year.
