Business Manchester Positioned as Key UK Hub for Innovation

July 24, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Manchester is gaining national attention as one of the United Kingdom’s leading centres for regional innovation, with a growing track record in science, technology and startup development.

The city ranks among the top five in the United Kingdom for university spinouts since 2011, the only area outside London, Oxford and Cambridge to achieve this. A key driver is the Oxford Road Corridor, a central innovation district anchored by the University of Manchester, Manchester Metropolitan University and the National Health Service. The zone hosts major research centres including the Graphene Engineering Innovation Centre and the Henry Royce Institute.

In 2024, Manchester launched Sister, a £1.7 billion innovation district offering over two million square feet of commercial space. It aims to attract science and tech firms while supporting housing growth, with early tenants like Sustainable Ventures already in place.

Manchester’s appeal is bolstered by a steady supply of skilled graduates, lower operational costs than London and strong transport links to international markets. Public and private partnerships are further strengthening the ecosystem, with new investment funds such as Northern Gritstone providing vital support for early-stage companies.

Beyond high-tech industries, city leaders are encouraging innovation in everyday sectors like education, care and local business, promoting wider economic inclusion.

Despite delays to national infrastructure plans, Manchester continues to grow, guided by its regional innovation strategy known as Innovation Greater Manchester, which targets 2030 for a fully integrated innovation economy.

With strong institutional backing, expanding infrastructure and increased investment, Manchester is fast becoming a model for regional innovation across the United Kingdom.
