Media Tiffany Meek Murder Case Moved to Gauteng High Court with New Pre Trial Date

November 6, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The murder case of Tiffany Nicole Meek, who stands accused of killing her young son Jayden Lee, has been transferred to the Gauteng High Court and postponed to 20 November for a pre-trial hearing. The move marks a significant procedural step in a matter that has drawn intense public attention due to the shocking nature of the allegations.

Meek was arrested earlier this year after emergency services responded to a call at the home where the child lived. Initial reports indicated that the boy had suffered severe injuries, and investigators later opened a murder case after forensic examinations raised further concerns. The circumstances surrounding the child’s death remain the subject of dispute and are expected to form the central focus of the trial.

During the recent court appearance, prosecutors told the bench that the complexity of the evidence and the seriousness of the charges warranted a transfer to the High Court. They confirmed that the state intends to present expert testimony, including medical findings and witness statements gathered during the investigation.

Meek’s defence team indicated they will challenge several aspects of the state’s case, particularly the interpretation of forensic evidence. They have argued that the initial stages of the investigation left key questions unanswered and claim that some findings may have been misinterpreted.

The postponement to 20 November will allow both sides to finalise submissions ahead of the pre-trial session. The Gauteng High Court is expected to determine the trial schedule and rule on several preliminary matters once proceedings resume.

Family members from both sides attended the hearing, with some visibly emotional as details of the case were briefly summarised. Community activists have also followed the matter closely and say they hope the trial will bring clarity and accountability.

The case remains one of the most closely watched in the province, and the upcoming High Court proceedings are expected to draw significant public interest.
