Media News Is Changing And Creators Are Leading the Way

October 7, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa Prepares for a New Era in Media Measurement

The News creators project report brings together findings from a detailed, multi phase study that included interviews with over 15 content creators and more than 25 experts on an advisory board. These creators work across platforms like YouTube, TikTok, Substack, and Instagram. The report combines academic research, platform policies, real life practices, journalism standards, and existing frameworks to provide new tools and insights for people creating news content online. One of the key outcomes of the report is a framework to help identify different types of news creators grouped broadly as investigators, explainers, and commentators.

These roles aren’t fixed; many creators shift between them depending on the topic or platform. The report also includes practical tools like a diagnostic for creators to assess their financial sustainability, along with guidelines to help them improve their work and build trust with audiences. To mark the report’s launch, a webinar brought together creators like Vitus "V" Spehar of Under the desk news , Politico’s VP of audio and video Ben Reininga, and Sruthi Gottipati, the founder of Spot on news. Each brought a unique perspective on how news is being reshaped by individual creators.

Spehar, who started out as a baker and now hosts a popular TikTok news channel, shared how her relationship with her audience makes her news coverage feel personal and engaging. Her followers are not just watching her report the news they’re part of her day, her choices, even helping her pick outfits for big interviews. This kind of intimacy, she says, is why her audience connects deeply with her work, and why they often say they dislike mainstream media not because they hate journalism, but because they feel disconnected from it. A major focus of the discussion was how news creators build trust and relevance by being transparent and accessible, which has allowed them to grow strong communities. But they also face real challenges, especially when it comes to making their work financially sustainable.

The report highlights three big issues. First, many creators reject the idea of “traditional objectivity” and focus instead on transparency and accountability. Second, making enough money to keep going is a major challenge. And third, there’s potential for more collaboration between creators and traditional media if it’s done with respect and understanding. To help creators overcome these hurdles, the report offers several recommendations tailored training in ethics and fact checking, recognition for impactful journalism, tools and algorithms that support original content, and funding programs that treat creators as serious, independent media businesses. It also calls for more support for creators working in under resourced or high risk regions.

This report is the first major output from the Global News Gaps Project, backed by the Google News Initiative, WAN IFRA, and FT Strategies. The project’s goal is to better understand and support the people who are shaping the future of news, particularly those outside traditional journalism institutions. As part of this, 300 digital native news creators will receive strategic advice and training, with some also receiving content packages and memberships to help grow their work further. At its heart, this project recognises that the news ecosystem is changing fast and that creators are now a key part of how people get their information. The report offers a roadmap for helping these new voices thrive.
