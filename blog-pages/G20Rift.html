Media Diplomatic Rift Deepens as Ramaphosa Refuses to Hand Over G20 Presidency to US Junior Official

November 21, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

A sharp diplomatic standoff has erupted between South Africa and the United States after President Cyril Ramaphosa refused to hand over the G20 presidency to a junior American official. The presidency confirmed that the United States intended to send its chargé d’affaires, Marc D Dillard, to receive the symbolic handover. Pretoria rejected this immediately, saying the position is too junior for an event of that stature.

Officials say the G20 presidency handover carries political weight and symbolic importance, and South Africa views the proposed level of representation as disrespectful. According to the presidency, the United States had already shown reluctance to participate in the summit and only re-engaged at the last minute, which made the choice of envoy even more contentious.

Government sources say a formal communication was received from Washington indicating a possible change of stance on attending parts of the summit. But this renewed engagement came with no clear commitment to send a senior figure, prompting frustration within the South African delegation. An insider said Pretoria believes the G20 cannot be treated as a casual event where global responsibilities are handed off to mid-level staff.

The presidency stressed that Ramaphosa’s refusal is grounded in principle. They argue that the G20 is a global forum of significant influence and that its processes must be respected by all member states. Officials insist that accepting a junior representative would set a poor precedent and undermine the seriousness of the institution.

The dispute has added to broader diplomatic tension. Analysts say South Africa has grown increasingly assertive about its role in global governance and is pushing back against what it sees as dismissive behaviour from powerful countries. The United States has responded with frustration of its own, accusing Pretoria of politicising the matter and escalating minor protocol issues.

With the summit approaching there is still uncertainty about who the United States will ultimately send and whether the standoff will affect the smooth transfer of the G20 presidency. For now, Pretoria is firm. The handover will not be made to a junior official, and South Africa expects full respect for the importance of the moment.
