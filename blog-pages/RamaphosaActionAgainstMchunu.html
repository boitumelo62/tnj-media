Media Ramaphosa to Consider Action Against Mchunu After Commission Report

February 23, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

President Cyril Ramaphosa is expected to act against Police Minister Senzo Mchunu following the release of a commission report that examined allegations of criminal infiltration and misconduct within the South African Police Service, the Presidency has confirmed.

The report forms part of a judicial inquiry established amid growing concerns about organised crime networks and alleged corruption involving senior police officials. Ramaphosa has previously pledged to restore public trust in law enforcement and strengthen accountability across the security cluster. Presidency spokesperson Vincent Magwenya said the President would study the commission findings before outlining the next steps. He added that the government remains committed to implementing recommendations that will improve oversight, professionalism and integrity in the police service.

Mchunu has been under intense scrutiny since allegations emerged that criminal syndicates had gained influence within parts of the police. While the minister has denied wrongdoing, critics have argued that decisive action is needed to rebuild confidence in the justice system and address rising concerns about violent crime.

The commission was appointed to investigate claims of corruption, interference in investigations and failures in leadership. Its findings are expected to shape reforms within the police and influence decisions regarding senior officials.

Analysts say the President faces pressure from political parties and civil society groups to demonstrate accountability, particularly as crime remains a major public concern. South Africa continues to grapple with high levels of violent crime, including murders, robberies and organised syndicate activity.

The Presidency has not indicated whether Mchunu could face suspension, dismissal or further disciplinary measures, but Ramaphosa is expected to outline his response in the coming days. Observers note that previous commissions of inquiry have led to major policy changes and resignations, including the State Capture inquiry that exposed systemic corruption across government and state entities.

The latest developments are likely to test the government’s commitment to reforming the police and restoring public confidence in the criminal justice system.
