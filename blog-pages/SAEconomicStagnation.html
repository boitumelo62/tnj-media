Business South Africa’s Economic Stagnation Sends a Warning to Investors

August 8, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa is facing growing concerns about its economic future, and it may soon lose its position as Africa’s largest economy. Azar Jammine, director and chief economist at Econometrix, pointed this out after the International Monetary Fund (IMF) kept South Africa’s 2025 growth forecast at just 1%. Meanwhile, the IMF revised growth forecasts upwards for several other sub-Saharan African countries, highlighting that South Africa is falling behind its peers in the region.

The IMF’s updated World Economic Outlook shows no real improvement for South Africa, with predictions of only 1% growth in 2025 and 1.3% in 2026. These are even lower than previous estimates, reflecting a 0.5 percentage point drop since April. This downgrade is largely due to weak trade activity with major partners and higher tariffs from the United States. At the same time, the National Treasury and South African Reserve Bank are slightly more optimistic, forecasting growth between 1.2% and 1.6% over the next couple of years. But even those estimates fall short compared to other African economies.

According to Jammine, South Africa’s sluggish growth is the result of both external challenges and domestic issues. One key factor is the new 30% US tariff on South African exports, which affects the automotive and agricultural sectors, particularly citrus and table grapes. While these industries aren’t massive drivers of the economy overall, the impact on them could lead to job losses and further strain. But Jammine emphasized that this is about more than just trade. Corruption scandals, including serious allegations against the police minister, are adding to the negative perception of the country. High crime levels remain one of the biggest barriers to economic growth. Even though energy supply has improved somewhat, logistics remain a mess, and the growing influence of organised crime only worsens the situation.

Jammine sees the IMF’s report as a clear signal to global investors, South Africa is no longer the top pick in Africa. Countries like Nigeria are stepping up, with growth now expected to reach around 4%, making them strong competitors. Sub-Saharan Africa as a whole is expected to grow by 4% this year and 4.3% next year, showing a stark contrast to South Africa’s weak projections.

What makes the difference, according to Jammine, is how other African countries are opening up their economies. Places like Nigeria, Kenya, and Ghana are fostering better business environments and letting smaller enterprises thrive.

These countries are moving away from government heavy models, and it’s paying off. In South Africa, by contrast, the economy is heavily controlled by government structures, state owned enterprises, powerful unions, and big businesses. Small businesses struggle to get funding and face too many regulations, making it hard to create jobs and stimulate growth. The message is clear, unless South Africa tackles these deep rooted issues, it risks being left behind in a region that’s moving ahead without it.
