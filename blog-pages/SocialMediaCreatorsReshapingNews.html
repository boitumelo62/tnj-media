Media How Social Media Creators Are Reshaping the News Landscape

November 28, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

More people are turning to social media and video based platforms such as Facebook, YouTube, Instagram and TikTok to follow news, and this shift is giving rise to a new class of news creators. These are individuals who share news, commentary or explanations directly with audiences online. Traditional news outlets still dominate on older platforms like Facebook, but newer, video heavy platforms are increasingly shaped by creators and influencers.

In countries like South Africa, traditional media still receive more attention on Facebook, YouTube and X, yet creators now dominate on Instagram and TikTok. This trend has caught the attention of politicians, who in many places are actively including creators in their media strategies. Even major international news brands have launched creator focused teams to reach younger people where they already spend their time.

A study covering 24 countries, including Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa, shows that creators fall into several loose groups. Many focus on strong political commentary, often more opinionated than what appears in mainstream media. Others specialise in investigative or open source reporting. Some creators focus on explaining the news in simple and engaging ways for young audiences, while specialists often former journalists cover niche topics through podcasts, YouTube channels or newsletters. There are also “news adjacent” creators, whose content is based on comedy, lifestyle, gaming or entertainment, and who often shape public conversations even when they are not reporting news directly.

Three major patterns stand out. First, creators have a much bigger impact in certain parts of the world, especially in Africa, Asia, Latin America and the US, where social media use is high and traditional media are sometimes weaker. Their role is smaller in northern Europe and Japan. Second, most of the prominent creators are men around 85% in the study especially in political commentary. Third, YouTube is the most important platform overall, though different countries rely on different networks. Facebook still matters in Kenya and parts of Asia; X plays a big role in South Africa, Nigeria, the US and Japan; and Instagram is important for political and lifestyle content in places like Brazil.

Looking specifically at Africa, the data mostly reflect younger English speaking online users. In Kenya, independent creators like Lynn Ngugi, Oga Obinna and The News Guy draw audiences with personal stories and interviews, while entertainment and celebrity gossip remain extremely popular through figures like Edgar Obare and the Nairobi Gossip Club. In Nigeria, activists, broadcasters and entertainment influencers stand out. VeryDarkMan is the most mentioned figure, known for his outspoken activism. Aisha Yesufu, Dan Bello, Linda Ikeji and Instablog9ja also have strong influence. In South Africa, there are fewer home grown creators in the top mentions compared to other countries. Elon Musk, Trevor Noah and TikTok creator Dylan Page appear alongside international figures like Joe Rogan, Candace Owens and Tucker Carlson. MacG remains the most prominent local creator, known both for his popular interviews and for controversy around some of his narratives.

Overall, the landscape of news and information has become far more fragmented. People now get updates from a mix of traditional outlets, online personalities, activists, comedians, lifestyle influencers and podcasters. Many creators offer fresh, engaging and sometimes deeply informative content, filling gaps left by mainstream media. But this ecosystem is also harder to trust and much more chaotic.
