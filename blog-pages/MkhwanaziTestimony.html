Media Mkhwanazi Implicates MPs, Business Leaders and Police in Day Two of Madlanga Commission

September 19, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

KwaZulu-Natal Police Commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi delivered explosive testimony on the second day of the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry, alleging that senior politicians, influential business figures and members of law-enforcement have deliberately undermined South Africa’s fight against organised crime.

The Madlanga Commission, chaired by Constitutional Court Justice Mbuyiseli Madlanga, was established to investigate allegations of political interference and systemic corruption within the country’s criminal justice system. Mkhwanazi used his second appearance to detail how critical investigations had been disrupted and how intelligence material had been misused.

Speaking under oath, the commissioner stated, “Some individuals who were entrusted with oversight responsibilities have instead abused their positions. They gained access to sensitive intelligence that was never meant to leave official channels, and in doing so they placed ongoing investigations and the lives of operatives at risk.”

According to Mkhwanazi, several Members of Parliament were among those who received or sought classified information. He further alleged that certain business people acted as intermediaries between criminal networks and political leaders, attempting to protect lucrative state contracts.

One of the most striking elements of his testimony was the claim that the arrest of a senior Crime Intelligence officer abruptly halted the work of the specialised Political Killings Task Team. “When that arrest happened,” he explained, “the entire investigation effectively had to stop. Key leads went cold overnight and officers feared that further action would compromise their own safety.”

Mkhwanazi also spoke of law-enforcement officials who allegedly warned suspects of impending operations or quietly supplied information that allowed criminals to evade capture. “It is impossible to fight organised crime when parts of the system are working against the very mission they are sworn to uphold,” he said.

Parliamentary representatives named in his testimony have publicly denied any wrongdoing, calling the allegations unfounded and politically motivated. Business figures mentioned in the hearing have so far declined to comment.

Legal analysts observing the proceedings noted that the commissioner’s detailed evidence, if substantiated, could open the door to criminal charges and significant reforms in intelligence oversight.

The Madlanga Commission resumes today, 19 September 2025, and is expected to hear further evidence from senior police and intelligence officials as it continues to probe the depth of alleged political and criminal collusion.
