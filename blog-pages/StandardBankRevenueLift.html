Business Standard Bank Sees Revenue Lift as Economic Conditions Improve

December 1, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Standard Bank says the economic backdrop has been steadily improving, and this is setting the group up for higher revenue. Even though global geopolitics and trade policy uncertainty remain, economic activity around the world has held up well over the first ten months of the year. Inflation has eased across most of the countries in which the bank operates, and interest rates have also started coming down.

South Africa has followed a similar trend, with both inflation and interest rates declining since the start of the year. While real GDP growth has been fairly muted, business and consumer confidence are beginning to improve. Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana recently introduced a new inflation target of 3%, and the South African Reserve Bank has cut interest rates by a total of 100 basis points this year.

For Standard Bank, banking revenue grew by mid to high single digits in the first ten months of the year. Net interest income increased thanks to growth in the lending book, supported by strong activity in Investment Banking. The benefit of this growth was partly offset by the effect of lower interest rates on endowment income.Non interest revenue held up strongly, helped by a growing and more active client base. Higher levels of client engagement boosted fee and commission income, while ongoing market volatility supported strong trading revenue. Although the bank saw higher activity related costs, overall cost growth remained well managed, with revenue growth slightly ahead of expenses.

The group’s credit loss ratio for the period was in the middle of its long term range of 70 to 100 basis points. Credit impairments in Corporate & Investment Banking increased compared to the previous period, although this was from a low base. Personal and Private Banking saw lower impairments due to fewer early arrears and reduced inflows from non performing loans. Business & Commercial Banking also reported lower impairments as credit performance improved.

Standard Bank’s Insurance and Asset Management business continued to perform well. Earnings were lifted by a stronger showing in the South African retail life insurance segment, thanks to better policy persistency and favourable risk trends. The short term insurance business also benefited from a better claims ratio, supported by the absence of major weather related disasters.

Looking ahead, the group remains focused on meeting the targets it set for the 12 months ending 31 December 2025. It aims to deliver mid to high single digit banking revenue growth, keep revenue growth ahead of operating expense growth to maintain or reduce its cost to income ratio, and achieve a return on equity of between 17% and 20%. Standard Bank will provide guidance for 2026 when it releases its full year 2025 results on 12 March 2026.
