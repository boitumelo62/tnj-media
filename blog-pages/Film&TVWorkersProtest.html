Media Film and TV Workers Take Protest to Parliament Over Failing Incentive Scheme

January 29, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Hundreds of film and television industry workers gathered outside Parliament in Cape Town to demand urgent reform of South Africa’s production incentive scheme, warning that continued delays are placing livelihoods at risk.

The protest was aimed at the Department of Trade Industry and Competition, which administers the incentive designed to attract local and international productions. Industry representatives say payment backlogs and administrative uncertainty have left many productions stalled or cancelled.

Crew members, producers, and freelancers described an industry under severe strain, with months of unpaid work and shrinking opportunities. Many warned that South Africa risks losing its competitive edge as an international filming destination if reforms are not implemented quickly.

The sector employs thousands of people across a wide range of skills, from technical crews to post production professionals. Protesters argued that instability in the incentive scheme disproportionately affects freelance workers who rely on consistent production cycles for income.

Industry bodies have called for clearer timelines, improved communication, and faster processing of claims. They also want government to recognise the film and television sector as a strategic contributor to economic growth and job creation.

While officials have previously acknowledged challenges within the system, workers say promises of reform have yet to translate into meaningful change.

The protest ended peacefully, but organisers warned that further action may follow if their concerns continue to be ignored. For many in the industry, the issue is no longer about growth, but survival.
