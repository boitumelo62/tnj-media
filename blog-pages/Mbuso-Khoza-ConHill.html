Lifestyle Mbuso Khoza Leads Cultural Revival at Constitution Hill

September 27, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Musician and actor Mbuso Khoza is preparing to debut the Amahubo Symphony at Constitution Hill in Johannesburg on Sunday, 28 September 2025. For Khoza, this isn’t just another performance it’s the beginning of something much deeper. He describes it as a journey to reconnect with and celebrate Zulu identity, ancestral knowledge, and oral traditions that have been passed down through generations.

“This is more than a concert,” he says. “It’s a cultural awakening a re anchoring of our identity. And, above all, an urgent act of preservation in the face of rapid erasure.” The Amahubo Symphony blends Western and traditional elements in a powerful musical experience. The ensemble includes eight horns, six drums, a four piece band, and a 16 member choir called Ijabu le Afrika. The music traces the history of the Zulu nation, covering the reigns of kings from King Malandela to the current monarch, King Misuzulu kaZwelithini. It doesn’t just focus on political leadership but also taps into the deep spiritual roots of Zulu heritage.

One of the most meaningful parts of the production is a segment called Amahubo Ezibongo, which will feature rare praise songs tied to different Zulu clans and surnames. These songs have been carefully collected through oral histories from across South Africa, offering a glimpse into cultural practices that are at risk of disappearing. Khoza sees this music as a way to strengthen spiritual connections and realign the self. He emphasized this in a recent Instagram post, reminding his followers about the performance and its importance. Joining him on stage will be award winning actor Thobani Nzuza, who brings his own powerful presence to a production that promises to be as moving as it is meaningful.
