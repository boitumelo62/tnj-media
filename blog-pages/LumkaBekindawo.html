Media Lumka Bekindawo’s Journey in Journalism

November 19, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Lumka Bekindawo is a journalist and producer who believes deeply in the power of storytelling to connect people, highlight important issues, and make sure that voices often overlooked are finally heard. Her love for journalism began when she was young, drawn to the rhythm of radio and the daily ritual of watching TV news. Today, she carries that same curiosity, integrity and empathy into every story she tells. As the world celebrates International Journalist Day on 19 November, her journey reflects the dedication of journalists who inform, educate and uplift their communities.

She remembers her childhood in the small, scenic town of Lusikisiki in the Eastern Cape with clear fondness. Growing up in a supportive and loving family shaped her into who she is today. Her mother’s bedtime stories sparked her passion for reading, while her grandfather’s love for radio news opened her eyes to the importance of staying informed. Together, they nurtured her interest in storytelling and current affairs, making journalism feel like a natural path.

Joining Iindaba Zethu, Newzroom Afrika’s Nguni news platform on DStv 163, felt like the perfect place for her to grow. The world class studio, high production standards, and talented journalists made a strong impression. But more than that, the launch of a Nguni news platform made her feel recognised and represented as a proud Xhosa woman. Reporting in her mother tongue is one of the joys of her work, and she loves discovering new isiXhosa words every day.

Balancing different assignments is part of her daily reality, and she approaches it with intention. She prioritises stories based on urgency, available resources, and impact, while staying in close communication with her editor and team to keep everything running smoothly.

Adaptability is at the heart of her reporting style. She recalls working in Soweto, where she interacted with people who spoke a variety of languages, each with its own cultural nuances. Learning simple greetings and phrases helped her connect with the community and made conversations more meaningful.

For her, adaptability allows journalists to truly meet people where they are and tell their stories with honesty and respect. As International Journalist Day honours reporters around the world who uphold truth and serve the public, her journey highlights the vital role of local language journalism in strengthening communities and supporting democracy. Looking ahead, Bekindawo embraces the opportunities of the digital age without losing sight of what matters most. She believes that quality, credible storytelling will always stand out, no matter the platform.

Integrity remains at the centre of her work, and she is committed to producing well researched stories that resonate with audiences. With a balance of ethical storytelling and openness to new tools, she believes the future of journalism will continue to be bright and meaningful. Outside of work, she treasures time with friends and enjoys simple moments of fun. She loves playdates, vacations, travelling, trying new foods, discovering music and, of course, a bit of shopping.
