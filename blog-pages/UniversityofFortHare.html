Media Operations at University of Fort Hare remain suspended as students head to court over eviction order

October 10, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The University of Fort Hare in Alice remains closed as student organisations prepare to challenge an eviction order in the Eastern Cape High Court. All academic and administrative operations have been suspended since earlier this week following violent protests and widespread damage to property.

Tension erupted after students accused the university’s management of failing to address serious concerns about housing, water supply and campus security. Demonstrations intensified after the appointment of an interim student council which students claimed was unconstitutional and meant to silence dissent.

During the unrest two major buildings were set alight including the administration block and the student affairs office. Preliminary estimates put the cost of the damage at around five hundred million rand. In response the university issued eviction notices ordering students to vacate all residences immediately. Management said the step was necessary to protect remaining infrastructure and ensure safety on campus.

Student leaders have since turned to the courts arguing that the eviction order violates their right to education and housing. Former SRC president Aphelele Khalakahla said the move was unjust and accused the institution of abandoning dialogue in favour of force.

The students’ application to halt the eviction is expected to be heard soon in the Eastern Cape High Court. Legal representatives have indicated they will also request that operations be reinstated under strict oversight.

The crisis at Fort Hare has exposed deeper governance problems that have simmered for years. Students and staff have repeatedly complained about corruption, poor maintenance and weak communication between management and the university community. The higher education ministry has urged calm and called for urgent mediation between the two sides.

As both parties wait for the court’s decision the future of one of South Africa’s oldest universities remains uncertain. The outcome could determine whether normal academic activities resume soon or whether the institution faces an extended shutdown that may disrupt the remainder of the academic year.
