Media Motorists Brace for Major Fuel Price Shock in April

March 10, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South African motorists may soon face one of the biggest fuel price increases in recent memory, with early projections pointing to steep hikes in both petrol and diesel prices next month.

Data from the Central Energy Fund indicates that petrol prices could rise by about R2.41 per litre in April, while diesel may increase by as much as R4.50 per litre depending on market conditions.

If the increases materialise, they will represent one of the largest monthly adjustments in South Africa’s fuel price history. Analysts say the surge is being driven by rising international oil prices and a weaker rand, both of which directly influence the cost of imported fuel.

Global tensions in the Middle East have also contributed to instability in oil markets. Concerns about disruptions to major shipping routes have pushed crude oil prices higher, which in turn impacts countries like South Africa that rely heavily on imported petroleum products.

The situation is further complicated by tax adjustments scheduled to take effect on 1 April. Changes to the general fuel levy, the carbon levy and the Road Accident Fund levy will add an additional cost burden for motorists already dealing with economic pressure.

Currently, inland petrol prices sit just above R20 per litre in many areas, meaning a significant increase could push prices closer to R23 or higher depending on how global markets behave in the coming weeks.

For households and businesses already grappling with high living costs, the potential hike raises concerns about inflation. Higher fuel prices typically lead to increases in transport, food and other essential goods, as companies pass on rising operating costs to consumers.

Energy analysts say the final price adjustments will depend on oil market movements and the strength of the rand before the official announcement at the end of March.
