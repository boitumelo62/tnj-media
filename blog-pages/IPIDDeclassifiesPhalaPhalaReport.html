Media IPID Declassifies Phala Phala Report but Withholds Release

March 3, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Independent Police Investigative Directorate has declassified its report into the controversial Phala Phala burglary, but the document will not yet be made public.

The report relates to the 2020 theft of foreign currency from President Cyril Ramaphosa’s farm in Limpopo, an incident that sparked widespread political debate and multiple investigations. While IPID has now lifted the classified status of its findings, it says procedural steps still need to be completed before the contents can be released.

IPID’s investigation focused on whether members of the South African Police Service acted improperly in their handling of the matter. Allegations previously surfaced that the burglary was not reported through standard channels and that parallel processes may have been followed.

The decision to declassify the report follows sustained public and political pressure for transparency. Opposition parties have argued that the public has a right to know the outcome of investigations involving senior political figures.

The Presidency has previously maintained that the President did not act unlawfully and has welcomed due process in all investigations.

IPID has not indicated when the report will be published, but officials say it will be released once all administrative requirements are finalised. The development is likely to reignite debate around accountability and transparency at the highest levels of government.
