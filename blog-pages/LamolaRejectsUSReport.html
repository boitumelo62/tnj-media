Business Lamola Rejects US Report as “Inaccurate and Misleading”

August 14, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Justice Minister Ronald Lamola has dismissed a recent report by the United States Department of State, labelling it “inaccurate, misleading, and damaging” to South Africa’s constitutional democracy. The report, which has not yet been made public in full, is believed to contain criticisms of South Africa’s governance, legal framework, and human rights record. Lamola insisted that the assessment misrepresents the country’s judicial independence and democratic institutions.

Speaking to the media, Lamola said South Africa’s Constitution remains a cornerstone of its democracy and that the judiciary continues to operate without political interference. He accused the report of undermining the progress the nation has made since 1994 and of disregarding recent reforms aimed at strengthening accountability and transparency.

“The report paints a distorted picture of South Africa’s legal and democratic systems. It fails to acknowledge the resilience of our institutions and the continuous efforts to uphold the rule of law,” Lamola stated.

The Justice Minister further called on foreign governments and international organisations to engage with South Africa directly before issuing public statements that could influence global perceptions and investor confidence.

Political analysts say the sharp rebuttal signals Pretoria’s growing frustration with external criticism, particularly from Western nations. However, some human rights advocates have urged the government to treat such reports as opportunities for reflection rather than confrontation.

The US State Department has yet to respond to Lamola’s remarks.
