Media Why Real Mentorship Matters in Content Creation

September 30, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Media Leaders Unite to Shape South Africa’s Digital Future

While it's easy for up and coming content creators in South Africa to find role models online, it's rare for them to actually get face to face guidance from people who’ve already walked the path. That’s what makes the dentsu school of influence (dSOI) so different. This programme isn’t just about watching from afar it’s about learning directly from experienced mentors who are deeply invested in helping emerging influencers grow, not just as creatives, but as professionals with long term potential. For the 2025 graduates, mentorship wasn’t just a nice bonus it was the heart of their growth.

One graduate shared how early guidance from mentors like Natalie Wilson and Candice Bresler shaped how she approached every part of her content journey. They consistently reminded her to stay true to her own voice, something she saw in action when another mentor, Mia Tess Smith, stood her ground on a creative decision during a shoot. That moment quiet but powerful left a lasting impression. But it wasn’t just talk. These mentors got involved. That same graduate talked about how Mia pushed her out of her comfort zone with a styling challenge that didn’t come naturally at first.

Rather than taking over or correcting her, Mia asked questions and guided her thinking, helping her produce something that was still authentically hers, but better than she thought she was capable of. For her, every bit of recognition, every moment of support from a mentor, gave her more confidence to push further next time. Another graduate reflected on how the programme changed the way they think about storytelling and emotional connection. Before dSOI, they hadn’t realised how intentional content creation needed to be how much thought should go into every decision. So when a mentor encouraged them to trust their creative instincts, it was a small moment with a big impact. It helped them face challenges with a new confidence, knowing that their ideas had value.

And when they hit a creative wall during an assignment, the mentor didn’t just offer feedback they reminded them of their strengths and helped them find a way forward. Sometimes, growth looks like someone else showing you what’s already within you.

That was the case when one graduate discovered they were more adaptable and creative than they’d ever given themselves credit for. With that insight from a mentor, they felt brave enough to take bigger risks and think in more original ways. For others, the biggest win was having direct access to people who actually work in the industry. It gave them real world insight that you can’t find in a textbook or a YouTube video. Learning how brands think, what they really look for in a creator, and how to position yourself for collaborations this kind of knowledge changed everything.

One graduate talked about how their mentor helped them see the bigger picture early on. That support didn’t just help them during the programme it sparked a mindset shift that’s still unfolding. By the end of their dSOI journey, they didn’t just feel more skilled they felt empowered. Not because someone handed them a blueprint, but because a mentor helped them discover they were capable of more than they thought. That kind of belief sticks. It shapes how you show up not only as an influencer, but as a person. And this is only the first part of the story.
