Business Rand Strengthens as Inflation Expectations Hit Record Low

September 16, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The rand showed a slight improvement on Monday, strengthening as the US dollar weakened, and government bonds remained steady. This shift came after a survey commissioned by the South African Reserve Bank showed that long term inflation expectations in the country have dropped to their lowest level on record. According to the latest data, the rand was trading at R17.3725 to the dollar, a small gain of 0.1% compared to Friday’s closing rate.

The inflation survey, conducted by the Bureau for Economic Research, reflected the views of analysts, business leaders, and trade unions. They now expect inflation to average 4.2% over the next five years, down from the previous forecast of 4.4%. This decline follows the central bank’s recent push to lower inflation expectations as it continues targeting a long term goal closer to 3%. Economists think the Reserve Bank is likely to hold interest rates steady at 7%, especially after the recent rate cuts. Holding the rate gives the central bank a chance to keep inflation moving toward its target without adding pressure to the economy.

By Tuesday, the rand was trading at R17.35 to the dollar, R23.61 to the pound, and R20.43 to the euro. Oil prices also dipped slightly, with crude trading at $67.59 a barrel. Several other important developments are also unfolding in South Africa. President Cyril Ramaphosa has issued a serious warning to the ANC, saying that the party’s political survival is at risk if service delivery doesn't improve. He told more than 4,000 councillors that failing to deliver basic services, especially in municipalities led by the ANC, could scare off investors and harm the country’s economic stability.

In the retail sector, Shoprite and Pick n Pay may soon face fresh competition. Walmart is looking to expand in South Africa, aiming to appeal to price sensitive consumers by offering lower prices and value driven products. This move could shake up the local grocery market significantly. Meanwhile Discovery Health Medical Scheme, South Africa’s largest open medical aid, is still seeing a drop in its membership. Although the decline is slowing now at 0.1% year on year, compared to 1.1% in June 2024 the trend continues to raise concerns.

On the financial front, Moody’s has issued a warning that South Africa is in a debt trap. The country’s high borrowing costs, driven by deep rooted structural issues and ongoing fiscal stress, mean it’s paying more to borrow money than many of its peers, even though it has one of the most developed capital markets in the region. More than 5,000 Ford Ranger vehicles have been recalled due to a defect in the pedals. The National Consumer Commission has advised affected owners to follow up for necessary repairs.
