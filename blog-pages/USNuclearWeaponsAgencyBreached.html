Security U.S. Nuclear Weapons Agency Breached in Microsoft SharePoint Cyberattack

The U.S. National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), which oversees the nation’s nuclear weapons stockpile, was among several federal agencies breached in a large-scale cyberattack exploiting vulnerabilities in Microsoft’s SharePoint software, officials and security researchers confirmed this week.

The breach was part of a global hacking campaign linked to state-sponsored actors from China. Although initial investigations indicate that no classified information from the NNSA was accessed, cybersecurity experts warn that the attack highlights serious risks to critical government infrastructure.

Details of the Attack

According to Microsoft, at least three China-linked hacking groups – codenamed Linen Typhoon, Violet Typhoon, and Storm-2603 – exploited a zero-day vulnerability in on-premises SharePoint servers. The flaw, dubbed “ToolShell,” allowed attackers to infiltrate nearly 100 organizations worldwide, including hospitals, schools, energy firms, and financial institutions.

Microsoft first issued a security patch earlier this month, but the fix was incomplete, leaving thousands of servers vulnerable. The company has since released an updated patch and urged immediate installation to prevent further compromise.

Impact on U.S. Agencies

The NNSA breach has raised concerns due to its critical role in maintaining America’s nuclear weapons. While classified systems remain secure, cybersecurity officials are working with the Department of Energy, the FBI, and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) to contain the intrusion and assess potential damage.

Global Reach of the Hack

Researchers say the campaign’s scale is unprecedented, targeting both government and private sector organizations across multiple countries. Security analysts also revealed that hackers may have stolen cryptographic keys, which could allow them to maintain long-term access even after servers are patched.

Ongoing Response

CISA has issued urgent guidance to all SharePoint users, particularly those with on-premises installations, to implement the latest security updates, rotate credentials, and isolate affected systems.

The attack comes amid heightened U.S.–China tensions over cybersecurity and underscores ongoing challenges in protecting widely used enterprise software from sophisticated state-sponsored operations.
