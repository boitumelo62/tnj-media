Media Witness Killed Outside His Home After Testifying at Madlanga Commission

December 8, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

A key witness who had been giving evidence at the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry was shot and killed in front of his family outside his Brakpan home, raising sharp concern about the safety of individuals who speak out against corruption and police misconduct.

Marius van der Merwe, known publicly during the hearings as Witness D, arrived home with his wife and children shortly after half past eight on Friday night. As he stepped out of his vehicle to open the gate, two armed men approached and opened fire. He died at the scene while his family watched in shock. Police later confirmed that an automatic rifle had been used in the attack.

No belongings were taken from him. His wallet, phone and personal firearm were all recovered, strengthening the belief that the incident was a targeted assassination rather than a robbery. The attackers fled in a white bakkie with a canopy. Police have launched a nationwide manhunt and assembled a specialised investigation team that includes crime intelligence, ballistic experts and senior detectives.

Van der Merwe had become known for his evidence at the Madlanga Commission, which was established earlier this year to investigate allegations of corruption, criminal infiltration and political interference in the justice and security sectors. In his earlier testimony, he claimed that a senior officer in the Ekurhuleni Metropolitan Police Department had instructed him to help cover up an unlawful killing. He stated openly that he feared for his life after speaking about the incident.

Authorities confirmed that the state had offered him formal protection after his appearance at the commission. However, he declined the offer. As the owner of a private security company and a former metro police employee, he believed he could manage his own security arrangements.

His killing has triggered widespread outrage. Civil society groups, opposition parties and policing experts have condemned the assassination, warning that it reflects a dangerous climate for whistleblowers and witnesses. Many have accused the state of failing to provide an effective protection system for those who step forward in sensitive investigations.

President Cyril Ramaphosa has described the murder as a direct attack on justice and accountability. He called for an urgent and thorough investigation, promising that the state would intensify its efforts to protect witnesses who assist national inquiries.

The Madlanga Commission is expected to continue its work, but questions are now being raised about the safety of other witnesses and the broader implications for public confidence in law enforcement. For many South Africans, the killing of van der Merwe has become a symbol of the risks faced by those who attempt to expose wrongdoing within the institutions meant to uphold the law.
