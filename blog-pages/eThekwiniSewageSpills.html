Media High Court Ruling Finds eThekwini Failed to Stop Sewage Spills on Durban Beaches

December 19, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The KwaZulu Natal High Court has ruled that the eThekwini Municipality failed to prevent repeated sewage spills onto Durban’s beaches breaching multiple environmental laws and constitutional obligations. The judgment is a major setback for the municipality and a victory for environmental groups and residents who have long complained about pollution along the city’s coastline.

The court found that ongoing failures in wastewater management resulted in untreated or partially treated sewage flowing into rivers and the ocean. This contamination has led to frequent beach closures damaged marine ecosystems and posed serious health risks to residents tourists and local businesses that depend on clean beaches.

In its ruling the court highlighted that the municipality had a legal duty to maintain its sewage infrastructure and to act swiftly when failures occurred. Instead evidence presented showed prolonged neglect inadequate maintenance and slow responses to known problems. The judge noted that the right to an environment that is not harmful to health and wellbeing is protected under the Constitution and that local government has a clear responsibility to uphold this right.

Environmental organisations argued that sewage pollution has become a persistent feature of Durban’s coastline rather than an occasional emergency. They pointed to repeated incidents where beaches were closed for weeks at a time undermining tourism and eroding public confidence in the city’s ability to manage basic services. The court agreed that the scale and frequency of spills could no longer be dismissed as isolated events.

The ruling is expected to place increased pressure on eThekwini to implement urgent corrective measures. These may include infrastructure upgrades improved monitoring systems and clearer accountability within municipal departments. Failure to comply with environmental laws could expose the municipality to further legal action and potential financial penalties.

Business owners along the beachfront have welcomed the judgment saying it validates years of frustration and lost income. Many believe a clean and reliable coastline is essential not only for tourism but for the city’s overall reputation.

The municipality has acknowledged the ruling and indicated that it is studying the judgment. The case has renewed calls for national and provincial government to step in where local authorities struggle to meet their obligations. For many Durban residents the ruling represents hope that long standing environmental problems will finally be addressed with the urgency they deserve.
