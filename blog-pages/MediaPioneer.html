Media From Media Player to Global Pioneer

September 23, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Rethinking Cultural Relevance

South Africa is at a critical moment of transformation. The media landscape is shifting rapidly, and audiences are no longer gathered in a single place they’re scattered across countless digital platforms, consuming content in fragmented, unpredictable ways. In this climate of disruption, there’s a powerful opportunity for South Africa to not just catch up to the rest of the world, but to lead. And to do that, the country must align cutting edge technology with a deep respect for its own culture, ethical use of data, and innovation driven by purpose.

Over the past few years, the media and entertainment industry in South Africa has grown significantly from R128.9 billion in 2018 to R171 billion in 2023. Digital media is becoming increasingly dominant, with projections placing it at over R20 billion by 2025. Social media adoption continues to grow steadily too, with hundreds of thousands of new users added each year, and people now spending over three and a half hours daily on these platforms. But these promising figures don’t tell the whole story. Traditional formats like broadcast TV are losing relevance. The industry is now at a crossroads, either double down on outdated platforms or embrace the uncertainty and potential of innovation.

One of the most important levers for change is data. But not just in terms of what we collect how we use it matters just as much. In a world where trust is fragile, ethical data practices can be a competitive advantage. Omnisient’s privacy focused data collaboration model is proof. It’s already helped reduce financial exclusion in South Africa by millions. This shows that doing the right thing with data doesn’t have to come at the cost of profit it can drive it. Another issue holding the industry back is how value is measured. Too often, decisions are made based on cost instead of impact. Short term metrics clicks, impressions, and banner ad reach are prioritised over meaningful engagement.

To truly thrive, South Africa needs to embrace its cultural depth. Our diversity isn’t a challenge to be managed it’s an asset waiting to be unlocked. Micro influencers, community radio, and hyper local content can build real loyalty and engagement, especially when paired with the right infrastructure and investment. This isn't just a social imperative it’s a business one too. There’s also huge potential for media formats tailored to local realities. We don’t need massive budgets to innovate we need imagination.
