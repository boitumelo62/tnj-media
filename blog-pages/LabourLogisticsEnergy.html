Business Labour and Logistics Improve, Energy Crisis Deepens

September 12, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa has made some encouraging progress in certain areas like labour and logistics, but it’s still facing serious issues in the energy sector that could have major consequences for the economy and ordinary citizens. In the labour space, there’s been movement after a long period of stagnation. The final Code of Good Practice on Dismissal has been released, and this is a significant step forward. It acknowledges the challenges small businesses face when it comes to following formal dismissal procedures something that’s often overlooked in labour regulations.

Experts say this is a positive change, especially since labour reforms had largely fallen off the national agenda. That said, most of the broader reforms agreed upon through the National Economic Development and Labour Council (NEDLAC) are still not being implemented, so there’s more work to be done. Logistics is another area where the country is showing signs of recovery. After years of poor management and dysfunction in ports and railways, things are beginning to turn around. August data showed that South Africa was handling around 19,000 twenty-foot equivalent units (TEUs) a day the highest daily volume since COVID-19 hit.

This improvement is largely due to better equipment, updated processes, and stronger collaboration within the industry. The freight industry sees this as a turning point after two very tough years. However, the sector is not out of the woods yet. Infrastructure is still old, port capacity is limited, delays are frequent, and costs are rising problems that could easily undo recent gains if not addressed.

The most concerning issues right now lie in the energy sector. There’s been very little progress, and things may get worse before they get better. South Africans are likely to face steep electricity price hikes due to a R54 billion error made by the National Energy Regulator of South Africa (NERSA).

This mistake came to light during a legal dispute between Eskom and NERSA over the Sixth Multi-Year Price Determination (MYPD6). NERSA had initially underestimated how much revenue Eskom was entitled to by miscalculating depreciation and asset values. After settling out of court, the revised price hikes are now as follows: for the 2026/27 financial year, consumers will face an 8.76% increase; for 2027/28, it’s expected to be 8.83%. These are higher than the initial projections and, although they won’t hit consumers this financial year, they are on the horizon.

One NERSA official has already been suspended due to the mistake, and this has added to the regulator’s long-standing credibility issues. Electricity Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa has said reforms are on the way to simplify how tariffs are set.
