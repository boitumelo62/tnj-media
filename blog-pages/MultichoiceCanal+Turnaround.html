Business Multichoice Pins Turnaround Hopes on Canal+ Amid Industry Turmoil

July 29, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Multichoice Swings Back to Profit with Big Cost Cuts

DStv and Showmax, both owned by Multichoice, are going through a rough patch. Multichoice is hoping that being taken over by the French company Canal+ might be the solution to its struggles. Recently, Multichoice posted a profit of R1.8 billion, which sounds good, especially considering it lost R4 billion the year before. But that profit isn’t from its core business it mainly came from selling part of its insurance arm to Sanlam.

When you look at the actual operations, the picture isn’t as bright. Revenue dropped by about 10% to R50 billion, and the number of subscribers keeps falling. DStv, which used to dominate pay TV in Africa, has been losing ground to international streaming platforms. In the last year alone, it lost over half a million subscribers in South Africa and another half a million across the rest of the continent. In total, it’s now sitting at 14.5 million subscribers — 7 million in South Africa and 7.5 million elsewhere. But if you look at active users over a 90 day period, a more accurate metric, the number dropped even further down 2.3 million, from 20.9 million to 18.6 million. On top of that, the amount of money Multichoice makes per user is also down. Average revenue per user fell from R229 to R222.

The decline in South Africa is especially concerning, hitting every customer tier: premium, mid-market, and mass market. In fact, the mass market alone lost 400,000 customers in just one year. Globally, pay TV is in decline, so Multichoice has been placing its bets on Showmax, its streaming service. They’ve tried to reboot it through a partnership with Comcast’s NBCUniversal, relaunching it as Showmax 2.0 earlier this year. The goal is ambitious R18 billion in revenue over five years, with hopes of becoming Africa’s top streaming platform despite fierce competition from Netflix, Disney+, Amazon, and Apple.

While Showmax has grown its paying subscriber base by 44%, revenue actually dropped from R2 billion in 2024 to R1.5 billion in 2025. Losses deepened as well, with trading losses going from R4.3 billion to a massive R9.1 billion. So despite more users, it’s burning through cash faster than expected. That’s where Canal+ comes in. The French company started buying into Multichoice in 2020 and hit a 35% ownership threshold in 2024, triggering a mandatory takeover offer.

Now it owns about 45% of the company and is pushing ahead with a full acquisition worth over R50 billion. Multichoice’s CEO, Calvo Mawela, is optimistic about the deal, saying it would create a powerful pan-African media company with nearly 50 million subscribers across both English and French-speaking markets. The larger scale would give them better leverage to negotiate content deals and compete more effectively against global streamers. The deal recently cleared a major hurdle, getting the go ahead from South Africa’s Competition Tribunal. Now they’re working through legal requirements, including foreign ownership restrictions. Part of that process involves spinning off Multichoice’s South African subscriber business into a separate company to hold the necessary licences.

Mawela sees this as a turning point and believes the new partnership can reshape the media landscape in Africa. But it’s not a done deal yet final approval is still needed from several regulatory bodies, including the Financial Surveillance Department, the JSE, the Takeover Regulation Panel, and Icasa. So while there’s hope for a turnaround, Multichoice still has a long road ahead before it can declare a true comeback.
