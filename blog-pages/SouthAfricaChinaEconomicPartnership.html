Business South Africa and China sign new economic partnership

February 9, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa and China have signed a new economic partnership agreement that will grant South African exports duty free access to the Chinese market while encouraging increased bilateral investment between the two countries.

The agreement is expected to benefit key sectors such as agriculture, manufacturing, mining, and value-added industrial production. Officials say the deal could provide South African producers with improved access to one of the world’s largest consumer markets, potentially boosting export volumes and foreign exchange earnings.

Government representatives have described the partnership as a strategic step toward diversifying South Africa’s trade relationships and reducing dependence on traditional markets in Europe and North America. The deal also aims to attract Chinese investment into local industries, infrastructure development, and technology transfer initiatives.

However, analysts have cautioned that trade imbalances remain a concern, with South Africa historically importing more from China than it exports. Experts stress the need for strong industrial policy to ensure local manufacturers are not undercut by cheaper imports and that the agreement leads to sustainable job creation.

Business groups have welcomed the deal but emphasised the importance of supporting small and medium enterprises to take advantage of new export opportunities.
