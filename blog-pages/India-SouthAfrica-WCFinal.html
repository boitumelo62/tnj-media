Sport India Triumph Over South Africa to Win Women’s World Cup

November 3, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

India were crowned world champions after defeating South Africa by 52 runs in the Women’s Cricket World Cup final at the DY Patil Sports Academy in Navi Mumbai on Sunday. It was a historic moment for the hosts who claimed their first ever One Day International World Cup title after a strong all-round performance.

India chose to bat first and set a commanding total of 298 for seven in their fifty overs. Opener Shafali Verma led the charge with a brilliant 87 from 78 balls that included seven fours and two sixes. Deepti Sharma added a composed 58 while captain Harmanpreet Kaur chipped in with a crucial 45. Despite a few breakthroughs from the Proteas attack, including three wickets for Ayabonga Khaka, India maintained control throughout their innings and finished strongly.

South Africa’s response was built around their captain Laura Wolvaardt who delivered a superb 101 from 98 balls with 11 fours and a six. She gave her side hope with a solid opening stand alongside Tazmin Brits before wickets began to fall at regular intervals. Anneke Bosch was dismissed without scoring and Sune Luus added 25 before being caught behind. Annerie Dercksen played a steady hand for 35 but her dismissal, bowled by Deepti Sharma, triggered a collapse. Wolvaardt’s exit soon after marked the turning point as India tightened their grip on the match.

Deepti Sharma was outstanding with the ball, claiming five wickets for 39 runs, and her efforts sealed the victory for India as South Africa were bowled out for 246 in the 46th over.

The win sparked celebrations across India as the team lifted the trophy for the first time. For South Africa it was another painful defeat in a world final, but their campaign showed progress and determination. Wolvaardt’s century in a high-pressure final was a highlight of their journey and a sign of the growing strength of the team.

Final score: India 298 for 7 in 50 overs and South Africa 246 all out in 45.3 overs. India won by 52 runs.
