Media South Korea Bans Mobile Phones in Classrooms to Curb Distractions

August 28, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Korea has passed a new law banning the use of mobile phones and smart devices during class hours in schools, joining a growing list of countries implementing restrictions on screen use among children and teenagers.

The legislation, approved this week, will apply across primary and secondary schools and is aimed at reducing distractions, improving academic focus, and tackling concerns over the impact of excessive screen time on mental health. Under the law, students will be required to store their devices during lessons, though schools may permit limited use for educational purposes under teacher supervision.

South Korean lawmakers highlighted studies linking heavy device use to lower concentration levels, disrupted sleep, and increased cases of anxiety among young people. Education officials also pointed to growing evidence that constant access to social media is affecting students’ well-being and academic performance.

The move aligns South Korea with nations such as France and the Netherlands, which have already introduced similar bans to curb classroom disruption. While the decision has been welcomed by many parents and teachers, some critics argue that a blanket ban may be difficult to enforce and that technology, if managed properly, can enhance learning.

The Ministry of Education has said it will monitor the law’s impact closely, with the aim of balancing digital literacy with student health and focus. The ban will officially take effect from the start of the next school year.
