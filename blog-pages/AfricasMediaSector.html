Media The Transformation of Africa’s Media Sector

July 22, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The Innovation Dilemma Facing Today’s Media Strategists

The media agency scene in South Africa and across Africa is changing rapidly. Digital innovation, the rise of AI, mobile first consumer habits, and a growing demand for storytelling that truly reflects local cultures are all driving this transformation. Publicis Groupe Africa, as one of the continent’s leading agency networks, is both shaping and responding to these shifts as they happen.

Looking ahead, there’s a sense of cautious optimism. Although the global economy is still unpredictable and local markets face their own challenges, the sector is proving to be adaptable and resilient. Digital advertising spend keeps growing, thanks to the explosion of social media, streaming services, and over the top platforms. In South Africa alone, digital ad spending is expected to exceed R17 billion by 2025, marking nearly a 10% increase from the previous year. Across many African countries, mobile phone use is hitting about 95%, giving brands incredible access to their audiences. Consumers want content that feels personal, relevant to their culture, and meaningful.

This is pushing agencies to rethink how they create, plan media, and tell brand stories. AI is playing a bigger role too helping with everything from predicting trends to automating campaigns but human creativity and cultural understanding are still essential, especially in Africa’s diverse markets. Publicis South Africa benefits hugely from being part of the global Publicis Groupe network. This connection gives them access to worldwide insights, top tier case studies, industry expertise, and tools, all combined with local cultural knowledge.

Over the past two years, their media practice has grown by more than 150%, highlighting the demand for integrated, tech driven media solutions. They have strong partnerships with media outlets, which helps them keep delivering fresh and innovative ideas. Their approach, called ‘Connected Creativity,’ links strategy, media, creative work, and data to create seamless, multi channel brand experiences.

However, the media industry still faces a major challenge: a shortage of experienced talent. Many senior professionals have left in recent years, and agencies are working hard to close this gap. Publicis Groupe Africa is investing heavily in developing young talent and future leaders through programs like Le Cub and specialized academies focused on digital, data, and media leadership. At the same time, clients increasingly want smaller, nimble agencies that can move fast and be available around the clock. This trend grew after Covid, with many independent consultants entering the market.

Publicis has responded by building more agility within its global structure, giving clients the responsiveness they want while providing the stability and resources of a large network. As mobile use, e-commerce, and digital payments expand across Africa, media agencies are uniquely positioned to create connected brand experiences that meet consumers where they are. Programmatic advertising and performance based campaigns are growing areas too. New communication channels and marketing tools are emerging all the time, so agencies must stay flexible and ready for platforms that don’t even exist yet.

Global politics, especially tensions involving big economies like the US, have created uncertainty for multinational companies. Still, many continue to operate across Africa and need local partners who understand the terrain and can help avoid costly mistakes. That’s why business hasn’t slowed for African media agencies or for Publicis Groupe Africa specifically. They keep adapting, innovating, and investing locally. Instead of relying mainly on Western markets, they are focusing on regional collaboration, local content, and mobile-first strategies to serve African consumers better.

While Africa’s media sector isn’t immune to global shocks, it’s not defined by them either. Even if big global brands falter, African agencies are building resilience by focusing on local growth, innovation, and creating value. This is about more than just surviving it’s about thriving in a world where being relevant, agile, and culturally insightful matters more than ever. Publicis Groupe Africa is proud to lead this change, working with brands, partners, and communities to shape what media looks like in Africa today and what it could become tomorrow.
