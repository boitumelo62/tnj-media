Media What Happens When Google Can’t Decide Which Page to Rank?

September 18, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

In the rush to climb Google’s rankings, many brands have lost sight of what truly matters. They’re caught in a constant cycle of producing SEO focused content without stepping back to see the bigger picture. The result? They're unknowingly competing with themselves, a mistake known as keyword cannibalism. This issue shows up more often than you'd expect during our audits and strategy reviews. While the intent is usually good boosting traffic and improving visibility the execution often backfires. Instead of building stronger SEO, it ends up weakening performance and creating content that feels more robotic than human.

Keyword cannibalism is what happens when multiple pages or blog posts on a website try to rank for the same keyword. You might think more content equals more chances to rank, but it actually confuses search engines. Google doesn’t know which page is most relevant, so none of them perform as well as they could. Imagine writing two similar posts one about “best running shoes” and another on “top running shoe reviews.” They sound different, but they’re targeting the same idea. Instead of creating one strong, trustworthy page, you split the impact between two weaker ones. Neither ranks well, and both underdeliver. We’ve seen companies with over a dozen articles all targeting nearly identical phrases.

The idea is to cover all angles, but what it really does is dilute the brand's voice and authority. Instead of reinforcing their message, they make it repetitive and uninspiring both for Google and for readers. When we help these brands clean up and align their content, the difference is immediate: higher rankings, clearer messaging, and a more cohesive voice. The problem often starts with a fixation on SEO. Chasing rankings, feeding the algorithm, ticking all the boxes it can feel productive. But when content becomes just a numbers game, it loses its soul. At Flow Communications, we always say content should be created for people first, and search engines second. SEO is a tool, not the goal. The best content doesn't just answer a query; it tells a story, builds trust, and leaves a lasting impression.

Keyword cannibalism is a sign of deeper issues content being created in silos, rushed publishing, and a lack of strategic direction. It shows a focus on quantity over quality. And while it might not be obvious at first, the signs do show up over time. You might see multiple pages going after the same keywords. Rankings may dip even as content output increases. Click through rates drop, bounce rates rise, and your content just doesn’t stick. Fixing this isn’t about working harder it’s about working smarter. Start by taking stock of your existing content. Group it by keyword and see where the overlaps are. If you’ve got five blog posts trying to say the same thing, consider merging them into one powerful piece.

Let it be the go to resource for that topic. Use other pages to support it, not compete with it. Think of your content as an ecosystem. Each page should have a purpose. Some will be your main pillars, others will support them. When they all connect properly, your SEO becomes stronger, your storytelling becomes clearer, and your readers stay engaged. And most importantly, always keep the audience in mind. Ask yourself what they really want to learn, not just what Google wants to see. That simple shift in thinking from algorithm driven to audience focused can prevent most of the common mistakes. SEO shouldn't be an afterthought or a last minute checkbox. It should be baked into your strategy from the beginning.

When it is, you don’t just create more content you create better content. Content that ranks well, yes, but also content that people actually want to read, share, and return to. Most brands don’t need to produce more. They need to create with more intention. With a clearer plan and a focus on people over keywords, they can reclaim their voice, fix the content clutter, and build a stronger presence. That’s what we help our clients do at Flow Communications make their content work together, not against itself. Because when everything clicks, your brand doesn't just show up in search results it stands out.
