Media Court Rules Luthuli Was Killed by Apartheid Police — Historic Inquest Overturned

November 1, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The KwaZulu-Natal High Court has overturned the 1967 inquest that ruled Chief Albert Luthuli’s death was accidental, finding instead that he was beaten to death by apartheid police and railway employees.

The court’s judgment marks a historic moment, correcting one of the darkest distortions in South Africa’s apartheid-era justice system. Luthuli, the former ANC president and Nobel Peace Prize laureate, was said to have been struck by a train near his home in Groutville. However, new evidence presented by human rights lawyers and forensic experts revealed that his injuries were consistent with severe physical assault rather than a train accident.

Judge Thabani Mthembu, delivering the ruling, said the original inquest had “failed in its duty to uncover the truth” and that the state had suppressed crucial information to shield those responsible. The families of Luthuli and other anti-apartheid activists present in court broke into tears as the ruling was delivered, hailing it as long-overdue justice.

Government spokespersons said the ruling could open the door to renewed investigations into other suspicious deaths of liberation figures during apartheid. The National Prosecuting Authority has indicated that it will review related case files to determine whether charges can still be pursued against surviving suspects.
