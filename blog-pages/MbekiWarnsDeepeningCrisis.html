Media Mbeki Warns of Deepening Crisis Facing ANC and the Nation

December 22, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Former President Thabo Mbeki has issued a stark warning that the African National Congress and the country it has governed for more than three decades are facing serious challenges with no clear solution in sight. Speaking candidly, Mbeki suggested that the current moment represents one of the most difficult periods in the party’s history, marked by declining public trust internal divisions and mounting socio-economic pressures.

Mbeki’s comments come at a time when the ANC is grappling with weakened electoral support service delivery failures and persistent allegations of corruption. He cautioned that the challenges confronting the party cannot be reduced to short term political disputes or leadership contests but reflect deeper structural and ideological problems that have been allowed to fester over many years.

According to Mbeki, the disconnect between the ANC and ordinary citizens has widened significantly. He pointed to unemployment inequality and failing institutions as key drivers of public frustration. Communities that once viewed the ruling party as a vehicle for liberation and progress are increasingly questioning its ability to deliver meaningful change in their daily lives.

The former president also raised concerns about governance and accountability, warning that the erosion of state capacity has undermined confidence in democracy itself. He argued that unless decisive action is taken to restore ethical leadership and rebuild institutions, the country risks prolonged instability and stagnation.

Political analysts say Mbeki’s remarks reflect growing anxiety within the ANC as it faces an uncertain future. While some leaders have acknowledged the need for renewal, critics argue that internal power struggles continue to overshadow genuine reform efforts.

Mbeki did not offer easy solutions but stressed the importance of honest self reflection within the party. He urged leaders to confront uncomfortable truths and to prioritise the national interest over factional loyalties.

As South Africa approaches another critical political cycle, Mbeki’s warning adds to the chorus of voices calling for a fundamental rethink of how the country is governed. Whether the ANC heeds this message remains to be seen, but the concerns he raised are likely to resonate well beyond party structures.
