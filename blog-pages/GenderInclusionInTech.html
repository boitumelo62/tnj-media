Tech News Why Gender Inclusion in Tech Will Shape South Africa’s Economic Future

January 20, 2026 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa is entering 2026 with a solid record of progress on gender equality built over the past decade. Today, the country has closed about 77 percent of its overall gender gap, which simply means that roughly three quarters of the differences between men and women have been reduced. This places South Africa ahead of the global average and consistently among the top three performers in Africa. Gains in education and health have been particularly strong, and women now occupy nearly half of the seats in Parliament.

Research by Briefly News shows that South Africa spent most of the last decade ranked among the top 25 countries in the world for gender equality. In 2025, the country slipped to 33rd place, not because it performed poorly, but because other nations progressed faster. Despite these encouraging figures, a more complex reality remains. Equality reflected in statistics has not fully translated into everyday economic outcomes, especially in the labour market.

Women in South Africa still earn about 20 percent less per hour than men, are more likely to work in informal employment, and remain underrepresented in high growth and high-income industries. This gap is particularly visible in the technology sector. Because technology is a major contributor to GDP and a key source of future jobs, gender imbalance in this space has serious long term economic implications.

Although South Africa ranks as the third most innovative country in Africa, it continues to trail behind countries such as the United Kingdom, Denmark, Sweden, Switzerland and the Netherlands. These nations benefit from fully using the talent, skills and knowledge of their entire populations. The comparison shows that global competitiveness depends not only on infrastructure and policy, but also on who is given the opportunity to help shape the future.
