Media The AI Hype Check for Marketers

July 24, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

While it’s easy to get swept up in the excitement of new technologies and shiny trends, the more grounded marketer knows that 2025 like every year before brings with it more questions than answers. Despite all the innovation and big promises, the future remains as uncertain as ever. Consumers are overstimulated, messages are increasingly fragmented, and in the race to keep up, marketing can sometimes feel like shouting into chaos.

It only takes a quick look back to see how we’ve been here before. Not long ago, buzzwords like NFTs, blockchain, the Metaverse, and IoT were all positioned as game-changers. Today, most of them sit quietly in the background, if not completely off the radar. Similarly, GenAI is now being touted as the all-in-one solution for modern marketing woes. Agencies and brands alike are quick to adopt the global narrative of digital transformation, AI-powered creativity, and connected commerce but how much of this is based on true value versus following the crowd?

If we’re serious about building long-term success, we need to stop chasing hype and start making more evidence-based decisions. Jumping headfirst into trends without asking hard questions could lead to expensive mistakes. Forbes recently pointed out that tech giants like Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Meta are pouring billions into AI, increasing their investments by 45%. Yet, returns on these investments remain underwhelming, and the pressure is mounting to justify inflated stock prices.

The enthusiasm feels forced, as if markets are trying to talk their way into the future to protect investments already made.

That said, not all AI investment is speculative. One area showing real promise is Agentic AI essentially a blend of traditional programming and advanced language models. These AI “agents” are built to handle specific tasks like automation and data analysis, and they’re already making waves in business operations. Tools like Salesforce’s Agentforce are helping marketers by handling audience segmentation, content creation, and performance tracking, all neatly integrated into daily workflows.

Agencies, too, are investing in internal and external AI tools. Omnicom’s Omni Assist and Publicis’s Marcel are examples of how AI is being woven into the creative process, helping teams collaborate and generate insights. Meanwhile, products like Quartile and CreativeX are targeting specific marketing needs, from ad optimisation to media buying, often doing the work in real time.

However, even the best of these tools need constant updates. An AI agent launched in January might already need a refresh by April. So the question becomes: are we really saving time, or are we just shifting our resources from doing the work to maintaining the tools that do the work?

A recent WARC survey of 700 senior marketers found an odd contradiction. While a significant number claim to understand AI or see its potential, far fewer are actually using it effectively. Only 22% are using AI to a “high extent,” and just 14% passed a basic AI knowledge test. The takeaway? Many in the industry are overestimating their grasp of this rapidly evolving field.

At its core, marketing is still about influencing behaviour reaching a disinterested consumer in a crowded world and offering something they want or need. But we’re increasingly distracted by technology that doesn’t necessarily help with that mission. The smarter move might be to focus on how people behave, rather than just how technology behaves.

According to a recent media forecast, digital will dominate even more by 2028, accounting for 78% of all media consumption. Social media continues to grow, but the most dramatic increases are coming from connected TV and music streaming which are expected to grow by 190% and 205% respectively. In fact, 2025 will be the year connected TV officially overtakes linear TV, signaling a big shift in how audiences consume premium content.

While AI and new tools are changing how we work, the fundamentals remain the same: know your audience, understand behaviour over time, and make decisions based on real insight, not just shiny new promises.
