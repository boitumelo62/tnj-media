Media Cradock Four Inquest Rekindles Quest for Justice After Four Decades

March 23, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The long-running inquest into the deaths of the Cradock Four has resumed in the High Court in Gqeberha, renewing hopes for long-awaited answers.

The latest proceedings are scheduled to run throughout the week and are expected to include testimony from former apartheid-era officials, among them ex-death squad commander Eugene de Kock. The inquest seeks to uncover the truth behind the 1985 abduction and killing of four anti-apartheid activists.

Despite earlier investigations and findings by the Truth and Reconciliation Commission pointing to state involvement, no individuals have ever been criminally prosecuted for the murders. Families of the victims have spent decades pursuing justice, often facing delays, legal challenges, and allegations of political interference.

The current inquest is widely viewed as a critical opportunity to address unresolved questions and examine whether there were deliberate efforts to block accountability. Legal representatives and advocacy groups argue that the case represents more than a historical inquiry.

It highlights broader failures in post-apartheid justice systems to fully prosecute crimes committed during the apartheid era. The proceedings are expected to revisit evidence, hear new testimonies, and assess the role of security structures at the time. While many of the alleged perpetrators have since died, the process remains crucial for establishing an official historical record.

For the families, the goal is not only justice in a legal sense but also closure after nearly four decades of uncertainty. As the hearings continue, South Africa once again confronts the enduring legacy of apartheid and the unfinished business of accountability.
