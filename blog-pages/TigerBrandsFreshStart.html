Business With Earnings Up Sharply, Tiger Brands Signals a Fresh Start

November 26, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Tiger Brands has reported a strong jump in earnings for the 2025 financial year and is rolling out a major brand refresh as it signals a new chapter for the company. Even with consumers under pressure, the group managed to grow its earnings and keep generating cash. Food and non alcoholic beverage inflation eased to 4.5% in September, but shoppers are still focused on stretching their money, and Tiger Brands says its strategy is centered on offering better value.

The company spent the year refining its pricing strategy and improving operations. Revenue rose 2.7% to R34.4 billion, supported by a 3.5% increase in volumes, even though prices fell slightly. It also improved efficiency in its factories and packaging, lifting its gross margin from 29.1% to 31.3%.Earnings per share climbed 30% to 2,482 cents, while headline earnings per share rose 15% to 2,056 cents. The gap between the two figures comes from the sale of the Baby Wellbeing division and the company’s Chilean associate, Carozzi. From continuing operations, earnings were even stronger, with EPS up 50% and HEPS up 31%.

Tiger Brands reduced its dividend cover, which resulted in a sharp rise in dividends. The final ordinary dividend jumped nearly 80%, bringing the total ordinary dividend for the year to 1,644 cents per share. The company also declared special dividends worth R5.8 billion and returned another R1.5 billion to shareholders through share buybacks.Tiger Brands products are a staple in South African households, with brands like Albany, All Gold, Black Cat, Doom, Energade, Jungle Oats, Mrs H.S. Balls, Oros, Koo, and Maynards forming part of daily life. But the company has faced major challenges over the past decade, including failed African expansion plans and the devastating 2018 listeriosis outbreak.

Since CEO Tjaart Kruger took over in November 2023, the company has shifted its attention inward improving its factories, automating processes, and simplifying its range of brands. Now it’s refreshing its corporate identity to match these internal changes. The company says the new look represents its commitment to making quality food and essentials more accessible and to supporting a stronger, healthier Southern Africa.

Part of the refresh involves working to regain market share and repositioning the business in areas like personal care. The company wants to firmly anchor itself in everyday life by offering real value to households. It acknowledges that many families still struggle to afford nutritious food and daily essentials, and says it is finding ways to bring more meaningful value to consumers.

Tiger Brands says its renewed purpose is to “cultivate and nourish lives every day and every tomorrow,” and it aims to create positive, sustainable impact across its supply chain.Regarding the listeriosis crisis, the company said its lead reinsurer, QBE Insurance, is still processing claims related to the 2018 outbreak, which killed more than 200 people and was traced back to ready to eat meat products produced at one of its facilities.
