Tech News Malaysia and Indonesia Block Grok AI Over Explicit Content

January 12, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Malaysia and Indonesia have become the first nations to restrict access to Grok, an artificial intelligence chatbot created by Elon Musk’s xAI, after authorities concluded that the tool was being abused to create sexually explicit and non-consensual images.

These decisions highlight rising international anxiety about generative artificial intelligence systems that can produce highly realistic images, audio and written content, while current safeguards remain insufficient to stop misuse. Grok, which operates through Musk’s social media platform X, has faced criticism for producing altered images, including portrayals of women in bikinis or explicit poses, as well as content involving children.

Officials in both Southeast Asian countries said existing controls had failed to prevent the production and circulation of fake pornographic material, especially content targeting women and minors. Indonesia temporarily suspended access to Grok on Saturday, with Malaysia following on Sunday.

Indonesia’s Minister of Communication and Digital Affairs, Meutya Hafid, said in a statement that non-consensual sexual deepfakes represent a serious violation of human rights, personal dignity and the safety of citizens in digital environments.

The ministry explained that the restriction was intended to protect women, children and the wider public from false pornographic material generated through artificial intelligence.

Alexander Sabar, the director general of digital space supervision, said initial assessments showed that Grok did not have adequate safeguards to prevent users from creating and sharing pornographic content based on real photographs of Indonesian citizens. He warned that such practices threaten privacy and image rights when photos are altered or circulated without permission, leading to psychological, social and reputational damage.

In Kuala Lumpur, the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission ordered a temporary limitation on Grok on Sunday, citing repeated misuse of the platform to generate obscene, sexually explicit and non-consensual manipulated images, including material involving women and minors.

The commission said that notices sent earlier this month to X Corp and xAI requesting stronger protective measures received responses that relied largely on user reporting systems.

It described the restriction as a preventive and proportionate step while legal and regulatory proceedings continue, adding that the block will remain in place until effective safeguards are implemented.

Grok was launched in 2023 and is available for free use on X. Users can pose questions to the chatbot and tag content they have created or replies to posts by others. Last year the company introduced an image generation feature called Grok Imagine, which included a mode capable of producing adult content.

The restrictions in Southeast Asia come as Grok faces increasing scrutiny in other regions, including the European Union, Britain, India and France. Last week, Grok limited image creation and editing features to paying users following global backlash over sexualized deepfake images, although critics argue that these measures do not fully resolve the issue.
