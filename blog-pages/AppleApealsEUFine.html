Tech News Apple Appeals €500 Million EU Fine Over App Store Restrictions

July 8, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Apple has officially filed an appeal against a €500 million fine imposed by the European Commission, contesting findings that the company unfairly restricted app developers from directing users to alternative payment options outside of its App Store.

The fine, announced earlier this year, followed a lengthy investigation triggered by a 2019 complaint from Spotify. Regulators concluded that Apple’s App Store policies were anti-competitive, particularly its limits on "steering," the practice where developers inform users of cheaper subscription options or other payment methods beyond Apple’s own system.

The Commission determined that Apple used contractual restrictions and design choices to prevent developers from communicating freely with users, a violation under the EU’s Digital Markets Act (DMA). Apple, however, argues that the Commission misinterpreted the rules and is now appealing to the EU's General Court in Luxembourg.

In its appeal, Apple stated that it has made changes to comply with the EU’s regulations, including policy updates introduced in March. These changes, the company insists, align with legal requirements and allow developers more flexibility. Apple further criticized the EU’s findings, saying that the fine lacked clear precedent and was based on a misreading of the DMA’s steering rules.

Despite the appeal, the EU continues to monitor Apple’s App Store practices closely. If the Commission determines that Apple’s recent changes still fall short of compliance, it may impose additional financial penalties.

This case represents one of the earliest and most significant tests of the Digital Markets Act, which came into full force earlier this year. It could set a crucial precedent for how major tech platforms interact with developers and consumers in Europe going forward.
