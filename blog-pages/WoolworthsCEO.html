Business Woolworths CEO Bags R80 Million as Group Profits Fall

September 4, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Despite Woolworths continuing to report a drop in earnings, its CEO Roy Bagattini walked away with almost R80 million in total pay for the 2025 financial year. For the year ending 30 June 2025, Bagattini received a base salary of R19.6 million, along with benefits worth R4.7 million. His guaranteed package came to R24.3 million. He didn’t get a performance bonus, but he did receive R12 million in long-term incentives, bringing his total direct remuneration to R36.5 million.

However, when including all share-based payments and performance-linked shares (often referred to as “single figure remuneration”), his total earnings climbed to R79.9 million up significantly from the R65.3 million he earned the previous year. This broader earnings figure reflects vested shares and deferred incentives from previous years, not just the current year’s performance.

He wasn’t the only one to benefit. Sam Ngumeni, who took over as CEO of Woolworths Food in July 2024 after serving as Chief Operating Officer, saw his remuneration rise from R20.9 million to R26 million. Group Finance Director Zaid Manjra, who stepped into the role in December 2023, saw his total pay rise from R5.2 million to R9.2 million, though the earlier figure only covered seven months.

Altogether, the three executives earned a combined R115.1 million in single figure remuneration in 2025 a sharp increase from R91.4 million the year before. These pay rises came at a time when the broader Woolworths group struggled.

While the food division performed strongly with double-digit turnover growth and robust comparable sales, other areas dragged down the group. Fashion, Beauty and Home (FBH) saw only modest growth, with adjusted profit before tax down 9.1% due to heavy promotions, supply chain issues, and high inventory.

Woolworths’ Australian division, Country Road Group (CRG), performed even worse. Sales declined, profit fell sharply, and the group impaired R917 million worth of brand assets. Adjusted earnings before tax dropped by more than 40%, and CRG ended the year with a loss.

This contributed to a 5.5% decline in group earnings per share, while headline earnings per share fell by over 26%. Dividends were cut significantly down to 188 cents per share from 265.5 cents last year. Even as Woolworths faced profit declines and cut shareholder dividends, its top executives particularly the CEO saw significant increases in total pay, largely thanks to long-term incentives vesting this year.
