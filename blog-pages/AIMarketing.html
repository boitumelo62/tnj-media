Media AI in Marketing, A Tool Not a Threat

September 2, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Popular Blogs:

Why Marketers Must Review Agency Partnerships Now

Business

Turning PR and Digital Marketing into a Growth Engine for Small Tech Businesses

Times are certainly shifting, and there’s no denying that artificial intelligence is at the heart of this transformation. While the hype around AI seemed to explode when tools like ChatGPT hit the scene, the truth is we’ve been living with AI for quite a while now many of us just didn’t realise it. If you’ve ever unlocked your phone with your face, asked Siri to remind you of something, gotten a spot-on show recommendation from Netflix, or used an app to order groceries or find transport, you’ve already been using AI in your day-to-day life.

It’s not just about convenience, either. AI is deeply integrated into how we live, shop, communicate, and even manage our health. Our world has been shaped by algorithms for years they just weren’t as obvious as a chatbot you could talk to. This isn’t the first time humanity has faced a technological leap that stirs anxiety. Think back to the Industrial Revolution. Jobs changed dramatically, and many feared being replaced. And yes, some were. But what often gets left out is that countless new opportunities were created too. Technology didn’t just eliminate work it changed the kind of work people did. The same pattern is unfolding today with AI.

In the creative industries, especially marketing, AI is powerful, but it’s not the full picture. It can sort through data faster than any human ever could. It can spot patterns, personalise content, and automate repetitive tasks. But what it can’t do at least not yet is think critically in a broader context, understand human nuance, or come up with truly original creative ideas. That’s where humans still matter.

A conversation with a client recently brought this home for us. They asked whether companies could afford to ignore social media in today’s world. The answer was a clear no not only because digital media gives you more reach, but because it’s measurable and adaptable. AI plays a major role here, helping us target content to the right audiences based on their preferences and behaviours. But when asked whether that meant we’d be out of jobs soon, the real answer hit home: AI might do the heavy lifting, but it still needs direction, strategy, and insight. The algorithm needs people just as much as people benefit from the algorithm.

Even leaders like Bill Gates see the same trajectory. In an interview with Jimmy Fallon, Gates reflected on how computing shifted from being an elite, inaccessible tool to something everyone could use. He pointed out that we’re now entering a new phase, where intelligence itself once a rare, expensive commodity is becoming accessible. AI won’t just help solve problems like shortages of doctors or educators, it will reshape entire industries. But even he acknowledged that while machines will take on more, humans will still hold on to certain roles and responsibilities.

We’re already seeing the practical side of this at work. At Uppe Marketing, we’ve started setting clear boundaries for AI use in our creative processes. Our designers use tools like Midjourney, but it’s not just plug-and-play. You need a skillset to prompt the right visuals, and more skill to refine them. The same goes for copywriting with tools like ChatGPT useful, fast, and effective, but not perfect. The human touch still matters, whether it’s crafting the final tone or making strategic decisions.

And when we look at results say, in advertising it’s clear that AI brings performance benefits. Google’s Performance Max campaigns, for example, outperform traditional ones by personalising content and placements using machine learning. But again, someone has to steer the ship, interpret the results, and adapt the strategy that’s us. We’re even exploring custom AI functionality for projects now, pushing into areas we hadn’t imagined possible before. And that’s the exciting part: as AI evolves, it’s not just taking tasks away. It’s opening up space for new thinking, new roles, and new opportunities that didn’t exist before.

The key to staying relevant is to embrace the tools and keep building the skills that AI can’t replicate easily empathy, creativity, insight, and critical thinking. It’s not a choice between people or AI. It’s both. The most successful people and businesses will be the ones who learn to work alongside AI, using it to amplify what they do best. At the end of the day, the real power lies in collaboration, not competition. And in that partnership, the future of creativity and innovation looks more promising than ever.
