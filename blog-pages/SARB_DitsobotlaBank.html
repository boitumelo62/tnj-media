Business SARB Moves to Liquidate Ditsobotla Co operative Bank

August 22, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The South African Reserve Bank (SARB) has taken steps to have Ditsobotla Primary Savings and Credit Co operative Bank Limited (DCB) liquidated. The matter will go before the High Court on 29 August 2025. This follows a recent decision by the Minister of Finance, who acted on SARB’s advice to place the bank in resolution earlier this month.

DCB is located in Lichtenburg, in the Ditsobotla Local Municipality of the North West province. Membership was open to people living within the Ngaka Modiri Molema District. The bank came about through the merger of three co operatives Aganang, Ikageng, and Itireleng which were originally linked to employees of Alpha Cement, the Lichtenburg Municipality, and Lafarge Cement. DCB was officially registered with the SARB in 2014. As of May 2025, it had 1,452 members and reported unaudited total assets of R8.3 million.

However, on 1 August 2025, it was placed in resolution because it could no longer meet key financial and legal requirements. Although it could still pay creditors at that point, its liquidity and compliance levels were below the legal standards for a bank.

The SARB found that the bank's internal controls, governance, and operational practices posed significant risks. Even after being warned and placed under close supervision, DCB did not follow through on key corrective actions. These included expanding its member base to avoid over reliance on the local municipality and making sure its leadership and staff were properly trained to manage the bank’s risks.

After DCB was placed under resolution, the SARB and the resolution practitioner, Mr Anoosh Rooplal, conducted deeper investigations. They discovered that the bank's financial situation was worse than what management had reported.

Rooplal concluded that the bank was insolvent and suffered from major compliance and operational failures. He informed the SARB that there was no reasonable chance DCB could recover, pay off its debts, or continue as a viable business. The bank’s liabilities are far greater than its assets, and it’s unlikely that any investor would come forward to save it.

Rooplal also questioned the bank’s ability to continue functioning as a proper financial institution and described its overall business model as unsustainable. The SARB now believes that liquidation is the best way forward not only to protect depositors and other creditors but also to allow for a thorough investigation of the bank’s affairs if needed.

Importantly, the SARB has confirmed that qualifying DCB depositors will still be paid out up to R100,000 per person, even if the liquidation goes ahead. These payments are handled by the Corporation for Deposit Insurance (CODI) using the Deposit Insurance Fund not DCB’s own funds. Anyone with more than R100,000 in their account will still have a claim against the bank for the remaining balance through the liquidation process.
