Media Amazon Expands Influence While Meta Redefines Creative

November 3, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

If you haven’t noticed, things are getting pretty intense in the world of ad tech. There’s a battle going on, and it’s happening on two fronts. On one side, Amazon and The Trade Desk are squaring off. On the other, Meta seems to be butting heads with advertising agencies themselves.

The first battle the “western front,” if you will hit a new level this week when a leaked Amazon presentation revealed that the company is offering brands a free way to test and move their ads from The Trade Desk to Amazon’s own demand side platform. This isn’t just a casual jab. It’s a bold move to dominate the open web and pull even more ad spending into Amazon’s ecosystem.

And honestly, that’s not surprising. Amazon has done this before over and over. It started with books, then took over e-commerce, then cloud computing, and now it’s a top player in digital advertising. This isn’t meant as criticism; it’s more like admiration. Amazon is simply a powerhouse that knows how to expand fast and effectively. But The Trade Desk isn’t an easy opponent. It’s carved out a strong position as the go-to platform for the open web and for publishers outside the influence of Google and Meta. It’s respected for its innovation and independence, especially in areas where the big tech “walled gardens” don’t play nicely.

Could Amazon actually take over ground that The Trade Desk has built? Maybe but not everyone wants another huge portion of the ad world controlled by Amazon, Google, or Meta. The open web and connected TV spaces need an independent force, and The Trade Desk fills that role well. So, for now, it might just be best to grab some popcorn and watch these two giants battle for dominance. Meanwhile, on the “eastern front,” Meta is clashing with agencies over something entirely different creative work. Earlier this year, Mark Zuckerberg said that advertisers will soon be able to go directly to Meta, use its AI tools to generate all the creative materials, and run their campaigns without needing agencies at all.

You’d think agencies would be furious about this, right? But so far, there hasn’t been much public reaction. Maybe that’s because some of the big holding companies have already made quiet deals with Meta to stay involved behind the scenes, perhaps helping with quality control or other backend work. AI-generated creative content is already a reality, and it’s not unique to Meta. Many agencies are adopting AI tools to help them create more versions of ads, reduce fatigue, and make their creative processes faster. It’s possible Meta backed off its earlier rhetoric after realizing agencies weren’t going to quietly accept being replaced or that Meta itself hasn’t fully mastered the AI creative market yet.

Either way, things in ad tech are heating up again. It’s not quite a full on Game of Thrones situation, but it’s definitely worth watching closely. Here’s the funny thing, though while all these companies fight for control and market share, they seem to forget one big truth people still don’t like ads. They skip them, block them, and ignore them whenever they can. They rarely click, and they almost never engage the way brands hope they will. Maybe instead of fighting over outdated ad formats and systems that consumers are already tuning out, these companies should start focusing on new ways to connect with audiences. Or maybe that’s a job for those of us watching from the sidelines out on the “outer rims,” as any Star Wars fan might say.
