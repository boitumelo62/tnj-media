Business Economy records slight rebound

December 3, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s economy grew by zero point five percent in the third quarter, marking a mild but meaningful improvement in a year dominated by slow growth, weak investment and uncertain global conditions.

Stats SA’s latest figures show that while the growth is modest, it represents an upward shift from earlier forecasts that warned of possible contraction. Several sectors contributed to the improvement, with mining showing a stronger than expected rebound, supported by improved commodity sales. The financial sector also posted steady gains, while manufacturing benefited from temporary stability in energy supply.

However, the overall picture remains mixed. Agriculture recorded another decline due to changing weather patterns and high input costs, while construction continued to struggle with low infrastructure investment and project delays. Analysts say these dips reflect long standing structural weaknesses that continue to limit broader economic momentum.

Household spending remained under noticeable pressure as consumers grappled with rising food costs, fuel increases and elevated debt levels. Economists warn that without stronger job creation and improved confidence, household activity will likely remain muted well into next year.

Government acknowledged the improvement but said that sustained growth requires more than quarterly gains. Officials emphasised the need for reliable electricity, faster infrastructure rollout, expanded investment zones and reforms in logistics, particularly rail and ports.

The new data will play a key role in upcoming policy discussions as the country works to stabilise growth heading into 2026.
