Business Debt Pressures Mount Across All Income Levels in South Africa

September 1, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa’s wealthiest people are now spending nearly half of their monthly income just to pay off debt. This insight comes from the latest Credit Stress Report by Eighty20, covering the second quarter of 2025. While the economy showed some signs of recovery such as lower petrol prices, better inflation figures, and a boost in consumer confidence debt levels tell a more complex story.

Even though people are taking out more loans compared to the past few years, especially since the Covid-19 pandemic, overdue payments are also on the rise. Home loans and credit card debt are proving especially problematic, with more borrowers struggling to keep up. For the first time since early 2023, there's been a noticeable increase in loan defaults. Overall, South Africans who are active credit users are spending about 28% of their take home pay on repaying debt. But this figure shoots up dramatically for the wealthiest 5% of earners the so called “Heavy Hitters” who are using 48% of their income on debt instalments.

Despite being high earners, this group is taking on more debt. Around 5,500 people newly entered the credit market in this segment, accounting for over 13% of all new loan balances. Their total debt rose to R1.7 trillion, making up two thirds of all personal debt in the country. Home loans are their biggest burden, with balances reaching R1 trillion and overdue amounts climbing to nearly R12 billion. Middle class workers, a large group of about 3.6 million people, are also feeling the pressure, with 37% of their income going to debt payments. While this group didn’t see many new entrants to credit, they still took out over a million new loans this quarter.

Most of these were unsecured, such as personal loans and credit cards. Though loan balances only grew slightly, overdue balances especially in credit cards and home loans continued to rise. The mass market, which includes a large number of lower income consumers, saw about 340,000 new people entering the credit system and taking out over 400,000 loans. Most of these were retail or unsecured loans. Interestingly, even though more people in this group are using credit, the number of people defaulting slightly declined. For older South Africans, known in the report as “Comfortable Retirees,” debt is becoming more of a concern too.

They now spend 22% of their monthly income on repayments, up from 21% a year ago. Despite being retired, this group is still taking out new loans around 180,000 this quarter alone, with a growing portion being unsecured. Their overdue balances grew by 6%, and the number of defaults also rose. Overall, the data paints a concerning picture. Although more South Africans are accessing credit, they are also falling behind on repayments. High earners are carrying the heaviest debt load, but signs of financial strain are visible across all income levels.
