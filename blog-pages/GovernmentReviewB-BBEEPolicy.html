Media Government to Review B-BBEE Policy in Two Phases, Minister Tau Confirms

November 27, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Trade, Industry and Competition Minister Parks Tau has announced that the long-awaited review of the Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment (B-BBEE) framework will be carried out in two phases, marking the most significant revision of the policy in more than a decade.

Speaking at a briefing earlier today, Tau said the review aims to modernise the legislation, strengthen its impact, and streamline compliance in response to concerns raised by businesses and industry bodies. The first phase of the review will focus on legislative updates and alignment across existing sector charters. Tau said this will include simplifying scoring criteria, addressing inconsistencies in implementation, and ensuring that the framework is suited to emerging industries such as digital services, advanced manufacturing, and the green economy.

The second phase will involve a broader structural assessment of the policy’s real-world outcomes. According to Tau, this phase will examine whether empowerment objectives—such as Black ownership, employment equity, and enterprise development—have translated into meaningful inclusion across the economy. Government will conduct national consultations with business groups, organised labour, SMEs, and community organisations, supported by economic modelling to evaluate the policy’s effectiveness.

Business groups have cautiously welcomed the announcement, saying the phased approach provides clarity and reduces the risk of sudden regulatory disruption. However, analysts warn that any significant changes to scoring mechanisms could affect procurement systems that rely heavily on B-BBEE ratings when awarding contracts. SMEs, meanwhile, have expressed hope that the review will strengthen support measures for emerging Black-owned businesses, which they argue remain constrained by access to capital and slow procurement turnaround times.

Tau stressed that government does not intend to weaken empowerment requirements but rather to improve their impact and make compliance more predictable. He said transformation remains central to South Africa’s economic strategy, particularly in sectors where Black participation remains limited despite two decades of policy implementation.

Phase-one recommendations are expected to be completed in early 2026, while phase-two outcomes will be guided by the consultation process. Until then, companies are required to continue operating under the existing B-BBEE codes while preparing for potential adjustments.

The review is expected to influence investment trends, procurement practices, and transformation targets, with stakeholders closely watching how government balances policy certainty with the need for more effective empowerment outcomes.
