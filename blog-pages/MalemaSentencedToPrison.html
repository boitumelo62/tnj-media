Media Malema Sentenced to Prison After Court Rejects ‘Celebratory Shot’ Defence

KUGOMPO CITY, In a dramatic development, Julius Malema, leader of the Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF), has been handed a five year direct prison sentence following his conviction in a firearm related case.

The ruling was delivered in the East London Regional Court, where Malema returned for a second day of sentencing proceedings that have drawn widespread public attention.

Malema was found guilty in October on five charges, including violations of the Firearms Control Act, after discharging a rifle during the EFF’s 2018 anniversary celebrations.

Presiding magistrate Twanet Olivier stated that the court had carefully reviewed all submissions presented by the defence before reaching its conclusion. She emphasized that sentencing must strike a balance between the nature of the offence, the individual circumstances of the accused, and the broader interests of society.

Addressing the court, Olivier rejected claims that the firearm was discharged as part of a harmless celebration. She described such arguments as misleading, asserting that Malema was fully aware that his actions were unlawful.

According to the magistrate, the incident was not spontaneous but rather the result of deliberate planning ahead of the event. She noted that the handling and firing of the weapon required forethought, including decisions about timing, location, and execution.

Olivier further stated that the act had been approved within leadership structures and carried out despite the foreseeable risks to people and property. She stressed that the court could not justify or excuse criminal conduct on the basis that it occurred during a celebratory occasion.

She added that even though the firearm was discharged briefly, the potential consequences remained serious and could not be overlooked.

Sentencing Breakdown:

  • Count 1: Unlawful possession of a firearm, 5 years imprisonment
  • Count 2: Unlawful possession of ammunition, 2 years imprisonment
  • Count 3: Discharging a firearm in public, R20,000 fine
  • Count 4: Failure to take reasonable precautions to prevent harm, R20,000 fine
  • Count 5: Reckless endangerment, R20,000 fine

For Counts 3, 4, and 5, each fine carries an alternative sentence of six months imprisonment if unpaid.
