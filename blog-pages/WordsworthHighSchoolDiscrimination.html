Media Wordsworth High School discrimination allegations spark debate on school policy and inclusivity

February 12, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Wordsworth High School in Benoni is facing allegations of discrimination after learners protested against what they claim are restrictive uniform policies affecting queer pupils. The protest follows reports that some learners were instructed to wear uniforms aligned with their assigned gender rather than their gender identity, raising concerns about inclusivity, dignity, and learners’ rights within the school environment.

Learners who participated in the protest said the policy made some pupils feel marginalised and unsafe at school. Advocacy groups have also weighed in, arguing that school uniform policies should reflect constitutional protections against discrimination and respect the diversity of gender identities among learners. They contend that rigid dress codes can contribute to stigma, bullying, and mental health challenges for LGBTQ+ youth.

The Gauteng Department of Education has acknowledged awareness of the situation and is expected to engage with the school’s management to clarify the policy and ensure compliance with national education guidelines. South Africa’s Constitution prohibits discrimination on the basis of gender, sex, and sexual orientation, and education authorities have previously emphasised the need for schools to foster inclusive and respectful environments.

School governing bodies typically have the authority to set uniform policies, but these policies must align with national laws and provincial regulations. Legal experts note that if policies are found to infringe on learners’ rights, schools could be required to amend them or face legal challenges.

The incident has sparked broader public debate about how schools balance tradition, discipline, and inclusivity. As discussions continue, stakeholders are calling for clearer guidelines and training for school administrators to ensure policies support all learners equally.
