Business Pepkor shines after record profit growth and banking ambitions

December 22, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Pepkor, South Africa’s largest clothing retailer, is starting to look increasingly attractive to investors, with strong financial performance and plans to launch a new bank adding to the optimism. Speaking on Business Day TV, Victor Seanie from Benguela Global Fund Managers named Pepkor as his stock pick after the group delivered a solid set of results.

In the 2025 financial year, Pepkor posted record profit growth of 170%, with profits rising from R2.1 billion to R5.6 billion. This was largely driven by lower capital related costs and improved gross profit. Basic earnings per share jumped by 153 cents, an increase of 171.3%. Headline earnings per share rose more modestly, up 14.8% to 161 cents, because non recurring and extraordinary items from the prior year were excluded. The group also increased its dividend by 9.2% to 53 cents per share.

Seanie highlighted Pepkor’s massive scale as a key advantage, pointing to its network of around 6,000 stores. He said the retailer dominates the value segment and is especially strong in children’s clothing, where it is considered the most essential player in the market. He added that there is still room for growth in adult wear, noting that the group has recently opened a female focused store in Ayana. Pepkor’s journey began in 1965 in Upington, when founder Renier van Rooyen started the business with just about R1,000.

While the retail business remains central, Seanie said the most exciting part of Pepkor’s story is its fintech operation. The fintech division grew by 31.1% to R16.6 billion, supported by strong growth of 61.4% in financial services and a 13.7% increase in Flash, the group’s informal market platform. According to Seanie, fintech revenue generates much higher margins than the traditional clothing business. Pepkor also has operations in Brazil, which he believes offer strong long term growth potential.

Adding to this momentum, Pepkor is preparing to launch a new bank in South Africa after receiving the necessary regulatory approvals. The group confirmed that it secured approval from the Prudential Authority in November, clearing the way for it to enter the highly competitive banking sector. This move was supported by Pepkor’s acquisition of fintech software provider Cloudbadger, which CEO Pieter Erasmus described as critical to the development of the bank.

The bank is expected to reveal its products and target market in March 2026. Pepkor will not be entering this space alone, as several South African retailers have already expanded into banking and financial services. Shoprite has its own banking offering, while Pick n Pay and The Foschini Group have strong partnerships with TymeBank. Other players challenging the traditional banks include Discovery Bank, Old Mutual’s OM Bank and Bank Zero, which was recently acquired.
