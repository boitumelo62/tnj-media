December 3, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The Future of TV Advertising Is Connected

For years, the industry has repeated the same old December cliché: once the long weekend of the 16th arrives and the summer holidays unofficially begin, everyone supposedly packs up, heads out and “switches off,” leaving their screens behind for long days staring at the ocean. But the reality and the experience of anyone stuck indoors during a rainy holiday tells a very different story. Holidays loosen the rhythm of daily life in the best possible way. Kids stay up later, teens stream movies between swims, parents finally catch up on the shows they missed during the year, and families settle in together for their festive favourites.

The truth is that viewing doesn’t slow down in December; it simply shifts. With fewer routines, fewer bedtimes and a more relaxed pace, people watch what they want, when they want. Global numbers back this up. Nielsen reports that streaming kept climbing over the 2024 holiday season, with December viewership rising 9% from November and accounting for almost half of all TV watch time. During the peak holiday period from Thanksgiving to Christmas audiences pushed four separate days past the 100 billion minute mark. Reports across the industry show the same trend platforms like Netflix, Amazon Prime Video and YouTube hit record highs in November and December thanks to festive programming and major live events.

From a South African perspective, it’s easy to see why we’re likely following the same trend. The country’s digital backbone has never been stronger. By early 2025, roughly 50.8 million South Africans were online almost 79% of the population and the country had over 124 million mobile connections, a clear sign that we stay connected no matter where we travel. Streaming is already firmly part of our behaviour too, with nearly half of all internet users subscribing to a streaming service by late 2024.

This is the setup families take with them on their holiday road trips. Screens get packed into cars alongside the padkos, keeping kids entertained along the N1 and N3. Once families arrive at their holiday spots whether it’s a caravan park or a coastal cottage the devices get plugged in and passed around between siblings and cousins. Viewing becomes wonderfully uncomplicated. People watch whenever they feel like it. Morning cartoons buy parents a slow start, afternoon movies help everyone unwind, and without the weekday rush or the pressure of “dinner time prime time,” viewers are more open and more relaxed a sweet spot for advertisers.

And the spending backs this up. December is when wallets loosen. Online retail sales jumped 23.3% year on year during the 2024 festive season, and cash orders reached R87.7 billion. People have more time, more intention and often a bit of bonus money to treat their families. Add Connected TV into the mix, and it becomes one of the most effective places for a brand to show up. CTV delivers premium content and accurate targeting, and it follows the audience wherever they go. Whether the family is watching on a smart TV in a holiday rental, on a tablet at a rest stop or on a phone between beach trips, the viewing experience stays seamless.

It remains high quality, brand safe and fully measurable even when people are scattered across the country. For brands, the message is simple December isn’t downtime. It’s a period of high attention and high spending, when viewers finally have the mental space to notice what’s in front of them, think about what they want and decide what to buy. The brands that stay visible during this time are the ones that carry momentum into the new year.South Africans don’t switch off in the holidays. They just change their scenery and their screens come along. For advertisers who lean into CTV, the audience is right there connected, engaged and ready to purchase.
