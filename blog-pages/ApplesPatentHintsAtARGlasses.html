Tech News Apple’s Patent Hints at a Fresh Modular Take on AR Glasses

July 29, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

According to a newly surfaced patent, Apple might be pursuing a modular design for its forthcoming augmented-reality glasses, one that sets it apart from competitors.

While many companies aim to replicate the look and feel of standard eyewear, Apple’s approach appears to diverge and significantly so. The concept revealed in the filing depicts what resembles a visor-style front mounted flush with the frame’s temples, rather than a compact, lens-like design. It also shows a foldable headband integrated into the frame’s arms, enabling the device to shift neatly between wearable and stowed configurations.

Under the central display frame, the patent describes a structure built to accept interchangeable elements such as optical lenses, transparent waveguides, or micro-displays. This plug-and-play scheme suggests Apple is seeking a highly customizable hardware architecture that could adapt to varying usage modes or user preferences.

In contrast to the goal shared by many rivals namely, creating smart glasses that are nearly indistinguishable from ordinary reading glasses Apple appears to be experimenting with a more visibly technical yet elegantly flexible form factor.

Taken together, the design reads as less minimalist and more modular: a system where components can be swapped or upgraded while maintaining a sleek, fashion-conscious silhouette. It’s a departure not only from the utilitarian, block-like styles of many AR headsets, but also from the all-glass aesthetics favored by some competitors.

While patent filings don’t guarantee future products, this design direction offers an intriguing glimpse of Apple’s broader strategy. Rather than chasing the “looks-like-normal glasses” ideal, it may be opting for a semi-modular wearable with visible structural elements that balance functionality with style.
