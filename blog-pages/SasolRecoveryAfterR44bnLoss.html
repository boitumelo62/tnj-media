Business Sasol Eyes Recovery After R44.3bn Loss

July 22, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Sasol is undergoing a major strategic shift and is beginning to see signs of relief after posting a significant loss of R44.3 billion in 2024. The company has said it's making steady progress on the strategic goals it laid out during its Capital Markets Day, aiming to strengthen and reposition the business.

Part of this transformation includes a project to improve the quality of coal it uses, as well as a legal victory where Sasol Oil received a net payment of R4.3 billion from Transnet. It's also working on technological upgrades and has started mothballing or shutting down certain operations in the US, Germany and Italy, which is part of its global restructuring effort. One of the big moves Sasol made in 2025 is at Natref, where the company commissioned the first of three low-carbon boilers in May.

This marks a key step in both securing long-term steam supply and cutting emissions. On top of that, the group has concluded new renewable energy deals in South Africa, securing an additional 160 megawatts through power purchase agreements. Of this, 150MW comes from the Ampli Energy joint venture with Discovery, which is expected to go live in the 2028 financial year.

These agreements now give Sasol access to 920MW of renewable energy in the country. Even with this progress, Sasol remains one of the biggest carbon emitters in South Africa, largely due to its dependence on coal, oil and petrochemicals. Its Secunda facility is still the single largest point source of carbon dioxide emissions in the world.

On the financial front, Sasol says it expects its earnings for the year ended 30 June 2025 to show improvement. It anticipates earnings per share will increase by more than 20% compared to the R69.94 loss per share it reported in 2024, which was heavily affected by remeasurement items like impairments totaling R88.13 per share.

While the final numbers aren’t confirmed yet, the group cautions that earnings could still be adjusted as the financial year-end processes are finalized. A full trading statement will be issued once there's more clarity.

Looking ahead, Sasol plans to maintain strong liquidity and keep a tight grip on costs to help build financial resilience. It’s also continuing its hedging strategy to manage risk and reduce exposure to volatile markets. On the global front, Sasol is navigating changes to US import tariffs.

After a temporary 90-day suspension, the US government announced that new tariff rates will be enforced from 1 August 2025. Sasol says it's in ongoing talks with stakeholders to manage any potential disruptions and to find opportunities where possible. More detailed financial results for 2025 will be released on 25 August.
