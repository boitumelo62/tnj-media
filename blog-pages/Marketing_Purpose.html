Media Reclaiming Marketing’s Purpose in the Age of Automation

September 9, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Why the Wall Between Media and Creative Has to Fall

“People don’t buy what you do; they buy why you do it.” Simon Sinek said that, and it’s never felt more relevant than now. In my last article, The shift from B2C to B2Me, I talked about how consumers have evolved. They’re not faceless demographics anymore. They’re individuals real people, with real lives, flooded by noise, choices, and distractions. Brands today aren’t just battling for attention. They’re fighting to matter.

This article is the next step. If B2Me was the wake-up call, this is the reality check. We have to stop chasing hype, feeding algorithms, and leaning on shallow tactics. It’s time to bring back what made marketing meaningful to begin with: humanity. Let’s be honest marketing is in a bit of a crisis. Scroll through your feed and you’ll see it: the same cookie-cutter ads, recycled messages, and influencer posts that feel more robotic than real. Why? Because we’ve started creating for algorithms, not people. We’re chasing likes, not loyalty. And we’re measuring success by metrics that don’t move hearts.

But here’s the thing no algorithm will ever make someone care. When we trade creativity for clicks, brands lose their voice. Their meaning. Their emotional connection to the people they’re supposed to serve. We’ve become so focused on performance marketing that we’ve forgotten how to build actual brands.

Now, to be clear B2Me wasn’t about pandering. It wasn’t a green light to stalk customers online with creepy ads or slap their name into an email subject line. It was always about personalisation with purpose. It was about treating people like people, not data points. Somewhere along the way, even that got diluted. B2Me became just another tactic. But the true next evolution isn’t another buzzword or trend. It’s about creating brands that feel human.

A human brand doesn’t just talk to people, it listens with empathy. It solves real problems. It speaks with honesty. It creates value first, builds trust over time, and shows up consistently even when there’s nothing to sell. Because real people don’t wake up thinking about your product. They wake up thinking about their lives. If your brand can become a meaningful part of that life, you’re not just relevant you’re irreplaceable.

For leaders CMOs, CEOs, entrepreneurs, agencies this is your moment. CMOs, reclaim your role at the leadership table. Be more than the campaign lead be the voice of the customer. Champion brand as a long-term business asset. CEOs, remember that your brand isn’t just a tagline it’s how your company operates. If your internal culture doesn’t reflect your external message, people will notice. And they’ll walk. Entrepreneurs, you don’t need the biggest budget. You need purpose. Clarity. Consistency. Build from truth, not trends. Agencies, it’s time to go deeper. Your clients don’t need more posts they need partners. People who will tell them the hard truths and help them build something that actually matters.

In a world where everyone has access to the same AI, the same tools, and the same platforms, what will make your brand stand out? The answer is simple: humanity. You can’t automate authenticity. You can’t program empathy. And you definitely can’t mass produce meaning.

The brands that win in the future won’t be the loudest or the trendiest. They’ll be the ones that feel the most real. This is where marketing is heading from B2C to B2Me, and now, to human brands. So let’s stop chasing impressions, and start making one.
