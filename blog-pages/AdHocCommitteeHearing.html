Media Ad Hoc Committee hearing sees heated exchanges and new revelations

October 10, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Ad Hoc Committee investigating allegations made by KwaZulu Natal Police Commissioner Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi resumed its hearings yesterday with new testimony and tense exchanges between Members of Parliament and senior police officials.

National Police Commissioner General Fannie Masemola appeared before the committee following Mkhwanazi’s earlier testimony. He faced rigorous questioning on internal divisions within the South African Police Service and on allegations that political interference has compromised law enforcement operations.

Masemola defended his leadership but admitted that factional tensions exist within the organisation. He told the committee that while there were individuals operating outside the official command structure, he was taking steps to address the issue.

Members of Parliament pressed him on the extent of his knowledge regarding alleged parallel networks and asked whether he had received reports about officials obstructing investigations. Opposition MPs accused him of evading direct questions and demanded that he submit documentation to support his statements.

The session also raised concerns about the overlap between the committee’s work and the ongoing Madlanga Commission of Inquiry which is investigating similar allegations of corruption and interference within the police service.

Proceedings stretched late into the evening as the committee debated accountability measures and the timeline for calling additional witnesses. Despite the tension, the chairperson maintained that the inquiry would continue until all evidence had been thoroughly examined.

The Ad Hoc Committee’s findings are expected to influence future policing reforms and could have far reaching implications for several high ranking officials. The hearings are set to continue today as Parliament seeks to conclude the current phase of testimony before compiling its interim report.
