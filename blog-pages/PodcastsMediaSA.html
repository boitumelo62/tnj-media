Media How Podcasts Are Changing the Media Game in SA

September 11, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Media

Reclaiming Marketing’s Purpose in the Age of Automation

Right now, nine out of ten South Africans with internet access are already tuning into podcasts. Most of them over 60% are between 18 and 34 years old. That same group marketers often call “hard to reach.” But let’s be honest, they’re not hiding. They’ve simply moved away from traditional media and into spaces that feel more personal, flexible, and on demand. Podcasts hit that sweet spot. And women? They’re showing up in powerful ways. Since 2019, there’s been a 273% increase in listenership of woman hosted podcasts in South Africa, according to Spotify.

Women are still underrepresented behind the mic, sure but the audience is clearly craving more diverse voices and stories. Now here’s where it gets even more interesting and more uniquely South African. Data costs are still a huge barrier for many people. That’s a reality. But it’s also an opportunity. WhatsApp is used by 93% of the population. That makes it an incredibly powerful, underutilized platform for podcast distribution. Imagine sending out an episode as a compressed voice note or audio clip that doesn’t chew through expensive data. Suddenly, access isn’t limited by budget or bandwidth.

And let’s not forget the option to download over Wi-Fi and listen later. That makes podcasting even more practical and shareable, especially in communities that aren’t always reached by mainstream media. It breaks down digital walls and brings more people into the conversation. Here’s the part that really matters though, podcast listeners give their full attention 20 to 40 minutes of it, on average. In an age where people skip ads in seconds and scroll endlessly, that kind of engagement is rare. And it’s powerful.

One episode can turn into so much more a PR story, a social media post, a training session for your team. It’s more than content. It’s connection. It’s trust. And audiences don’t just listen passively. According to Nielsen, more than half of them notice sponsorships and act on them. That’s marketing that actually works. The South African podcast scene is still wide open. It’s not crowded like in the US or Europe. For brands, that means there’s still space to lead not just to join the noise, but to shape the narrative entirely.
