Business Chinese Refineries Turn to Russian Oil as Global Trade Shifts

August 21, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Chinese refiners have placed fresh orders for Russian crude, redirecting supplies that would typically flow to India. The move follows a drop in Indian demand, triggered by new U.S. tariffs introduced under Donald Trump’s administration.

This development highlights how quickly global energy trade routes can shift in response to political and economic pressure. For Russia, increased Chinese orders provide a vital outlet for its oil exports, cushioning the impact of Western sanctions and market restrictions. For China, the move secures cheaper energy supplies at a time when global oil prices remain volatile.

India, however, faces a more complicated picture. With tariffs disrupting its ability to take in Russian crude at favorable rates, it will be forced to seek alternative suppliers likely at higher costs. That could put upward pressure on domestic energy prices and inflation.

The broader consequences may be felt across the global oil market. A tighter competition for Middle Eastern and African crude could emerge, while Russia becomes increasingly dependent on China as its primary energy customer. This dynamic not only strengthens the Beijing Moscow partnership but also risks deepening divisions in global energy politics.

If current trends continue, the world could see a more polarized oil market: Western-aligned nations trading among themselves, while China and Russia consolidate their own energy axis. Such a split may further weaken the traditional influence of organizations like OPEC, and leave smaller importers struggling to adapt.
