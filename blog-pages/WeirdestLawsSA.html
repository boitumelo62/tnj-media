Lifestyle Some of South Africa’s Weirdest Laws – And Yes, They’re Real

August 17, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa has its fair share of unusual laws and by-laws – the kind that make you think, “Wait, that’s actually illegal?” From beachwear rules to cows having more road rights than your car, here’s a list of some strange but true legal quirks that you could technically get into trouble for.

Watch That Bikini – Beachwear Can Offend

In Cape Town, what you wear to the beach could land you in hot water. According to the city’s by-laws, it’s illegal to appear naked or even partially clothed in a way that might cause offence unless you're in a designated nudist spot like Sandy Bay. So, even if you're not planning to tan topless on Clifton Beach, it's best to keep things modest. That Speedo? Think twice.

Kissing Could’ve Got You Locked Up

Believe it or not, there was a time in South Africa when teens could face jail time just for kissing. Under the old version of the Sexual Offences Act, even consensual kissing or touching between minors under 16 could be prosecuted. Thankfully, that's been reformed – but it's a wild reminder of how the law used to look at teenage romance.

Cows and Goats > Your Car

Here’s one for the books: under the National Road Traffic Act, livestock like cows, goats, and sheep have legal right of way on the road. So if a herd blocks traffic, your shiny German car (or your beloved Corsa with the popcorn exhaust) just has to wait. No hooting – the goat wins.

No Bad Singing on the Bus

Thinking of doing your best Idols audition on public transport? Maybe don’t. Noise nuisance laws can apply to loud, strange sounds in public – including singing along to your music with earphones in. So your dramatic “wooden mic” performance could technically land you in trouble.

Leave Your Poodle Its Natural Colour

Planning to dye your dog pink for Instagram clout? That’s a no-no. The Animals Protection Act of 1962 makes it illegal to colour animals like puppies or ducklings for decoration. Paris Hilton-style pets are not a vibe here – at least not pink ones.

You Can Own a Crocodile (With a Permit)

Yes, you read that right. In some provinces, it’s totally legal to keep exotic animals like crocodiles or massive snakes as long as you’ve got the right permit. So if your idea of a pet is more Jurassic Park than Petco, it might just be possible.

Witchcraft? Legal. Calling Someone a Witch? Not So Much

South Africa’s Witchcraft Suppression Act of 1957 is still in play, but here’s the twist: practicing witchcraft isn’t actually illegal. What is illegal is accusing someone of being a witch or claiming you can harm others with magic. So if you’re thinking of threatening someone with muti, you might want to think again.

Also, You Still Need a TV Licence (Even If You Only Watch Netflix)

Yes, this one feels particularly outdated. South Africans are still legally required to have a TV licence, even if all you do is stream shows on your phone or laptop. Old school, but still enforced.

Imagine if all of these were actually enforced? We’d have police patrols on beaches measuring bikinis, goat traffic officers in the Karoo, and animal fashion police on standby. South Africa’s legal system may be serious when it needs to be, but it definitely has its odd moments too.
