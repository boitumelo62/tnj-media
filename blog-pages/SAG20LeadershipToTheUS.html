Media South Africa hands G20 leadership to the United States

November 26, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa has completed its term as president of the G20 and has officially handed over leadership to the United States. The announcement was confirmed by DIRCO, marking the end of a pivotal year in which South Africa hosted global heads of state, financial bodies and multilateral organisations to address worldwide economic and developmental challenges.

During its presidency, South Africa placed strong focus on reforming global financial inequality, expanding trade participation for emerging markets and driving climate finance conversations. Energy security, industrial development and global governance reform also formed part of South Africa’s priority list while moderating growing tensions between competing economic blocs.

Analysts believe the handover comes at a time when global cooperation is under strain, with wars, trade disputes and currency volatility shaping the world economy. South Africa’s role was to act as a bridge between powerful economies and developing regions, particularly Africa, which continues to push for greater influence in the G20 space.

The United States will now assume responsibility for steering the agenda through 2026. Expectations are high that Washington will continue working on debt relief structures, funding for climate mitigation and improved market access for the Global South. How priorities shift under American stewardship remains to be seen, but the political tone of the next year will likely reflect US domestic interests and foreign policy direction.

South Africa leaves its presidency with notable progress on global development funding, expanded representation conversations and renewed diplomatic engagement across continents. Government has praised the collaborative effort behind hosting major summits and facilitating agreements, expressing confidence that momentum will continue under the new leadership.

As the G20 cycle enters its next phase, South Africa remains a permanent voice at the table, now moving from chair to contributor, shaping the future of global cooperation from a new angle.
