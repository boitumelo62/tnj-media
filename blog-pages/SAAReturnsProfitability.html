Business SAA returns to profitability after years of losses

February 10, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South African Airways has returned to profitability, posting a R155 million profit for the year ending March 2025, following a R354 million loss in 2024. The turnaround marks a significant milestone for the national carrier after years of financial instability, government bailouts, and restructuring.

The airline has attributed the improved performance to cost cutting measures, route optimisation, improved load factors, and disciplined financial management. Management has also pointed to operational efficiency gains and stronger demand in both domestic and regional markets.

Government officials have welcomed the profit as a sign that the airline’s restructuring efforts are beginning to yield results. SAA underwent a business rescue process and partial privatisation attempts in recent years, with ongoing debates about the role of a state-owned national carrier in South Africa’s aviation market.

Aviation analysts caution that while the profit is a positive development, SAA remains vulnerable to fuel price volatility, currency fluctuations, and intense competition from private carriers. They say sustained profitability will depend on continued cost control, network expansion that is commercially viable, and stable governance.

Labour unions have called for clarity on job security and future growth plans, while business groups say a stable national airline could support tourism, trade, and regional connectivity. SAA has indicated that it will focus on consolidating its route network, improving customer experience, and strengthening its financial position going forward.
