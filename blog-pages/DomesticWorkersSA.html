Business South African Domestic Workers Still Underpaid

September 8, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

A new SweepSouth Report for 2025 has revealed that domestic workers in South Africa continue to face widespread underpayment, with 39% still earning below the national minimum wage. The findings shine a light on one of the most vulnerable sectors of the labour force, where poor enforcement and unequal power dynamics leave many workers trapped in poverty.

Domestic work remains one of the largest sources of employment for women in South Africa, particularly in low-income communities. Yet despite the critical role these workers play in supporting households, raising children, and caring for the elderly, their earnings remain among the lowest in the economy.

Labour regulations set clear wage thresholds, but implementation has been slow and often inconsistent. Because most domestic workers are employed in private households, they are less protected by traditional workplace oversight. This makes it harder to monitor compliance, and many employees fear losing their jobs if they speak out about unfair pay.

The report also highlighted that underpayment adds to the financial pressures workers already face, with many unable to meet basic needs such as food, transport, education, and healthcare for their families. Rising living costs have only deepened the crisis.

Advocacy organisations and unions have urged the government to step up enforcement efforts, calling for stronger penalties for employers who fail to comply. They have also emphasised the importance of raising awareness among both workers and employers about legal wage requirements.

Experts warn that unless there is urgent intervention, the cycle of exploitation will continue. The SweepSouth Report argues that ensuring fair pay for domestic workers is not just a legal obligation but also a matter of dignity and social justice.
