Media Parole Denied for Donovan Moodley as Victims Families Seek Closure

December 22, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Correctional Services has rejected convicted murderer Donovan Moodley’s latest bid for parole, ensuring that he will remain behind bars for at least another two years. The decision has been welcomed by the families of victims who say the denial offers a measure of relief after years of trauma and grief.

Moodley is serving a lengthy sentence for murder and has previously applied for parole on several occasions. His latest application was considered by the relevant parole board which assessed factors such as the seriousness of the crime his behaviour while incarcerated and the impact of his possible release on the community.

According to officials the board concluded that Moodley has not met the requirements necessary for parole. Considerations included the need for accountability rehabilitation and the interests of justice. The decision means that he will only be eligible to apply again after the stipulated period.

Families affected by the crime have expressed support for the outcome saying it affirms their belief that justice is being upheld. For them the parole hearings often reopen old wounds forcing them to relive painful memories. The denial is seen as recognition of the lasting harm caused by violent crime.

Correctional Services has reiterated that parole is not a right but a privilege granted under strict conditions. Each application is evaluated carefully with public safety remaining a central concern. The department has stressed that the seriousness of the offence plays a critical role in decision making.

Legal experts note that parole decisions frequently attract public attention especially in high profile cases. They emphasise that the system aims to balance rehabilitation with accountability and the protection of society.

The case has once again sparked debate around sentencing parole and victims rights. Advocacy groups argue that victims families should continue to have a strong voice in parole proceedings given the lifelong impact of violent crimes.

As Moodley remains incarcerated the focus for many is on healing and moving forward. While the decision cannot undo the loss suffered it provides reassurance to families that the justice system has taken their pain into account.
