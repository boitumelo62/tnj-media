Tech News Tech Revolution Set for Women’s Rugby World Cup 2025

August 4, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

As the countdown begins for the Women’s Rugby World Cup 2025 in England, the tournament is not only shaping up to be the biggest celebration of women’s rugby in history it’s also emerging as one of the most technologically advanced sporting events ever held. From player safety to fan engagement, the Rugby World Cup will debut several cutting-edge innovations designed to enhance the experience on and off the field.

Player Safety Goes High-Tech

One of the most significant developments is the mandatory use of smart mouthguards. These devices are fitted with motion sensors that track head impacts in real time. If a player experiences a collision with forces above a certain threshold, Bluetooth alerts are sent instantly to medical staff, triggering immediate Head Injury Assessment (HIA) protocols. This marks the first full-scale implementation of sensor-based head impact monitoring at a women’s rugby tournament. The move reflects World Rugby’s growing commitment to tackling head injury risks, especially in a sport that has seen increasing attention around player welfare.

Adding another layer to the safety net is the introduction of autonomous drones, which will hover above matches capturing detailed multi-angle footage of in-game collisions. Using AI-powered systems, these drones position themselves in real time without human guidance. The footage will support concussion analysis and long-term injury prevention research.

Investing in Women’s Health

Though less flashy than drones and sensors, perhaps the most impactful initiative is happening off the field. In partnership with the Rugby Football Union (RFU), World Rugby is supporting the rollout of a women’s health and hygiene toolkit at over 500 clubs across England. This includes free access to sanitary products, improved facilities, and resources to help clubs better support female athletes. It’s part of a broader “legacy project” aimed at making rugby more accessible to women and girls, especially in under-resourced communities.

A Blueprint for the Future

The 2025 Women’s Rugby World Cup is shaping up to be more than just a sporting spectacle. With its blend of sport, science, and storytelling, the tournament is setting a new benchmark for how elite women’s sport can leverage technology for progress.

From smarter safety tools to deeper fan connections, every innovation introduced this year could very well become the standard for future global competitions. As England prepares to host the world, it’s clear the real game-changer in 2025 isn’t just the players on the pitch it’s the tech transforming the way the game is played, seen, and remembered.
