Media Cinema Still Thrives Among South Africa’s Middle Class

August 27, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

When asked about what they enjoy most in their free time, South Africa’s middle and upper income consumers those earning R10,000 or more per month place movies firmly in their top three interests, just after music and travel. This comes from BrandMapp, a large scale survey that tracks what drives and entertains these households. With our current obsession with short form videos, endless scrolling, and quick content on small screens, it might be easy to assume the days of watching a full length film in a cinema are fading. But that’s not really the case.

Brandon De Kock, who works closely with BrandMapp, says we shouldn’t buy into the idea that cinema is dying just because everything is faster and more digital now. Sure, streaming platforms have changed how we watch things, and yes, the pandemic hit cinemas hard. But people with disposable income in South Africa are still showing up at theatres. The latest data shows that two thirds of this consumer group still go to the movies. About 4.6 million South Africans are considered regular moviegoers, going around six times a year. And nearly 2.5 million are full blown fans they’re at the movies at least once a month. For them, the experience isn’t just about the film it’s the atmosphere, the social outing, and yes, the cinema popcorn you can’t quite replicate at home.

De Kock notes that there’s little difference between how often middle income and top income earners go to the movies. Deals and partnerships like Ster Kinekor’s tie ins with Discovery Vitality or Edgars Club make the movie experience more accessible. And with things like midweek throwback specials, it’s clear that movie night is something people are willing to make room for in their budgets, even when times are tough. Ster Kinekor’s CEO, Mark Sardi, puts it simply: nothing compares to watching a story unfold on the big screen. It’s not just entertainment it’s an escape. It draws you in with the scale of the visuals, the power of surround sound, and the collective experience of sharing it with a room full of people, all immersed in the same story.

That’s something no home setup can fully recreate. And despite all the noise about streaming taking over, cinema isn’t going anywhere. De Kock compares the fear around cinema’s decline to the panic in the ’80s when people said video would kill the radio. We know how that turned out radio is still very much alive. Likewise, while watching a movie on your phone or laptop might be convenient, it doesn’t compare to the emotional impact of the cinema experience. For many South Africans, that magic is still very much worth leaving the house for.
