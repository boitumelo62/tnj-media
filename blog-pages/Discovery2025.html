Business First Bank Breakthrough Marks Discovery’s 2025 Success

September 11, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Discovery had a strong financial year ending 30 June 2025, with earnings rising significantly and its banking division turning a profit for the first time over an extended period. The group reported a 30% increase in both headline earnings and normalised headline earnings, reaching R9.6 billion and R9.8 billion, respectively. This pushed its return on equity up to 15.4%, from 13.5% the previous year.

Despite operating in a tough global environment with geopolitical tensions, uncertain policy shifts, and sluggish economic growth in many of the regions where it operates, Discovery managed to deliver solid results. Falling interest rates helped improve the investment landscape, but risks remained high. The company noted that ongoing technological and demographic changes supported its shared value model and unique use of data, which it believes positions it well for long term growth. Discovery said it has now moved past a period of heavy investment, which focused on building new areas of long term growth, and is entering a new phase of focused, organic expansion. This will be driven by its global Vitality business and its South African operations.

Earnings per share rose sharply, with basic EPS up 26% to 1,402.2 cents and headline EPS up 30% to 1,447 cents. The company declared a final dividend of 152 cents per share, adding to the 87 cents declared earlier in the year, bringing the total to 239 cents per share. The South African business posted a 22% increase in normalised profit from operations. Discovery attributed this to strong customer engagement, improved claims experience, and higher client retention. The introduction of a new HealthyFood partner also contributed to growth, although it came with some once off costs that are expected to ease in the next financial year.

Discovery Bank continued to grow rapidly, with a 30% increase in clients. Loans grew by 39% and deposits rose by 26%. As a result, revenue climbed, and the bank became profitable in the second half of the year, ahead of schedule. While the bank still ended the full year with a loss of R68 million, this was a dramatic 85% improvement compared to the previous year.

Other parts of the group also performed well. Discovery Health’s operating profit rose 7%, supported by continued investment in technology and AI. Discovery Life’s operating profit increased by 14%, mainly due to fewer claims. Discovery Invest grew profit by 29%, helped by rising asset values and some once-off benefits.

Discovery Insure also had a standout year, with profit jumping by 229% thanks to improved pricing, better claims management, and favourable weather conditions. Internationally, Discovery’s Vitality business recorded a 70% surge in normalised profit to R3.2 billion. The group credited this to the successful restructuring of its global operations into a single, focused unit and strong growth, especially in the UK. Overall, Discovery said it is now well positioned for its next phase of growth, backed by a profitable bank, strong local performance, and momentum in its international business.
