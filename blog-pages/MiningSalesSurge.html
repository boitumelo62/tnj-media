Business Mining Sales Surge Despite Dip in South African Production

October 17, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Mining production in South Africa took a slight dip in August 2025, falling by 0.2% compared to the same month last year and by 1.2% from July. This came after a fairly strong performance in July, when output rose by over 5% year on year. The biggest contributors to the drop in August were platinum group metals (PGMs), gold, and manganese ore, all of which saw production fall by around 3%. However, the overall picture isn’t entirely negative. While production slowed, mining sales actually increased sharply, thanks to rising commodity prices particularly gold.

According to Katrien Smuts, although sales figures can vary quite a bit month to month, the surge in gold sales was striking: a 471% increase compared to the same time last year. PGMs also saw a healthy 44% rise in sales. Gold’s popularity is growing again, driven by global uncertainty. With tensions rising due to U.S. tariffs and a recent government shutdown, investors and central banks are turning to gold as a safer bet. On top of that, the U.S. dollar’s weakness and speculation around possible interest rate cuts have made gold even more attractive.

PGMs, while not climbing as fast as gold, are also seeing a rebound. South Africa holds nearly 80% of the world’s PGM supply, and prices have risen after several tough years. The increase in value is tied to ongoing production challenges in the country, such as high operational costs, limited investment in new mining projects, and few mergers or acquisitions. Even though prices are improving, Smuts warned that it’s not easy for mining companies to quickly ramp up production to match demand. Mining wasn't the only sector with a mixed performance in August. Retail sales also slowed, falling by 1.2% from July. But on a year on year basis, they still grew by 2.3%, although this was less than the 5.7% rise seen in July.

Some retailers like those selling hardware, paint, and glass performed fairly well, while sales of food, beverages, and tobacco fell and dragged down overall numbers. Wholesale trade posted modest growth, rising by 1% compared to July and by 1.7% from August 2024. Smuts pointed out, though, that in real terms, wholesale volumes have been mostly flat for the past two years. Meanwhile, the motor trade sector continued to do well. Sales rose by 0.5% from July and by 1.9% year on year in August. Sales of both new and used vehicles, as well as fuel, supported this growth.
