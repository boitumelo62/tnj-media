Media Babita Deokaran Lecture Shines Spotlight on Corruption and The Cost of Speaking Out

December 10, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The inaugural Babita Deokaran lecture delivered a powerful and emotional reflection on the ongoing fight against corruption in South Africa, placing particular focus on the vulnerability of whistleblowers. Named in honour of the late health department official who was murdered after exposing misuse of public funds, the lecture drew academics, policy makers, activists and members of the public who continue to demand accountability and deeper systemic protection for those who come forward.

Speakers described corruption as a destructive force that eats away at institutions, service delivery and public trust. They argued that corruption flourishes because individuals who attempt to stop it are often left isolated, unprotected and in some cases targeted. The lecture highlighted Deokaran’s courage as a reminder of the urgent need for stronger safety measures within both government and corporate environments.

A recurring theme was the emotional and financial toll experienced by whistleblowers. Several contributors called for an independent support office, formal compensation structures and legal safeguards to ensure that no one who exposes wrongdoing is left to face retaliation alone. The event ended with a renewed commitment to honouring Deokaran’s legacy by pushing for reforms that uphold integrity and transparency across all levels of leadership.
