Media What to Do When Your Brand Becomes the Punchline

December 4, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

When your brand suddenly becomes the centre of a national joke, it can feel as if everything is spinning out of control. One day you’re minding your business selling croissants and almond milk, and the next, you’re trending because someone claimed they carried R300 000 in cash out of a bank in one of your shopping bags.

That’s exactly what happened when Vusimuzi “Cat” Matlala testified before Parliament and casually referred to his Woolworths bag as “the money bag.” The internet didn’t miss a beat. Memes, jokes, commentary the whole country joined in. Overnight, the simple Woolies bag became an accidental character in a political spectacle.

To their credit, Woolworths responded quickly and cleverly. Switching their black bags to white, more transparent ones was a subtle way of saying, “We’re all about transparency literally.” They didn’t rant, deny, or make things bigger than they were. They simply nudged the narrative back in their favour.But what should a brand do when negativity starts spreading online and you’re suddenly a punchline? Here’s how to navigate that moment with calm and strategy.First, respond without overreacting. You don’t need to mirror the chaos happening online. Woolworths kept things light and steady no defensive press statements or aggressive positioning.

A simple, good humoured acknowledgment is often enough to show you’ve seen the noise but aren’t defined by it.It also helps to lean into what your brand stands for. These moments, even when they’re chaotic, give you a chance to restate your values. Woolworths is built on trust, quality, and ethics and a viral moment can’t erase that unless the brand lets it.Humour can be a powerful tool too. When the public is laughing, it’s sometimes best to smile along with them gently and safely. You don’t mock the situation or the people involved you simply show that you’re human, self aware, and not threatened by the joke.

But humour alone isn’t enough. If you go silent, the meme becomes the whole story. Instead, redirect attention with positive storytelling. Share new products, community initiatives, sustainability work, or feel good moments with staff and customers. Flooding timelines with meaningful content helps shrink the meme back down to its proper size.

Your brand community can also become your biggest shield. Loyal customers often defend you without being asked. By amplifying genuine voices from influencers to everyday shoppers you let the public reshape the narrative organically, which is far more effective than any statement you could publish.

Behind the scenes, every brand should have a plan for these moments. You should know who signs off messaging, what tone to use, what assets are ready, and how to monitor the conversation in real time. A plan turns panic into precision.And finally, know when to step back. There’s a difference between going quiet because you’re scared and going quiet because the moment has passed. Once you’ve steered the conversation back to your values and the noise has died down, let it fade. The internet always moves on unless you keep reminding it not to.

The Woolies moment is a perfect reminder sometimes you don’t get to choose the story that drags your brand into the spotlight. But you do get to choose how you show up. Instead of fighting the trend head on, guide it, reshape it, and move forward with confidence, clarity, and personality.
