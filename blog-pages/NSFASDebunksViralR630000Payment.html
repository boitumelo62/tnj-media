Media NSFAS Debunks Viral R630,000 Payment Claim, Warns Against Misinformation

March 18, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The National Student Financial Aid Scheme has firmly rejected a viral social media claim suggesting that a student received more than R630,000 in funding, describing the post as false and misleading.

The claim, which circulated widely across platforms, alleged that a student had been paid over R630,000 in a single transaction. The post quickly gained traction, sparking confusion and frustration among students who rely on the scheme for financial support. However, NSFAS has clarified that no such payment was ever made.

According to the scheme, initial checks revealed that the image used to support the claim had been manipulated and may have been generated using artificial intelligence. Officials indicated that the content does not match any legitimate transaction within NSFAS systems, dismissing it as fabricated.

NSFAS further stated that the individual behind the post likely created the content to gain attention on social media, rather than to reflect any real financial activity. The scheme expressed concern about the spread of such misinformation, particularly at a time when many students are already dealing with funding uncertainties.

Importantly, NSFAS explained that its funding model does not allow for large lump sum payments to be made directly to students. Instead, funds are transferred to universities and colleges, which then distribute allowances such as accommodation, food, and transport in line with established guidelines. These processes are governed by strict financial controls to prevent irregularities.

The organisation has reassured the public that its systems remain secure and that no breach or error has occurred in connection with the viral claim. At the same time, it has warned that the deliberate creation and sharing of false information could lead to legal consequences.

Students and the public are being urged to rely only on official NSFAS communication channels for accurate updates, as the spread of misleading content continues to pose risks to trust in the system.
