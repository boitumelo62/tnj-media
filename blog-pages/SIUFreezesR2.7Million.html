Business SIU Freezes R2.7 Million Linked to Alleged Irregular Lottery Funded Land Deal

December 18, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Special Investigating Unit has secured a preservation order to freeze R2.7 million linked to the sale of land allegedly funded through irregular National Lottery Commission grants. The move forms part of an ongoing investigation into the misuse of public funds intended for community development and charitable purposes.

According to information released by authorities, the frozen amount is believed to be connected to a land transaction where lottery funds were allegedly diverted from their approved purpose. The SIU says the preservation order is a crucial step to prevent the dissipation of funds while investigations continue. The order allows the state to safeguard the money pending the outcome of possible civil recovery proceedings.

The National Lottery Commission has been under intense scrutiny in recent years following multiple allegations of corruption, maladministration, and the misuse of grants. Funds meant to support vulnerable communities, non profit organisations, and development projects have instead been linked to questionable purchases, including luxury properties and personal assets.

In this case, investigators are examining how the land was acquired, who benefited from the transaction, and whether proper approval processes were followed. The SIU has indicated that several individuals and entities may be implicated, although no names have yet been officially released. Further court applications may follow if evidence supports claims of unlawful enrichment.

Legal experts note that preservation orders are often used in corruption investigations to ensure that funds can be recovered if wrongdoing is proven. If the SIU succeeds in its case, the money could eventually be returned to the state or redirected to legitimate development projects.

The National Lottery Commission has previously stated that it supports efforts to root out corruption and has pledged to strengthen its oversight mechanisms. However, critics argue that repeated investigations point to systemic failures that require deeper reform and stricter accountability.

The freezing of the R2.7 million has renewed calls for transparency in the administration of lottery funds. Civil society organisations say restoring public trust will depend on visible consequences for those who abuse resources meant to uplift communities.

The SIU has confirmed that investigations are ongoing and that more developments are expected as the matter progresses through the courts.
