Media Court Reveals Magudumana Was Paid for Netflix Documentary

September 12, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Popular Blogs:

Thabo Bester Moves to Block Netflix Documentary Release

SABC on the Brink of Collapse, Warns Parliamentary Committee

Proceedings in the Pretoria High Court have revealed that Dr. Nandipha Magudumana was paid by the production company behind the Netflix documentary she is attempting to block. The payment was for archive material used in the three-part series “Beauty and the Bester.”

Magudumana and Thabo Bester have brought an urgent application to interdict Netflix from releasing the documentary. Her legal team argues that the contract allowing the use of her material is invalid and that she had the right to review the final product before its release, a right she claims was denied.

Netflix’s lawyers have countered that Magudumana was properly represented when signing the agreement and that most of the documentary is based on information already in the public domain, including court records and parliamentary proceedings. They argue that blocking the release would amount to restricting media freedom.

Judgment has been reserved and is expected to be delivered shortly before the scheduled release of the documentary. Netflix has also indicated that it may seek a personal costs order against Magudumana and Bester’s legal team, accusing them of abusing the court process.

Thabo Bester, a convicted rapist and murderer, escaped from Mangaung Correctional Centre in May 2022 after allegedly faking his own death. Magudumana, a well-known medical doctor, is accused of assisting him in the escape. The pair were arrested in April 2023 in Tanzania and brought back to South Africa to face trial. The Netflix series “Beauty and the Bester” explores the prison escape, Magudumana’s alleged involvement, and the broader scandal surrounding Bester’s case.
