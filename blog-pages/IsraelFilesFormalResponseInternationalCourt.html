Media Israel Files Formal Response in South Africa’s Genocide Case at International Court

Israel has formally submitted its response in the genocide case brought against it by South Africa at the International Court of Justice, marking a significant development in one of the most closely watched legal battles on the global stage.

The case, filed by South Africa in late 2023, accuses Israel of committing acts of genocide during its military operations in Gaza. South Africa argues that Israel’s actions violate the Genocide Convention, which obliges states to prevent and punish acts of genocide. In its response submitted to the court in The Hague, Israel rejects the allegations, maintaining that its military campaign is directed at the militant group Hamas rather than the civilian population of Gaza.

Israeli officials have consistently argued that the country is exercising its right to self defence following attacks carried out by Hamas militants. South Africa’s case has drawn international attention, with several countries and legal experts closely monitoring the proceedings.

Pretoria’s legal team previously presented arguments claiming that Israeli military operations have resulted in widespread civilian casualties and humanitarian devastation, which they argue meets the threshold for genocidal conduct under international law. The case is expected to take years before a final judgment is delivered. However, the court has already issued provisional measures earlier in the proceedings, instructing Israel to take steps aimed at preventing acts that could fall under the Genocide Convention and to allow humanitarian assistance to reach civilians in Gaza.

The submission of Israel’s response now moves the process into the next legal phase, where both parties will continue presenting arguments and evidence. Legal scholars say the outcome of the case could carry major implications for international humanitarian law and how states are held accountable for conduct during armed conflicts.

For South Africa, the case represents a major diplomatic and legal undertaking, positioning the country at the centre of a global debate on accountability, war and the protection of civilian populations in conflict zones.
