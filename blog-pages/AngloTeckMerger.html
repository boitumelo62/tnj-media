Business Anglo Teck Merger Signals Shift in Global Mining Strategy

September 9, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Anglo American has agreed to merge with Canada’s Teck Resources in what’s shaping up to be one of the largest mining deals in over a decade. The deal is structured as a so-called “zero premium” transaction, meaning neither company is paying extra above the other’s current market value. Instead, shareholders of Anglo American will own just over 62% of the new combined company, while Teck’s investors will hold the remaining 37.6%.

As part of the deal, Anglo will pay its shareholders a one-time $4.5 billion special dividend before the merger is finalized. This confirms earlier reports from Bloomberg suggesting a deal was close. If regulators give the green light, the merger should be completed within 12 to 18 months. This move is part of a broader wave of dealmaking in the mining industry, particularly among the world’s largest players. Both Anglo and Teck have been the targets of takeover attempts in recent years Anglo rebuffed a massive $49 billion offer from BHP Group last year, and Teck turned down a bid from Glencore in 2023.

Those failed deals sparked a new push across the sector, as mining executives began quietly evaluating each other for possible mergers, especially with demand for copper on the rise. Copper is central to the global shift toward renewable energy, and both companies have been focusing on simplifying their businesses to become more copper-focused. Teck recently sold a majority stake in its coal operations to Glencore. Anglo, meanwhile, has exited platinum mining and is trying to sell off both its coal assets and its troubled De Beers diamond business.

Even though Anglo spun off its platinum arm (now Valterra Platinum), its South African iron ore unit, Kumba Iron Ore, will remain an important part of the new merged company. The boards of both companies have fully backed the deal. Anglo’s CEO Duncan Wanblad will lead the new group, while Teck’s CEO Jonathan Price will serve as his deputy. The new company will be headquartered in Vancouver, with corporate offices in London and Johannesburg.

Interestingly, the deal allows either company to consider better offers if they come along, leaving the door open for last-minute bids from competitors. That’s not out of the question Teck’s copper assets have long been attractive, and the news of this merger could easily bring other suitors back to the table. Teck’s flagship project is the QB2 copper mine in Chile, which sits near Anglo’s Collahuasi mine.

Combining these operations could unlock new efficiencies and profit opportunities. This merger may also help Anglo defend itself against future takeover attempts. By growing bigger and more focused, it could become a tougher target for potential acquirers. But the success of this deal still hinges on regulatory approval especially from the Canadian government, which has taken a tougher stance on foreign takeovers of mining firms involved in critical minerals. Ottawa has made it clear that approvals will only be granted in “the most exceptional of circumstances.”

On both sides, this merger comes after some struggles. Teck is reviewing its QB2 mine following years of delays and setbacks, while Anglo is still trying to offload assets that have become harder to sell, like its coal mines and De Beers. The Keevil family, which controls Teck through special high-vote shares, is backing the deal. So is China Investment Corp., Teck’s largest shareholder. But whether the deal survives the scrutiny of regulators or whether it triggers a new round of bidding wars in the sector remains to be seen.
