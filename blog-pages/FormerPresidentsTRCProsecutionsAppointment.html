Media Former Presidents Challenge New TRC Prosecutions Appointment

January 13, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Former presidents Thabo Mbeki and Jacob Zuma have formally lodged objections to the appointment of Justice Sisi Khampepe as chair of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission prosecutions commission. The objections introduce a new layer of complexity to ongoing debates around accountability and historical justice in South Africa.

Both former leaders have raised concerns regarding the appointment process and the mandate of the prosecutions commission, which is tasked with addressing unresolved cases linked to apartheid era crimes. The commission is seen as a key mechanism for advancing long delayed justice and closure for victims.

Legal experts say the objections could result in delays or judicial review, depending on how the matter is handled by relevant authorities. They also note that the move highlights enduring political sensitivities surrounding the legacy of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission.

Supporters of Justice Khampepe argue that her legal credentials and independence make her well suited for the role, particularly given the need for credibility and public trust. Others caution that political interference could undermine the effectiveness of the prosecutions process.

The developments come amid renewed public interest in unresolved TRC matters, with many South Africans calling for decisive action and clarity. Government has yet to indicate whether the objections will alter the appointment or prompt further consultation.
