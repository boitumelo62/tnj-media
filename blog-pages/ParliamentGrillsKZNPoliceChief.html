Media Parliament Grills KZN Police Chief Amid Deepening Crime Allegations Crisis

March 18, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

KwaZulu-Natal Police Commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi has returned to Parliament today, where he is expected to face intense questioning from lawmakers as scrutiny over alleged corruption and criminal influence within the justice system continues to grow.

Mkhwanazi is appearing before Parliament’s SAPS Ad Hoc Committee, a body established in response to explosive claims that emerged in 2025. His testimony forms part of ongoing efforts by legislators to unpack allegations involving senior police officials, politicians, and suspected underworld figures.

The commissioner’s reappearance is particularly significant because he was the first witness to testify when the committee began its work last year. His earlier statements, including a widely publicised press briefing in July 2025, played a key role in triggering both the parliamentary inquiry and the broader commission investigating systemic failures in law enforcement.

Since then, multiple testimonies have painted a troubling picture of alleged interference in policing and the justice system. Claims of collusion between criminal networks and individuals within state institutions have raised serious concerns about governance, accountability, and public trust.

During today’s session, Mkhwanazi is expected to respond directly to issues raised by other witnesses who have already appeared before the committee. His testimony is likely to address contradictions, clarify earlier statements, and provide further insight into the extent of alleged corruption within policing structures.

The hearing also marks what is expected to be his closing appearance before the committee, adding further weight to the proceedings. Lawmakers are under pressure to extract clear answers that could inform future recommendations and possible reforms within the South African Police Service.

The outcome of these engagements may prove critical in shaping the next phase of investigations and determining whether accountability measures will follow. For many South Africans, the process represents a pivotal moment in confronting allegations that have shaken confidence in the country’s criminal justice system.
