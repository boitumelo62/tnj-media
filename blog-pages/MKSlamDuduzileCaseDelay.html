Media MK Party Slams Court for Delay in Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla Case

November 13, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party has slammed what it calls “judicial stalling” after the case against Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla, daughter of former president Jacob Zuma, was once again postponed in the Johannesburg Regional Court.

Zuma-Sambudla faces charges of incitement to commit public violence linked to the July 2021 unrest that claimed over 300 lives in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng. The unrest followed the imprisonment of her father and was one of the most destructive episodes in post-democracy South Africa.

The court adjourned proceedings to next Monday after the defence requested additional time to finalise documents and consult with key witnesses. Prosecutors confirmed that the state was ready to proceed but did not object to the postponement.

Reacting to the decision, the MK Party issued a strongly worded statement accusing the courts of deliberately dragging out the case. “This constant postponement is nothing, but a delaying tactic aimed at frustrating justice and weakening revolutionary forces,” said MK Party spokesperson Nhlamulo Ndhlela. “Our comrade is being persecuted for her political beliefs and her association with progressive movements that challenge the status quo.”

The National Prosecuting Authority has rejected suggestions of bias, maintaining that all procedures are being handled in accordance with the law. Legal experts say the defence’s approach appears strategic, as delays can often be used to exhaust public interest and weaken the state’s case.

Zuma-Sambudla has remained active on social media throughout her trial, continuing to criticise the government and voice support for her father’s political legacy. The case continues to attract strong political attention, with MK Party supporters expected to gather outside court when proceedings resume next week.

If convicted, Zuma-Sambudla could face a significant prison term or a suspended sentence, depending on the court’s findings on intent and direct involvement in incitement.
