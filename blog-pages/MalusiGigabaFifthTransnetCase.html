Malusi Gigaba Expected To Hand Himself Over Amid Transnet Corruption Case

Former minister Malusi Gigaba has been formally charged and added as the fifth accused in the long running Transnet corruption investigation. The charges relate to allegations surrounding procurement processes, large scale cost inflation, and the awarding of contracts during his time in public office.

According to the National Prosecuting Authority, the case is rooted in claims that senior officials and associates manipulated procurement decisions to benefit specific private companies while enabling large sums of public money to be diverted through inflated contracts. Gigaba has been linked to decisions taken during a period when Transnet faced severe governance failures and escalating project costs.

Prosecutors allege that during key procurement stages Gigaba either enabled or failed to prevent irregular conduct that contributed to the losses suffered by the state owned company. The investigation focuses particularly on major rail and port upgrade projects that ballooned in cost and became central to broader state capture claims in the logistics sector.

Gigaba appeared in court earlier today where the charges were read out. His legal team has indicated that he will contest the allegations, insisting that political motives and unreliable testimony have distorted the case. They argue that decisions attributed to him were taken within collective structures and that he did not personally direct procurement outcomes.

The state however maintains that Gigaba was positioned at the heart of strategic oversight and exercised authority that linked him directly to the alleged wrongdoing. Prosecutors say the evidence includes communications, meeting records, and testimonies from former Transnet officials who claim they were pressured to approve contracts that did not meet proper evaluation standards.

The addition of Gigaba as the fifth accused extends the scope of the case and increases scrutiny on the network of individuals alleged to have benefited from irregular contracting. Investigators say they expect more witnesses to testify about the decision making environment within Transnet and the extent to which political office bearers influenced operational processes.

The case is regarded as one of the most significant corruption matters currently before the courts, given Transnets role in the countrys economy and the scale of the financial losses alleged. Proceedings are expected to continue throughout next year as the state seeks to establish the full chain of responsibility behind the procurement decisions now under examination.
