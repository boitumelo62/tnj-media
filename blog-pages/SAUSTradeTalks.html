Business South Africa Still Waiting on US to Set Dates for Trade Talks

January 22, 2026 ● Tebogo Zongwana

South Africa is still waiting for the United States to confirm dates to resume bilateral trade negotiations, a delay that government officials say is slowing progress on issues critical to economic stability and job protection. Trade and Industry Minister Parks Tau has confirmed that while communication between the two countries remains open, the next phase of formal discussions cannot begin until dates are set by the US government.

The negotiations are expected to address trade frameworks that support South African exports, particularly in sectors such as manufacturing, agriculture and automotive production. These industries rely heavily on predictable access to the US market and employ thousands of workers across the country. Any uncertainty in trade arrangements can have a direct impact on production planning, investment decisions and long-term growth strategies.

Economists have warned that prolonged delays in trade talks may increase pressure on exporters already facing challenges from global economic volatility, rising input costs and logistical constraints. Business leaders have repeatedly called for clarity, arguing that stable trade relations with major partners like the United States are essential for rebuilding confidence in the local economy.

Tau has emphasised that South Africa remains committed to constructive and mutually beneficial engagement. He has indicated that the country is prepared to move forward as soon as dates are confirmed, adding that strong trade ties benefit both economies by supporting growth, innovation and employment.

Government has also stressed that trade negotiations form part of a broader strategy to strengthen international partnerships while advancing domestic industrial development. As the country awaits a response from Washington, officials say they will continue engaging through diplomatic channels to ensure momentum is not lost.
