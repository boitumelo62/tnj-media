Lifestyle Twice-Weekly Exercise Significantly Reduces Risk of Diabetes, Heart Disease and Early Death

A new study has found that engaging in just two workout sessions each week can cut the risk of developing major health problems such as type 2 diabetes, heart disease and premature death by up to 33 percent.

Researchers from the National Centre for Sport and Exercise Medicine in the UK analyzed health data from over 8,000 adults between the ages of 40 and 69. Participants were monitored over a span of several years, with particular attention paid to the frequency and intensity of their physical activity. The study revealed that individuals who exercised intensely one or two days per week experienced nearly the same health benefits as those who spread moderate activity across several days. Activities that qualified included running, cycling, swimming or playing sports that elevate heart rate.

Lead author Dr. Aiden Doherty emphasized that the findings highlight how even a small commitment to physical activity can lead to meaningful improvements in long-term health outcomes. Crucially, the research found that these infrequent but vigorous exercise sessions lowered the likelihood of early death and reduced the chances of being diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. The results were consistent regardless of gender or pre-existing risk factors such as weight and smoking history.

Experts believe the findings offer reassurance to those with busy schedules who may struggle to meet the recommended 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week. According to the team, quality and intensity appear to be as important as frequency. The researchers hope the study will encourage more adults to adopt at least some level of physical activity, noting that even limited workouts can play a vital role in boosting overall health.
