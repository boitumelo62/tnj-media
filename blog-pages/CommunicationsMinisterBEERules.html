Business Communications Minister Defends Easing of BEE Rules for Foreign ICT Investors

December 17, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Communications Minister Solly Malatsi has defended his decision to relax Black Economic Empowerment (BEE) requirements for foreign investors in South Africa’s ICT sector amid rising debate over economic transformation and local enterprise opportunities. The directive, aimed at attracting international investment, has generated controversy among industry leaders, opposition parties, and empowerment advocates.

Malatsi stated that the move is intended to stimulate investment in a sector critical to national development while maintaining provisions for local participation. “Our goal is to create an environment where South Africa remains competitive globally without abandoning the principles of empowerment,” he said. Critics, however, argue that the relaxation of BEE requirements may undermine decades of progress in creating equitable opportunities for local ICT businesses.

Industry stakeholders are divided. Some welcome the initiative, suggesting it will accelerate technology infrastructure, innovation, and job creation. Others remain wary, pointing out that foreign companies might exploit the relaxed rules without contributing meaningfully to local enterprise or skills development. Economists have weighed in, emphasizing the need for carefully structured safeguards to balance investment growth with empowerment objectives.

Malatsi confirmed ongoing stakeholder consultations to ensure the policy meets both investment and local development goals. He insisted that measures remain in place to promote skills transfer, technology partnerships, and meaningful participation for South African businesses and workers. The minister added that regulatory frameworks will be monitored closely to prevent abuse and ensure compliance.

The debate has sparked broader discussions about South Africa’s approach to economic transformation in a globalized economy. Proponents argue that attracting foreign investment is essential for national growth, while critics fear it could dilute the intent of BEE policies. Analysts note that the ICT sector’s rapid expansion requires flexible strategies to balance growth and inclusion effectively.

As the government continues to refine the approach, opposition parties have called for transparent reporting and clear benchmarks to ensure local interests are protected. Public engagement sessions are expected to be held to address concerns and provide clarity on the policy’s implementation.
