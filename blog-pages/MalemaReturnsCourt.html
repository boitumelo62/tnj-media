Media Malema Returns to Court as Sentencing Phase Looms

January 23, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

EFF leader Julius Malema is set to appear again at the East London Magistrate’s Court on Friday, where proceedings will move into the pre-sentencing stage following his conviction on several firearms related offences.

Malema was found guilty on charges that include unlawful possession of a firearm and ammunition, discharging a weapon in a public space, and reckless endangerment. Some of these convictions carry prescribed minimum sentences that could extend to as much as 15 years imprisonment.

According to legal analyst Ulrich Roux, the court will now shift its focus to determining an appropriate sentence by carefully weighing a range of considerations. These include Malema’s personal circumstances, the seriousness of the crimes, and the broader message the judgment sends to society.

Roux explains that sentencing is not only about punishment but also about deterrence and accountability. The court must decide whether the outcome signals a firm stance against similar conduct or reflects leniency based on remorse and mitigating factors.

As part of this process, Malema is expected to submit a pre sentence report, typically prepared by a social worker or an official from the Department of Correctional Services. This report aims to persuade the court that a non custodial sentence would be appropriate, meaning that Malema would avoid serving time behind bars.

Roux notes that substantial evidence may still be presented before a final sentence is delivered, highlighting that the matter is far from concluded.

The stakes extend beyond the courtroom and into the political arena. Under South African law, any Member of Parliament sentenced to more than 12 months in prison without the option of a fine automatically loses their parliamentary seat.

Roux cautions that Malema’s attitude during sentencing could prove decisive. A genuine display of remorse could work in his favour, while continued claims that the case is politically motivated may harm his position.

If responsibility is not accepted and remorse is absent, Roux warns that a prison sentence exceeding 12 months remains a real possibility, a development that could significantly reshape Malema’s political future.
