Lifestyle Scorpion Kings Set to Light Up Loftus in Historic Amapiano Showcase

August 30, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The countdown is on, and the energy is already in the air. Come Saturday, 30 August 2025, Loftus Versfeld Stadium in Pretoria will be transformed into the heart of amapiano. This isn’t just another big gig for Phori it’s personal. It’s a celebration. It’s home. He and Kabza De Small, the legendary duo known as the Scorpion Kings, are gearing up to deliver a night that will go down in music history.

It’s more than a show; it’s an amapiano experience, one that brings the community together to honour a genre that’s grown from the townships into a worldwide movement. Maphorisa, in his usual calm but confident way, made it clear what this night means. “Piano by any other name is not amapiano,” he said with that trademark smile. He’s not just hyping it up. This is real for him the beats, the stories, the rise of a sound that’s deeply South African.

From soulful melodies to dance-floor anthems, the concert will be a full-spectrum celebration of where amapiano has come from and where it’s going. Expect dancers like Tom London to add flair, expect surprise guest performers, expect unforgettable moments. While they’re keeping tight-lipped about who exactly will hit the stage, the vibe around the show promises something special. These two are known for shaking things up you never know who might step up to the mic or the decks.

The day kicks off early, with gates opening at 1 PM. From 2 to 6 PM, it’s all about showcasing fresh amapiano talent – the future of the genre, live and direct. Then, from 6 PM into the night, it’s the main event. And trust, it’s going to be packed. But beyond the pyrotechnics and big drops, this concert is about more than music. It’s about the culture, the unity, and the power of sound to bring people together.

What started in small township rooms is now filling stadiums. And on this night, amapiano speaks for itself proudly, loudly, and with heart. As Maphorisa put it simply, “This is more than a concert. It’s amapiano telling its own story.”
