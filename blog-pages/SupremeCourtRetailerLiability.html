Media Supreme Court Ruling Puts Retailer Liability Under Sharp Legal Spotlight

January 29, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Supreme Court of Appeal has ruled that Pick n Pay can be held liable for damages claimed by Maria Williams, the widow of late Springbok rugby legend Chester Williams, following a slip and fall incident at one of the retailer’s Cape Town stores in 2017.

The judgment confirms that retailers have a legal duty to ensure their premises are reasonably safe for customers. In this case, the court found that Pick n Pay failed to demonstrate that sufficient measures were in place to prevent the hazardous condition that caused the fall.

Maria Williams sustained serious injuries during the incident, which she argues had a lasting impact on her quality of life. The ruling does not yet determine the final amount of damages to be paid but clears a major legal hurdle by establishing liability.

Legal experts say the decision reinforces existing principles around occupiers’ liability while sending a clear message to large retailers about accountability. Businesses open to the public are expected to take proactive steps to identify and address risks rather than react only after incidents occur.

The case has drawn public attention due to the Williams family’s prominence, but analysts note that its implications extend far beyond this single matter. The ruling could influence how similar claims are approached in future and may encourage stricter safety protocols across the retail sector.

Pick n Pay has not yet indicated whether it will pursue further legal avenues. The matter now proceeds to the damages phase, where the extent of compensation will be assessed.
