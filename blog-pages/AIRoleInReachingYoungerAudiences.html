Media Rethinking AI’s Role in Reaching Younger Audiences

July 23, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Popular Blogs:

Media

Rethinking Traditional Advertising

Media

The Future of Advertising Is Prompted

Younger consumers are often seen as early adopters of new technology, so it might seem natural to assume they’d be all in on AI. But Gen Z and Millennials, who’ve grown up surrounded by tech, are starting to ask harder questions. They understand both the convenience and the downsides of artificial intelligence, especially when it shows up in advertising. Increasingly, they’re pushing for a more genuine, human connection with the brands they engage with.

When should AI step in, and when is it time to step back?

AI is incredibly useful behind the scenes. For market researchers, it’s a goldmine offering deep insights into customer preferences, behaviours and trends. This helps brands tailor products and messages to individual people in ways that feel almost personal. It's also being used in more public ways—think chatbots handling complaints or virtual influencers promoting everything from high fashion to energy drinks. But the enthusiasm is starting to wane. In fact, consumer comfort with AI in advertising dropped from 57% in 2023 to just 46% in 2024, according to Statista.

Luzuko Tena, Chair of the IAB SA Youth Action Council, has worked across brand, social and advertising, and he sees the challenge clearly. The next generation of marketers, he says, is being pulled in two directions. On one hand, there’s a demand for precision data, metrics and performance. On the other, there’s a real need to stay emotionally connected with people. In short, knowing the numbers isn’t enough. You have to understand the people behind them.

This push for authenticity also brings influencers into the spotlight. Some consumers are wary of AI-generated influencers, but that doesn’t mean human influencers automatically get a pass. After all, a feed full of perfectly curated, filtered content can feel just as artificial.

Anele Maphanga, who leads PR and influencer work at Unilever, believes that what makes an influencer feel authentic is purpose. It's not about just selling products—it’s about standing for something, whether that’s mental health, sustainability or culture. When content reflects a person’s real values and life experiences, it resonates.

Live streaming, says Lineo Msimka from The Foschini Group, takes that authenticity a step further. It’s raw, real, and largely unedited. People show up as they are, and that vulnerability builds trust. She points to streamer Kai Cenat, whose relatable content helped him amass millions of followers. Tapping into that kind of authenticity can be a powerful way for brands to connect.

Still, influencer marketing has to be handled with care. Pierre Cassuto, CMO of Humanz, warns that scripted, robotic influencer content won’t fool anyone. Audiences can tell when someone’s just repeating a brand message. Instead, marketers should trust influencers to use their own voices and bring their own personality to the table. That’s where real connection happens.

Cassuto also highlights the role of community. People aren’t just looking for content—they’re looking for connection. Social media gives brands a way to be part of those conversations, but it’s not about creating a branded bubble and hoping people show up. It’s about joining in where people already are, contributing something meaningful and understanding the platform culture. In other words, don’t throw a party no one asked for. Be a guest who adds value to the one already happening.

So, is there still a place for AI in all of this? Absolutely—but context is everything. As Cassuto points out, AI is efficient. It can create content faster, cheaper and often more effectively. Maphanga adds that it depends on what the content is for. Unilever’s Dove campaign, for example, is built on the idea of celebrating real beauty—so using AI-generated women wouldn’t fit. But when it comes to things like product shots? No problem.

Younger audiences are sharp. They can spot AI content almost instantly. That doesn’t mean it should be scrapped altogether—it just means it can’t be the only tool in the box. The real magic happens when brands find a balance: using data and automation where it makes sense, but always leading with authenticity, storytelling, and the kind of human touch that builds lasting trust.
