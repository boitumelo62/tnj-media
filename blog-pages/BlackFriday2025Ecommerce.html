Business Black Friday 2025 Marks a New Era in South African E-Commerce

October 9, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Black Friday 2025, landing on 28 November, offers an enormous opportunity for South African retailers. To put things in perspective, consumers spent over R30 billion through just three banks during Black Friday 2024. That’s a 14% year on year increase in online transactions alone. And that’s just a slice of the total impact Nedbank alone processed over R3 billion in settlements, while the wider economic boost reached R88 billion, with December retail sales surging to R169 billion compared to the R114 billion monthly average.

But here’s the catch, if you're only planning for the Black Friday weekend, you’re missing out on the vast majority of the potential. In fact, over 80% of the opportunity lies in what happens before and after those few days. That’s something The Digital Media Collective (TDMC) has seen firsthand. They've helped South African e-commerce brands navigate peak periods for years, and they’ve picked up some key insights along the way. A major takeaway is that consumer behaviour has shifted. People are no longer only shopping on Black Friday. November has become a warm up period shoppers are scouting deals, wishlisting products, and preparing for the festive season in general.

A smart calendar spreads the strategy across three stages, start early in November with teaser campaigns and acquisition efforts, ramp up through Cyber Week, go strong into December with festive promotions that focus on increasing basket size, and finish with January campaigns aimed at back to school and resolution shoppers while clearing out remaining stock. This wider approach means you're not just capturing sales, but building momentum. Another big shift is how people are paying. Card payments have dropped from 93% in 2022 to 84% in 2024.

Buy Now, Pay Later services like PayJustNow and Mobicred, digital wallets like SnapScan and PayFast, and services like PayShap are becoming essential. If your store doesn’t offer them, you risk losing sales. Same goes for mobile optimisation since mobile now accounts for 67% of all transactions, slow load times or clunky checkouts can cost you big. When it comes to discounts, TDMC advises caution. Yes, shoppers love deals, but offering steep discounts across the board can erode your brand’s long term value.

This builds your first party data while keeping your pricing strategy sustainable. Your creative and media strategy needs just as much thought. As the holiday season wears on, consumers are bombarded with promotions, so the same ad over and over won’t cut it. Aim for 5–8 creative variations tailored to different audiences, and be prepared to ramp up your media budget by at least 25–30% to stay competitive. Platforms like Google, Meta, and TikTok are especially important if you want to reach younger buyers who are heavily influenced by social commerce.

But don’t stop at the sale.
Post purchase, you need smooth communication order confirmations, shipping updates, and customer service that’s actually helpful. For brands that can’t or don’t want to compete on discounts like premium or luxury labels there are alternatives. Campaigns that focus on quality, sustainability (think “Green Friday”), local support, or experience based offerings can still capitalise on the season without hurting your positioning.

It’s also important to remember that Black Friday doesn’t end when the sale ends. Brands that treat it as a one and done event lose out. Instead, use it as a gateway to build longer term relationships.
