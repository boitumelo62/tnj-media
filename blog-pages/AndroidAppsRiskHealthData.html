Tech News Android Apps Risk Exposing Sensitive Health Data

February 25, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Millions of Android users could be unknowingly exposing highly sensitive medical information through popular mental health applications available on the Google Play Store, according to a recent security report.

Researchers from mobile security firm Oversecured analysed a selection of mental health and wellbeing apps and discovered a large number of vulnerabilities that could allow hackers to access confidential data. The affected apps, which have collectively been installed more than 14 million times, store deeply personal information such as therapy session records, medication schedules, mood tracking data and indicators of self harm.

The investigation identified more than 1,500 security flaws across just ten applications, with dozens rated as high or medium risk. Some vulnerabilities could allow attackers to intercept login credentials, manipulate notifications or gain access to therapy transcripts and other protected health information.

Experts warn that medical records are especially valuable to cybercriminals, often fetching far higher prices on illicit markets than financial data. Even apps that claim to use encryption were found to have weaknesses that could still expose user data if exploited.

Privacy advocates say the findings highlight ongoing risks in digital health platforms, particularly as more people turn to mobile apps for mental health support. While such apps can provide convenient access to resources and monitoring tools, poor security practices can leave vulnerable users exposed to data breaches and potential exploitation.

Google Play Store policies require developers to protect sensitive user information, but researchers say enforcement and security testing remain inconsistent. Users are advised to be cautious about the permissions they grant, keep apps updated, and avoid sharing unnecessary personal details through mobile platforms.

The report adds to growing concerns about privacy and cybersecurity in the expanding digital health sector, where convenience is often prioritised over robust data protection measures.
