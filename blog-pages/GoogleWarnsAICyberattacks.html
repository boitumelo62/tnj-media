Tech News Google warns AI tools are being used to accelerate cyberattacks

February 10, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Google has warned that its Gemini AI system is being abused by cybercriminals and state backed hacking groups to speed up digital attacks, according to a new report from the Google Threat Intelligence Group.

The company said it has observed actors linked to China, Iran, North Korea and Russia using Gemini at different stages of cyber operations, including researching targets, crafting social engineering messages, translating content, testing vulnerabilities and fixing broken code during intrusions.

Google stressed that AI is not introducing entirely new hacking methods, but is helping attackers work faster and more efficiently. Tasks that once required time consuming manual effort can now be automated or completed in minutes, reducing delays that defenders might otherwise detect.

In one example, a China linked operator used Gemini to simulate an expert cybersecurity analyst, generating vulnerability assessments and tailored testing plans in a fictional scenario. Other actors reportedly used the tool for technical guidance and debugging while carrying out intrusions.

Security researchers said the biggest concern is not more sophisticated attacks, but the increased pace of operations. Faster targeting and development cycles give organisations less time to detect threats and respond before damage occurs.

Google also highlighted a separate risk involving model extraction, where actors with legitimate access attempt to replicate an AI model’s behaviour by flooding it with prompts and analysing the responses. This could lead to intellectual property theft and the creation of competing models with similar capabilities.

The company said it has acted by disabling accounts linked to abuse and strengthening detection systems within Gemini. Additional safeguards and monitoring are being implemented to prevent further misuse.

For cybersecurity teams, Google’s message is clear: expect AI assisted attacks to move quicker rather than smarter and prepare for shorter windows between early warning signs and real world impact.
