Lifestyle How Plant Parenting Is Helping South Africans Heal

October 19, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

There’s a new trend that’s really taken off, especially among millennials and Gen Z, and it’s all about plants. More than just a hobby or home decor, this movement being called “the green shift” is about turning to plants for emotional and mental wellness. People are now using plant parenting as a form of self care, and it’s becoming a way to feel more grounded in an increasingly fast paced and uncertain world. South African plant stylist Nkhensani Rikhotso says this isn't just something happening locally it’s a global thing.

All over the world, from New York cafés to Tokyo apartments, people are filling their spaces with greenery. But what makes the South African version of this trend unique is how it blends in with local culture. Instead of just copying Pinterest or Instagram styles, people here are mixing indigenous plants with tropical ones and using recycled pots or locally inspired designs. It’s not just about having green in your home it’s about creating spaces that feel expressive and meaningful. A big push behind this shift came during the Covid-19 lockdowns. When we couldn’t go outside, people brought nature indoors.

And it turned out to be more than just a temporary fix people noticed they felt calmer, more relaxed, and even happier. Plants started to represent something deeper: a way to care for something, and in turn, care for yourself. When it comes to what kinds of plants are popular, Rikhotso says it depends on your lifestyle. Some people go for hardy, forgiving plants like snake plants, peace lilies, and pathos (which are great for beginners). Others love more dramatic or sculptural plants like the monstera (also called the Swiss cheese plant because of the holes in its leaves) or the fiddle leaf fig.

Then there are trailing plants like string of pearls and string of hearts that add softness and movement to a room. But this movement isn’t just about aesthetics. Rikhotso works as a plant stylist, which is like being an interior designer but focused on plants. She helps people choose plants that match their space, lifestyle, and energy. It’s not just about putting a pretty pot in the corner it’s about understanding light, temperature, and even how often someone travels. That way, they get plants that they can actually care for and connect with.

She’s been doing this for about five years now, and it all started when she began sharing her plant journey on social media. People were drawn in, started asking for advice, and it naturally grew into a career. For her, plants are more than decor they’re a form of therapy. Watering her plants on Saturdays is her quiet time, her way of being present. And interestingly, she says her plants reflect her mood. If she’s not doing well emotionally, her plants start to droop too.

Earlier in 2024, she even hosted a plant parenting workshop for beginners, and it turned out to be a huge success. Events like the Houghton Bonsai Succulent and Plant Show in Johannesburg offer even more opportunities for both newbies and experienced plant lovers to explore the world of greenery. At the heart of all this is a simple idea: taking care of something living helps you take care of yourself. It’s not just a trend it’s a quiet, meaningful shift in how people are choosing to live.
