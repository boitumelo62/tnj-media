Media What 2025 Taught South African Marketers And How to Win in 2026

December 12, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Why South African Brands Need to Think Like Newsrooms in 2026

2025 marked a turning point for marketers in South Africa. Consumer behaviour shifted faster than anyone expected, technology advanced at full speed, and brands that once leaned on traditional media rhythms suddenly had to rethink everything from strategy and storytelling to the pace at which they operate. It wasn’t a year defined by trends for the sake of being trendy it was about clarity, precision and showing up where South Africans actually are.

AI moved from being a nice to have to a non negotiable. It became the engine behind content creation, customer journeys, predictive insights and smarter media buying. As Nofal puts it, AI levelled the playing field. It allowed brands to scale quickly, reach wider audiences and do more without burning through budgets. Anyone who ignored it in 2025 was already falling behind.Personalisation also became mainstream. With such a diverse mix of languages, cultures and regions, South African brands used data and AI to craft messages that felt personal and relevant. Whether it was a product recommendation tailored to someone’s habits or messaging in their home language, this approach made every interaction feel intentional. It strengthened loyalty by showing customers that brands were finally listening.

Short form video dominated the year. TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts pulled in audiences across generations, with Gen Z leading the surge. Brands leaned heavily into micro influencers, more vernacular content and shoppable videos. As Nofal notes, short form video became the heartbeat of digital culture people now discover products, form opinions and connect emotionally in under 10 seconds.Purpose driven marketing also became a decisive factor in purchasing behaviour. South Africans especially millennials and Gen Z gravitate toward brands that stand for something real, whether it’s sustainability, social impact or ethical practices. Purpose wasn’t just a marketing angle it became a trust marker. And where trust grew, so did engagement and sales.

The creator economy continued its rise, becoming a massive force in its own right. With global creator spend climbing and South Africa embracing the same momentum, brands increasingly turned to micro and nano influencers. These creators, with their niche but deeply engaged communities, often delivered more real and relatable impact than big celebrities. Their authenticity became a powerful currency.

Looking back, 2025 taught marketers a simple truth marketing isn’t about making noise, it’s about making connections. The brands that succeeded were the ones that embraced technology, understood local cultures and communicated with honesty. As 2026 approaches, the message is clear stay agile, stay real and stay human, even in a digital first world. Adaptability will continue to be the determining factor in a landscape as vibrant and fast moving as South Africa.
