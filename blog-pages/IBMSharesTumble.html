Business IBM Shares Tumble as AI Tool Sparks Fears Over Legacy Systems

February 24, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Shares in International Business Machines suffered their sharpest single day decline in more than two decades after investors reacted to a new artificial intelligence tool that could disrupt one of the company’s most entrenched technologies.

IBM’s stock dropped by more than 13 percent on Monday, marking its worst daily performance since 2000. The sell off was driven by concerns that AI driven software could accelerate the replacement of COBOL, a decades old programming language that still underpins many IBM mainframe systems used by banks, insurers, and government departments.

The market reaction followed an announcement by AI startup Anthropic, which introduced a new coding assistant designed to help organisations modernise legacy systems. In a blog post, the company said artificial intelligence could significantly reduce the time and cost involved in updating COBOL based infrastructure, which traditionally required large teams and lengthy projects.

Analysts say the announcement raised questions about the long-term demand for IBM’s mainframe services, which have remained a stable revenue source due to the complexity of replacing older systems. Investors appear to be reassessing whether AI could speed up the shift away from proprietary platforms toward newer architectures.

The broader technology sector also came under pressure, with several software and cybersecurity stocks declining as investors weighed the disruptive potential of advanced AI tools. Market sentiment has been fragile in recent months as companies and investors debate how quickly artificial intelligence could transform established business models.

Anthropic’s growing profile has also drawn the attention of policymakers. Reports indicate that its leadership is expected to engage with United States defence officials over the potential military use of its AI systems, highlighting the strategic and ethical questions surrounding the technology.

While IBM has not publicly commented on the market reaction, industry observers say the episode underscores the growing tension between legacy technology providers and emerging AI driven platforms. As artificial intelligence tools mature, traditional software giants may face increasing pressure to adapt their business models or risk losing long standing clients.
