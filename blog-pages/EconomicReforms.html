Business Government Urged to Fast Track Economic Reforms

September 22, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

South Africa is at a critical juncture, and it can no longer wait for external forces to fix the country’s struggling economy. The government must accelerate reforms and urgently address the issues plaguing state owned companies. Busi Mavuso, CEO of Business Leadership South Africa (BLSA), points out that while recent efforts from the state are positive, like negotiating lower tariffs with the United States and strengthening trade relations across the globe, there are no guarantees these efforts will bear fruit. Therefore, the government must prepare for all scenarios, making it clear that South Africa’s solutions must come from within, not from waiting on external help.

Mavuso emphasizes that the coming months will test the country’s resolve and adaptability. While there may be breakthroughs in trade talks or challenges ahead, the key goal remains, South Africa must drive its economic transformation through diversified partnerships, better infrastructure, and building investor confidence. The slight growth South Africa achieved in the second quarter of the year, with a 0.8% increase in GDP, was encouraging, but Mavuso notes that this growth is far from enough. South Africa cannot afford to rely solely on outside solutions, and it is time for both business and government to collaborate with urgency to unlock the investments and productivity needed for sustainable growth and job creation.

Although the country’s growth trajectory showed improvement, the investment portion of the GDP figures was disappointing, with investment levels at their lowest since 2003. Public corporations like Transnet and Eskom were particularly weak. Mavuso says the government’s infrastructure investment target of R1 trillion over the next three years is crucial, but so far, these plans aren’t getting off the ground. She stressed that the country’s efforts to resolve its energy and logistics crises are critical to making this happen.

There is a significant gap between the ambitious plans and the reality of execution. Regarding trade with the United States, there’s still no certainty that the 30% tariffs on South African imports will be reduced. Despite President Ramaphosa attending the UN General Assembly and Trade Minister Parks Tau’s recent visit to the US, Mavuso suggests that while discussions show momentum, South Africa can’t rely on the US to change its policies. These tariffs are affecting key sectors, especially automotive and agriculture, and have already led to job losses as factories shut down production lines.

Mavuso also notes that while South Africa remains an important trade partner for the US, its sub 1% GDP growth is making it less relevant compared to faster growing economies like Angola and Nigeria. To make matters more complicated, the US is also demanding changes to domestic policies, like foreign ownership rules, making a simple resolution unlikely. She points out that US policies are unpredictable, and South Africa cannot afford to put all its hopes on them. Instead, Mavuso stresses that South Africa should lean into alternatives. The African Continental Free Trade Agreement can help boost trade within Africa, and agreements like those signed with India and the UK can simplify trade and reduce barriers.

The BRICS partnership offers enhanced trade and investment opportunities and a chance for a more equitable global trade system. The Department of Trade, Industry, and Competition (DTIC) is also planning a trade mission to Indonesia, Vietnam, and Malaysia next month to explore new trading corridors. These efforts are gaining support from businesses and reflect a global trend where countries are diversifying away from the US economy. Mavuso urges South Africa to seize every opportunity to build new markets and secure its place in the global economy.
