Business Economic Uncertainty Weighs on SA Growth

August 14, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Standard Bank has revised its expectations for South Africa’s economic growth in 2025, now predicting the country’s GDP will grow by less than 1% specifically, around 0.9%. This is a step down from the 1.7% it projected earlier this year. The bank attributes the downgrade to global uncertainty, ongoing trade tensions particularly the United States' 30% tariff announcements and local political developments that have shaken investor and business confidence. While there's hope for some recovery in 2026, with growth expected to tick up to 1.3%, this is still lower than what the bank previously anticipated.

Despite the less optimistic economic outlook, Standard Bank remains confident in its financial performance. In the first half of 2025, the group posted an 8% rise in headline earnings, reaching R24 billion, and delivered a strong return on equity of 19.1%. Its operations in South Africa contributed R11.6 billion to earnings, while the broader African region added R9.7 billion, and offshore businesses brought in R1.6 billion. Countries like Angola, Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, and Zambia continued to be strong contributors to its performance across the continent.

Inflation in South Africa has been trending lower and is now sitting comfortably below the Reserve Bank’s 3–6% target range. This has allowed for some relief in interest rates, with cuts totaling 75 basis points so far this year. Inflation is expected to remain low through the rest of 2025 and into 2026, and while the repo rate is expected to stay flat for now, a further cut may come early next year. However, the central bank’s preference to keep inflation closer to 3% makes the longer-term interest rate path harder to predict.

Even though trade tensions and uncertainty are affecting global markets, Standard Bank doesn't see this derailing long-term opportunities across Africa. The bank continues to see the continent as a key growth area. For the full year, it’s aiming for mid to high single-digit growth in banking revenue (measured in rands), a stable or slightly improved cost-to-income ratio, and a return on equity that remains within its target range of 17% to 20%.

With headline earnings per share expected to grow by 8% to 12%, the bank’s outlook for its own performance remains solid. It’s upped its interim dividend by 10%, with a payout ratio of 56%, showing confidence in its results even in the face of weaker economic growth at home.
