Media Johannesburg Residents Rise Up Amid Citywide Service Collapse

October 30, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Johannesburg, once celebrated as South Africa’s economic powerhouse, is facing a deepening crisis that has driven residents, businesses, and civic groups to unite in an unprecedented call for accountability and reform.

On Saturday, 1 November 2025, citizens are expected to gather outside the Johannesburg Council Chambers in a peaceful protest demanding urgent action to resolve what they describe as a “human rights and economic emergency.”

At the centre of their frustration is the worsening water shortage that has left entire suburbs without a reliable supply for weeks at a time. The coalition organising the protest said that residents have run out of patience after years of empty promises and inconsistent service delivery.

“This is no longer just a water issue,” the group said in a statement. “It is about dignity, responsibility, and the right to live in a functioning city.”

The coalition has submitted a formal letter to Executive Mayor Dada Morero, outlining eight key demands. Among them are the protection of municipal funds intended for water infrastructure, the recovery of the missing R4 billion from Joburg Water, and the inclusion of civil society and business leaders on the city’s water board for proper oversight.

Saturday’s gathering will feature testimonies from residents and experts, calling directly on the Mayor and the Gauteng government to respond transparently. Organisers have stressed that their aim is collaboration, not confrontation, saying that communities, government, and the private sector must work together to rebuild public confidence and restore basic services.

Depending on the city’s reaction, the group plans either to escalate its campaign or to work with officials on meaningful reforms that can stabilise Johannesburg’s failing systems.
