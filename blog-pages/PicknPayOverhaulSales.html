Business Strategic Overhaul Lifts Pick n Pay’s Sales

October 10, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Pick n Pay is starting to show signs of improvement after a rough couple of years. The company had a tough run with its failed Ekuseni strategy, significant financial losses, and even found itself technically insolvent. To turn things around, it made big changes including bringing back former CEO Sean Summers, launching a R4 billion rights offer, and listing one of its strongest performing assets, Boxer.

In its latest update, covering the 26 weeks up to 31 August 2025, Pick n Pay reported some encouraging developments. Group turnover increased by 4.9%, with like for like sales up 4.7% compared to the same time last year. This was driven by improved performance across the company’s different supermarket formats, especially in the last two months of the reporting period. Pick n Pay South Africa saw its like for like sales rise by 4.3%, which the company described as a solid outcome given how stretched consumers are and the low food inflation environment.

The group also stuck to its plan of closing or converting underperforming stores. While that weighed down total turnover growth, the stores that remained open generally performed better. Boxer stood out once again, delivering a 13.9% increase in turnover and 5.3% growth in like for like sales. Clothing sales in standalone stores also did well, growing by 12%, although momentum eased slightly toward the end of the period. Online sales were a major highlight, jumping 34.4%, thanks largely to the continued success of Pick n Pay asap! and its grocery presence on the Mr D app.

Internal selling price inflation was kept at 2.1%, matching what was reported for the full financial year and staying well below the 4.6% CPI food inflation rate. That suggests the company has been careful about passing costs on to customers, which could be helping sales. Despite these positive trends, the group still expects to post a loss for the period. However, that loss should be smaller than before. Improvements in Pick n Pay’s core trading performance, continued strength from Boxer, and better results in net funding interest have all helped soften the blow.

The downside is that Boxer’s IPO means 34.4% of its profit now belongs to external shareholders, reducing the group's share. On top of that, the recent rights offer increased the total number of shares in circulation, which affects how much loss is reported per share. The average number of shares in issue rose by 25%, from about 588 million to roughly 735 million. While Pick n Pay isn’t back in the black just yet, the signs are much more promising than they’ve been in a long time.
