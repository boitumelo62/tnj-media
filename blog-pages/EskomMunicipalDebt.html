Media Eskom Sounds Alarm Over Soaring Municipal Debt

March 20, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Eskom has issued a stark warning that municipal debt could rise by as much as R240 billion over the next five years, posing a serious threat to its restructuring plans and overall financial stability. The utility says unpaid bills from municipalities remain one of its biggest challenges, undermining efforts to secure long term sustainability.

According to Eskom, many municipalities are struggling to collect revenue from consumers, leading to a growing backlog in payments owed to the power supplier. This cycle of non-payment has placed significant strain on the utility, which is already dealing with operational and financial pressures.

The projected increase in debt has raised concerns among policymakers and economists, who warn that it could derail ongoing reform efforts. Eskom’s restructuring strategy is seen as a crucial step in stabilizing South Africa’s energy sector, but rising debt levels could complicate these plans.

Government has previously intervened with support measures, including debt relief arrangements and financial oversight mechanisms for struggling municipalities. However, experts argue that more sustainable solutions are needed to address the root causes of the problem.

Energy analysts highlight the importance of improving municipal governance and revenue collection systems. Without these reforms, they warn, the burden on Eskom will continue to grow, potentially impacting service delivery and electricity supply reliability.

The warning serves as a reminder of the interconnected challenges facing South Africa’s energy and local government sectors. As discussions continue, stakeholders are calling for coordinated action to prevent the situation from escalating further and to ensure a more stable and resilient power system for the future.
