Media McKinsey Begins Long Journey to Rebuild Trust in South Africa

December 4, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

McKinsey & Co.’s leaders in Africa say rebuilding the firm’s reputation in South Africa will take years. After being caught up in major corruption scandals linked to state capture, the company knows that trust isn’t something it can win back quickly. Gregor Theisen, who heads McKinsey in Africa, said the firm accepts that this will be a long journey and that the past will always stay with them.

In its effort to repair the damage, McKinsey has fired implicated employees, strengthened its compliance processes and cooperated with investigators. It also did some work for free this year on business events tied to South Africa’s G20 hosting. Even so, the firm still faced criticism from the president’s office, showing just how sensitive its presence in the public sector remains.

McKinsey was one of several global companies drawn into corruption at state owned enterprises during the state capture years. It has since paid more than $122 million in the US to settle criminal allegations related to its work with Transnet, and it has refunded fees to Eskom and South African Airways. The company stopped doing work for South African state entities more than eight years ago and hasn’t had any formal talks about returning.

According to its South Africa managing partner, Adolf Makgatho, McKinsey will only reconsider government work if it feels completely confident in its internal risk safeguards. Despite the setbacks, the firm has kept its commitment to the country. McKinsey believes that South Africa is central to its African business because so many major clients operate there. Theisen said you can’t have a strong Africa practice without a strong South African one, and the firm is still heavily invested in the local market.

That stance differs from competitor Bain, which shut down its South African consulting business in 2023 after being banned from government work. McKinsey, meanwhile, continues serving clients in sectors such as financial services, mining and manufacturing. Makgatho noted that some former clients who distanced themselves during the scandals are now approaching the firm again to understand the reforms it has made and to explore working together.

Across the continent, McKinsey sees Africa as a major growth region. It’s advising more companies building tech hubs in East Africa and expects rising interest in areas like clean energy, food security, new markets and talent development. Theisen said the firm is hiring in all its African locations, reflecting its belief that the region will play an increasingly important role in its global strategy.
