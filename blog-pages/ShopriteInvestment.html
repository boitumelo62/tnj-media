Business Shoprite warns local turmoil hurting supplier investment

November 12, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

One of South Africa’s largest food retailers is sounding the alarm about a growing problem in the local supply chain and the knock-on effects for the broader economy. The company says the country is “paying the price” as disruption and uncertainty discourage investment by key suppliers.

At its recent company update, the retailer flagged significant constraints on the domestic sourcing of goods. Among the issues, major stock distributors are holding back on capital investment in production and logistics because of what the speaker described as adverse conditions in South Africa. These conditions include erratic supply infrastructure, logistical bottlenecks, and regulatory or policy uncertainty.

The consequence, the retailer warned, is a weaker supply base, fewer locally produced goods and an increased reliance on imported items or alternative sourcing strategies. The company emphasised that this dynamic adds cost, reduces margin flexibility and ultimately can push prices upward for consumers.

Notably, the retailer said it is having to actively find “work‐around” supply chain solutions to offset the decline in investment by large domestic suppliers. The challenge is not simply one of inflation or currency weakness but of a structural deficit in investment in manufacturing capacity and distribution within the country.

The firm’s message underscores wider concerns about South Africa’s industrial and manufacturing environment. When suppliers delay or cancel planned investment in capacity, the ripple effect touches retailers, consumers, manufacturing jobs and ultimately economic growth.

While the retailer did not release specific figures in this update, its commentary is consistent with third-party analysis showing that local production investment has lagged and that global companies are reconsidering South Africa as an investment destination. One earlier report noted that firms are responding to low domestic production by increasing inventory of imported goods.

For South Africa, the warning from a leading player in the retail sector is a stark reminder that supply side problems are as much a threat as demand side pressures. If supplier investment continues to be subdued, the country risks higher consumer prices, weaker job creation and slower growth in a sector that is a critical employer.

Industry analysts will be watching closely whether this latest signal prompts policy makers and business to step up efforts to stabilise the environment for investment, strengthen local manufacturing and shore up the domestic supply chain.
