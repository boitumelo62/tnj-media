Media Ramaphosa Pauses National Health Insurance Rollout Pending Constitutional Court Ruling

February 25, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

President Cyril Ramaphosa has paused the implementation of the National Health Insurance system pending a ruling by the Constitutional Court on legal challenges to the law.

The NHI Act, signed into law in 2024, aims to provide universal health coverage and reduce inequalities in access to healthcare. However, it has faced multiple legal challenges from opposition parties, business groups and civil society organisations who argue that it is unconstitutional and financially unsustainable.

In a statement, the Presidency said the decision to pause implementation reflects respect for the rule of law and the ongoing judicial process. The government will await clarity from the Constitutional Court before proceeding with key aspects of the policy.

Health experts have expressed mixed reactions. Supporters of the NHI say the pause delays much needed reforms to a fragmented healthcare system that leaves millions reliant on overstretched public facilities. Critics argue that the pause is prudent given concerns about funding, governance and capacity.

The Department of Health said preparatory work would continue, including strengthening public hospitals and clinics, while the legal process unfolds.

The Constitutional Court is expected to hear arguments later this year, with a ruling that could shape the future of healthcare reform in South Africa.
