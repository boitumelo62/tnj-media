Business Watchdog Rules Against “Anywhere Delivery” Claim

March 19, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

South Africa’s advertising landscape has been thrust into the spotlight following a ruling by the Advertising Regulatory Board against retailer Checkers. The board found that the company’s Sixty60 “anywhere delivery” claim was misleading, raising broader questions about truth in advertising and consumer expectations in the rapidly growing on demand delivery sector.

The ruling stems from complaints that the “anywhere” phrasing created unrealistic expectations among customers who found that delivery services were limited to specific geographic zones. While Checkers Sixty60 has been widely praised for convenience and speed, the board determined that the wording overstated the service’s reach and could deceive consumers.

This decision highlights the increasing scrutiny brands face as competition intensifies in the digital retail space. With more South Africans relying on app based grocery services, clarity and transparency in marketing have become essential. Consumers are no longer just influenced by bold promises but are also quick to challenge them when reality does not match expectations.

For Checkers, the ruling may not significantly dent its market dominance, but it does signal a need for more precise communication. Adjusting marketing language to reflect actual service coverage could help rebuild trust and avoid further regulatory issues. More broadly, the case sets an important precedent.

It reinforces the role of regulatory bodies in holding major brands accountable and protecting consumers from exaggerated claims. As e commerce continues to expand, companies across sectors may need to revisit their messaging to ensure compliance.

Ultimately, the ruling serves as a reminder that in a competitive market, credibility can be just as valuable as innovation. Brands that strike the right balance between ambition and honesty are more likely to retain long term customer loyalty.
