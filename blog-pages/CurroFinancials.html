Business Private School Group Curro Stays Profitable in Challenging Market

August 29, 2025 ● TNJ Media Desk

Curro, South Africa’s largest independent school group, has seen a slight drop in student numbers over the past year, but the company remains positive about its future, especially with hopes for an improving local economy. Even though the number of schools and campuses in the group has grown now at 189 schools and 81 campuses the average number of learners has dipped by 1.4%, from around 72,758 to 71,749.

Despite having fewer learners, Curro still managed to grow its revenue by 4.7%, reaching R2.7 billion.

School fee income also increased by 4.1%, thanks to annual fee hikes, which helped make up for the drop in student numbers. Discounts offered on school fees were kept under control and actually decreased slightly from the previous year.

The group's headline earnings per share saw only a small increase, rising from 40.2 cents to 40.3 cents. However, overall headline earnings were down 1.8%, dropping from R228 million to R224 million. More notably, earnings per share fell sharply by 36.1%, mostly due to a R74.1 million impairment on land bought for future school development. That charge affected the earnings per share figure but was excluded from headline earnings.

Curro didn’t declare an interim dividend for the period, following its usual policy of basing dividends on recurring headline earnings which remained flat in this period. The group acknowledged the challenges it faces in the education sector. While more parents are turning to private schools as an alternative to the public system, ongoing economic pressures are tightening household budgets. Even so, Curro says it’s managing to hold its ground financially, highlighting its ability to generate cash and keep earnings stable despite fewer learners.

CEO Loubser emphasized that Curro remains committed to providing high quality education while staying financially sustainable. The group invested R264 million during the period and plans to ramp that up to R630 million in the coming year.

These funds will go toward expanding and upgrading facilities, especially to meet the growing demand for high school education. Other investments during the period included R32 million from changes in money market and other financial assets. Curro believes its strategy focused on both delivering strong educational outcomes and improving financial efficiency puts it in a good position to benefit as the broader economy begins to recover.
