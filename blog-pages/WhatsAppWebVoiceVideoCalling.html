Tech News WhatsApp Web Gains Built in Voice and Video Calling

WhatsApp has begun rolling out built in voice and video calling on its web platform, a long-awaited feature that brings the browser-based version closer to its mobile and desktop counterparts.

The update allows users to place one on one voice and video calls directly from a web browser without installing the dedicated WhatsApp desktop app. Call icons now appear within individual chats, enabling users to start calls with a single click. The feature is being rolled out gradually, with early access available to users on WhatsApp’s beta programme, before a wider public release in the coming weeks.

Calls made through WhatsApp Web remain protected by end-to-end encryption, meaning only the participants in the call can access the audio and video. Meta, WhatsApp’s parent company, cannot listen to or view the conversations. The platform also supports screen sharing during video calls, making it more suitable for remote work, online meetings, and collaboration.

At launch, the calling functionality is limited to individual chats. However, reports indicate that group voice and video calling is under development, with support expected to mirror existing limits on mobile and desktop platforms, where group calls can include up to 32 participants.

The addition of browser-based calling is expected to benefit users in corporate environments, educational institutions, and public sector organisations where installing desktop software is often restricted. It also provides a more accessible option for users on lower powered devices or operating systems with limited desktop support.

By expanding WhatsApp Web’s capabilities, Meta is positioning the platform as a more complete communication tool, competing more directly with services such as Google Meet and Microsoft Teams, which already offer browser-based calling.

The update reflects WhatsApp’s broader push to unify features across platforms and strengthen its role in both personal and professional communication, as more users rely on web-based tools for daily messaging and calls.
