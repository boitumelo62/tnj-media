A major breakthrough in the fight against corruption has been made after the arrest of a company director and a financial adviser linked to an alleged R800,000 kickback scheme involving the Small Enterprise Development and Finance Agency.

The arrests were carried out by the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation in Limpopo following an extensive investigation into suspicious activities at the agency’s offices in Tzaneen. Authorities say the probe was triggered by a tip-off pointing to possible fraud, corruption, theft, and money laundering within the institution.

According to investigators, the case centres on a scheme in which an official allegedly worked closely with company directors to manipulate funding applications. Businesses that were approved for funding are believed to have paid kickbacks exceeding R800,000 in return for assistance in securing financial support.

The investigation dates back to April 2025, when law enforcement executed a search and seizure operation at the agency’s offices. During the raid, electronic devices and documents were confiscated and later analysed, helping authorities build a case against those involved.

Officials have described the arrests as a significant development, but warned that the investigation is far from over. There are strong indications that more individuals could be implicated as authorities continue to examine the evidence and financial trails connected to the scheme.

The two suspects are expected to appear in the Tzaneen Magistrate’s Court, where they will face multiple charges, including fraud, corruption, theft, and money laundering. Provincial Hawks leadership has issued a stern warning to those entrusted with public funds, stating that any abuse of state resources will be met with decisive action. The case has once again highlighted the vulnerability of funding systems intended to support small businesses, raising concerns about how such programmes can be safeguarded from exploitation.

For many South Africans, the developments signal a renewed push to hold individuals accountable and to protect public resources meant to drive economic growth and opportunity.
