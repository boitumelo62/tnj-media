Media Court hears claim that Makate cancelled deal with wrong company

November 17, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

A new legal dispute has emerged around Please Call Me inventor Nkosana Makate, with former funders telling the Johannesburg High Court that they are entitled to forty percent of his recent settlement from Vodacom. They argue that Makate cancelled his agreement with the wrong company, meaning the original funding contract is still in force.

The dispute dates back to an arrangement signed in 2011 when Makate was preparing for a long legal battle with Vodacom. Under that agreement, businessman Chris Schoeman and the company Black Rock Mining undertook to provide financial support for Makate’s case in exchange for forty percent of any eventual payout.

Makate later cancelled the deal in 2015, claiming that the funders had failed to honour their commitments. However, his termination letter referred to a different company named Raining Men Trade. The current applicants say this means that Black Rock Mining was never formally released from the contract and remains entitled to its share of the settlement.

In new court papers, Black Rock is asking the court to order Vodacom and Makate’s attorneys to withhold any payment until the matter is resolved. They insist the agreement they signed in 2011 remains binding because Makate cancelled the contract with the incorrect entity.

Makate rejects the claim. He says only a small initial payment was made to his legal team more than a decade ago and that Black Rock did not follow through with the financial support it had promised. He has described the renewed demand for forty percent as baseless and an attempt to benefit from a case they abandoned years ago.

The application comes shortly after Makate reached an out of court settlement with Vodacom, bringing an end to nearly twenty years of litigation over compensation for the Please Call Me idea. While the value of the settlement has not been disclosed, analysts believe it is substantial, making the outcome of the new dispute potentially significant.

The High Court is expected to set dates for further hearings as both sides prepare to argue whether the 2011 contract is still valid.
