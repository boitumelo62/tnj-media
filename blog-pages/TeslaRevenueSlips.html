Tech News Tesla Revenue Slips as Musk Bets the Company Future on AI and Robots

Tesla has reported its first annual revenue decline, marking a major turning point for the electric vehicle maker as it shifts its focus away from cars and deeper into artificial intelligence and robotics.

The company said total revenue fell by 3 percent in 2025, while profits dropped sharply by 61 percent in the final quarter of the year. The results underline growing pressure on Tesla as competition intensifies and its long dominant position in the EV market weakens.

As part of its strategic pivot, Tesla confirmed it will end production of its Model S and Model X vehicles. The California factory that previously built those cars will now be repurposed to manufacture Optimus, Tesla’s humanoid robot designed for industrial and commercial use.

The announcement comes after China based automaker BYD overtook Tesla as the world’s largest electric vehicle producer earlier this year. Analysts say Tesla’s ageing vehicle lineup and slowing demand have contributed to its financial slowdown.

Tesla also disclosed a two billion dollar investment in xAI, an artificial intelligence company founded by Elon Musk. Musk said the decision was driven by investor pressure, despite a recent shareholder vote where opposition and abstentions outweighed support for the move.

Last year, Tesla shareholders approved a record breaking compensation package for Musk that could be worth close to one trillion dollars if ambitious market value targets are met over the next decade.

The company is also preparing to significantly increase spending, with capital expenditure expected to rise by as much as twenty billion dollars. Musk described the investment push as laying the groundwork for a transformative future.

Tesla shares rose modestly in extended trading following the announcement.

The company’s shift comes amid controversy surrounding Musk’s political involvement, which has sparked protests at Tesla dealerships and alienated some customers. At the same time, changes to US government incentives for electric vehicles have added further pressure.

Industry analysts say scaling back low volume models to focus on mass market vehicles and emerging technologies may be Tesla’s most practical path forward.
