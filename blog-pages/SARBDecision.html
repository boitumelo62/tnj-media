Business SARB Reflects on Previous Cuts Before Making Moves

September 19, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The South African Reserve Bank (SARB) decision to keep interest rates steady this week might have caught some off guard, but economists believe it’s just a temporary pause. The SARB’s Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) voted 4-2 to maintain the country’s repo rate at 7.00%, with the prime lending rate staying at 10.50%. This move surprised some, especially considering that key economic indicators were quite favorable for a rate cut.

For instance, consumer inflation for August came in much lower than expected at 3.3%, the rand has been holding strong, and even the US Federal Reserve recently reduced its rates. Despite these factors, the MPC chose to take a longer term view of the economy. They pointed to potential inflation risks ahead, such as global economic uncertainty and rising prices from services like electricity tariffs. There’s also the question of a potential new inflation target of 3%, which would significantly shift the central bank’s approach. According to the Bureau for Economic Research (BER), most economists expected the SARB to hold rates steady, and in fact, two thirds of the MPC members voted for that.

This alignment suggests that the decision wasn’t completely out of left field. However, the SARB hasn’t ruled out future rate cuts. The central bank itself said it needed more time to assess the impact of the previous two rate cuts before making further moves. This pause in rate cuts is seen as temporary, especially by those in the property sector, who are hopeful that reductions will come soon. Ahead of the decision, economists like Annabel Bishop, chief economist at Investec, predicted a 25 basis point cut in 2025. She emphasized that a cut is needed soon, as current rates are restrictive and demand is weak. The SARB’s own projections also point toward a potential 25 basis point cut before the end of this year, with a final meeting scheduled in November.

Looking ahead to 2026, the central bank’s forecast includes a further 50 to 75 basis point reduction. Nedbank’s chief economist, Nicky Weimar, suggests that much will depend on how inflation plays out, especially with the new target. While the SARB expects a small increase in inflation in the short term, they forecast it to gradually drop to 3% by the end of 2027. Weimar believes the SARB will likely overlook any temporary inflation spikes and maintain its current rate stance for the time being. However, if inflation remains stable and the rand stays strong, further cuts could be on the table. If trends like a stronger rand, falling maize prices, and lower fuel costs continue, this could reduce inflationary pressure. In that case, it’s more likely that the SARB would consider another rate cut in the near future.
