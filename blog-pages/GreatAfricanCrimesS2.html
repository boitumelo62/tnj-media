Great African Crimes is back with a second season, premiering this Friday on the HISTORY Channel Africa (DStv 186), and it promises to be just as gripping as ever. Hosted once again by award-winning journalist and true crime author Mandy Wiener, this two-part documentary series dives deep into two of South Africa’s most fascinating and lesser-known historical crimes. True crime is booming around the world, and South Africa is no exception. The first season of Great African Crimes was the top-rated primetime show on the HISTORY Channel Africa in 2024 no surprise considering the gripping stories and powerful storytelling.

Wiener believes part of the show’s success lies in how close to home the stories hit. "People are naturally curious about criminal minds," she says. "There’s something about the drama, the psychology, even the emotion tied into these cases that just draws people in."

The new season kicks off with the story of Pierre Basson, thought to be South Africa’s first unrecorded serial killer. Operating in the early 1900s, he’s linked to as many as nine murders all of them connected by a thread of insurance fraud. The second episode goes even further back, to the 18th century, telling the shocking story of Maria Mouton, the only white woman ever executed for murder in the Cape Colony. Along with two enslaved people, she was found guilty of killing her husband. But the trial itself revealed secrets that scandalised society at the time.

As always, Wiener doesn't just retell the story from afar. She travels to the actual locations where these events unfolded, immersing herself in the past. One moment that stayed with her was filming in the torture chamber at the Castle of Good Hope in Cape Town. “It was chilling,” she recalls. “You could feel the history in that room what happened there, the energy. It stays with you.”

Produced by The Schultz Creative, the show does more than recount gruesome details. It places each crime within its historical context, painting a broader picture of the time and place in which it happened. The team dug through old records, interviewed historians and experts, and even tracked down descendants like a ninth-generation family member of one of the victims, who shared deeply personal insights that brought a new perspective to the case.

The idea behind this season was to go deeper with more research, more rich detail, and more nuanced storytelling. These aren’t stories you’ll see on every “top 10 true crime” list. They’ve been buried by time, and unearthing them is like unlocking forgotten time capsules. The two cases featured this season were carefully chosen for their mystery, emotional weight, and storytelling power. According to Wiener, both are “gripping, layered narratives” that merge history, crime, and the human experience into compelling television.

Great African Crimes Season 2 premieres Friday, 3 October at 21:05 on HISTORY Channel Africa (DStv 186).
