Media Court Declares Operation Dudula Actions at Clinics Unlawful

November 5, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Johannesburg High Court has ruled that Operation Dudula’s attempts to block foreign nationals from accessing healthcare at public facilities were unlawful, delivering a major victory for human rights advocates.

The ruling came after several months of legal wrangling between the civic movement and public interest organisations who accused the group of intimidation, discrimination, and obstruction of essential services.

Operation Dudula, which emerged in 2021 as a vigilante-style movement claiming to advocate for South Africans’ rights, has faced widespread criticism for targeting foreign nationals in housing, employment, and healthcare sectors. Members of the group were seen at several Gauteng hospitals earlier this year, where they allegedly confronted nurses and security staff, demanding that foreign patients be turned away.

In its judgment, the court stated that the actions violated the constitutional right to healthcare and the principles of equality and human dignity. The court further issued an interdict barring Operation Dudula from engaging in any form of obstruction or harassment at public facilities.

Judge Thembi Maseko emphasised that “healthcare is a fundamental human right that applies to all persons within South Africa’s borders, regardless of nationality.”

The ruling has been praised by advocacy groups including Doctors Without Borders and Lawyers for Human Rights, who called it a landmark moment in defending access to medical care.

Operation Dudula representatives, however, said they plan to appeal the decision, arguing that their actions were motivated by legitimate frustrations over overcrowded clinics and strained resources.

Authorities have since deployed additional security at key hospitals in Gauteng to ensure safety and compliance with the ruling.
