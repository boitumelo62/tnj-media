Rethinking AI’s Role in Reaching Younger Audiences

As artificial intelligence continues to transform how we live, work, and interact, it's pushing marketers to rethink everything from how campaigns are built to how brand stories are told. Everyone's asking the same big questions: What will marketing actually look like in an AI-driven world? How can we make sure we’re using this tech in a way that’s ethical, responsible, and still rooted in human creativity?

There’s no doubt the potential is there—leaders across the industry see it. But the truth is, many are still trying to figure out how to make the most of it. When most people hear about AI in marketing, they think of the flashy stuff: AI writing copy, designing visuals, even editing video. And while that’s certainly part of it, the deeper transformation is happening under the surface. It’s in the way AI is being used to analyze data, make real-time decisions, and power smarter strategies across media and creative.

These technologies are becoming like the operating systems of modern marketing—quietly working in the background to simplify complex processes and highlight what really matters. One of the more exciting developments in this space is something called the Model Context Protocol, or MCP. It’s essentially a common language for AI systems that ensures they all understand the same brief—everything from tone of voice to campaign goals to ethical boundaries. By giving these systems a shared framework, brands can maintain consistency across platforms and touchpoints, which has always been one of the biggest challenges in multi-channel marketing.

MCP is especially powerful when combined with foundational AI systems like the Large Marketing Model (LMM), which is part of WPP’s wider platform for integrating AI across their agency network. At VML, for example, they’ve started using this combination to run real campaigns across regions like EMEA. The LMM brings together data from every corner of a campaign—channels, touchpoints, customer behaviour—and turns it into strategic insights. When guided by the context-aware instructions from MCP, it doesn’t just follow orders; it aligns everything it does with brand strategy and values.

This setup unlocks a range of new capabilities. AI can now personalize content based on what customers actually need, in the moment, while keeping the message on-brand. It can create and tweak creative elements on the fly, adjusting to what’s working in real time. It can fine-tune where your media budget goes, choosing channels that will deliver the most impact. It even comes with guardrails—ethical safeguards that protect privacy, ensure fairness, and build trust with customers.

Perhaps most importantly, this technology isn’t just for the big players. Smaller brands now have access to tools that let them compete at a higher level. That’s helping to drive innovation across the board.

When all these systems are aligned—when data, AI models, and context are working together—you don’t just get better marketing. You get smarter marketing. More personal, and more effective. You also open the door to new ways of thinking about the customer journey, storytelling, and long-term brand growth.

The bottom line? AI is changing marketing, not just by doing things faster or cheaper, but by fundamentally reshaping what’s possible. The real question isn’t whether your brand will adapt—it’s whether you’re ready to lead in this new era.
