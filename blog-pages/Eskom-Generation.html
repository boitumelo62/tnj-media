Business Eskom Surpasses Generation Targets

September 25, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Eskom is showing real signs of turning a corner, with its latest performance figures hitting the highest levels seen in years. The power utility has managed to push its Energy Availability Factor (EAF) up to nearly 73% in the past two weeks and has stayed above 70% for about a month. That’s a big deal it's even better than what the board had aimed for, even though it's still a bit behind the original timelines.

What’s striking is just how much better this performance is compared to recent years. In 2021, the best Eskom could do was just under 70%. Things dipped in 2022 and 2023, dropping to around 64% and then to a worrying 59%. 2024 was the year Eskom started to bounce back, briefly climbing above 70%, but now in 2025, it has surpassed even that. According to energy analyst Chris Yelland, Eskom's performance in the 38th week of 2025 is 12 percentage points higher than the same time last year.

And here’s the kicker these improved numbers don’t even include Kusile Unit 6, which has been quietly adding 720MW to the grid since March, even though it's not officially in commercial operation yet. That unit is expected to come fully online by the end of September, which could give the numbers an even bigger boost. Even without Kusile, the EAF has been hovering between 70% and 74%, averaging 71.12% for the month so far. That’s supported by lower unplanned outages and slightly less planned maintenance. Eskom is doing fewer repairs, but also breaking down less, which is exactly the combination you want.

The utility hit a milestone on 14 September, recording its lowest level of unplanned outages in eight years just under 6,000MW. That’s a drop of over 5,600MW from a year ago, the equivalent of adding back the entire Medupi power station. More units are available, more are working reliably, and the country is reaping the benefits. South Africans have now enjoyed 131 straight days without load shedding. Since the beginning of April, there have only been 26 hours of load shedding in total an almost unthinkable improvement compared to the past few years. Yelland praised the Eskom Generation team, led by Bheki Nxumalo, for what he called a “dramatic” improvement.

Eskom’s board chair, Mteto Nyati, also applauded the team and their efforts, but acknowledged that not everyone is seeing the benefits. Many households are still being hit by load reduction, which is different from load shedding. Load reduction isn’t part of the national schedule it happens locally when power systems are overwhelmed, often in areas with high levels of illegal connections. These put strain on the infrastructure, forcing Eskom to cut power to protect the system.

Eskom says it's aiming to reduce load reduction cases by 20% by March 2026, and eventually get rid of the practice altogether within two years. That won’t be easy. It will take more than just technical fixes it’ll require cooperation between communities, law enforcement, and other government departments.

The utility plans to crack down on electricity theft and remove over 600,000 illegal connections. It’s also rolling out millions of smart meters, with nearly 880,000 already installed. These will help monitor usage more accurately and prevent tampering. Eskom also wants to upgrade local networks and register more households for free basic electricity, so people don’t resort to unsafe and illegal connections. Electricity Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa is set to share more details on the government’s broader plans to tackle load reduction on Thursday.
