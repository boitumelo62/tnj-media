Tech News Coffee Meets Code: How Starbucks Is Betting on AI and Robots to Win Customers Back

Starbucks is charting an ambitious path to restore customer loyalty and quicken service by leaning into artificial intelligence and automation across its cafés and customer touchpoints. The global coffee giant’s latest strategy centres on tools that help employees and enhance the customer experience rather than replace baristas entirely.

At the heart of this shift is Green Dot Assist, a generative AI assistant developed using Microsoft Azure and OpenAI technology. Designed to be used on devices behind the counter, the system allows baristas to get instant, conversational answers to questions about drink recipes, preparation steps, or troubleshooting problems without searching through manuals or internal systems. Staff can interact with the tool by typing or speaking naturally, streamlining workflow and potentially reducing order times during peak hours.

The rollout began with pilot programmes in selected stores across the United States and Canada. Starbucks plans to expand the technology more broadly during its 2026 fiscal year as part of a wider turnaround strategy aimed at addressing slower service and operational strain in some outlets.

Beyond AI support for staff, the company is also exploring automation and robotics in other areas of store operations. These technologies could assist with inventory management, equipment monitoring, and even elements of the ordering process. While fully automated coffee making remains unlikely in the near term, Starbucks is testing ways to use data and machine learning to anticipate customer preferences and improve efficiency behind the scenes.

Company leadership has stressed that these innovations are designed to enhance, not replace, human interaction. Executives maintain that the Starbucks brand remains rooted in personal connection and customer experience. By automating routine or time-consuming tasks, the company hopes to free up baristas to focus more on service and engagement.

As competition intensifies in the quick service and premium coffee markets, Starbucks’ investment in AI and robotics signals a strategic shift. The company is betting that a blend of technology and human touch will help it regain momentum and strengthen its connection with customers in an increasingly digital world.
