Media Activists Urge Authorities to Treat Mugabe Son Case as Serious Criminal Matter

February 24, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Activists and the Citizens Forum have urged South African authorities to treat the attempted murder case linked to the son of the late former Zimbabwean president Robert Mugabe as a serious criminal matter. The group says the case raises concerns about accountability, justice, and the influence of powerful individuals in the region.

The incident, which reportedly involved an alleged attempted murder, has sparked public debate and scrutiny, with activists calling for a thorough and impartial investigation. They argue that any perception of preferential treatment could damage public confidence in the justice system and strain diplomatic and legal cooperation between South Africa and Zimbabwe.

The Citizens Forum has emphasised that the rule of law must apply equally to all individuals, regardless of political connections or family background. The organisation has called on prosecutors and law enforcement agencies to ensure that the case is handled transparently and without interference.

Legal experts say attempted murder is a serious offence under South African law and carries significant penalties if proven in court. They note that the case could test the independence of the justice system, particularly in high profile matters involving politically connected figures.

Authorities have not publicly disclosed detailed information about the investigation, citing legal and procedural constraints. However, officials have reiterated their commitment to upholding the law and ensuring that all suspects are treated according to established legal processes.

Activists say they will continue to monitor the case and engage with authorities to push for accountability. They argue that how the case is handled could set an important precedent for cross border justice and the treatment of influential individuals accused of serious crimes.

The matter remains under investigation, with further developments expected as authorities assess evidence and determine the next legal steps
