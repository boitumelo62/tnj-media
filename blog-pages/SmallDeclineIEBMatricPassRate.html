Media Small Decline in IEB Matric Pass Rate Seen as Normal Fluctuation

January 12, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The Independent Examination Board (IEB) recorded a slight decline in its matric pass rate for the class of 2025, dropping to 98.31%. According to News24, this is marginally lower than the 98.47% achieved in 2024. The IEB is mainly used by private schools across South Africa and offers the National Senior Certificate alongside the version administered by the Department of Basic Education. In 2025, the bachelor’s pass rate stood at 89.12%, down slightly from 89.37% the previous year. There was also a small shift in the number of learners qualifying for diploma studies, changing from 7.56% to 7.83%.

Despite these movements, IEB CEO Confidence Dikgole said the changes are not cause for concern, explaining that as the IEB grows, small fluctuations in pass rates are normal and are commonly seen in stable education systems around the world. A total of 17,143 candidates wrote the IEB exams in 2025, including both full time and part time learners, compared to 16,304 in 2024. This number remains small when compared to the government-run National Senior Certificate exams, which were written by more than one million learners across over 9,400 examination centres nationwide.

Basic Education Minister Siviwe Gwarube is expected to announce the official NSC results later today, 12 January 2026, with learners gaining individual access to their results from 13 January. There is cautious optimism that the national pass rate will improve on 2024’s figure of 87.3%, even as the private school pass rate has dipped slightly. The 2025 matric exams have not been without controversy. The examination process came under scrutiny after an exam leak involving 40 pupils affected papers in English, Mathematics and Physical Science. Investigations revealed that two officials from the Department of Basic Education were responsible for the leak, and both have since been suspended.

One of the officials reportedly had a child who was writing the matric exams. This incident has raised serious concerns about the government’s ability to manage the examination process effectively. South Africa has three examination authorities: the Department of Basic Education, the Independent Examination Board and the South African Comprehensive Assessment Institute. VF Plus MP Dr Wynand Boshoff said the IEB and SACAI have consistently conducted their examinations without fraud or controversy, arguing that the recent leak further undermines confidence in the public education system. He suggested that independent education is likely to grow as a result, especially if the DBE continues to hold exclusive control over final examinations.

Boshoff also pointed out that what makes this year’s incident particularly troubling is that the breach reportedly occurred at departmental headquarters rather than at a remote exam centre.In response, the Department of Basic Education defended its systems, stating that the detection of the leak shows the strength of its quality assurance and monitoring processes. The department emphasised that the incident involved only 40 learners, a tiny fraction of the roughly one million candidates who wrote the 2025 matric exams. It also noted that while independent schools are often associated with the IEB, many of them still choose to write the NSC examinations administered by the DBE.
