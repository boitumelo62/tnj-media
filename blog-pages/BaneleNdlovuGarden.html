Media Youth Environmentalist Grows Hope on Former Dumpsites

Octeber 11, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Banele Ndlovu openly admits that back in high school, he used to litter without giving it much thought. He remembers casually throwing snack wrappers around, not really thinking about the impact. But he wasn’t completely indifferent whenever there were clean up activities, he took part actively. What really shifted his perspective, though, was a friend who constantly called him out for littering. Over time, that steady reminder began to sink in.

Now, at 24, Banele considers himself a self taught environmentalist. He runs a recycling business and manages a community garden in his hometown of Pimville, Soweto. His journey into environmental work was inspired by the tragic stories of children in the Eastern Cape dying from hunger. That moved him deeply. He saw open spaces in his neighbourhood areas used for illegal dumping and imagined something better. With so much unused land and so many people going hungry, he thought: why not grow food here? He started transforming one such dumpsite into a vegetable garden.

His hope is that if people can see real benefits fresh food growing where there used to be trash they might change how they treat their surroundings. But it's not been easy. One of the hardest parts for him has been finding reliable people to work with. On top of that, the field where he grows the veggies doesn’t have a proper irrigation system. Things got worse when a violent robbery in a nearby yard where he was borrowing water led to a complete cutoff of his water source. The attack left the community traumatised and put a pause on the garden's progress. Still, Banele remains hopeful. As he puts it, “Sometimes setbacks lead to better comebacks.”

He’s received some valuable support, particularly from ward councillor Sebenzile Mabuza, who sees Banele as a bright light in the community someone who’s not just sitting around waiting for opportunities, but creating them. She’s proud of how his recycling business gives local youth a way out of crime and drugs, making him a role model. Banele believes this kind of support is critical. With more land provided by the councillor and more hands on deck, his team has been able to step up their efforts in a big way. They’ve already planted 1,000 trees in just one year all thanks to the growing involvement of the community. But he’s thinking even bigger. Banele dreams of planting a million trees in Soweto.
