Media Women Rewriting the Rules and Reshaping South Africa

October 13, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Power is not something handed over without effort it’s something we claim, shape, and live into being. This year’s celebration women’s issue is built around the theme Power Moves Women shaping systems, shifting cultures, and driving South Africa forward. It’s both a celebration of how far we’ve come, and a call to action for how much more needs to be done.

There are women changing the game rewriting what leadership looks like, rebuilding businesses from the ground up, reshaping society in every space they enter. But we can’t ignore the difficult truths. We can’t pretend South Africa is transforming when gender based violence still lives in our homes and communities. We can’t speak of equality when women are still blocked from executive roles, when becoming a mother often comes at the cost of professional growth, or when bias both unconscious and systemic continues to hold women back. These realities are uncomfortable, but they must be faced.

Real empowerment doesn't come from good intentions. It comes from urgent, deliberate action. Still, we are seeing power shift. Women particularly in small and informal businesses are creating impact with minimal support. Imagine what could happen if they had access to real financial inclusion, targeted funding, and open markets. Picture that potential. In education, business, and science, young black women are showing us what leadership of the future looks like. They’re proving that planning for succession isn’t optional it’s essential. And in politics and policy making, women’s voices are being heard more clearly. This is no longer just a slogan. It’s starting to become a reality.

There are global lessons we can learn from too like investing with a gender lens, or creating workplaces that actually consider the needs of women in every stage of life, including midlife and menopause. Right here in South Africa, some companies have shown what real transformation looks like when inclusive policies are not just written, but deeply lived out across leadership and culture. These aren’t just feel good stories they’re proof that when the system works for women, everyone benefits. But progress is fragile. Technology, especially the rapid rise of AI, threatens to undo decades of hard won gains especially for women working in the kinds of roles most likely to be automated.

Without intentional action, the future of work risks repeating the mistakes of the past. So we must ask why are there still so few women CEOs in South Africa’s biggest companies? Why is true gender parity still out of reach at the top? Maybe it’s because the solution isn’t just quotas or checkboxes. Maybe it’s because we need to rethink leadership itself. What if empathy, emotional intelligence, and collaboration weren’t seen as “soft” but as powerful? Women are leading this shift, proving that this kind of leadership isn’t just modern it’s necessary.

Even so, we can’t do this alone. Gender equity isn’t a women’s issue it’s a societal one. Men must be part of the solution. That means taking accountability, challenging exclusionary cultures, confronting bias, and standing firmly against violence in all forms. This isn’t just a feature. It’s a message. An invitation. Let’s celebrate the women making power moves. Let’s challenge the systems that hold others back. Let’s act with purpose, because the time for token gestures is over. The time for real power shifts lasting, honest, inclusive is now.
