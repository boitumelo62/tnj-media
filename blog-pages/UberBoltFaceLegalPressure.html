Media Uber and Bolt Face Legal Pressure as Compliance Deadlines Approach

February 17, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

E hailing platforms Uber and Bolt are facing mounting legal pressure in South Africa as deadlines for compliance with new government regulations draw closer, raising concerns for drivers and passengers alike.

The Department of Transport has been tightening enforcement of updated rules aimed at formalising the e hailing industry. These include requirements for operating licences, vehicle roadworthiness documentation, and proper driver registration with provincial authorities. Government argues the measures are necessary to improve passenger safety, regulate competition with traditional taxi operators, and bring the sector into line with existing transport laws.

However, industry stakeholders say the compliance process has been uneven and complicated, with many drivers struggling to secure the required permits due to administrative delays and inconsistent provincial processes. Driver groups have warned that stricter enforcement could see thousands of drivers taken offline, potentially reducing incomes and limiting transport options in major cities.

Legal experts say platforms could face fines, court challenges, or even operational restrictions if they are found to be facilitating non-compliant operations. Municipal authorities in several metros have already signalled plans to increase inspections and enforcement operations targeting unlicensed vehicles and drivers.

Uber and Bolt have previously said they are engaging with regulators to simplify compliance processes and assist drivers with documentation and training. Both companies maintain that clear and consistent regulations are necessary to ensure the long-term stability of the sector and protect both drivers and riders.

The situation highlights the ongoing tension between innovation in the gig economy and the need for formal regulation, as South Africa attempts to balance economic activity with safety, labour protections, and fair competition in the transport sector.
