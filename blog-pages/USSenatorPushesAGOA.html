Business US Senator pushes AGOA extension but excludes South Africa

November 26, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Popular Blogs:

South Africa Risks Losing Key US Market Access

United States Senator John Kennedy has submitted a bill calling for the renewal of the African Growth and Opportunity Act for another two years, but with a major omission. South Africa would not be included in the extended trade arrangement if the bill passes.

AGOA has been central to South Africa’s export access into the US market for nearly twenty five years, supporting key sectors such as automotive, agriculture, wine, steel and manufactured goods. Removal from the legislation would place billions in trade exposure at stake and could reshape South Africa’s global export strategy.

The proposal comes after months of strain in diplomatic relations, with American lawmakers questioning South Africa’s foreign policy positioning and strategic partnerships. Washington has increasingly raised concerns about the country’s neutrality stance on global conflicts and its growing relationship with rival economies. The exclusion clause in the new bill appears to reflect this political tension.

Economists warn that South Africa’s removal could disrupt supply chains and put jobs at risk in industries heavily dependent on US market access. Exporters may face tariff barriers where trade was previously duty free. For small and medium enterprises, particularly in agriculture and textiles, a shift like this could narrow profitability and reduce long term growth outlook.

Government is expected to respond through diplomatic channels, engaging with US lawmakers to argue for continued inclusion. Business associations have urged immediate talks to prevent further uncertainty, saying AGOA remains a critical pillar for trade stability and investor confidence.

If the bill proceeds unchanged, other African states would continue to benefit from the programme while South Africa navigates a new and more competitive path into the American market. Much will depend on negotiations in the coming months, where diplomatic leverage, economic argument and political balance will determine the final outcome.

South Africa now waits to see whether dialogue can shift the position or whether a significant recalibration of trade strategy will be required moving forward.
