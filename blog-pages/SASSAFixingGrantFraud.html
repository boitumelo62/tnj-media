Business SASSA Reports Progress in Fixing Grant Fraud Disruptions

September 20, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

SASSA says it is making meaningful progress in addressing grant fraud after a nationwide verification failure caused major disruptions earlier this year. The agency confirmed that thousands of beneficiaries were affected when irregularities in the verification system flagged legitimate recipients as potential fraud cases, leading to suspended payments and long queues at local offices.

SASSA officials say the disruption was triggered by a technical fault combined with internal weaknesses that allowed fraudulent applications to filter into the system. In response, the agency launched an intensive review process aimed at identifying false claims while restoring payments to legitimate beneficiaries.

According to SASSA, more than half of the affected cases have now been resolved, with payments reinstated in phases to avoid overwhelming the system. The agency has deployed additional staff to regional branches, implemented manual verification checks for high risk categories, and introduced new monitoring tools designed to detect suspicious activity without interrupting the flow of legitimate grants.

Beneficiaries and advocacy groups have welcomed the progress but warned that many vulnerable households remain under pressure. Some recipients say they have spent weeks seeking clarification, often travelling long distances multiple times to resolve cases. Civil society organisations argue that while the crackdown on fraud is necessary, communication from the agency has been too slow and inconsistently applied.

SASSA has acknowledged these concerns and outlined several corrective steps, including improved data tracking, expanded call centre capacity, and closer coordination with the Department of Social Development. Officials say the long-term goal is to build a verification system that protects public funds while ensuring that disruptions do not cut off access to essential support.

Economists note that grant stability is critical ahead of the festive season, when household expenses rise sharply and the impact of delayed payments is felt most severely by low-income families. They warn that any further disruption could erode confidence in the social protection system and increase pressure on community support structures.

SASSA maintains that it is on track to finalise outstanding cases and has assured the public that no legitimate beneficiary will be excluded once verification is complete. Further updates are expected as the agency continues its recovery plan.
