Business Rate Cuts Likely to Continue but SARB Expected to Stay Cautious

October 14, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Economists aren’t completely sure if South Africa will cut interest rates again in 2025, but many agree the country is still in a rate cutting cycle, and more cuts are expected down the line. Annabel Bishop, Chief Economist at Investec, explained that the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) has been cautious in its approach, making cuts in a stop start way since the process began in September 2024. In the past year, SARB has already lowered rates by 125 basis points closely following the US Federal Reserve’s moves.

The US is expected to cut rates by another 50 basis points in 2025, which increases the odds that South Africa might do something similar. But it’s not guaranteed. Even though South Africa usually follows US rate trends, the country is now working towards a lower long term inflation target of 3%, down from the previous 4.5%. That change matters because it affects how the SARB approaches interest rate decisions. Getting inflation down to a steady 3% typically takes a couple of years, and during that time, the central bank is more cautious about making big policy shifts.

According to Bishop, aiming for lower inflation is helping restore confidence in the country’s monetary policy. And with inflation expected to trend down, South Africa could be heading for a longer term environment of lower interest rates. Financial markets already seem to expect a small 25 basis point cut before the end of 2025, most likely at the SARB’s November meeting. But some economists think the SARB may wait until 2026 to resume cutting, just to stay on the safe side. If the bank does go ahead with a cut in November, it opens the door for another 50 basis points worth of cuts in 2026.

If they wait until next year, though, the total cuts in 2026 could be a bit more possibly around 75 basis points. Bishop pointed out that domestic demand in South Africa is weak, inflation is trending lower, and interest rates are currently very tight. All these factors make a good case for further cuts next year. Interestingly, if the SARB decides not to cut in the short term while the US Fed does, the rand might actually benefit. That’s because a bigger gap between South African and US interest rates typically strengthens the rand against the dollar.

The US is expected to make deeper cuts up to 100 basis points by 2027. While this alone doesn’t guarantee a stronger rand, it could help push the currency below the R17.00/$ mark. With the US Federal Open Market Committee more focused on the job market and less worried about short-term inflation jumps caused by tariffs or other temporary factors, the Fed might even cut rates more than current forecasts suggest. South Africa’s final interest rate decision for 2025 will be announced at the SARB’s last meeting of the year on 20 November.
