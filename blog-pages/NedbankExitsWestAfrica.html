Business Nedbank Exits West Africa with R1.8bn Ecobank Stake Sale

August 15, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Nedbank is selling its stake in the Togo based financial group Ecobank for about R1.8 billion, as part of its decision to scale back operations in West Africa. The bank initially bought a 21.2% stake in Ecobank back in October 2014 as part of its push to grow beyond South Africa. However, Nedbank now says the investment no longer aligns with its strategy, especially given regulatory uncertainties and increased capital demands in the region.

The buyer is Bosquet Investments, the private investment company of Alain Nkontchou, a well known figure in African finance. Nkontchou co founded Enko Capital, which manages $1.2 billion in assets, and he has a long history with Ecobank serving as a non executive director since 2015 and becoming chairman in 2020. Bosquet has already provided financial guarantees to show that it can fund the R1.8 billion deal, which is expected to be completed by the end of 2025, pending regulatory approvals.

Nedbank CEO Jason Quinn said the decision came after reviewing how well the investment fit with the bank's long-term goals, financial performance, and strategy. He said the move is part of Nedbank’s broader effort to better allocate capital and concentrate on core areas of growth. Going forward, the bank plans to focus more on the SADC and East Africa regions, stepping back from West Africa.

At the same time, Nedbank is doubling down on South Africa. Earlier this week, it announced it would spend about R1.65 billion in cash to buy iKhokha, a local company that makes card machines and supports digital payments. iKhokha handles over R20 billion in digital transactions each year and has provided more than R3 billion in working capital to small and medium sized businesses. Quinn believes the iKhokha acquisition could open up new growth opportunities both locally and internationally.
