Business Pepkor’s Banking Ambition Marks a New Era in South Africa’s Retail Finance

November 20, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Africa’s largest clothing retailer, Pepkor, is preparing to move into banking in South Africa a move that could challenge Capitec’s strong position in the low cost banking market. The idea is to offer simple, zero fee banking services from inside Pepkor’s massive network of 6,000 stores. Since these stores draw millions of customers every day, Pepkor believes it can roll out banking services quickly and at a low cost.

The project, nicknamed “Pep Bank,” is still in its early stages. Pepkor is in discussions with Investec as a potential partner, although nothing has been finalised yet. If the partnership goes ahead, profits would likely be shared. For Investec, which usually serves high income clients, teaming up with Pepkor would provide a way to tap into South Africa’s mass market banking sector at a time when the country’s economic growth has been sluggish. The bank has already been expanding its business banking services and developing payment solutions for companies dealing with large numbers of small transactions, so this move would fit into its broader push for new revenue streams.

Pepkor brings powerful advantages to the table. Its extensive store footprint means it can open banking points in locations that customers already visit regularly, avoiding the heavy costs of setting up standalone branches. It also has huge amounts of customer data from its retail operations, giving it insights into the financial behaviour and needs of lower income households. This mirrors a growing trend among retailers: companies like Shoprite are already using loyalty data to offer financial services to people who remain unbanked or underserved by traditional banks.

For years, South Africa’s big four banks Absa, Standard Bank, FNB, and Nedbank focused mainly on middle class clients through traditional branch based models. This left many lower income people without accessible banking options and created opportunities for newcomers. Capitec proved how successful a low cost, convenience driven model could be.

When Capitec launched in 2001, it offered simple, affordable banking that appealed to working class and previously unbanked South Africans. Longer branch hours and easy to use services helped it stand out at a time when banking was often inconvenient and intimidating.From just 55 branches and 25,000 customers, the bank grew steadily and transformed itself from a microlender into a full service bank. Today, it serves about 25 million clients more than half of South Africa’s adult population making it the country’s largest digital bank. It has also become especially popular among younger customers, where older banks have struggled to connect.

Capitec continues to grow strongly. In its latest reporting period, headline earnings rose 26% to R8 billion for the six months ending August 2025, marking the fourth straight year of record profits. CEO Graham Lee has credited the bank’s scale for helping reduce transaction costs and supporting more investment in digital products. The bank now has 9.4 million fully banked clients, while its wider personal banking services reach more than half of all adults.

Pepkor’s entry into banking would arrive in an increasingly competitive market, especially among lower income consumers. Offering zero fee banking inside stores that people already trust and visit regularly could give the company a powerful foothold setting the stage for a major shift in South Africa’s retail banking landscape.
