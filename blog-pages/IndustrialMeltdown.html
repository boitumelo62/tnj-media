Business South Africa Faces Industrial Meltdown Amid Job Cuts and Policy Failures

September 5, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Ford, Glencore, and ArcelorMittal SA have just announced thousands of job cuts in South Africa, dealing a harsh blow to an already struggling economy. The country is facing serious problems – massive unemployment, stagnant growth, and a manufacturing sector that’s falling apart. South Africa’s industries are buckling under pressure from several sides. Electricity is both expensive and unreliable, infrastructure is decaying, and imports from Asian countries are making it hard for local manufacturers to compete. On top of that, the US recently slapped 30% tariffs on some South African exports, making it even harder for companies to survive.

Critics, including opposition parties and unions, say the government has failed to protect or grow the industrial sector. Matthew Parks from COSATU, the biggest labour federation, described the situation as a full-blown crisis. And many agree because this isn’t just a few factories closing; it’s a clear sign that South Africa’s industrial base is eroding fast. During apartheid, the country built much of its industrial capacity on the back of cheap Black labor and major investments in power and transport infrastructure. By the time of the first democratic elections in 1994, industry made up nearly a quarter of the economy. That’s now dropped to just 13%, and it's still declining.

Labour has become more expensive over the years as unions fought to narrow South Africa’s deep inequality. At the same time, government-introduced labour laws have made it harder for companies to hire and fire workers, which discourages investment. Now the unemployment rate is hovering around 33%, near historic highs. Electricity prices have skyrocketed – up 600% since 2006 – and logistics have worsened. Rail services have deteriorated, and the country’s ports are now among the most inefficient in the world, choking exports. Meanwhile, the US has imposed the highest tariffs on South African goods of any country in sub-Saharan Africa, in part because of political tensions tied to South Africa’s foreign policy and Black empowerment laws.

This is significant because the US is South Africa’s second-largest trade partner. Workers are feeling the impact. Rian Visser from the Solidarity union says workers are being punished for poor government policy and Eskom’s ever-increasing electricity tariffs. ArcelorMittal is shutting down steel operations that support 3,500 jobs, after two years of failed talks with the government. Ford is letting go of 470 workers at its plants near Pretoria and Gqeberha. Glencore, which closed ferrochrome plants in May, is also starting layoff talks. These job losses follow Goodyear’s recent shutdown, which cost 900 jobs, and Mercedes-Benz’s move to cut 700 positions earlier this year.

Irvin Jim, from the National Union of Metalworkers, puts it bluntly: South Africa is caught in a perfect storm of de-industrialization and bad geopolitical timing. He says the government lacks a solid industrial strategy to deal with any of this.

The numbers back that up. Since 2000, about 70,000 companies have gone under, and nearly 3 million jobs have disappeared, according to research by Haroon Bhorat at the University of Cape Town. That’s a clear sign the country is steadily losing its industrial core. Government officials say some of the job losses are due to local market conditions, while others are tied to global restructuring. But for many, that sounds like an excuse. The Economic Freedom Fighters say what’s happening is the systematic dismantling of the country’s remaining industrial base, and they’re accusing the government of standing by as communities are hollowed out and unemployment deepens.

Right now, it feels like South Africa’s economy is bleeding and there’s no clear plan to stop it.
