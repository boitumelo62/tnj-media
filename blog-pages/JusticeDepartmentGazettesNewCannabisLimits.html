Media Justice Department Gazettes New Cannabis Limits for Private Use

February 5, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The Department of Justice and Constitutional Development has officially invited public comment on new draft regulations for the Cannabis for Private Purposes Act. The proposed rules, gazetted this week, aim to clarify the "grey areas" left by the 2018 Constitutional Court ruling by establishing clear legal boundaries for possession and cultivation.

Under the new draft, an adult may possess up to 750 grams of dried cannabis in a private place for personal use. Crucially, the regulations also set a limit for public spaces, allowing adults to carry up to 750 grams on their person, provided it is concealed from public view. For those with a "green thumb," the draft limits cultivation to a maximum of five cannabis plants per adult at a private residence.

The regulations also address the transportation of cannabis. While the 750-gram limit applies to transport, the department has specified that the substance must be stored out of sight—ideally in a vehicle's boot or an enclosed compartment.

The move is seen as a significant step toward implementing the 2024 Act, though it strictly excludes commercial trade. "Matters relating to the buying or selling of cannabis fall outside the scope of this Act," the Department noted, emphasizing that the focus remains on personal privacy rights. The public has until March 5, 2026, to submit written comments before the limits are finalized and sent to Parliament for approval.
