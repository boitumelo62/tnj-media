Tech News Uber Eats tests AI Cart Assistant in the US, South Africa rollout unclear

Uber Eats has introduced an AI powered Cart Assistant aimed at simplifying grocery shopping by helping users build baskets, suggesting products, and personalising recommendations based on past orders. The feature is designed to compete more directly with dedicated grocery platforms and to increase basket sizes on the delivery app.

At launch, the AI Cart Assistant is being rolled out only in the United States, where it is integrated with several major retail partners including Albertsons, Aldi, CVS, Kroger, Safeway, Sprouts, Walgreens, and Wegmans. The tool can suggest missing staples, propose complementary items, and help users complete a weekly grocery shop with fewer manual searches.

Uber says the assistant is part of a broader push to use generative AI across its consumer platforms, with the goal of making ordering faster and more intuitive. For retailers, the feature could drive higher order values and improve product discovery, while users benefit from reduced friction when shopping for groceries and household essentials.

However, Uber has not confirmed when the AI Cart Assistant will be available outside the United States, and no timeline has been announced for South Africa or other African markets. The company typically follows a phased rollout approach, testing new features in mature markets before expanding globally, which means South African users may wait months or longer for access.

The move highlights how global delivery platforms are racing to embed AI into everyday commerce. For South Africa, it also underscores the ongoing lag between feature launches in developed markets and their availability locally. While Uber Eats continues to expand grocery partnerships in the country, advanced AI shopping tools remain a future prospect rather than an immediate offering.

As competition intensifies in the online grocery space, AI driven shopping assistants are likely to become a key differentiator, but their impact in emerging markets will depend on rollout speed, local partnerships, and consumer adoption.
