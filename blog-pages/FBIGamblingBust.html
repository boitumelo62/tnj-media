Media FBI Arrests 31, Including NBA Player, in Nationwide Gambling Bust

October 24, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The FBI has arrested 31 individuals, including an active NBA player and several alleged members of organized crime families, following a sweeping investigation into an underground gambling and money laundering network operating across multiple states.

Federal agents carried out simultaneous raids early Friday morning in New York, New Jersey, Nevada, and California, targeting what authorities describe as one of the largest sports betting crackdowns in recent years. According to the FBI, the group had been running high-stakes betting rings tied to professional sports, with millions of dollars exchanged through encrypted online platforms and offshore accounts.

Among those taken into custody was a current NBA player whose identity has not yet been released pending formal charges. Investigators allege that the player not only participated in the illegal betting operation but also facilitated bets on NBA games through intermediaries. The FBI is reportedly working closely with the league’s integrity unit to determine the extent of the player’s involvement and whether any games may have been influenced.

Several others arrested are believed to have ties to known mafia organizations in the Northeast. Prosecutors say the network operated for years, disguising gambling proceeds through shell companies, cryptocurrency wallets, and luxury assets.

“The message is clear,” said FBI Assistant Director Michael Driscoll. “No one is above the law—whether you’re an athlete, a businessman, or a member of organized crime. This operation was about protecting the integrity of sports and dismantling criminal enterprises that profit from addiction and deceit.”

The Justice Department confirmed that the suspects face a range of charges including illegal gambling, racketeering, conspiracy, and money laundering. If convicted, several could face decades in federal prison.

The NBA issued a brief statement saying it is aware of the situation and is cooperating fully with federal authorities, emphasizing its “zero tolerance” policy toward gambling-related misconduct.

The investigation, which reportedly began more than two years ago, stemmed from intelligence gathered during previous mob-related probes. Officials say more arrests could follow as they continue to trace the financial networks linked to the operation.

The suspects are expected to appear in various federal courts next week as prosecutors begin formal proceedings.
