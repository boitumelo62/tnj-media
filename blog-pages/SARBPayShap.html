Business SARB to Own PayShap in Full Push for Cashless SA

August 28, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The South African Reserve Bank (SARB) is taking a big step towards making the country less reliant on cash. It’s planning to take full control of BankservAfrica—the company behind the development and processing of PayShap, the instant payment system that's been gaining traction in the country.

The Competition Commission has already given the green light, recommending that the Competition Tribunal approve the deal without any conditions. SARB had already bought 50% of BankservAfrica back in late 2024, and this move would give it full control. BankservAfrica plays a key role in how money moves between banks in South Africa. It’s the behind the scenes operator for payment clearing and settlement services and also handles interbank transactions. One of its most important developments is PayShap, a real time payment system that launched in 2023 and has already processed over R285 billion in transactions. Eleven major banks support it.

The Reserve Bank is constitutionally tasked with protecting the value of the rand and ensuring the country’s financial stability. Part of that job includes overseeing the National Payment System—all the processes and systems involved from the time someone makes a payment to when that payment is finally settled between banks. When SARB first invested in BankservAfrica, its goal was to modernise South Africa’s payment systems—making them faster, safer, and more inclusive. PayShap is a key part of that plan. The ultimate vision? A cashless society.

The central bank argues that South Africa relies too heavily on cash, which comes with risks and inefficiencies. Its 2024 Digital Payments Roadmap found that nearly half of South African adults take out all their money as soon as it hits their accounts. There’s still a lack of trust in banks, card fees can be high, and many informal businesses don’t accept cards at all. But services like PayShap are helping to change that. Because it's simple, fast, and doesn’t require traditional card infrastructure, it’s starting to catch on even in areas that were previously cash only.

SARB Governor Lesetja Kganyago has been clear about the bank’s goals—reduce cash usage, modernise the entire payment ecosystem, and educate South Africans about the benefits of digital payments. If the full acquisition goes through, the Reserve Bank will be able to push forward with these goals even more effectively.

According to the Competition Commission, the deal won’t harm competition or the public interest, so there are no major concerns on that front. All signs are pointing to a major shift in how money moves in South Africa—and SARB is putting itself in the driver’s seat.
