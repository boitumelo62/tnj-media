Media The Future of Advertising Is Prompted

July 7, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Agentic marketing is really just a fancy way of saying, using AI agents to do work for you. At first, a lot of people worried that these agents would replace jobs. But the real opportunity is in how much more efficient they can make advertising especially the foundational stuff like media buying, trafficking, and optimization.

At its core, an "agent" is just a smart algorithm that follows a set of rules. It can automate tasks like deciding where to place ads, how much to spend, and what tweaks to make along the way. These agents are often described as “working on your behalf,” but remember—they’re programmed. They don’t have opinions. They follow commands.

Now, this kind of automation isn’t new. Machine-learning-based ad optimization has been around for years. Agencies have often pitched it as a competitive edge, but let’s be honest: if everyone has access to the same tools, it’s not really a differentiator. Where things get more exciting and different is with prompt-based agents. Think of it like how people use AI tools like Midjourney or Veo 3 to create stunning videos or images by writing detailed prompts.

That same kind of "vibe coding" is now being applied to media planning and buying. Instead of manually setting up campaigns, media planners can now guide AI agents by just giving them instructions in natural language. These agents can build audiences, choose channels, pull creative assets, and optimize performance—all while factoring in things like time of day, how often someone’s seen an ad before, and more.

As this evolves, we’re going to see fewer players dominating the ad space—platforms like Meta, Google, Amazon, and The Trade Desk. Because of this, it's becoming easier to create a connected system of agents that work seamlessly across these platforms.

This changes the role of media planners entirely. They’ll need to get really good at communicating with AI—not coding, but guiding. It's about being strategic in how you tell the agents what you want them to do.

Another important point: cookies aren't going anywhere. That means agents will continue to read user data to make decisions. So, the base layer of advertising—targeting, placement, optimization—is going to be almost fully automated. The creative ideas are layered on top.

It’s a wild time. I came up through traditional agencies. That experience taught me a lot and gave me the room to grow my skills. But I do worry. With so much of the groundwork becoming automated, there may be far fewer entry level roles in the future.
