Business SARS Moves to Tax Foreign Retirement Funds from 2026

August 18, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

The South African Revenue Service (SARS) and the National Treasury are planning to change the way foreign retirement funds are taxed. They've published a new draft law, the Taxation Laws Amendment Bill of 2025, and are asking the public to comment on it. The proposed change would scrap an existing tax exemption on foreign retirement income. If this goes ahead, South Africans who have worked overseas and built up retirement savings there as well as foreign nationals now living in South Africa could be taxed on those funds from 1 March 2026.

Right now, there's a special rule in place that allows lump sums, pensions, or annuities earned abroad to be exempt from South African tax, especially if they were earned while someone wasn’t a South African tax resident. This was originally put in place to avoid double taxation when two countries both try to tax the same income. But SARS now says this rule could be leading to a different problem: some retirement income is not being taxed by any country at all.

The National Treasury says this happens in two key ways. In some cases, foreign countries don’t tax the retirement income because their own laws or tax treaties don’t require it. And since South Africa also doesn’t tax it due to the exemption, the income ends up not being taxed at all. In other cases, even when international tax treaties say South Africa has the right to tax the income, it doesn’t use that right so the foreign country ends up taxing it instead, even though it technically isn’t supposed to. According to Treasury, this means South Africa is losing out on tax money it’s entitled to collect.

Tax experts are particularly concerned because South Africa is a popular destination for retirees, including expats returning home and foreigners choosing to settle here. The proposal could impact their retirement plans significantly. Financial advisers are warning that anyone who fits this profile should urgently take note of these changes and consider how they might be affected. Public comments are open until 12 September 2025. You can send your feedback to both the National Treasury and SARS at the following email addresses:

  • 2025AnnexCProp@treasury.gov.za
  • 2025legislationcomments@sars.gov.za

This is a major shift in tax policy, and if you're a South African tax resident with retirement savings overseas, it’s worth paying close attention.
