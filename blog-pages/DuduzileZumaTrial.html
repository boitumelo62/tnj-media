Media Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla to Stand Trial Over July Unrest Charges

November 4, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Former president Jacob Zuma’s daughter, Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla, is set to stand trial next week on charges of incitement to commit terrorism and public violence related to the July 2021 unrest that swept across KwaZulu-Natal and parts of Gauteng.

The National Prosecuting Authority confirmed that the case will be heard in the Johannesburg High Court, marking a significant moment in ongoing efforts to bring accountability for one of the most destructive episodes in democratic South Africa’s history. The July unrest left more than 350 people dead, thousands of businesses looted or destroyed, and billions of rand in damages.

Prosecutors allege that Zuma-Sambudla used social media platforms to encourage acts of defiance and unrest following her father’s imprisonment for contempt of court in 2021. They claim her posts contributed to the spread of violent protests and property destruction.

Her legal representatives have dismissed the charges as politically motivated, maintaining that her statements were expressions of opinion and solidarity with protesters, not direct calls to violence. Zuma-Sambudla has consistently denied any involvement in organizing or promoting violence.

Security analysts say the case could set a precedent for how digital communication is interpreted under South African law in matters of public security. It will also test the State’s ability to link social media activity to real-world consequences in the context of mass unrest.

Public opinion remains divided, with some viewing the charges as part of a political vendetta, while others believe they are necessary for accountability. The court has confirmed that proceedings will begin on Monday, with a full security detail expected given the public attention surrounding the trial.
