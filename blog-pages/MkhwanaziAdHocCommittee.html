Media Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi Faces Intense Questioning at Ad Hoc Committee Hearing

October 9, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

The ad hoc parliamentary hearing into alleged interference within the South African Police Service continued to expose deep divisions in the country’s security structures on Wednesday. Testifying before the committee, Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi, the Provincial Commissioner of KwaZulu Natal, presented a series of findings that painted a troubling picture of political influence, compromised investigations, and criminal infiltration at senior levels of law enforcement.

Mkhwanazi’s testimony followed weeks of speculation after leaked intelligence reports suggested that several covert police operations in the province had been compromised. Yesterday’s session confirmed that he had submitted both written statements and supporting data to the committee, detailing instances where high-profile investigations were allegedly obstructed or redirected. He said these interventions often came from individuals with direct political ties or business interests that overlapped with state contracts.

During the hearing, Mkhwanazi described what he called a “deliberate weakening of investigative capacity” through the removal of key officers and the disbandment of the Political Killings Task Team. According to him, the unit had been close to identifying networks behind a series of politically motivated murders before its work was abruptly halted. He claimed that some of those decisions were made without operational justification and warned that the consequences for public safety were already evident.

Committee members pressed the Provincial Commissioner to clarify whether his evidence had been verified through official channels. In response, Mkhwanazi insisted that his testimony was supported by documented communication records and witness accounts, which he said had been preserved for formal review. He further maintained that his goal was not to discredit the police service but to restore its integrity and independence from political manipulation.

The session also addressed the growing tension between the police leadership and Parliament. Several lawmakers questioned whether Mkhwanazi’s statements could undermine public confidence in law enforcement, while others commended his willingness to speak openly about systemic challenges. Observers present in the chamber noted a visibly tense atmosphere as questions about accountability and transparency dominated the day’s proceedings.

Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi concluded his testimony by urging the committee to ensure that protective measures were put in place for whistle-blowers and investigators. He warned that continued silence on internal corruption would endanger both officers and civilians and called for an urgent review of oversight mechanisms within the security sector.

The ad hoc committee adjourned late in the afternoon, announcing that the hearing would continue today, with additional witnesses expected to appear. The inquiry remains one of the most closely followed developments in Parliament, as it seeks to determine the scale of political interference in the country’s policing and intelligence networks.
