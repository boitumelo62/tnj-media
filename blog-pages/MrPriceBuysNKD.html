Business Mr Price Buys European Retailer NKD in Landmark R9.66bn Deal

December 10, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Mr Price is moving into Europe in a big way with its plan to buy NKD Group, a well established value retailer based in Germany, for about R9.66 billion. The company has agreed to acquire all the shares in Pegasus Group Holding GmbH, which owns and operates NKD’s retail business.

NKD has been around for 60 years and runs a large value focused apparel and homeware chain. Its head office is in Germany, and it operates more than 2,100 stores across seven European countries, including Germany, Austria, Italy, Croatia, Slovenia, the Czech Republic and Poland. The group employs around 10,000 people and reported net sales of €684.57 million for the year ending December 2024.

Mr Price described NKD as a retailer that understands its market well. It focuses on quality, affordable private label products for the whole family and avoids high fashion risk. NKD’s small store format about 300 square metres on average keeps rental and operating costs low, especially because many of its stores are located in smaller towns. The company also benefits from efficient logistics, disciplined spending and a strong supply chain, which have helped it grow margins. Mr Price also praised NKD’s experienced management team, saying their culture aligns closely with its own.

NKD sees room to grow further in all seven countries where it already operates, and it is also looking at long term expansion opportunities across Europe. Mr Price has been expanding rapidly in South Africa over the past few years and has spent a lot of time researching new markets. Studies show that value retailing is growing faster than the overall global retail market and now makes up about 22% of total European retail.

If the deal goes through, it will significantly increase the size of the Mr Price Group, lifting revenue to around R53 billion, pushing the store count beyond 5,000 and increasing total employees to more than 40,000. CEO Mark Blair said that after meeting NKD’s leadership, it became clear that they shared the same focus on value retail and knew exactly how to serve their core customer.

The structure of the deal is fairly straightforward. NKD is owned by Pegasus, a German company, which in turn is owned by a UK firm called Fliegendes Pferd. Mr Price will buy Pegasus as well as the shareholder loan receivables that were previously extended to NKD. The agreed upon purchase price for Pegasus is €415 million, which will increase slightly depending on the time between the valuation date and the actual closing date. NKD’s enterprise value after taking debt, cash and working capital into account is €500 million.

Mr Price is also paying €38.5 million for the shareholder loan receivables. Altogether, the maximum transaction value comes to €487 million, or roughly R9.66 billion. The entire purchase will be paid in cash, using a mix of the company’s own funds and new debt. The transaction is expected to be finalised in the second quarter of 2026, provided regulators give their approval.

For the six months ending June 2025, NKD generated €344 million in sales and posted a profit after tax of €6.49 million, excluding the impact of certain financial charges. The net assets being acquired as of June 2025 are valued at €91.14 million. The deal marks a major step for Mr Price as it moves beyond South Africa and into a growing segment of the European retail market.
