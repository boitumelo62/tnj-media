Business Boxer On Track for 60 Stores as Earnings Hold Strong

October 13, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Boxer has had a busy and largely successful start to its 2025 financial year. In the first half alone, the retailer opened 25 new stores and is planning to add another 35 by year end, putting it on track to meet its target of 60 store openings for the full year. In its interim financial results, Boxer reported a strong performance despite the tough retail environment marked by low selling price inflation.

The company’s ability to grow volumes at existing stores helped drive its results, which it sees as a positive sign heading into the second half of the year. Turnover for the six months rose by 13.9%, reaching R22.5 billion. Trading profit was also up, increasing 15.1% to R931 million, with a trading margin of 4.1%. Even though Boxer is incurring extra costs as a newly listed company, it managed to keep its trading margin steady thanks to strong sales performance and solid growth in commission and other income.

Boxer has also started tapping into customer data through its new loyalty programme, the Boxer Rewards Club, which is expected to be a useful tool going forward. Headline earnings for the period rose by 5.3% to R518 million. Boxer says this is a solid outcome considering the costs associated with its IPO. However, the company did see a steep 79.1% jump in net finance costs, largely due to balance sheet restructuring linked to its stock market debut in November 2024.

Its rapid store expansion also brought accounting challenges, particularly a rise in lease related interest costs under IFRS 16. Although headline earnings increased, headline earnings per share dropped by 30.3%. This was due to the increase in shares in circulation following the IPO, which saw 157.4 million new shares issued effectively diluting existing shareholders. Still, the company declared its first post-IPO dividend at 45.3 cents per share, representing a 40% payout.

Looking ahead, Boxer says trading momentum picked up in July and August and has remained strong in the six weeks since the end of the interim period. While this is encouraging, the company notes that its performance for the second half will heavily depend on how it does during Black Friday and the festive season both key trading periods. The company expects some margin pressure in the second half compared to the 5.4% margin it achieved in the full 2025 financial year, but the final outcome will depend on how well the business performs during those peak months.
