Media How SAB Balances Bold Creativity with Purpose and Impact

August 26, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Back in November 2024, South African Breweries (SAB) had a big night at the Effies South Africa awards, picking up nine accolades across different categories. Among them were two standout campaigns that became Grand Effie Contenders one was Carling Black Label’s Carling Cup 2023 with its Fak’UGesi initiative, and the other was Castle Lager’s Bread of the Nation, which turns brewing by product into bread to fight food insecurity.

These aren’t just clever marketing plays they’re about finding purpose driven ways to connect with people on real issues. At the time, SAB’s vice president of marketing, Vaughn Croeser, said their goal is to put people at the heart of their campaigns, using creativity to address not just consumer needs, but societal challenges too. When we caught up with him recently, that same thinking came through strongly in how he explained SAB’s continued cultural relevance in South Africa.

Vaughn believes the key to SAB’s long standing success is a focus on staying relevant without chasing every fleeting trend. Instead of jumping from one “next big thing” to another, they stick to what really matters: telling stories that resonate with people, staying consistent, and always innovating. Their brands are built on trust and shared moments the kind that happen at a braai, a soccer match, or a concert. They’ve earned their place in South African life by delivering quality and creating emotional connections that last.

When asked about navigating the country’s complex and diverse cultural landscape, he explained that SAB doesn’t view this as a challenge, but rather as a responsibility. Their campaigns are grounded in deep local insights and crafted to speak authentically to the communities they serve. It’s why bold efforts like the #NoExcuse campaign against gender-based violence work because they’re honest, thoughtful, and built on respect. It’s never about ticking boxes, it’s about reflecting real people’s lives and doing it with care.

At the centre of SAB’s marketing strategy is the belief that brand love is something you earn. It’s not about gimmicks it’s about being responsible, relevant and innovative. Whether that’s launching alcohol free options like Castle Free or creating unforgettable experiences like Castle Lite Unlocks or the Corona Sunset Festivals, everything they do is about reflecting how people’s needs and lifestyles are changing. But even as they evolve, they stay anchored in purpose whether it's promoting moderation or empowering communities.

With such a wide range of brands under the SAB umbrella, you might wonder how they keep everything cohesive without blending them all together. According to Vaughn, the trick is giving each brand a clear purpose and distinct personality. So while Castle Lager might represent national unity and pride, Brutal Fruit is all about self expression and empowerment. Stella Artois appeals to those seeking premium, refined experiences, and Black Crown speaks to boldness and modern African royalty. They’re all different, but they’re all grounded in quality, relevance and innovation that’s the golden thread.

On the topic of AI, which has become the “shiny new thing” in marketing, Vaughn explained that SAB is embracing it but not in a way that replaces human creativity. AI helps them be smarter and faster with data, campaign optimisation, and audience engagement. But at the end of the day, it’s still human intuition and storytelling that give their work soul and meaning. That’s the balance they’re committed to keeping, now and in the future. Winning the Effie Top Marketer award was more than just a trophy it was a powerful validation of their approach.

For Vaughn personally, it was a celebration of the team’s efforts people who blend commercial smarts with bold creative thinking. The recognition wasn’t just for one campaign, but for a system that prioritises impact, quality and purpose. Not every campaign lands perfectly. Vaughn is quick to admit that they’ve got things wrong before and he thinks that’s how it should be. Pushing boundaries and staying culturally relevant means sometimes missing the mark. What matters is how they respond by learning, adjusting, and continuing to move forward with humility and intention. That, more than anything, is probably the heart of SAB’s staying power. They’re not trying to be perfect. They’re trying to be meaningful. And in a fast-changing world, that’s what keeps people coming back.
