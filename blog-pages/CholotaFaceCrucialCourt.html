Media Cholota to Face Crucial Court Decision in Asbestos Case Next Month

January 27, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Moroadi Cholota, the former personal assistant to ex Free State premier Ace Magashule, will have to wait until next month to learn whether the state’s case against her in the asbestos fraud and corruption matter will move forward.

Cholota avoided prosecution last year after the Bloemfontein High Court upheld her special plea challenging the legality of her extradition from the United States. She argued that the process was unlawful and motivated by bad faith after she declined to testify against Magashule.

The state later took the matter to the Constitutional Court, which last week ruled that Cholota’s extradition had indeed been unlawful. However, the apex court overturned the earlier decision that barred her prosecution, opening the door for the case against her to continue.

As a result, Cholota will now be allowed to present further arguments in support of her special plea when the trial within a trial resumes next month. These proceedings will determine whether the corruption charges against her can proceed despite the extradition ruling.

Judge Philip Loubser addressed the matter during short proceedings in the Bloemfontein High Court on Monday. He confirmed that the trial within a trial has been postponed to Tuesday 3 February 2026, with the court aiming to deliver judgment by 18 February 2026 in line with the Constitutional Court’s directive.

Loubser also announced that the main trial involving all accused parties has been postponed to 2 March 2026.

The case centres on alleged fraud and corruption linked to a R255 million asbestos removal tender in the Free State. When the main trial resumes in March, all accused are expected to face charges relating to the controversial contract.
