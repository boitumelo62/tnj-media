Media Tragedy at Ekapa Mine Ends as All Five Trapped Workers Are Recovered

March 23, 2026 ● Tebuho Zongwana

All five miners who were trapped underground following a mudslide at the Ekapa Mine have now been recovered, bringing a tragic end to weeks of rescue efforts. Authorities confirmed that the final bodies were retrieved after an intensive operation led by specialised mine rescue teams.

The miners had been trapped since a mud rush incident in February, which flooded underground sections of the mine and made access extremely difficult. Initial recovery efforts were slowed by hazardous conditions, including water accumulation and unstable debris.

The first body was brought to the surface earlier in March, with subsequent recoveries taking place over several days until all five victims were located. Minister of Mineral and Petroleum Resources Gwede Mantashe visited the site following the final recovery, as government pledged support for the affected families and called for a full investigation into the incident.

The tragedy has also raised renewed concerns about safety standards in the mining sector. Industry stakeholders and labour representatives have pointed to the need for stricter oversight to prevent similar disasters. Compounding the situation, the mine has halted operations and entered a process of liquidation, leaving more than a thousand workers facing an uncertain future without jobs or income.

The incident has sent shockwaves through the mining community and reignited debate over worker safety, corporate responsibility, and the sustainability of struggling mining operations. As investigations begin, attention will turn to determining the exact cause of the mudslide and whether any preventative measures could have avoided the loss of life.

For now, the focus remains on supporting grieving families and ensuring that the victims are laid to rest with dignity.
