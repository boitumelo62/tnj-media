Media Joburg woman Loses Big as Court Hands Husband a Rare Divorce Victory

January 21, 2026 ● Tebogo Zongwana

A woman’s extramarital affair and her decision to celebrate it publicly on Facebook have ended in a costly legal defeat after a court ordered that she forfeit her share of her husband’s pension in divorce proceedings.

The South Gauteng High Court in Johannesburg ruled that the wife’s conduct, which included having a child with her husband’s friend and publicly announcing a baby shower on social media, amounted to serious misconduct that resulted in her being unduly benefited.

The couple were married in community of property in January 2011. In August 2024, the wife initiated divorce proceedings, seeking a decree of divorce and an equal division of the joint estate. Both parties accepted that the marriage had irretrievably broken down and initially agreed that the estate should be divided equally.

The dispute arose when the husband applied for an order that his wife forfeit her fifty percent share of his pension benefits from the Municipal Gratuity Fund after it emerged that she was involved in a relationship with his friend.

In evidence before the court, the husband testified that he discovered the affair and later learned that it had resulted in a child. He told the court that the situation became intolerable and led to his wife being chased from the marital home.

The husband further explained that throughout the marriage the wife was unemployed and that he carried the responsibility for meeting the family’s financial needs. He said he only became aware of the pregnancy after seeing a Facebook post announcing his wife’s baby shower.

In its analysis, the court emphasised that the burden rests on the party seeking forfeiture to prove that the other party would be unduly benefited if such an order is not granted. The court considered the factors set out in section 9 of the Divorce Act, including the duration of the marriage, the circumstances leading to the breakdown and any substantial misconduct.

Judge Noluntu Bam acknowledged that although the husband was the primary financial provider, this did not mean the wife made no contribution to the marriage. The court recognised the role of a stay at home parent and the unseen work involved in maintaining a household and supporting family life.

However, despite recognising these non financial contributions, the court found that granting the wife half of the husband’s pension would amount to an undue benefit. Significant weight was placed on her relationship with a third party and the fact that she had a child with that person while still married.

The court further found that the public announcement of the baby shower on Facebook humiliated the husband and aggravated the misconduct.

Judge Bam stated that the misconduct went beyond the affair itself and included procreating with a third party and publicly celebrating the birth on social media. The combination of these actions was found to be deeply humiliating to the husband.

The court ultimately ruled in favour of the husband and ordered that the wife forfeit her share of his pension benefits.
