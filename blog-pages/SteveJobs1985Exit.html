Tech News Steve Jobs' 1985 Exit from Apple Helped Shape the Mac's Future

July 25, 2025 ● Tebuho Zongwana

Forty years ago, Steve Jobs, Apple’s iconic cofounder, was forced out of the company he helped build. While the move shocked the tech world at the time, it ultimately played a pivotal role in shaping the evolution of the Mac and Apple’s long-term success.

Jobs’ departure in 1985 came after internal power struggles following the disappointing commercial performance of the original Macintosh. Although the machine broke ground in personal computing, sales fell short of expectations. This prompted Apple’s board to support then Chief Executive Officer John Sculley in removing Jobs from any significant role in the company.

Freed from Apple’s internal politics, Jobs went on to create NeXT, a computer company that, while not commercially dominant, focused on high-performance machines for education and business. Importantly, NeXT's software innovations laid the groundwork for what would later become the Mac operating system. When Apple acquired NeXT in 1997, Jobs returned to the company, bringing that advanced software architecture with him.

The influence of NeXT is still evident today in Apple’s operating systems. macOS, iOS, and their successors all trace their foundations to NeXTSTEP, the operating system developed under Jobs’ leadership during his time away from Apple.

Ironically, being removed from Apple allowed Jobs to explore bold ideas without corporate constraints. These ventures proved critical to Apple’s future, helping turn it into the global technology powerhouse it is today.

Although his exit was seen as a major loss at the time, it may have been one of the most beneficial events for both Jobs and Apple. His return marked a new era, introducing the iMac, iPod, iPhone, and a revitalized Mac lineup. These products would go on to define the brand for decades.
