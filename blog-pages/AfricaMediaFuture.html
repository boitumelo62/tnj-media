Media Designing Africa’s Media Future

October 1, 2025 ● Uhone Kwinda

Popular Blogs:

Marketing’s Mismatch Consumer Attention vs. Media Investment

In Africa, a continent rich in diversity but full of contradictions, trust has become both its most precious and most fragile currency. This isn’t just about governments or institutions anymore. In today’s digital age, it's about who holds the mic, who gets heard, and whether their influence is rooted in integrity or opportunism. The recent scandal involving South African influencers promoting a so-called “work-study” program in Russia later linked to forced labor was more than just a social media misstep. It tore open a deeper issue.

Creators like Seemah and Zillewizzy, once basking in the spotlight, quickly fell from grace. Millions of followers vanished almost overnight. And the backlash wasn’t limited to likes and comments it spilled into public discourse and reputations. But Provit Chemmani, a leader at Havas Media Network, sees this moment not as an isolated scandal but as a defining fork in the road. He compares it to the era when doctors endorsed cigarettes before public awareness caught up. Now, he says, Africa must decide: chase virality at any cost, or build something more meaningful an ecosystem rooted in responsibility, transparency, and cultural awareness.

Africa’s creator economy is growing fast, worth more than $3 billion. But growth without values is a dangerous game. Influence, without integrity, becomes a liability. Chemmani is calling on brands, agencies, and platforms to build a new foundation where ethics aren't an afterthought, but a starting point. To him, trust isn’t a metric you track in spreadsheets it’s a public good. In a place where media often fills in where governments or institutions fall short, the role of influence is even more crucial. It shouldn’t just drive sales. It should educate, empower, and build communities.

At Havas, this thinking is already shaping how they work. Through Havas Play, they’re connecting brands with African culture in ways that matter not just financially, but socially. And Havas Market is shifting the focus from transactions to transformation, aiming to create real, measurable change in people’s lives. But the conversation doesn’t stop at content. Media buying how ads are placed, tracked, and paid for is also evolving. At the recent Audience & Advertising Summit, leaders across the industry acknowledged a need for change. With tools like AI and blockchain, campaigns can now be more targeted and less prone to fraud. Still, Chemmani cautions, tech can’t fix a trust crisis. Algorithms don’t build relationships people do.

He emphasizes that true leadership in this space means knowing the communities you’re trying to reach, respecting their contexts, and being fully accountable for what your brand puts out into the world. Africa’s media environment is incredibly fragmented, mirroring its many cultures and markets. But to Chemmani, that’s not a problem it’s an opportunity. It’s a place to show a new kind of leadership, one that’s agile, inclusive, and deeply rooted in local understanding. He says Africa doesn’t need more handouts it needs agency. That starts with Africans owning their data, building their infrastructure, and telling their stories on their own terms. From Nigeria’s booming film industry to Kenya’s tech innovations and Ghana’s vibrant creative sector, the continent is proving that growth and authenticity can go hand in hand.

At the Africa CEO Forum earlier this year, one question dominated: can a stronger partnership between government and business unlock Africa’s potential? For Chemmani, the answer lies in execution getting clear on strategy, and doing the hard work of delivery. He believes media leaders today aren’t just selling ads they’re shaping the future. Their job is to build trust, not just traffic. To uplift creators who care about truth, not just trends. And to make sure every campaign adds to the common good, not just the bottom line. Africa is no longer waiting for validation. It’s creating its future boldly and unapologetically. And those who want to play a role in that future need to bring more than budgets and buzzwords. They need values.
